## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Nava is not a ferro-alloy company by profit. It is a Zambian coal-power IPP with an Indian ferro-alloy and power side business.**
   - FY26 MEL (Zambia): revenue **US$256.0 Mn**, EBITDA **US$151.4 Mn**, PAT **US$81.4 Mn** [V Q1 FY27 PPT, FY26 column]. At ~₹88/$ that is ≈ ₹2,250 Cr revenue (~52% of consolidated ₹4,291 Cr) and ≈ ₹1,330 Cr EBITDA (~70% of company EBITDA ₹1,905 Cr) [E].
   - Nava's ferro alloys: FY26 SiMn **sales 134,410 t**, production 127,486 t; FeSi sales 8,039 t [V]. At an assumed ₹80–90k/t realisation that is ≈ ₹1,100–1,250 Cr revenue [E]. The segment EBIT is not in the files fetched (screener segment values are login-gated).
   - Any "ferro-alloys sector" aggregate that includes Nava's consolidated P&L is **~60–70% Zambian power by EBITDA**. Part 3 therefore shows (a) the straight two-company sum and (b) a ferro-alloy-only lens, built from IMFA plus an [E] for Nava's alloy segment, and labels each.
2. **Nava's "EBITDA" has three numbers.**
   - **Company EBITDA FY26 ₹1,905.2 Cr = 42.5% of *total income* ₹4,478.7 Cr.** It includes other income (₹187.7 Cr) and an **expected-credit-loss (ECL) reversal of ₹137.1 Cr**, booked as a negative expense as ZESCO paid down arbitration arrears [V Q1 PPT].
   - **Screener operating profit FY26 ₹1,718 Cr (40% of revenue)** excludes other income [V screener].
   - **Clean operating EBITDA ex-ECL ≈ ₹1,581 Cr (36.8% of revenue)** [E: 1,718 − 137]. That falls inside management's own "35–40%" consolidated guide [V Q1 call]. The primer's "~44% margin" is the company figure on a total-income basis *plus* ECL write-backs; FY25 was flattered even more.
   - **For the peer table use screener OP (₹1,718 Cr, 40%), and say in prose that ~₹137 Cr of it is non-recurring ECL reversal.** ECL reversals are now "minuscule" (Q1 FY27 ₹4.4 Cr) because arrears are nearly fully realised [V].
3. **Nava's PAT: pre-minority vs attributable.** Screener and company "PAT" (FY26 ₹1,039 Cr; Q1 FY27 ₹332.8 Cr) are **before the 35% non-controlling interest in MEL**. Attributable profit is lower:
   - FY26 EPS ₹27.80 × ~28.3 Cr shares ≈ **₹787 Cr attributable** [E].
   - Q1 FY27 EPS ₹9.80 → ≈ **₹277 Cr** [E].
   - TTM: screener P/E 19.0 on m-cap ₹14,416 Cr implies **₹759 Cr attributable TTM** vs ₹972 Cr pre-minority TTM [E].
   - Oddity: FY23 and FY24 EPS × shares ≈ reported PAT (₹1,222 / ₹1,256 Cr), i.e. near-zero NCI share in those years. FY25 implies ~₹1,100 Cr attributable vs ₹1,434 Cr. The likely cause is MEL's earlier accumulated losses or NCI deficit absorbing the minority share, but this is **unverified; flag it**. **Always quote P/E on attributable earnings (screener's 19.0×), never m-cap ÷ ₹1,039 Cr.**
4. **Nava standalone ≠ India operating profit.** FY26 standalone PAT **₹910.9 Cr (+116%)** includes a **₹403.9 Cr exceptional gain** and **₹317 Cr of other income**, mostly dividends and buyback proceeds upstreamed from Nava Global (US$50 Mn buyback in FY26; a US$15 Mn dividend in Q1 FY27) [V Q1 PPT; primer]. Q1 FY27 standalone other income ₹166.6 Cr also includes the Nava Global dividend. **Standalone profit is a cash-upstreaming number, not an Indian operating number.** Indian operating EBITDA ex other income was ≈ ₹152 Cr in Q1 FY27 [E: 318.6 − 166.6].
5. **Nava's tax line is a kwacha bet.** MEL's deferred tax swings with the ZMW/USD rate. FY26 consolidated tax was ₹462 Cr (31% effective) including ~₹261 Cr deferred tax. Q4 FY26 tax was 58% (₹188.8 Cr on PBT ₹325 Cr); Q1 FY27 carried a **₹25.2 Cr DTL charge "with further appreciation of Zambian kwacha"** [V PPT; V call]. The CFO said it "may get even reversed". The FY26 PAT fall (−28%) was **mostly tax, not operations** (PBT fell only 6.6%, from ₹1,607 to ₹1,501 Cr) [V screener].
6. **IMFA: standalone ≈ consolidated, but "revenue" differs by ~₹66 Cr between sources.** Screener FY26 sales **₹2,826 Cr** = company "revenue from operations" ₹2,826.31 Cr [V]. The primer's "~₹2,892 Cr" is **total income** (revenue + ₹65 Cr other income). Growth: +10.2% on revenue (₹2,565 → ₹2,826 Cr), not the primer's "+9%" on a mixed base. Use ₹2,826 Cr.
7. **IMFA capacity has three numbers in the same filing pack.**
   - The earnings release says "installed furnace capacity 289 MVA capable of producing **434,000 tpa**".
   - The presentation says installed capacity "**FY26: 384,000 TPA; FY27: 484,000; FY28: 534,000**".
   - The primer says "~0.5 MTPA" and "past half a million tonnes" *already in FY26*.
   - Reconciliation [E]: legacy Therubali + Choudwar ~284k tpa (190 MVA) + KNR-2 100k tpa (99 MVA) = **384k tpa**. The 434k figure probably counts KNR-2's pending **50k tpa furnace (still awaiting environmental clearance; "middle of 2027 or slightly longer")** [V call]. **KNR-1 greenfield (100k tpa, 66 MVA)** came on in Aug–Oct 2026 → 484k (FY27) → 534k (FY28, with the 50k furnace) = **355 MVA**.
   - **Production ≠ capacity:** FY26 production was 267,301 t. FY27 guidance was **cut from ~400k to ~380k t** on the 4-Aug call (KNR-2 transformer and gas-cleaning issues). FY28 guidance of 475–500k t is maintained [V call]. The primer still carries 400k for FY27.
8. **"Export share" for IMFA is 60:40, 80% or 95% depending on the speaker.**
   - FY26 exports ₹2,396 Cr = **85% of revenue** [V release].
   - Q1 FY27: ₹793.6 Cr of ₹960.5 Cr = **83%**. The MD said "95% exports at this stage"; the head of ferro alloys said **~19% domestic** sales [V call].
   - "~80% export sales supported through long-term contracts, primarily Far East" [V PPT].
   - The primer's "60:40 export-to-domestic target" is a **stated aspiration** (management has talked of raising domestic to ~40% as FACOR and Indian stainless grow), not the current mix.
9. **"Ferro alloys" covers two separate businesses with different end-markets and cost drivers. Never average them.**
   - **Chrome alloys (FeCr; IMFA, Tata Steel FAMD, FACOR, Balasore):** ~90% goes to **stainless steel**. Inputs are chrome ore (~2.5 t ore per t FeCr at IMFA [V call]), coke (~0.65 t/t [V call; "kg" in the transcript is a slip]) and power (~3,500–4,000 kWh/t). Price is set by China's stainless mills and South African supply. India is a net **exporter**.
   - **Manganese alloys (SiMn, FeMn; Nava, Maithan, Shyam, Sarda, many Chhattisgarh/Odisha/AP units):** ~90%+ goes to **carbon steel** (~10–12 kg Mn alloy per t steel). Inputs are manganese ore (mostly imported: South Africa, Gabon, Australia) and power (~3,800–4,500 kWh/t SiMn). India is the world's **largest exporter** of Mn alloys [V-snip IFAPA], with domestic overcapacity.
   - Nava (SiMn) and IMFA (FeCr) therefore share almost nothing commercially except the smelting technology and power intensity.
10. **IMFA's per-tonne numbers are company-defined.**
    - "Average realisation per tonne" (₹/t of FeCr sold): **Q1 FY26 ₹95,165; Q4 FY26 ₹109,352; Q1 FY27 ₹119,888** ("just shy of ₹120,000, blended").
    - "EBITDA cost per MT": **₹77,575 → ₹81,932 → ₹85,884** [V PPT p.9–10; ordering confirmed by the MD on the call: cost "rising from closer to 82,000 to 86,000"].
    - EBITDA/t computed on sales tonnes: **Q1 FY27 ₹35,484/t** (₹281.27 Cr ÷ 79,268 t); **FY26 ₹21,739/t** (₹587.23 Cr ÷ 270,124 t) [E arithmetic]. Management's "through-cycle ~₹20–22k/t" framing (an analyst's paraphrase on the call; the MD declined to guide) fits FY26.
    - Realisation − cost ≈ ₹34,000/t in Q1, close to the computed EBITDA/t. The ~₹1,500/t gap is other operating income and timing [E].

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Two members, two different metals, one common physics: electric smelting is a power business.**
  - **IMFA** is a pure-play, fully integrated **high-carbon ferrochrome** producer. Captive chromite (Sukinda, Mahagiri; FY26 ore raising **810,612 t**, record) feeds 200 MW of captive coal power + 4.55 MWp solar, which feeds furnaces at Therubali, Choudwar and (since FY26) Kalinganagar. Output goes to stainless mills, **~83–85% exported**, mostly to China, Japan, Korea and Taiwan [V release; screener "about"].
  - **Nava** is a **silico-manganese** producer (Paloncha, Telangana; Kharagprasad, Odisha) with **434 MW of Indian thermal power** (8 plants, ~414 MW operating; partly captive, mostly merchant) and, decisively, **Maamba Energy (Zambia): the country's largest coal mine + a 300 MW mine-mouth plant on a 20-year PPA with ZESCO**. ### 1.2 Value chain (ore → alloy → steel mill)

1. **Mine.** Chrome ore (IMFA mines its own: Sukinda + Mahagiri; FY26 810,612 t, of which >536,000 t underground) [primer; V release]. Indian chromite is mined by open-cast in the Sukinda ultramafic belt but is moving underground (IMFA's Sukinda 3.51 → 6 lakh tpa by switching opencast→UG; Mahagiri UG 3 → 6 lakh tpa) [V ICRA]. Manganese ore for SiMn is largely bought (MOIL monthly price list; imports CIF Vizag).
2. **Beneficiate and agglomerate.** Friable chromite (Indian ore is mostly friable/fine) must be briquetted or pelletised and sintered before it can go into a submerged-arc furnace (SAF). That costs capex and power, and it is a reason Indian FeCr is costlier than South African lumpy-ore smelting [K].
3. **Smelt.** In a submerged-arc furnace, ore + reductant (met coke ~0.65 t/t FeCr at IMFA [V call]; char, anthracite) + flux are melted at 1,600–1,700°C. Ratings in MVA: IMFA's 190 MVA legacy → 289 MVA with KNR-2 → 355 MVA with KNR-1 [V]. Electricity is the dominant variable cost.
4. **Sell.** FeCr goes to stainless mills: export (China, Japan, Korea, Taiwan; Europe minor) or domestic (Jindal Stainless, JSL Hisar/Jajpur, Viraj, Chromeni, SAIL Salem) [K]. SiMn goes to carbon-steel mills (integrated BF-BOF and induction-furnace secondary steel) and export (Europe ~40% of India's ferro-alloy exports per Argus; Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Middle East) [V-snip].
5. **Captive power** sits beside both. Coal-fired CPPs (linkage, e-auction, washery rejects) and now RE PPAs. Surplus goes to the state grid or power exchanges (IEX). In Odisha the grid tariff for surplus is "non-remunerative" (ICRA) [V].

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **Ore ownership captures the most value in chrome.** Integrated producers (IMFA; Tata Steel FAMD; Glencore-Merafe; Samancor; ERG) earn the ore rent. Converters buying ore at OMC auctions or importing South African UG2 concentrate earn only a conversion margin. India's auction regime has pushed premia so high that legacy leases are now the moat. ICRA: IMFA's captive mines, "awarded under the earlier allotment regime are valid till 2049 and 2055," give "a distinct competitive edge" [V].
- **Power cost captures the most value in manganese alloys**, because Mn ore is a traded commodity everyone pays the same for. A SiMn maker on captive coal power at ~₹3–4/kWh vs grid at ~₹6.5–8/kWh saves ~₹10,000–15,000/t on a ~₹75–80k/t product [E].
- **Nava's real value capture is outside ferro alloys:**
  - MEL's mine-mouth coal plant under a US$ PPA (FY26 EBITDA US$151 Mn on US$256 Mn revenue = 59% incl. ECL reversal) [V].
  - India merchant/IPP power (2,274 MU sold FY26) [V].
  - Ferro alloys earned only **₹40 Cr EBITDA in FY25 (after a ₹59 Cr loss in FY24)** per CRISIL [V CRISIL Feb-2026]. On ~₹1,000–1,200 Cr of alloy revenue that is a **~3–4% margin [E]**, not the primer's "mid-teens".

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Stainless steel (for FeCr) — ~90% of chromium demand** [primer; K ICDA].
   - **China (64% of stainless):** property weakness 2023–25 hurt 300-series demand, but output still grew. Mill tender prices (Tsingshan, TISCO, Baosteel Desheng) set the world FeCr price monthly. Chinese FeCr is smelted from **imported South African ore**, so Chinese cost = SA UG2 concentrate price + Chinese power. When SA ore is scarce or dear, the Chinese cost floor rises (ICRA: Chinese smelting costs "heavily influenced by the seaborne price of chrome ore … mainly from South Africa") [V ICRA].
   - **Indonesia:** Tsingshan/Delong nickel-pig-iron-to-stainless clusters (Morowali, Weda Bay) produce 200/300-series. Increasingly integrated with local FeCr smelting (imports of chrome ore from SA/Philippines) [K].
   - **India:** ~4.5–5 mt stainless [K]. IMFA's Q1 caller flagged that Indian stainless re-rollers import Chinese/Indonesian flats instead of melting domestically, which weakens Indian FeCr demand. The MD declined to comment beyond "level playing field" [V call]. Stainless is **excluded** from India's flat-steel safeguard duty [V-snip].
   - **Japan/Korea/Taiwan/Europe:** long-term contract buyers for IMFA; "~80% export sales supported through long-term contracts primarily in Far East markets" [V PPT].
2. **Carbon steel (for Mn alloys) — ~90% of Mn-alloy demand** [K].
   - ~10–12 kg of Mn alloy per tonne of crude steel; secondary (induction furnace) steel uses more SiMn per tonne; BOF flat steel uses more FeMn [K].
   - India: steel demand +8–9%/yr; capacity build-out toward 300 mt; **flat-steel safeguard duty 12% (to Apr-2026) → 11.5% → 11% (to Apr-2028)** (customs notification 02/2025-Customs (SG), 30-Dec-2025, retrospective from the provisional duty of 21-Apr-2025) [V-snip]. Rolled steel imports fell 38.7% YoY in H1 FY26 to 3.04 mt (MyMetalogic) [V-snip]. That supports domestic SiMn/FeMn demand.
   - Exports: the **EU (~40% of India's ferro-alloy exports)** imposed **definitive safeguard quotas from 2026: India SiMn 126,800 t (threshold price €1,392/t), FeMn 69,900 t (€1,316/t)**. Argus says they cut India's potential EU shipments "by almost half" [V-snip Argus]. Plus CBAM (see §7). **The EU export door is narrowing for Nava/Maithan-type exporters** [E].
3. **Power (for Nava's earnings):**
   - Zambia's grid is ~80%+ hydro (Kariba, Kafue). The 2024 drought slashed hydro output and caused 17–21-hour load-shedding; Maamba's dispatchable coal became critical [K]. The government is adding solar (Chisamba 200 MW, commissioned Jul-2026 [V-snip]; Nava's 100 MW Maamba solar, Sep-2026 [V]).
   - India: merchant power prices on IEX (₹4–6/kWh averages; summer peaks at the ₹10 cap) drive Nava's merchant units. Nava sold 893 MU (Nava) + 714 MU (NBEIL) merchant in FY26 [V PPT].

**End-market split for the two members [E]**
- IMFA: ~100% stainless (export ~80–85%, domestic ~15–20%).
- Nava (consolidated revenue FY26 ₹4,291 Cr): Zambia power + coal ~52%; India power ~20–25%; ferro alloys (carbon steel) ~25–28%; agri/healthcare/other ~1–2% [E from MEL US$256 Mn, India power units × ~₹4.5–5/kWh, SiMn tonnes × ~₹85k/t].

## 7. Regulation and policy

**7.1 Mining law (chrome ore, manganese ore)** [K unless tagged]
- **MMDR Act 1957, amended 2015:** auctions became mandatory for new mineral concessions.
  - Section 8A(5): existing merchant leases were extended to 31-Mar-2020 and then auctioned. Odisha auctioned most expired merchant mines in 2020, including chromite blocks, at very high premia.
  - Section 8A(6): captive leases were deemed extended to **31-Mar-2030 or 50 years from the date of grant, whichever is later**. This is why IMFA's leases run to **2049 and 2055** (ICRA) [V] rather than expiring in 2030. **The "2030 expiry" risk applies to captive leases granted before ~1980; it does not appear to bite IMFA** [E inference from ICRA; verify lease grant dates].
- **MMDR Amendment 2021:** abolished the captive/merchant distinction for future auctions. **Captive mines may sell up to 50% of annual production** in the open market after meeting the linked end-use plant's needs, on payment of an additional amount. Leases can be transferred. Statutory clearances transfer to the new lessee for 2 years after a lease change [K]. For IMFA this gives an option to sell surplus ore (it now has ~6 lakh t of ore stock, built to feed expansion) [V call].
- **Royalty:** chromite **15% of the IBM-published average sale price (ASP)**; manganese ore 5% [K]. Plus **DMF** (30% of royalty for pre-2015 leases; 10% for auctioned) and **NMET** 2% of royalty [K]. Auctioned mines additionally pay the bid premium (% of the value of dispatched mineral). Higher FeCr prices raise ore ASPs, and so raise IMFA's royalty: the MD cited "only if royalty prices move up a little bit, that has an impact" [V call].
- **Supreme Court, 25-Jul-2024 (Mineral Area Development Authority v SAIL)** and the **14-Aug-2024 retrospectivity order:** states may tax mineral rights and mineral-bearing land. Retrospective demands can go back to 1-Apr-2005, payable over 12 years from 1-Apr-2026, with interest/penalty waived for the period before 25-Jul-2024. **Odisha has not yet decided** (as of ICRA Dec-2025) [V ICRA]. This is a contingent liability for every Odisha miner, IMFA included. Status in 2026 unverified.
- **Sukinda environment:** stricter rules against shallow open-cast chromite mining; hexavalent-chromium contamination of the Brahmani/Damsal watershed [K]. IMFA's shift underground (Sukinda opencast → UG 6 lakh tpa) [V ICRA].
- **Odisha auctions/OMC:** OMC holds large chromite leases and sells through monthly e-auctions with base prices revised monthly (−6% for Dec-2025, +13% for Feb-2026) [V-snip]. The Odisha Directorate of Mines tenders chromite blocks periodically; no 2025–26 chromite premium was found [V-snip gap].
- **Critical minerals:** chromium is on India's 30-mineral critical list (Jul-2023) [K]. IMFA bid for critical-mineral blocks but lost [V call].

**7.2 Trade policy** [verify current CBIC tariff]
- **Chrome ore exports:** "restricted" under DGFT export policy (HS 2610), canalised through MMTC/with authorisation. Historic **30% export duty** on chrome ore (a 2016–17 budget proposal to scrap it drew industry criticism; 2023 reports say the duty is retained) [V-snip eximguru/BigMint]. **Current 2026 rate unverified; flag.** The effect is that Indian ore stays home, which makes Indian FeCr smelting the only route to monetise Odisha chromite.
- **Ferro-alloy exports:** no export duty known [K]. Exports earn RoDTEP remission (rates ~0.5–1% [K, unverified]).
- **Import duty on ferro alloys:** BCD low (IMFA MD: "duty protection is hardly 2%, 3%") [V call]. Mn ore and coking coal/met coke: low BCD (met coke ~5% [K]); DGTR has had anti-dumping actions on met coke (2025) [K, unverified].
- **Steel safeguard:** 12% / 11.5% / 11% over Apr-2025 → Apr-2028 on flat products (stainless excluded) [V-snip]. Supports domestic steel output and hence Mn-alloy demand.
- **EU:** (a) **CBAM definitive period from 1-Jan-2026.** Importers must surrender certificates for embedded emissions on 2026 imports (purchase from Feb-2027). Default values carry a **10% mark-up in 2026, rising to 30% from 2028**. Argus estimates CBAM adds **~5–10% to landed prices** of ferro alloys at default intensities (e.g. FeMn ~2.07 tCO₂/t × ~€80/t ≈ **€165/t**) [V-snip]. **Scope trap [K, verify against Annex I]:** CBAM's iron & steel chapter covers **FeMn (7202 11/19), FeCr (7202 41/49) and FeNi (7202 60)**; **SiMn (7202 30) and FeSi (7202 2x) are generally understood to be outside CBAM's CN list**, though the EU safeguard covers them. A coal-power-based Indian FeCr can carry 5–8 tCO₂/t on a scope-1+2 basis [E], which is why IMFA's push to ~40% non-fossil power by mid-2027 [V] matters for any EU sales (small for IMFA; Far East dominates). (b) **EU ferro-alloy safeguard quotas** on India from 2026 (SiMn 126,800 t, FeMn 69,900 t) [V-snip].
- **US:** Sec 232 50% applies to steel/aluminium and derivatives; ferro alloys (HS 7202) are not steel derivatives under the 232 lists [K, verify]. The US is a minor market for both members.

**7.3 Power regulation**
- **India:** captive power under the Electricity Act 2003 (≥26% ownership + ≥51% consumption rule for captive status) [K]. Odisha OERC tariffs; cross-subsidy surcharge and additional surcharge on open access [K]. Green energy open access rules 2022 allow ≥100 kW open access for RE, which enables IMFA's hybrid RE PPAs (JSW Energy corridor from June-2026; EG Urja/Enfinity 65 MW, 29-year) [V call/PPT].
- **South Africa:** Nersa approved Eskom's multi-year price determination (MYPD6) increases of **12.74% (FY2025/26), 5.36% (FY2026/27), 6.19% (FY2027/28)** for standard tariffs [K, Jan-2025 decision]. The ferrochrome **NPA at 62 c/kWh for 5 years** sits outside it, and Eskom bears the shortfall "directly" without recovering it from other customers [V-snip]. Manganese and silicon smelters were "left in the dark" (Daily Maverick, Apr-2026) and are now lobbying for similar terms [V-snip; V IMFA call].
- **Zambia:** ERB regulates ZESCO's end-user tariffs. IPP PPAs are bilateral with ZESCO (Maamba's 20-year US$ PPA, capacity + energy charges [primer]). No confirmed 2026 ERB tariff order found [V-snip gap].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

- **1960s–80s: the Odisha chrome base.** IMFA was founded in 1961 by Dr Bansidhar and Ila Panda; Therubali came first, then Choudwar (ex-Kalinga Tubes, mid-1980s) [primer]. Nava (then Nava Bharat Ferro Alloys) started in 1972 with ferro-silicon at Paloncha, Telangana [primer; V screener].
- **1990s–2000s: integration.** Both built captive power. IMFA built captive mines and became India's largest FeCr exporter; Nava added SiMn, sugar and power [primer].
- **2007–08 super-cycle:** European benchmark FeCr hit records (~US$2/lb in Q3 2008 [K]); Indian ferro-alloy capacity boomed; then the GFC crash.

**Cycle anatomy (ferrochrome) [E]:** The cycle runs 3–5 years. Price is set by the marginal Chinese smelter's cost, which is SA ore CIF plus Chinese power. Upswings come from SA supply shocks (power, logistics, strikes) rather than demand. Downswings come from Chinese capacity additions and SA restarts. **Indian integrated producers earn the gap between the Chinese marginal cost and their own captive cost.** That gap widens when ore is dear (ICRA: the "upward shift in the cost curve … unlikely to lead to a mean-reversion" near term) [V].

**Cycle anatomy (Mn alloys) [E]:** Driven by Chinese steel output and SA/Gabon/Australian Mn ore (South32 GEMCO cyclone damage, Mar-2024, removed ~10% of seaborne Mn ore for a year [K]). Indian SiMn has structural overcapacity (8 mt ferro-alloy capacity vs ~5–6 mt output [E]), so spreads revert to near cash cost quickly.

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Decarbonisation of smelting.** Coal-power-based FeCr carries 5–8 tCO₂/t (scope 1+2) [E]. EU CBAM, buyer Scope-3 targets (Outokumpu, Acerinox, Aperam, Japanese mills) and green-steel premia favour hydro- or RE-powered FeCr (Finland, Norway for Mn alloys, Kazakhstan partly). IMFA's 135 MW hybrid RE (→ ~40% non-fossil by mid-2027) [V] is the right direction. Nava's Indian SiMn on coal power, and Maamba as a coal plant, carry ESG/financing risk (primer bear case).
2. **Pre-reduction / closed-furnace / DC-arc technology.** Outotec sintered-pellet closed furnaces cut power to ~3,100–3,300 kWh/t; SA's Glencore Premus process ~2,500–2,800 kWh/t [K]. Older Indian open/semi-closed furnaces run at ~3,800–4,200 kWh/t [K]. IMFA's digital/Kaizen programmes are incremental [V call].
3. **Low-grade ore use (UG2 concentrates, friable Indian ore)** needs pelletising, which favours integrated producers with agglomeration plants [K].
4. **Stainless technology shifts:** more 200-series (Mn-Ni-Cr, lower Cr) in India/China reduces FeCr per tonne of stainless (200-series ~13–16% Cr vs 304 at 18%) [K]. Scrap recycling (EAF stainless uses scrap for ~50–60% of Cr in Europe/US) reduces primary FeCr demand there; China/Indonesia run more primary units [K].
5. **Indonesia's NPI-integrated stainless + local FeCr** shifts trade flows away from Indian exports to China over the long run [K/E].
6. **Battery/energy storage:** not a direct chromium demand driver (Fe-Cr flow batteries are niche). Manganese's battery demand (HPMSM for LMFP/LMR) uses Mn ore/EMM, not SiMn [K].
7. **For Nava:** solar plus storage in Zambia (Nava's own 100 MW solar, the government's Chisamba 200 MW) cuts the country's need for coal baseload in the long run, but helps drought resilience in the short run [V-snip; E]. SMRs and RE + storage are being studied by Nava [V call].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Integration is everything: whoever owns ore and power owns the margin.**
   - FeCr sells at one global price. IMFA's cash cost (~₹78–86k/t) sits ~₹15–30k/t below a non-integrated Indian converter's [E], and that gap is the whole equity value.
   - Indian chromite auctions now go at very high premia, so legacy leases valid to 2049/2055 (ICRA) are the hardest-to-replicate asset in the sector [V].
   - For Mn alloys, nobody owns the ore (except MOIL, which doesn't smelt at scale), so captive power is the only edge. Hence Nava's thin alloy margins.
2. **South African power decides the world ferrochrome price.**
   - SA holds ~70%+ of world chromite reserves and was ~⅓ of FeCr supply [K]. Eskom tariffs rose ~3× in a decade, to ~135 c/kWh by end-2025. That shut 44 of 48 smelters (Jan-2026) and lifted the Chinese cost curve via ore prices [V-snip].
   - The **62 c/kWh, 5-year NPA** (Feb–Jun 2026) restarts Glencore-Merafe and Samancor. That is bearish for FeCr prices in H2 2026–2027, but bullish for SA ore *consumption*, which tightens ore supply for Chinese smelters. The IMFA MD's thesis: "chrome units" balance out [V call].
3. **China's stainless mills and smelters set the price; India is a price-taker with a cost advantage.**
   - China is 64% of stainless and ~52% of FeCr. Monthly Tsingshan/TISCO tenders are the reference.
   - Indian duty protection is ~2–3%, so domestic price = export parity [V call]. The rupee (₹94.9/$) is a structural tailwind for exporters.
4. **Power is the largest controllable cost, and the transition is now a market-access issue.**
   - ~3,500–4,500 kWh/t. Captive coal at ~₹3–3.5/kWh vs grid at ₹6–7 is worth ₹10–15k/t.
   - CBAM (definitive from 1-Jan-2026; default-value mark-ups rising from 10% to 30% by 2028) and buyer decarbonisation push smelters to RE PPAs (IMFA ~40% non-fossil by mid-2027) [V; V-snip].
5. **Nava is a sovereign-credit and FX story wearing a ferro-alloy label.**
   - ~70% of EBITDA comes from one Zambian plant with one state off-taker. ZESCO arrears were cut from ~US$518 Mn to US$13.4 Mn [V/primer].
   - The new risks are Phase II execution (slipped to ~Jun–Jul 2027), kwacha-driven deferred tax, the coal/ESG discount and the 35% minority leakage.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Ferrochrome (IMFA):**
EBITDA = Sales tonnes × (Realisation/t − Cash cost/t) + other operating items.
Cash cost/t ≈ ore (2.5 t × captive cost incl. royalty) + coke (0.65 t × price) + power (3.5–4.0 MWh × ₹/kWh) + conversion/overheads.

*Worked example, Q1 FY27 actual [V data; E arithmetic]:*
- 79,268 t × (₹119,888 − ₹85,884) = 79,268 × ₹34,004 = **₹269.5 Cr** vs reported EBITDA **₹281.3 Cr**. The difference of ~₹11.8 Cr is forex gains/other operating income ("we had got some forex gain") [V call].
- Annualised at FY27 guide 380k t × ₹30,000/t = **₹1,140 Cr EBITDA [E]**. At ₹22,000/t (FY26-like) = **₹836 Cr [E]**. At FY28's 490k t × ₹22,000 = **₹1,078 Cr [E]**.
- **Sensitivities [E]:** ±₹5,000/t realisation (≈ ±4%) = ±₹190 Cr EBITDA at 380k t; ±₹1/$ on ~85% exports ≈ ±₹1,050/t ≈ ±₹40 Cr; ±10% met coke ≈ ±₹1,800/t ≈ ±₹70 Cr; ±₹0.50/kWh on 3,800 kWh ≈ ±₹1,900/t (only on non-captive tonnes).

**Silico-manganese (Nava's alloy business):**
EBITDA = Sales t × (SiMn price − [2.0 t Mn ore × ore price + 4,200 kWh × power cost + 0.45 t reductant + other]).
*Worked example [E]:* 134,410 t × (₹82,000 − ₹78,500) ≈ **₹47 Cr** (CRISIL FY25: ₹40 Cr) [V for FY25 point].

**Mine-mouth IPP (Maamba):**
Revenue = Capacity charge (contracted MW × availability × US$/kW-month) + Energy charge (MU × US$/kWh fuel pass-through) + coal outside sales.
*Worked example FY26 [V data; E split]:* 2,115 MU × ~US$0.11/kWh ≈ US$232 Mn + coal ~US$24 Mn = **US$256 Mn**. EBITDA US$151.4 Mn (incl. ECL reversals); clean ~US$125–130 Mn [E] ≈ ₹1,100–1,150 Cr at ₹88. **Attributable to Nava (65%) ≈ US$82 Mn EBITDA** [E].
*Phase II [V call; E]:* +300 MW ≈ +2,100 MU at ~85% PLF → **~US$200 Mn revenue** (company), EBITDA at 45–50% ≈ US$90–100 Mn, minus interest on US$300 Mn of debt (~8–9% ≈ US$25 Mn [E]) → PBT ~US$45–55 Mn before any tax holiday. Equity US$100 Mn at ~15% ROE ≈ US$15 Mn PAT; this seems conservative vs the arithmetic [E — flag].

**Group (Nava consolidated):** EBITDA ≈ MEL (US$ × FX) + India power (MU × (tariff − coal cost/kWh)) + alloys (as above) − corporate − intersegment eliminations. The CFO guides consolidated EBITDA at 35–40% of revenue [V call].
