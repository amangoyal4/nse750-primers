## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **BKT is not (yet) an Indian tyre company in the MRF sense — but it is becoming one.**
   - FY26: ~90% of revenue from off-highway tyres (agri, OTR, industrial), **~70% of revenue outside India** (Europe ~43%, Americas ~16%) [primer; V Kotak Neo "OHT ~90%"]. It sells by the **tonne** (FY26 3,17,356 t; Q1 FY27 a record **93,770 t, +16%**) [primer; V Kotak Neo].
   - From Apr-2026 it supplies TBR, has launched select 2W tyres, plans car tyres from Nov-2026, and on 22-Oct-2026 signed the Pirelli off-take + JV agreement [V].
   - **Aggregates in Part 3 show BKT separately** ("export OHT") — mixing its 21–22% margin, euro-linked revenue and tonnes with four domestic road-tyre makers produces a meaningless "sector" margin. The **"domestic four" (MRF, Apollo, CEAT, JK)** is the correct base for Indian road-tyre economics.
2. **Revenue growth in FY27 is mostly price, not volume.**
   - CEAT Q2 FY27: consolidated +26.2%, of which only ~40% volume; standalone volume ~+10% [V MOSL]. Price hikes since 1-Apr-2026: CEAT replacement 12–13%, OEM 14–15%; JK 4–5% (Q1) + 7% (Q2) = 11–12% in H1, with 4–5% more planned in Q3 [V tyre-trends]; Apollo ~11% done of the 15–16% it needs [V DSIJ]; BKT ~5% in Q1 [V-snip].
   - CEAT and JK Q2 YoY growth also carries acquisition/merger effects (CAMSO consolidated from 1-Sep-2025).
   - **Always split value growth into volume + price/mix** before calling it "demand".
3. **Apollo's FY26 reported PAT (₹1,372 Cr) is the net of two large one-offs.**
   - Screener FY26 "other income" is **−₹900 Cr** (Q1 −351, Q2 −150, Q3 +23, Q4 −421) — this is where Europe restructuring/Enschede closure charges are booked [V screener; nature E].
   - FY26 tax rate is **−5%** (Q4 tax −290%: a large deferred-tax credit, likely Netherlands/Europe) [V screener].
   - So reported PAT is depressed by the exceptional and flattered by the tax credit. **Pre-exceptional, normal-tax PAT ≈ ₹1,650–1,700 Cr** [E: PBT ₹1,309 Cr + ~₹980 Cr exceptional (assuming normal OI ~+₹80 Cr) ≈ ₹2,290 Cr × (1 − 27%)].
   - Q1 FY26 PAT of ₹13 Cr is the restructuring-hit base for Q1 FY27's "+2,500%". Never quote that growth rate.
4. **CEAT's Q1 FY27 PAT of ₹4 Cr is a tax-rate artefact plus the margin squeeze.**
   - PBT ₹32 Cr, tax 103% (screener) — MOSL shows reported PAT ₹4 Cr vs adjusted ₹9 Cr [V]. CEAT's FY26 exceptional was ₹71 Cr (VRS/labour-code-type items in Q3 ₹58 Cr, Q4 ₹10 Cr) → **FY26 adjusted PAT ₹750 Cr vs reported ₹697 Cr** [V MOSL table].
5. **JK's "EBITDA" includes other income; screener's OP does not.**
   - Q2 FY27: company EBITDA ₹343 Cr (7.1%) vs screener OP ₹334 Cr (6.9%); company PAT ₹60 Cr vs screener ₹65 Cr (rounding/minority/OCI basis) [V both].
   - FY26 company EBITDA ₹2,089 Cr (12.8%) [primer] vs screener OP ₹2,031 Cr (12.4%). FY26 revenue ₹16,384 Cr (primer, company total income) vs ₹16,327 Cr (screener revenue from operations).
   - Use screener in tables; quote company figures in prose with "incl. other income".
6. **Market-share claims are segment- and channel-specific, and often self-reported.**
   - "MRF ~35% India share" [primer] is unsupported; industry convention (revenue basis, domestic) puts MRF at ~25–30% [E/K; FLAG — no ATMA share data are published].
   - Apollo: **TBR replacement share >30%, PCR replacement ~21%** (management, Q1 FY27) [V DSIJ]; JK: ~30% of TBR (claim) [primer]; CEAT: ~25% share of PC/UV **EV OEM** fitments and >30% of 2W EV OEM [V MOSL]. These are not comparable to each other or to an overall share.
   - Write "X claims ~Y% of segment Z (basis)", never "X has Y% share".
7. **Replacement share: use each company's own disclosure.**
   - CEAT: replacement 50%, OEM 30%, exports 20% (consolidated, Q2 FY27) [V MOSL]; standalone ~51/30/19 [primer].
   - Apollo: ~65% replacement [Apollo primer]; the MRF primer's "Apollo ~75%" is a third-party figure and conflicts [primer vs primer].
   - JK: ~61% replacement [MRF primer, third-party] — not refreshed; Q2 FY27 OEM grew +65% so replacement share is falling this year [V tyre-trends].
   - MRF does not disclose a channel split [primer]. Industry: replacement ≈ two-thirds of domestic demand [V-snip ICRA].
8. **Natural rubber "price" has three meanings.**
   - **Domestic RSS-4 (Kottayam/Kochi, ₹/kg, Rubber Board daily)**: record **₹284.50/kg on 13-Oct-2026** (previous record ₹284 on 1-Aug-2026) [V Onmanorama].
   - **International RSS-4/RSS-3 (Bangkok FOB, converted to ₹/kg)**: ₹282.16/kg on the same day — domestic is now **above** international [V Onmanorama]. CEAT: international NR ~US$2,200/t at start of Q2 → ~US$2,600/t (≈ ₹247/kg at ₹94.9 — TSR20/SMR20 benchmark, not RSS) [V MOSL].
   - **Landed import cost** = international + freight + **25% customs duty or ₹30/kg, whichever is lower** (for dry NR) [K]. At ₹280/kg the ₹30/kg cap binds (25% would be ₹70/kg), so landed imported RSS ≈ ₹315–320/kg [E] — i.e., imports are dearer than domestic only by the duty; Indian tyre makers can import duty-free under **Advance Authorisation** against exports [K].
   - Always say which price and which grade (RSS-4 vs TSR20/ISNR-20 vs latex).
9. **"Capacity" units differ, and BKT's figures are easy to conflate.**
   - BKT: tyre capacity in **MTPA** — achievable ~3.6 lakh t (primer), expanding by +35,000 MTPA to **4.25 lakh t** under the ₹6,800 Cr plan (Kotak says "taking total OHT capacity to 425,000 MTPA") [V]. **Carbon black**: captive capacity scaled toward **360,000 MTPA by Q1 FY27** [V Kotak Neo] — the same number as the tyre capacity, which is why the BKT primer reads ambiguously.
   - CEAT: Nagpur 2W plant **80k → 100k tyres/day** [V MOSL].
   - JK: **38 mn+ tyres p.a.** combined installed capacity, 11 plants (India 9 + Mexico 2 per presentation) [V JK Q2 FY27 PPT].
   - Yokohama India: ~4.5 mn units/yr [V tyre-trends].
   - MRF: primer's "over 1.1 million tonnes of rubber products" is unverified [FLAG].
10. **Gross margin vs RM%: the only clean quarterly series this session is CEAT's.**
    - CEAT RM cost % of sales (MOSL): Q1 FY26 63.2 | Q2 59.1 | Q3 60.1 | Q4 60.3 | **Q1 FY27 66.1 | Q2 65.4** → gross margin 36.8 / 40.9 / 39.9 / 39.7 / **33.9 / 34.6%** [V MOSL; GM = 100 − RM%].
    - For the other four, use screener OPM as the margin proxy (Part 3 grid). Screener material-cost lines were not pulled; do not invent gross margins.

### 1.2 Value chain (inputs → customer)

1. **Raw materials (≈60–66% of revenue in FY27; ~55–60% in a good year)** [V MOSL CEAT RM% 59–66%; primer "60–65%"]:
   - **Natural rubber (NR)** — the largest single input. Grades: RSS-4/5 (ribbed smoked sheet, Kerala smallholders), ISNR-20/TSR-20 (technically specified, block), latex. India produces ~9 lt and consumes ~14.3 lt [V UNI]. Truck tyres are NR-heavy (NR ~70–80% of the rubber in a TBR tread/carcass); PCR uses more synthetic [K].
   - **Synthetic rubber (SBR, PBR/BR, butyl/halobutyl for inner liners)** — crude/naphtha → butadiene/styrene derived. Indian makers: Reliance (SBR, PBR, halobutyl at Jamnagar), Indian Synthetic Rubber (IOC–Trimurti), Synthetics & Chemicals legacy; much imported [K].
   - **Carbon black** — reinforcing filler (~25–30% of tyre weight [K]); made from carbon black feedstock oil (CBFO, coal-tar or FCC decant oil). Indian suppliers: **PCBL (Phillips Carbon), Himadri, Birla Carbon (Aditya Birla), Continental Carbon**; BKT is captive [K; V Kotak]. (PCBL/Himadri are covered in the sibling "carbon black" sub-sector report.)
   - **Steel cord & bead wire** — for radial belts (TBR, PCR). Suppliers: Bekaert (Ranjangaon), Tata Steel (wire), Kiswire, imports from China/Korea [K].
   - **Tyre cord fabric** — nylon 6/66 (bias tyres, cap plies), polyester (PCR carcass), rayon (high-performance). Suppliers: SRF (India's largest nylon tyre cord maker), Century Enka, Kordsa, Hyosung (imports) [K].
   - **Chemicals & oils** — accelerators, antioxidants (6PPD/TMQ), sulphur, zinc oxide, silica (green tyres), process oil (TDAE/RAE) [K]. Suppliers include NOCIL (rubber chemicals) [K].
2. **Manufacture** — mixing (Banbury) → extrusion/calendering → bead/belt building → green tyre building → curing (presses) → inspection. Plant cost ~₹1,000–1,500 Cr per 100 t/day-equivalent of radial capacity [E; FLAG]. Utilisation is high this year: Apollo India 91%, Europe 94% (Q1 FY27) [V filingreader/DSIJ]; CEAT "high, particularly 2W and TBR" [V MOSL].
3. **Channels**:
   - **Replacement (≈2/3 of domestic demand)** [V-snip ICRA]: company depots → distributors → ~5,000–10,000 dealers per major (JK: 6,250+ dealers & distributors, 900+ brand shops, 95+ retread centres [V JK PPT]); brand shops (MRF T&S, Apollo Zone, CEAT Shoppe, JK Steel Wheels) [K].
   - **OEM (~1/3)**: annual price contracts with quarterly RM pass-through formulas; OEM price changes "typically lag by a quarter" [V MOSL CEAT].
   - **Exports**: ATMA FY26 **₹27,312 Cr** record (+9%) — US 15% (₹4,082 Cr), Germany 7%, Italy 5%, Brazil 5%, France 4% [V-snip autocarpro/knn]. BKT alone is roughly a third of India's tyre exports [E: BKT FY26 export revenue ~₹7,500 Cr ÷ ₹27,312 Cr].
4. **End users**: fleet owners (TBR — very price/CPKM-sensitive, buy on cost per km), individual car/2W owners (brand-led, dealer-influenced: "channel influences 80–85% of purchases" per Yokohama India [V tyre-trends]), OEMs, farmers, mines/contractors (OHT).
5. **Aftermarket services**: **retreading** (TBR/TBB casings retreaded 1–3 times; precured tread rubber — MRF, Indag, Elgi; organised + many unorganised retreaders) [K]; GST 2.0 cut tyres to 18% but retreaders complained of inverted treatment [V-snip tyre-trends].

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **Brand + replacement distribution captures the margin**; the factory converts commodities at a thin spread. Replacement margins exceed OEM by an estimated 5–8 pp of EBITDA [E; FLAG — no company discloses channel margins].
- **Scale in TBR** (the largest value pool, ~35–40% of domestic tyre revenue [E/K]) is the profit centre for MRF/Apollo/JK.
- **OHT export niches** (BKT, CAMSO) earn structurally higher margins: BKT 21–25% OPM through cycles [V screener 10-yr: 18–31%]; CAMSO gross margin visibility ~50–55% (management) [V MOSL].
- **Upstream** (NR planters, carbon black, SR) captures value when inputs spike — e.g., PCBL/Himadri margins vs tyre margins in FY22 vs FY24 [V-sib carbon black report hook].

### 5.3 Imports and China

- **History:** Chinese TBR imports surged in 2015–17 (one 2017 report: imported TBR ~40% of TBR replacement, ~92% of it Chinese — FLAG, industry claim) [V-snip autocarpro/antidumping.vn]. India imposed **anti-dumping duty on Chinese TBR in Sep-2017: US$245.35–452.33/t for 5 years** [V-snip S&P/autocarpro] — lapsed ~2022 [E].
- **2020 import restriction:** DGFT moved new pneumatic tyres for cars, buses/trucks and motorcycles (HS 4011.10, 4011.20, 4011.40) from "free" to **"restricted" (licence required) on 12-Jun-2020** [K] — the single biggest trade-defence measure; imports collapsed thereafter. Also a CVD investigation on Chinese TBR (DGTR final findings 2020) [K; FLAG — whether imposed].
- **BIS:** tyres are under a compulsory QCO — Pneumatic Tyres and Tubes for Automotive Vehicles (Quality Control) Order, **2009**; IS 15633 (PCR), IS 15636 (TBR/LT), IS 15627 (2W) [K]. Foreign plants must hold BIS licences (a non-tariff barrier for small Chinese makers).
- **APTA route:** Chinese tyres historically entered at a concessional ~8.6% under the Asia-Pacific Trade Agreement [V-snip].
- **2025–26 import data (HS 4011):** not sourced this session — see §H. Indian makers' current import threat is mostly **Chinese-brand premium-ish PCR and TBR via Thai/Vietnamese plants** (Chinese tyre makers relocated to SE Asia to avoid US AD duties) [K]. CEAT Q2: export price hikes limited by "premium European manufacturers and lower-priced Chinese brands" [V MOSL].
- **India is a large net exporter of tyres** (ATMA ₹27,312 Cr exports vs a small import bill) [V-snip; E].

### 5.4 Retreading and the unorganised segment

- Retreading extends TBR/TBB casing life; organised precured-tread suppliers (MRF, Indag, Elgi, Apollo/JK retread centres — JK has 95+ retread centres [V JK PPT]); thousands of unorganised hot-cure retreaders [K].
- Radialisation (radial casings retread less often in poor-road conditions) and better roads slowly shrink retreading; GST 2.0 tyre rate cut to 18% narrowed the new-vs-retread price gap — retreaders "cried foul" [V-snip tyre-trends].
- Unorganised new-tyre manufacturing is negligible outside bicycle/cycle-rickshaw tyres [K].

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Taxes and duties
- **GST:** 28% → **18%** on most tyres from 22-Sep-2025; tractor tyres/tubes 5% [V-snip].
- **NR customs duty:** 25% or ₹30/kg (lower); compounded rubber 25% (raised from 10% in Feb-2023) [K]. Industry lobbies for lower NR duty / easier Advance Authorisation; Rubber Board/planters lobby the other way [K].
- **Tyre import duty:** ~10% BCD + restriction (licence) since Jun-2020 [K].
- **Inverted duty:** the Apollo primer notes India's duty structure taxes NR (input) more than some finished-tyre routes (FTA/APTA) [primer].

### 7.2 Trade defence
- ADD on Chinese TBR (2017–2022) [V-snip]; import licensing (Jun-2020) [K]; BIS QCO (2009) [K]. 2025–26 DGTR tyre cases: none found this session [V-snip search negative].
- **Abroad:** US AD/CVD on Indian OTR (2017 order; Mar-2026 review CVD BKT 0.57%) [V; V-snip]; US reciprocal tariffs 2025–26 (see §3.4); EU anti-dumping on Chinese TBR (2018) helped Indian TBR exporters [K].

### 7.3 Product regulation
- **BIS star-labelling / AIS-142:** AIS-142 sets rolling-resistance, wet-grip and rolling-noise limits for C1 (car), C2 (LCV) and C3 (truck/bus) tyres; Stage 1 applied to new tyre types from **Oct-2022**, all tyres from **Apr-2023** [K; FLAG — exact stage dates to verify on the MoRTH/BIS notification]. BEE launched a **star-labelling programme for tyres** (fuel-efficiency, rolling-resistance based) — voluntary phase [K; FLAG].
- **EV tyres:** low rolling resistance, higher load index, low noise — JK "JUXe/Ranger HPe/Jetway JUXe" EV range; CEAT ~25% of PC/UV EV OEM fitments [V JK PPT; V MOSL].
- **TPMS/smart tyres:** JK launched an embedded-sensor smart tyre for cars (first in India) [V JK PPT].

### 7.4 Environment
- **EPR for waste tyres:** Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Amendment Rules, **Jul-2022** — producers (incl. importers) must ensure end-of-life tyre recycling (targets ramp from 2022-23: 35% → 70% → 100% of tyres placed on market, by weight) [K; FLAG — exact targets]. Pyrolysis/crumb-rubber recyclers buy EPR certificates; cost to tyre makers is small (<0.5% of sales) [E].
- **EUDR (EU Deforestation Regulation):** rubber is a covered commodity (tyres in Annex I). Application postponed by Reg (EU) 2025/2650 to **30-Dec-2026** (large/medium operators) and **30-Jun-2027** (micro/small); due-diligence statement only by the "first placer" on the EU market; the Commission's May-2026 package did **not** reopen the text but plans a delegated act to trim Annex I scope [V-snip JDSupra/Latham/ERJ/VinciWorks]. Impact: BKT, Apollo Europe (Vredestein) and CEAT/CAMSO exports to the EU need geolocated, deforestation-free NR supply chains from 30-Dec-2026; ETRMA lobbied for "real simplification" [V-snip ERJ]. Pirelli is already FSC-certified on NR [V Pirelli boilerplate].
- **ESG ratings (company claims):** JK CDP A−, EcoVadis Silver, CareEdge-ESG 1+ [V JK PPT].

### 7.5 Competition law — the CCI tyre cartel case
- **CCI order (Aug-2018, published Feb-2022):** Apollo, MRF, CEAT, JK, Birla and ATMA found to have acted in concert in 2011–12 to raise prices of **cross-ply/bias tyres** in the replacement market and limit supply; penalties **~₹1,788 Cr** — MRF ₹622.09 Cr, Apollo ₹425.53 Cr, JK ₹309.95 Cr, CEAT ₹252.16 Cr, Birla ₹178.33 Cr, ATMA ₹0.084 Cr [K for split; V-snip for total].
- **Madras HC** dismissed the companies' writ (6-Jan-2022); **Supreme Court** dismissed SLPs (28-Jan-2022) [V-snip].
- **NCLAT (1-Dec-2022)** set aside and **remanded** to CCI for fresh consideration (and suggested reviewing penalties to protect domestic industry) [V-snip Business Standard].
- **CCI appealed to the Supreme Court**; SC issued notice (11-Apr-2023) [V-snip Bar & Bench]. **2025–26 status not found** — FLAG; companies carry it as a contingent liability (paid 10% deposits [K]).

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **Licence Raj to radials (1926–1990s):** Dunlop India (1926), Firestone, Goodyear, Ceat (Italian origin, 1958 in India), MRF (1946 balloons → tyres 1960 with Mansfield), JK (Kankroli 1977), Apollo (1972, Perambra 1977) [K; primer]. Bias tyres dominated.
2. **Radialisation (2000s–2020s):** PCR ~98%+ radial by 2010s [K]; **TBR radialisation** went from ~15–20% (FY10) to ~55–60% (FY24–25) of truck tyre tonnage [K/E; FLAG — ICRA/CRISIL series]; JK built India's first TBR plant (Banmore, 1999) [primer: "pioneers of radial technology"].
3. **Global M&A wave:** Apollo bought Dunlop SA (2006, later sold) and **Vredestein (2009)**, failed **Cooper Tire (US$2.5 bn, 2013)** [primer]; JK bought **Tornel (Mexico, 2008, ~US$68 m)** [primer] and **Cavendish (Kesoram's Laksar, 2016)**; CEAT bought **CAMSO compact line from Michelin (2024–25, US$225 m)** [V-snip]; Yokohama bought **ATG (2016)**; BKT stayed organic.
4. **Capex boom and margin bust (FY18–FY23):** Apollo Hungary (€475 m, 2017) and AP plant (2018); net debt peak >₹6,000 Cr [primer]. RM spike of FY22–23 (NR + crude after Russia–Ukraine) crushed OPM: MRF 10–11%, CEAT 8–9%, JK 9%, Apollo 12–14% [V screener]; PAT troughs MRF ₹669 Cr (FY22), CEAT ₹71 Cr (FY22), JK ₹201 Cr (FY22) [V screener].
5. **Super-cycle FY24:** RM eased → OPM peaks MRF 17%, Apollo 18%, CEAT 14%, JK 14%, BKT 24%; PAT MRF ₹2,081 Cr, Apollo ₹1,722 Cr, JK ₹806 Cr [V screener].
6. **FY25 squeeze, FY26 recovery:** NR rose in 2024 (Kottayam ₹180s → ₹200+) → FY25 OPMs fell 2–4 pp; FY26 recovered with GST 2.0 volumes [V screener].
7. **FY27 West Asia shock:** RM +25–30% in six months; record NR; margins halved at CEAT/JK in H1 [V]; demand strongest in years.
8. **Diversification:** BKT into on-highway (TBR Apr-2026; car tyres Nov-2026; **Pirelli 22-Oct-2026**) [V].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **The raw-material spread and its lag (the master variable).** RM is 60–66% of revenue; NR alone ~35–45% of the basket. A 10% NR move ≈ 160 bp of margin (MRF rule [primer]). FY27 shows the force at full strength: RM +25–30% in six months, OPM halved at CEAT (13.3% → 9.6%) and JK (13.3% → 7.1%) in Q2 even with revenue +20–26% [V]. Analysts model tyre earnings as "RM index lagged one quarter vs price index" — every forecast is a view on NR and crude.
2. **Replacement vs OEM mix (and brand).** Replacement (~2/3 of domestic demand) carries the margin and passes cost through within 1–2 quarters; OEM is volume and fleet-seeding with lagged formulas [V-snip ICRA; V MOSL]. FY27's OEM boom (SIAM PV +28%, JK OEM +65%) is mix-negative for margins at exactly the wrong moment [V].
3. **Capacity/capex cycle and balance sheets.** ~₹30,000 Cr of capex over 4–5 years (ATMA) and FY27 programmes of ₹1,500–5,000 Cr per company are being funded into a margin squeeze — CEAT and JK debt up 20–25% in six months [V]. Over-building leads to price wars once RM eases (FY18–20 precedent).
4. **Trade regime both ways.** Domestic protection (2020 import licensing, BIS QCO, past ADD) keeps Chinese tyres out; India's exporters face US tariffs (50% → ~10% Sec 301), US OTR AD/CVD, EUDR from 30-Dec-2026 and freight shocks [V/V-snip]. BKT's earnings (70% exports) and Apollo's Europe ride this force.
5. **Premiumisation and the global majors' India push.** Rim-size inflation, SUVs and EVs move value to the premium end where MNCs (Michelin, Bridgestone, Yokohama ~10% of premium PCR, now Pirelli via BKT) compete [V tyre-trends; V BKT PR]. Domestic majors must keep investing in PCR premium capacity (JK Chennai, CEAT Chennai, Apollo) to defend.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Domestic road-tyre maker:**
EBITDA = Σ_segments [Tonnes (or units) × Realisation per kg (or ASP)] − RM cost per kg × tonnes − conversion cost (power, labour, overheads) − selling cost (A&P, freight, dealer incentives)

- Realisation = f(channel mix: replacement > exports > OEM; segment mix: PCR premium > TBR > 2W per kg; price hikes lagging RM by 1–2 quarters).
- RM cost per kg of tyre = Σ (input share by weight × input price) / yield. For a TBR: ~30–35% NR, ~4–6% SR, ~25–28% carbon black, ~12–15% steel cord & bead, ~3–5% fabric, ~10–15% chemicals/oils [E/K composite].

**Export OHT maker (BKT):**
EBITDA = Tonnes × (EUR/USD price per kg × FX) − RM per kg × tonnes − conversion − freight; plus carbon black third-party sales.

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed, and new primer issues

**Insights an expert would add**
1. **The squeeze is deeper and broader than "CEAT and JK".** Screener's material-cost schedule shows MRF's gross-margin proxy falling 6 pp in Q1 FY27 (39% → 33%) — as much as CEAT — and JK's 8 pp (38% → 30%). MRF's 11.8% OPM held up only because its overheads are lower; the brand did not buy it any extra pricing power in the first quarter of the shock. BKT (−2 pp) is the only member insulated at the gross-margin line.
2. **The second oil leg arrives in Q3, not before.** EIA Brent averaged $114 in September and ~$126 in the first three weeks of October (peak $135.51), while the companies guided Q3 RM at only +3–5% in mid-October. With crude derivatives lagging 1–2 months, Q3 FY27 may *not* be the trough — the writer should frame "Q3 trough" as the companies' view, with the EIA series as the counter-evidence.
3. **US tariff exposure splits by product, not by company.** On-road tyres (PCR/LT, and TBR per CEAT) carry the Sec 232 auto-parts 25% — untouched by the IEEPA ruling — while OHT pays ~10%. That favours BKT and CEAT-CAMSO and penalises PCR/TBR exporters (MRF, JK, Apollo's US PCR line); BKT may also get IEEPA refunds (a one-off upside, timing unknown).
4. **Apollo's FY26 "tax-flattered" PAT is an India policy effect, and it recurs.** The ₹574 Cr credit came from adopting the 25.17% regime under the new Income-tax Act 2025; Apollo's FY27+ tax rate falls by ~10 pp vs its old 34.94% standalone rate — an earnings tailwind other members may already enjoy (MRF/CEAT/JK/BKT pay ~24–31% on screener).
5. **Rating headroom is the hidden constraint for CEAT and JK.** CareEdge downgrade triggers: CEAT net debt/PBILDT >2.5× (2.34× already at Sep-25 on its basis), JK PBILDT <10% sustained or >3.5× (H1 FY27 PBILDT ~6.9%). Both are front-loading capex into the squeeze; a weak Q3 would put ratings, not just EPS, in play. MRF (AAA, ₹7,572 Cr liquid funds) and Apollo (AA+, 0.4×) have room.
6. **BKT is already an India story by volume.** India was 36% of BKT's FY26 volume and ~40% in Q1 FY27 (Europe 38%); the "~70% exports, Europe-led" description is out of date even before on-highway tyres or Pirelli add domestic revenue. The FY30 plan (₹23,000 Cr: OHT 70%, carbon black 10%, on-highway 20% ≈ ₹5,000 Cr) implies on-highway reaching roughly CEAT-CAMSO scale in four years.
7. **Management turnover in the squeeze.** Apollo's CFO (22 years, ran the Enschede exit) announced his departure on the Q1 call; BKT changed CFO (mid-2026) and hired an outside carbon-black head; JK's net debt guidance (+₹500–700 Cr) was already exceeded by June (+₹500 Cr in one quarter).
8. **Legal tail risk is wider than the 2018 cartel case.** CEAT faces a second CCI matter (2013 Himachal Pradesh STU tender; DG found cartelisation, Feb-2025); the 2018 case (₹1,788 Cr) was last heard in the SC in Sep-2025. Nobody has provided for either.
