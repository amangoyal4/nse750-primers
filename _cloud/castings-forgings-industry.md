## 1. What the sub-sector does: products, value chain, where value is captured

### 1.1 Processes (what "castings" and "forgings" mean)

| Process | What happens | Strength/shape trade-off | Typical products | Members |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sand / green-sand casting (ferrous)** | Melt iron/steel in induction or cupola furnace; pour into sand mould | Complex shapes cheaply; lower strength than forged | Engine blocks, housings, brackets, pump/valve bodies | CIE (iron foundry), the bulk of India's MSME foundries |
| **Alloy / high-chrome casting** | Melt steel scrap + ferro-chrome (+ Mo, Ni) in induction furnaces; cast into moulds; heat-treat | Extreme hardness/wear resistance; brittle if badly made | **Grinding balls, mill liners, diaphragms, crusher wear parts** | **AIA** (largest high-chrome grinding-media maker outside the duopoly partner Magotteaux) |
| **Investment ("lost-wax") casting** | Wax pattern → ceramic shell → wax melted out → metal poured in | Near-net shape, thin walls, fine detail; expensive | Turbine blades, impellers, aero-engine structural castings, valve parts | **PTC** (steel/superalloy for decades; titanium via Aerolloy) |
| **Vacuum melting (VIM/VAR/EB/PAM)** | Melt reactive/clean alloys under vacuum or inert gas to make ingots | Needed for titanium (reacts with air) and aerospace superalloys | Ti-6Al-4V and superalloy ingots/billets | **PTC/Aerolloy** (VAR 1,500 tpa; EB 5,000 tpa; PAM ~600 tpa) |
| **Aluminium die-casting (HPDC/LPDC/gravity)** | Inject/pour molten aluminium into steel dies | Light; fast at volume | Transmission/e-motor/inverter housings, battery enclosures | CIE (CIE Aluminium Casting India — being merged in) |
| **Closed-die hot forging** | Heat steel billet to ~1,150–1,250 °C; press/hammer it into dies | Grain flow → best fatigue strength; capital heavy | **Crankshafts, front axle beams, steering knuckles, connecting rods, gear blanks** | **CIE** (India + Spain + Lithuania), **Balu** |
| **Open-die forging** | Large ingot squeezed between flat/simple dies | Very large parts, discs, shafts | Aero-engine discs/shafts, heavy rings | **PTC** (4,500/5,100 t press, commissioned FY26) |
| **Shell forging / flow-forming** | Billet pierced, drawn, machined into a shell body | Pressure/fragmentation-critical | **155 mm / 152 mm artillery shell bodies (empty)** | **Balu** (automated line, 360,000/yr) |
| **Stamping** | Press sheet steel | Fast, thin-margin | Chassis/body sub-assemblies | CIE (large in Europe) |
| **Machining / gears** | CNC finish of forged/cast blanks; gear cutting | Where most of the value-add is | Finished crankshafts, gears, machined castings | All four; Balu & CIE (Metalcastello, Italy) especially |

### 1.2 Value chains (inputs → customer)

1. **High-chrome grinding media (AIA model).**
   - Inputs: **steel scrap** (~70–80% of charge [E]) + **high-carbon ferro-chrome** (~10–15% of charge for 12–28% Cr alloys [E/K]) + moly/nickel for some grades; **power** (induction melting ~600–800 kWh/t liquid metal [E/K]); moulds; heat treatment.
   - Product → exported (>90% of output [primer]) via **Vega** stock-points in Chile, Peru, Australia, Africa, North America → **mine's SAG/ball mill** (consumed continuously) or **cement plant ball mill**.
   - Value capture: AIA captures the **total-cost-of-ownership saving** at the mine (fewer kg of media per tonne ground, better throughput/recovery) rather than a metal spread. Hence 26–30% operating margins on what looks like a commodity [primer; V screener].
2. **Aerospace titanium (PTC model; the global chain).**
   - Ilmenite/rutile → **TiCl₄ (chlorination) → titanium sponge (Kroll process)** → (sponge + master alloy + scrap) **VAR/EB/PAM melting → ingot** → billet/bar/plate (forging/rolling) **or** investment casting → machining → NDT/certification → OEM (Safran, Airbus, Rolls-Royce, Pratt) or defence integrator (BrahMos, DRDO labs, ordnance).
   - Aerospace-grade sponge makers: **USA (TIMET/Precision Castparts), Japan (Toho, Osaka Titanium), Russia (VSMPO-AVISMA), Kazakhstan (UKTMP), China (large volume, increasingly aerospace)**; India has a small **KMML/DMRL-ISRO sponge plant at Chavara, Kerala** [K]. Our PTC primer names only US/Russia/Kazakhstan/Japan — see §3.4.
   - PTC's pitch: **melt → cast → forge → machine on one Lucknow site** (UP Defence Industrial Corridor), with an MoU for an aerospace-grade **sponge plant in Odisha (~₹1,000 Cr-class)** to close the loop [primer].
3. **Auto forgings and castings (CIE model).**
   - Special bar quality (SBQ) steel from JSW/Kalyani/Jindal/Mukand/Sunflag (India) or European mills → cut billets → induction heat → press → trim → heat treat → machine → OEM line (M&M, Maruti, Tata, Ashok Leyland, tractors; in Europe VW, Stellantis, Renault, trucks) [primer; K].
   - **Steel is passed through** with a ~1-quarter lag; value-added = processing + machining [primer].
4. **Export crankshafts and defence shells (Balu model).**
   - Historically **bought-in or own forgings → precision machining → export aftermarket (80+ countries) and OEM** [primer]. Now integrating backward into heavy forging (8,000 t mechanical press + 25 t hammer, Hattargi/Belagavi) [V-snip themachinemaker].
   - Defence: shell-body forging + machining → supplied **empty** to an ammunition integrator (domestic private/OFB successors; NATO-affiliated buyer under a five-year MoU) [primer; V-snip].

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **Process know-how + qualification beats tonnage.** The rank of margins inside the group follows the height of the qualification wall: Aerolloy **45%** EBITDA (aerospace titanium) > Balu **27–28%** (machined crankshafts/shells) ≈ AIA **26–29%** (engineered consumable) > PTC Trac UK **~8.5%** (job-shop machining) and CIE India **~17%** / Europe **~13–16%** (auto Tier-1) [V PR; V screener; V-sec].
- **Typical Indian MSME foundry/forge** earns **8–14% EBITDA** [E/K, rating-agency norms] — the sector's "base rate".
- **Market price of scrap, ferro-alloys and SBQ steel is almost always passed through**; the margin lives in **₹/kg value-add** and **utilisation**.

### 3.1 Mining (AIA ~60% of volume)

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **Pre-2000: foundries as MSME clusters** serving pumps, valves, textile machinery and the nascent auto industry. AIA founded 1978 (Ahmedabad Induction Alloys) [primer]; PTC incorporated 1963 (Precision Tools & Castings, Lucknow) [primer].
2. **2003–2008 super-cycle:** commodity boom; mining capex surge; Indian auto exports start. **AIA IPO 2005** [primer]. Bharat Forge's European acquisitions (2004–06) [K].
3. **2008–09 crash:** Indian forgers and European forgers hit hard; CIE SA expands by acquisition [K].
4. **2013: Mahindra–CIE alliance** — CIE SA takes ~53% of Mahindra's forging/component businesses (incl. Mahindra Forgings Europe: Germany, Italy (Metalcastello), Lithuania, UK) → Mahindra CIE Automotive [primer; K].
5. **2016: Bill Forge acquisition** (~₹1,331 Cr) by Mahindra CIE [primer].
6. **2016–2020: AIA mining pivot** — mining becomes the larger segment; capacity > 300 kt [primer].
7. **2020 COVID; 2021–22 freight/commodity spike:** AIA FY23 revenue peak ₹4,909 Cr on freight/alloy-driven realisations (volume flat) [V screener; primer].
8. **2022: Russia–Ukraine war** → (a) **aerospace titanium de-risking from VSMPO** (Boeing stopped buying; Airbus phased out direct Russian supply but Canada later allowed permits) [V-snip]; (b) **NATO artillery-shell scramble** → opportunity for Indian shell-body makers; (c) energy shock for European forgers → **CIE exits German forging** (sold to Mutares; CY22 loss on disposal) [primer; K].
9. **2023:** Mahindra exits CIE India (May); company renamed **CIE Automotive India** [primer]. **Safran–PTC LEAP contract (Nov-2023)** [primer]. Balu Forge begins Belagavi greenfield [primer].
10. **2024:** AIA's first buyback (₹500 Cr @ ₹5,000) [primer]. **PTC acquires Trac Precision Solutions (UK) — completed 19-Dec-2024** [V-snip]. Balu FY25 breakout (+65% revenue, PAT +118%) [primer; V screener].
11. **2025:** US Sec 232 auto-parts 25% (May) and steel-derivative 50% (Jun/Aug) [K]. PTC inaugurates Lucknow titanium/superalloy re-melting complex (May-2025) [primer]. Balu commissions automated empty-shell line (Dec-2025) [primer].
12. **2026:** Balu IT search (Jan; concluded, no incriminating findings) [primer]; PTC PAM furnace (Jan) and 4,500 t forging press [V-snip; primer]; SCOTUS strikes IEEPA (20-Feb); West Asia war (28-Feb → ceasefire 7-Apr) lifts energy/freight costs [V-sib]; Section 301 10% from 24-Jul; PTC–Airbus agreement (Aug); PTC ₹1,800 Cr QIP (13-Oct) and Safran LEAP titanium order (14-Oct) [V].

**Cycle pattern:** Forgers follow the **vehicle production cycle with ~1.5–2× operating leverage**; AIA follows **ore milled + commodity prices with a smoothing annuity**; aerospace follows **OEM build rates (multi-year) but with qualification steps**; defence follows **budgets and wars**. The four rarely peak together — the subgroups are partial hedges for each other.

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **EV transition (CIE, Balu):** deletes crankshafts/con-rods/ICE gears; adds e-axle gears, aluminium housings. CIE: "powertrain-agnostic" for chassis/axle/steering; crankshaft slice at risk [primer]. Balu: CV/genset/marine crankshafts are slower to electrify; aftermarket for the ICE installed base lasts decades [primer; E].
2. **Additive manufacturing (PTC):** 3D-printed titanium/nickel parts (GE's LEAP fuel nozzle) can substitute some investment castings; conversely PTC's metal powders/ingots could feed AM [K/E].
3. **Gearless/HPGR & stirred mills (AIA):** **High-pressure grinding rolls (HPGR)** and vertical stirred mills reduce ball-mill duty and media consumption per tonne in new concentrators [K]. A slow-moving but real threat to grinding-media tonnage; AIA counters with mill-liner/NGDS bundles [V-sec mentions NGDS].
4. **Ceramic grinding media** in fine/regrind mills (cement VRM shift too: vertical roller mills displacing ball mills in cement since the 2000s) [K]. Cement's shift to VRMs is why AIA's cement volume is mature.
5. **Near-net forging / flow-forming / hot isostatic pressing** reduce machining (Balu's value-add) [K/E].
6. **Decarbonisation:** induction-furnace foundries on renewable power gain under CBAM; coke-cupola MSMEs lose [K/E]. AIA's ~37 MW + new hybrid renewable plant is a moat here [primer; V-sec].
7. **Titanium supply technology:** new sponge routes (e.g. electrolytic/FFC Cambridge, Armstrong) could lower sponge cost; PTC's EB cold-hearth recycles scrap into ingot — a cost advantage if aerospace revert (scrap) qualifies [K/E].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert leads with)

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**General form:** Revenue = Σ (tonnes or parts) × realisation per unit; EBITDA = Revenue − (metal input × tonnes, passed through with lag) − energy − labour − freight − overheads; for qualification businesses, add **"number of qualified part-numbers × annual call-off × price"**.

### C.1 AIA — one tonne of high-chrome grinding media (FY26) [E composite]
- Realisation: ₹4,420 Cr ÷ 258,002 t = **₹1.71 lakh/t (~US$1,805/t at ₹94.9)** [E].
- Costs per tonne: raw materials (scrap + FeCr + alloys) ~₹68–72k; power ~₹11–14k; freight & other ~₹26–31k; employees ~₹7–9k → total cash cost ~₹1.22 lakh/t [E split; total from screener expenses ₹3,158 Cr ÷ 258 kt = ₹1.224 lakh/t V/E].
- **Operating EBITDA ₹48.9k/t (28.5%)**; add other income ₹18.8k/t (treasury) → company-style EBITDA ~₹67.6k/t [E].
- **Equation:** AIA operating profit ≈ 258 kt × ₹48.9k = **₹1,261 Cr** [V screener]. A 10% volume uplift (+25.8 kt) at constant spread adds ~₹126 Cr OP (+10%); a ₹5k/t spread squeeze (e.g. freight + FeCr not passed through) removes ~₹129 Cr [E].
- Q1 FY27 check: 64,644 t × ₹47.6k/t ≈ ₹308 Cr OP [V screener; E].

### C.2 PTC — consolidated mix equation (Q1 FY27) [V PR; E]
- Total income ₹197.1 Cr = Aerolloy ₹74.3 Cr (EBITDA 45.0% → ₹33.4 Cr) + Trac UK ₹71.4 Cr (EBITDA ₹6.1 Cr, 8.5%) + legacy/eliminations ~₹51.4 Cr (EBITDA ~₹14.6 Cr, ~28% implied) → **group EBITDA ₹54.2 Cr (27.5%)**; D&A ~₹14 Cr, interest ~₹4 Cr, tax ~21% → **PAT ₹29.2 Cr** [V PR; V screener; E residual].
- **Sensitivity:** every +₹100 Cr of Aerolloy income at 45% adds ~₹45 Cr EBITDA (~₹30 Cr PAT); every +₹100 Cr at Trac adds ~₹8.5 Cr [E]. The mix, not the total, drives PTC's margin.
