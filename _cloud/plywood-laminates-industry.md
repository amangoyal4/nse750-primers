## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Plywood volume units differ by company.**
   - Century reports plywood in **CBM**: FY26 **485,403 CBM** at **₹55,878/CBM** [V IP Q1 FY27].
   - Greenpanel and Greenply report it in **sq m**: Greenpanel FY26 ~5.2 mn sqm at ~₹256/sqm [V BOBCAPS 18-May-26]; Q1 FY27 1.3 MSM at ₹255/sqm [V PL 11-Aug-26].
   - Conversion: 1 CBM of 18 mm ply ≈ 55.6 sq m. Century's ₹55,878/CBM therefore ≈ ₹1,005/sq m at 18 mm, about 4× Greenpanel's ₹255/sq m [E]. Thickness mix, the share of thin and commercial ply, and the low-end "Sainik" mix all distort this.
   - **Never compare ₹/CBM with ₹/sqm.** Compare growth rates, or convert with a stated thickness.
2. **MDF and particle board are in CBM everywhere, but "plain" vs "prelam" changes the realisation.**
   - Century MDF FY26: plain ₹26,905/CBM; prelam ₹41,448/CBM; blended **₹29,527/CBM** [V].
   - Greenpanel MDF FY26 blended **₹28,403/CBM** [V BOBCAPS].
   - Thin MDF (<6 mm, ~25% of the market [V BOBCAPS]) realises more per CBM. Export MDF realises less.
3. **Segment margins in Century's deck come in three flavours. Our primer mislabels them.**
   - The deck shows, per segment: (a) EBITDA, (b) **EBITDA excluding forex** (the headline number) and (c) **segment profit (= EBIT after depreciation and allocated unallocables), excluding forex**.
   - FY26 EBITDA ex-forex margins: plywood **15.2%**, laminates **8.5%**, MDF **12.7%**, particle board **1.2%**, logistics **16.3%**.
   - FY26 segment-profit (EBIT) ex-forex margins: plywood **14.8%**, laminates **5.0%**, MDF **8.4%**, particle board **−8.2%**, logistics **10.3%** [V IP Q1 FY27].
   - The primer's chart "FY26 segment EBIT margins (approx.)" prints the **EBITDA** numbers (15.2/12.7/8.5/1.2) under an EBIT label. See §3.4.
4. **Forex is material at Century and Greenpanel.**
   - Both carry foreign-currency capex loans and buyers' credit (euro machinery for MDF and particle board lines).
   - Century FY26: total forex loss **₹57.3 Cr** = ₹41.1 Cr inside EBITDA + ₹16.2 Cr treated as borrowing cost [V].
   - That gap is why "EBITDA" is ₹660.6 Cr (12.2%) but "EBITDA ex-forex" is **₹701.7 Cr (13.0%)**. Screener's operating profit is **₹651 Cr (12%)**.
   - Greenpanel: ₹49 Cr forex loss in FY26 on ₹303 Cr of unhedged euro borrowings [V ICRA]. Its Q1 FY27 currency loss was ₹1.87 Cr [V PL].
   - **Always state the basis.** Use the company's ex-forex EBITDA in prose and screener OP in peer tables.
5. **Consolidated vs standalone at Century: the AP greenfield sits in a subsidiary.**
   - The **Badvel (Kadapa, Andhra Pradesh) MDF and laminate greenfields are owned by Century Panels Ltd**, a 100% subsidiary [V IP capex note].
   - So **standalone MDF** (Hoshiarpur only) was 241,388 CBM in FY25, against **consolidated MDF of 350,474 CBM** [V IP Q4 FY25].
   - Century Panels lost **₹23.5 Cr at PAT in Q1 FY27** on ₹176 Cr revenue [V IP Q1 FY27 slide 18]. Standalone PAT (₹94.5 Cr) therefore exceeds consolidated PAT (₹83.3 Cr).
   - Use consolidated numbers throughout.
6. **Century has a sixth reporting line most readers miss: "Logistics Services" (container freight station).**
   - FY26 revenue **₹159 Cr**; EBITDA ex-forex ₹25.9 Cr (16.3%) [V].
   - Assets: a CFS (₹83 Cr capex) and **"Century Port" (₹193 Cr capex)** at Khidderpore Docks, Kolkata [V capex table; terminal per investywise V-snip].
   - Screener labels it "Container Freight Station Services" [V screener segment labels].
   - Management is **"evaluating a strategic partner to unlock shareholder value"** for logistics [V PL 4-Aug-26].
   - "Others" (₹94.6 Cr FY26) is a separate line (veneer trading, PVC and similar).
   - Screener names the plywood segment **"Timber & Allied Products"** [V].
7. **Laminate "volume" can fall while revenue rises, because the count is in pieces.**
   - Century sold 72.5 lakh sheets in FY26 (−2.5%) while laminate revenue rose 12.6%. Realisation was +15.5% to **₹1,001/sheet** [V].
   - Q1 FY27: sheets −8.4%, realisation +26.7% (₹1,166). The company's footnote says it "sold more of higher thickness material" [V].
   - Read laminate growth as value, not sheets.
8. **Particle board: "+38%" is revenue, not volume, and Q2 FY26 sales were capitalised.**
   - FY26 PB volume **+53.4%** (107,500 CBM); realisation **−10.4%** (₹18,257/CBM); total income +38.2% [V].
   - During trial runs at the new Chennai line, ~10–11k CBM (**₹23 Cr of sales**) in Q2 FY26 was **capitalised, not reported as revenue** [V Q2 FY26 transcript].
   - The new Chennai line raised PB depreciation from ₹5.1 Cr (FY25) to ₹33.8 Cr (FY26) [V].
9. **Market-share claims use different denominators.**
   - "Century has ~29% of the organised plywood market" [screener key points; primer].
   - "Century holds ~9.5–10% of the overall plywood market" [V PL 4-Aug-26, Q1 FY27 call]; "below 10%" [V Q2 FY26 transcript].
   - "Greenpanel ~21% market share in MDF" [V ICRA] is closer to its **capacity** share (891k of ~4.0–4.5 mn CBM ≈ 20–22%). Its **volume** share is ~16–17% (495k of ~3.0 mn CBM) [E].
   - Greenlam claims **17.8% of the organised laminate market and >29% of laminate exports** [V-snip].
   - Always name the denominator.
10. **Imports: CY (Comtrade) vs FY (FIPPI/DGCI&S), USD vs ₹, and HS 4412 "Others".**
    - Comtrade CY2024 4412 plywood imports were **$256.5 m** [V]. FIPPI's FY25 figure for plywood + MDF + PB is **₹3,431 Cr**, of which plywood ~73% (≈₹2,500 Cr ≈ $300 m) [V FIPPI].
    - FIPPI found that **96–100% of post-QCO Vietnamese and Chinese plywood was declared under the "Others" sub-heading of 4412** to avoid BIS checks. Code-level import statistics after Feb-2025 therefore understate non-compliant flows [V FIPPI].

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Four panels, four economics, one distribution system.**
  - India's wood-panel market (plywood + laminates + MDF + particle board) is roughly **₹55,000–65,000 Cr** at manufacturer prices in FY26 [E; built bottom-up in §2].
  - **Plywood** is the largest panel (~₹29,000–31,000 Cr [E]), ~70% unorganised [screener/primer] and growing **~5–7%/yr** [V PL Q1 call].
  - **Decorative laminates** are ~₹11,000–13,000 Cr [E], organised-heavy and export-capable (Greenlam, Merino, Stylam, Century, Royale Touche, Advance and others).
  - **MDF** demand is **~3.0 mn CBM in FY26, +15%** (≈₹8,500–9,000 Cr) [V BOBCAPS; E for ₹] against **4.0–4.5 mn CBM of capacity**, i.e. ~65–75% industry utilisation [V BOBCAPS; E].
  - **Particle board** is the smallest (~₹3,000–4,500 Cr [E]) and the most price-taken.
- **Century is the only scaled four-panel player.**
  - FY26 consolidated revenue **₹5,397 Cr (+19.2%)**, EBITDA ex-forex **₹701.7 Cr (13.0%)**, PAT **₹268 Cr (+44%)** [V IP; V screener].
  - **Plywood is 54% of revenue and ~62% of segment EBITDA** (₹444 Cr of ₹713 Cr summed segment EBITDA ex-forex) [V; E share].
  - Q1 FY27 was a record quarter: revenue **₹1,561 Cr (+33.5%)**, EBITDA ex-forex ₹202.5 Cr (13.0%), PAT ₹83.3 Cr (+57%).
  - Inside that quarter the panels split:
    - **Plywood EBITDA margin rose to 16.9%** on volume +28.8% and realisation +4.8%.
    - **MDF EBITDA margin fell to 7.0%** (segment EBIT 2.8%): a planned shutdown for the AP line extension plus a failed ~15% price hike that was rolled back.
    - **Particle board stayed at 3.7%** EBITDA [V IP Q1 FY27; V PL].
- **The defining near-term force is MDF oversupply plus a chemical-cost shock.**
  - Industry capacity grew **15–20% in FY26** [V-snip India Ratings via unlistedzone] against demand of ~12–15%.
  - Greenpanel's new 231k CBM AP line (Mar-2025) runs at **56% utilisation (FY26)** [V ICRA].
  - Greenpanel's MDF realisation fell **−9.5% YoY in Q4 FY26** to ₹27,120/CBM; MDF EBITDA margin was 7.8% [V BOBCAPS].
  - Two more lines (~400k CBM combined, one in Madhya Pradesh, one in AP) arrive in H2 FY27 [V BOBCAPS].
  - The **West Asia war pushed resin chemicals up ~40–45%** (urea-formaldehyde, melamine, methanol, phenol) [V BOBCAPS, Greenpanel Q4 call].
  - The industry raised MDF prices ~15% in Apr-2026; **almost all of it was rolled back**, with only 1–2% sticking in select markets [V PL 11-Aug-26].
  - Greenpanel's **market cap fell from ~₹2,300 Cr (11-Aug) to ₹1,605 Cr (23-Oct)**; the stock is at its 52-week low (₹130) [V PL; V screener].
- **The defining structural force is formalisation, which the QCOs accelerated.**
  - The **Wood Based Boards (Quality Control) Order, 2024 took effect 11-Feb-2025**, and the **Plywood & Wooden Flush Door Shutters (QC) Order, 2024 took effect 28-Feb-2025** [V FIPPI].
  - Only BIS-licensed foreign makers can ship to India. **No Chinese or Vietnamese plywood maker holds a BIS licence**, and only **two foreign MDF makers (one Thai, one Malaysian)** do [V FIPPI].
  - Plywood imports went from **₹369 Cr in Feb-2025** (pre-deadline stockpiling) to **₹42–54 Cr/month in Apr–May 2025** [V FIPPI]. MDF imports went from ₹88 Cr (Feb-25) to ~₹20 Cr/month [V FIPPI]; per a broker, ~20,000 CBM/month (H2 FY25) to ~1,000 CBM/month (Q2 FY26) [V-snip BOBCAPS Nov-25].
  - CY2025 vs CY2024 import values: **plywood (4412) −43% to $145 m; MDF/fibreboard (4411) −36% to $56.5 m; particle board (4410) −50% to $10.3 m** [V Comtrade].
  - Century's MD: the QCO "stopped all the imports… by 80%" [V Q2 FY26 transcript].
  - **But veneer imports (4408) rose 35% to $485 m in CY2025** [V Comtrade]. Value-add moved onshore, not the timber.
- **Valuation dispersion is extreme** [V screener 23-Oct-26]:
  - Century trades at **47.4× TTM P/E, 5.4× book**, m-cap ₹14,079 Cr (52-week range ₹618–859; the stock is near its low).
  - Greenpanel trades at 1.2× book on near-zero earnings.
  - Stylam (laminates, ~55% export volume) trades at **30.8× P/E with a 26.6% ROCE**.
  - Greenlam trades at 58× P/E on an 8% ROCE (capex digestion).
  - Rushil trades at **0.68× book**.
  - **The market pays for branded plywood and laminate franchises and refuses to pay for MDF capacity.**
- **Capex cycle and ROCE dilution.**
  - Century spent **~₹2,300 Cr in FY23–FY26** (AP MDF ₹730 Cr, AP laminates ₹210 Cr, Chennai PB ₹694 Cr, Punjab plywood, port) [V capex table].
  - Debt went from ₹239 Cr (Mar-23) to **₹1,644 Cr (Jun-26)**.
  - **ROACE fell from 30.9% (FY23) to 12.3% (FY25)**; it recovered to 13.4% (FY26) and 14.4% (Q1 FY27 annualised) [V IP].
  - The next leg is announced: **plywood and MDF capacity each +>50% by FY29** [V HSIE 6-Feb-26]; a UP plywood plant targeted for Q1 FY28; the Hoshiarpur plywood greenfield in Q3 FY27 [V PL; V-snip investywise].

### 1.2 Value chain: inputs to end user

1. **Timber.**
   - Agro-forestry plantations of poplar (Punjab, Haryana, UP), eucalyptus (AP, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Gujarat) and melia/mixed species.
   - Farmers sell through mandis (Yamunanagar is the poplar hub) or direct to mills.
   - Imported face veneer and logs come from Africa (Gabon), Southeast Asia and Latin America.
   - Century owns a **Gabon veneer unit (₹22 Cr capex)** [V capex table]. It impaired ₹49 Cr on its **Myanmar subsidiary in FY23** [V IP].
2. **Chemicals.**
   - Urea + methanol → formaldehyde → **UF resin** (MR ply, MDF, PB).
   - Melamine (from urea) → MUF / melamine resin (laminate top, prelam).
   - Phenol + formaldehyde → **PF resin** (BWP/marine ply, laminate core).
   - Most large players run captive resin plants. Century lists a **₹19.9 Cr resin plant** [V capex].
   - Methanol and phenol are imported and priced off gas/crude, so the West Asia crisis passes straight through [V Q2 FY26 transcript: chemicals "linked to global commodity movements… natural gas or… crude" and freight].
3. **Panel manufacturing.**
   - Plywood is labour-intensive batch pressing: multi-daylight hot presses, low capex per tonne, many small units.
   - MDF and PB are capital-intensive continuous lines: ~₹600–750 Cr per 2–3 lakh CBM greenfield. Century AP MDF cost ₹730 Cr; Chennai PB ₹694 Cr [V capex].
   - Laminates use multi-daylight presses. A new press in an existing plant cost Century only ₹25 Cr, against ₹200 Cr for the first two presses including infrastructure [V PL 4-Aug-26].
4. **Distribution.**
   - Manufacturer → distributor → dealer/retailer (the "plywood & hardware" shop) → carpenter/contractor/interior designer → homeowner.
   - OEM/project channel: modular-furniture factories, builders, Pepperfry/Livspace-type firms.
   - Greenpanel: 2,300 distributors and 12,500 retailers; OEM 20–25% of MDF mix [V ICRA; V PL]. Rushil: 600+ direct distributors and 4,400+ retailers [V screener].
5. **End markets.**
   - New housing fit-out, which lags handover by 12–24 months [primer].
   - Renovation/replacement.
   - Commercial interiors (offices, retail, hospitality).
   - Furniture manufacturing (domestic and export).
   - Construction shuttering (low-grade ply).

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **Brand + channel in plywood.**
  - Century's plywood earns **15.2% EBITDA (FY26) and 16.9% (Q1 FY27)** [V], while Greenpanel's plywood earns **~4–5%** [V BOBCAPS].
  - The difference is brand pull with carpenters, the Sainik value range, warranties (Century's "Total Cover Insurance": full furniture replacement on plywood failure, claim ratio ~0.06% [V PL]) and dealer credit.
  - Plywood is low capital intensity, so its ROCE is high [E].
- **Design library + export reach in laminates.**
  - Stylam (~20–21% OPM, 26.6% ROCE, ~55% of sheets exported) [V screener; V-snip] vs Century laminates at 8.5% EBITDA [V] and Merino laminates at 11.8% [V-snip].
  - Exports to Europe/US carry better prices but take freight and tariff risk.
- **Scale + utilisation in MDF/PB.**
  - Value is captured only when lines run at ≥80% and price discipline holds.
  - FY22–23 MDF margins were 28–32% at Century (FY22 31.7% EBITDA) and ~27–31% at Greenpanel [V IP; V-snip zappchai]. They fell to 7–13% in FY25–26 as capacity outran demand.
- **Logistics** is a captive-turned-merchant CFS/port business at Century, unrelated to panels. It is a candidate for a strategic partner [V PL].

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Housing completions and fit-out (12–24-month lag).**
   - Anarock top-7 sales **Q3 CY2026: ~100,220 units (+3% YoY)**; launches 114,320 (+18%); **unsold inventory 6.31 lakh units vs 5.62 lakh a year ago**; prices +7% YoY [V-snip constructionworld/businesstoday 29-Sep-26].
   - PropEquity top-9: **103,170 units (−6% YoY)**, new supply 98,165 (−2%) [V-snip].
   - Read for panels: the 2022–24 launch boom is now in handover, which supports FY27 fit-out demand. But sales have plateaued and the 5.50% repo hike (7-Oct-2026) raises EMIs, so FY28–29 fit-out demand is the risk [E].
   - Panel demand lags housing *sales* by ~2–3 years and housing *completions* by 6–18 months [E; primer says 12–24 months].
2. **Renovation and replacement** is roughly a third to a half of plywood/laminate demand [E, FLAG: no primary source].
   - It is steadier and less rate-sensitive.
   - It is the channel where the carpenter's brand choice matters most.
3. **Furniture industrialisation (MDF/PB pull).**
   - Modular kitchen and wardrobe factories, flat-pack (IKEA India), office furniture and contract manufacturing for export.
   - Management at Century and Greenpanel see MDF industry growth of **15–20%** [V Q2 FY26 transcript] or **mid-teens** [V PL Greenpanel Q1 FY27].
   - Greenply saw **MDF growth of 35–40% YoY** in its latest quarter from a small base [V apacnews].
   - Plywood grows **5–7%** [V PL].
4. **Formalisation / share shift (independent of the housing cycle).**
   - Unorganised plywood is ~70% [screener].
   - Century's plywood volume grew 16.5% (FY26) and 28.8% (Q1 FY27) against industry growth of ~5–7%, i.e. share gain [V].
   - The QCO-driven import cut (−80%) handed volume to domestic branded players [V transcript].
   - Century targets **~15% of total plywood within 5 years** (from ~9.5–10%) [V PL].
5. **Commercial interiors.** Office absorption and hospitality/retail fit-outs support laminates, compacts and cladding [K]. Not quantified [gap].
6. **Exports.**
   - Laminates go to Europe, the US, the Middle East and SE Asia.
   - MDF goes to the Middle East.
   - **The US is ~10–15% of Indian laminate exports** [E, FLAG]. Exposure to the 10% Section 301 tariff is moderate for laminates and negligible for MDF/plywood ($6.5 m of 4411 exports to the US in CY2025 [V Comtrade]).

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 BIS Quality Control Orders (the defining 2025 event)
- **Wood Based Boards (Quality Control) Order, 2024** covers MDF (IS 12406), particle board (IS 3087/IS 12823 prelam) and block board (IS 1659) [K for IS numbers].
  - **Effective 11-Feb-2025** for large and medium units; small 11-May-2025; micro 11-Aug-2025 [V FIPPI; V-snip plyreporter on the DPIIT notification of 12-Mar-2024].
- **Plywood and Wooden Flush Door Shutters (QC) Order, 2024** covers IS 303 (general plywood), IS 710 (marine), IS 4990 (shuttering), IS 2202 (flush doors).
  - **Effective 28-Feb-2025**; small 28-May-2025; micro 28-Aug-2025 [V FIPPI; V-snip].
- **History of deferrals:**
  - The original 2023 orders were deferred one year by DPIIT notifications of **12-Mar-2024** (boards) and **15-Mar-2024** (plywood); BIS circulated them on 26-Mar-2024 [V-snip plyreporter/BIS circular].
  - FIPPI had sought a 1.5–2-year extension in Dec-2023 [V-snip].
  - DPIIT signalled "no further extension" [V-snip plyreporter].
- **Effect:**
  - Imports collapsed (§5.3).
  - Small domestic mills must certify or exit, a formalisation tailwind.
  - Enforcement gaps exist: "Others" misclassification and Nepal licence misuse [V FIPPI].
- **Status after Nov-2025:** the government rescinded several chemical/polymer QCOs in Nov-2025 (sibling dossiers). **No evidence was found that the wood-panel QCOs were rescinded**. Managements in Nov-2025 and Aug-2026 still cite them as in force [V transcript Q2 FY26; V-snip]. Writer: say "in force as of the latest management commentary".

### 7.2 Trade remedies
- **No current anti-dumping duty on MDF, plywood or laminates was found in this session** [gap §H].
  - DGTR has historically looked at MDF from Vietnam/Indonesia/Thailand/Sri Lanka and decorative paper [K, FLAG — not verified].
  - The QCO is functioning as a non-tariff barrier instead.
- **Basic customs duty** on plywood/MDF is ~10–20% plus SWS [K, FLAG]. Not re-verified.
- **India–ASEAN FTA (AITIGA)** lowers duty on ASEAN panels [K]. That is why Vietnam, Thailand and Malaysia dominated imports, and why the QCO, not tariffs, was the lever.

### 7.3 Forestry and agro-forestry
- Plantation timber (poplar, eucalyptus, melia) is the backbone. Many states have **exempted plantation species from transit permits** to encourage agro-forestry [K].
- Greenply cites a "One Sheet, One Tree" programme with **>90 million trees planted (target 100 mn)** [V apacnews].
- State permission is required for new veneer/plywood units in some states under the 1996 Supreme Court (Godavarman) order and state wood-based industry committees [K]. This historically capped unorganised capacity in some states.
- **Imported logs and veneer:**
  - Gabon banned log exports (2010), so processing moved to Gabon SEZs; Century's Gabon veneer unit is one example [K; V capex].
  - Myanmar's 2014 log-export ban [K] is behind Century's Myanmar impairment of ₹49 Cr (FY23) [V].

### 7.4 GST
- **GST 2017 → Nov-2017 cut.** Plywood, MDF and particle board started at **28%** in July 2017. They were **cut to 18% on 10-Nov-2017** (GST Council, 178-item cut) [V-snip CBEC press release dated 16-Nov-2017 via search; K].
- **GST 2.0 (22-Sep-2025):** plywood, MDF, particle board, veneers and laminated wood (HSN 4410–4412) **remain at 18%** under Notification 9/2025-Central Tax (Rate), Schedule II [V-snip taxgarden; K].
  - Wood chips, sawdust, pallets, packing cases and bamboo flooring are at 5% [V-snip].
  - **Conflict:** two secondary sources say raw timber (4403) moved **5% → 18%** from 22-Sep-2025 [V-snip axis.bank.in, busy.in]. Not verified against the notification [FLAG].
  - **Effect:** GST 2.0 did **not** lower panel rates. Many other building materials fell (cement 28% → 18% [K, sibling]). The branded-vs-unorganised price gap from tax arbitrage is unchanged. A timber rate rise, if confirmed, would raise input costs for mills that cannot claim input credit, i.e. small/unorganised ones [E].
- **E-way bills and e-invoicing** (2018–2023) pushed formalisation more than the rate did [K].

### 7.5 Environment and product safety
- **Formaldehyde emission classes:** E0/E1/E2 per IS and EN 717. Branded players market "E0/E1" boards [K].
- **CARB-II/TSCA Title VI** compliance is required for US exports of composite wood (MDF/PB/HWPW) and laminates on substrates [K]. This raises the bar for India-to-US exports.
- ICRA flags formaldehyde emissions and fossil-fuel use as Greenpanel's environmental risks [V ICRA].

### 7.6 US tariffs (export exposure)
- Since **24-Jul-2026, 10% Section 301 applies to Indian goods on top of MFN** (prompt context; under challenge).
- The panel industry's US exposure is small:
  - 4411 MDF exports to the US were **$6.5 m in CY2025** [V Comtrade].
  - Laminates are the exposed product: Stylam's Panchkula plant is "designed primarily for export markets like the US" [V-snip]; Greenlam and Merino have US distribution [K].
- **Freight and the Gulf matter more than US tariffs in 2026.** The Red Sea/Hormuz disruption hit Middle East MDF exports to zero [V PL Greenpanel] and delayed ₹27 Cr of Greenlam exports [V-snip].

### 7.7 Incentives
- **EPCG** (export promotion capital goods): Greenpanel booked ~₹25 Cr of EPCG benefit in FY26, with ~₹26 Cr remaining, contingent on exports [V BOBCAPS]. This links capex subsidies to export volumes that the Gulf crisis disrupted.
- **State capital subsidies** apply in AP (Badvel/Chittoor) and Punjab (power subsidy at Hoshiarpur) [V IP; K].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **1980s–2000s: the plywood era.**
   - Century was incorporated in **Jan-1982** (screener) and is described as established in Kolkata in 1986 (primer). Greenply was founded in 1984 [K].
   - Branded plywood grew alongside a vast unorganised base around Yamunanagar, Kerala and Assam.
   - The **1996 Supreme Court Godavarman orders** shut Assam/NE forest-based plywood mills, and the industry moved to plantation timber in the North and South [K].
2. **2009–2014: first MDF wave.**
   - Greenply's Rudrapur MDF (2010) and Rushil's Chikmagalur plant [K].
   - Imports from Thailand, Vietnam and Malaysia under the ASEAN FTA capped pricing [K].
3. **2016–2018: GST and the second MDF wave.**
   - Century's Hoshiarpur MDF (2016–17), Greenply's AP MDF (2018) and Action Tesa's Sitarganj line (Feb-2018, 511k CBM) [V-snip].
   - Panels were at 28% GST, then 18% (Nov-2017).
   - MDF utilisation crashed, to ~40% in some months of 2018 [V-snip plyreporter archive].
4. **2019: Greenply/Greenpanel demerger** (effective 1-Jul-2019) [V ICRA].
5. **FY22–FY23: the post-COVID super-cycle.**
   - Plywood and MDF prices rose with timber and resin inflation, and imports were constrained by freight.
   - **MDF EBITDA hit 31.7% at Century (FY22) and ~31% at Greenpanel**; Greenpanel's ROCE was 29% (FY22) [V IP; V screener; V-snip].
   - Century's ROACE was 30.9% (FY23) [V].
6. **FY24–FY26: the glut.**
   - Everyone announced capacity: Century AP MDF + Chennai PB + AP laminates; Greenpanel AP 231k CBM; Greenply Vadodara MDF; Rushil; Merino Halol PB; Greenlam Naidupeta PB; Action Tesa Vizag.
   - Chinese/ASEAN imports surged into the QCO deadline, with Feb-2025 plywood imports at ₹369 Cr [V FIPPI].
   - MDF realisations fell **−13.7% at Century in FY25** (₹33,638 → ₹29,032/CBM) [V].
   - Greenpanel's FY26 PAT was −₹29 Cr [V screener]. Merino's panel business lost ₹120 Cr [V-snip].
7. **Feb-2025: QCOs bite.** Imports fell 36–50% (CY2025). Branded plywood volumes accelerated: Century plywood +16.5% FY26, +28.8% Q1 FY27 [V].
8. **Mar-2026 onward: the West Asia war.**
   - Chemicals rose 40–45%, Gulf export lanes shut and freight multiplied ~10× [V BOBCAPS; V PL].
   - A 15% MDF price hike failed (rolled back) and plywood +7% stuck [V PL].
   - The repo hike to 5.50% (Oct-2026) adds a demand-side worry.
   - Greenpanel's stock halved (₹309 → ₹131, 52-week high/low) [V screener].

### 9.3 MDF operating-leverage arithmetic [E; FLAG composite]
- Take a 3 lakh CBM line at ₹30,000/CBM. Revenue at 100% utilisation is ₹900 Cr.
- Assume variable cost (timber + resin + power + freight) ~₹21,000–23,000/CBM and fixed cash cost ~₹60–75 Cr/yr. Then:
  - 85% utilisation: revenue ₹765 Cr; contribution ~₹190–230 Cr; EBITDA ~₹120–165 Cr (16–21%).
  - 60% utilisation: revenue ₹540 Cr; contribution ~₹135–160 Cr; EBITDA ~₹60–100 Cr (11–18%).
  - 60% utilisation with a 10% price cut (₹27,000/CBM): revenue ₹486 Cr; contribution ~₹80–105 Cr; EBITDA ~₹5–45 Cr (1–9%). **This is roughly Greenpanel's FY26** (EBITDA/CBM ~₹2,000 [E: MDF EBITDA ~₹103 Cr of FY26 company EBITDA per BOBCAPS fig 2 labels; FLAG]).
- Add D&A of ~₹45–60 Cr/yr and interest on ₹500–700 Cr of project debt. A new line at 60% utilisation loses money at PAT: Century Panels' Q1 FY27 PAT was −₹23.5 Cr [V].
- **Each 10-point change in utilisation moves EBITDA/CBM by ~₹1,000–1,500** [E].
- **Each ₹1,000/CBM change in realisation moves MDF EBITDA by ~₹43 Cr/yr at Century's FY26 volume** (~6% of company EBITDA) [E].

### 9.4 Chemical sensitivity [E]
- A +40% resin shock on resin at ~15–20% of MDF COGS adds ~6–8% to MDF cost per CBM (~₹1,800–2,400/CBM). That requires a ~7–8% price rise to hold margin.
- The industry tried 15% and failed. Q1 FY27 shows the result: Century MDF EBITDA 7.0%, Greenpanel MDF 10.3% (helped by cheaper timber) [V].

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **MDF/HDF and PB substitute for plywood in factory furniture.**
   - India's mix is plywood 54% vs a global ~20% [V-snip]. Every share point that moves from plywood to MDF/PB shifts value from a brand-led, asset-light business to a capital-heavy one.

2. **Continuous-press technology and Chinese machinery.**

5. **Formaldehyde-free resins** (pMDI, soy-based, lignin) are an emerging requirement for US/EU export compliance [K].
6. **Digital channel tools:** carpenter loyalty apps, QR-code authentication (anti-counterfeit) and insurance-backed warranties (Century "Total Cover") [V PL]. A brand moat that is hard for unorganised mills to copy.

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Formalisation via the QCO (structural, multi-year).**
   - BIS QCOs (Feb-2025) cut imports by 36–50% and force small mills to certify or exit. On top of that, the carpenter-led brand moat lets branded plywood grow ~3–5× the market: Century +16.5% FY26 and +28.8% Q1 FY27 against an industry at 5–7%.
   - This is the main reason Century's plywood margin is at a record 15–17%.
2. **MDF capacity glut (cyclical, 2025–2028).**
   - Capacity is 4.0–4.5 mn CBM against demand of ~3.0 mn CBM.
   - Another ~0.4 mn CBM arrives in H2 FY27, plus Greenply's line in 2027 and Century's planned +50% by FY29.
   - MDF margins sit at 7–13% against 27–32% at the FY22–23 peak. Analysts see pricing power returning only above ~80% industry utilisation, around FY28 [V-snip India Ratings].
3. **Input-cost shocks you cannot hedge.**
   - Timber is local, monsoon-driven and species-dependent.
   - Resins are global, driven by gas, crude and freight; the West Asia war added 40–45% to chemicals.
   - Pass-through works in branded plywood (+7% stuck) and fails in MDF (+15% rolled back) [V PL].
4. **Capex and ROCE dilution.**
   - MDF/PB lines need ~3–4× the capital per rupee of sales that plywood needs.
   - Century's ROACE fell from 30.9% to 12.3% (FY23 → FY25) as debt rose from ₹239 Cr to ₹1,383 Cr. The next ₹1,500–2,500 Cr capex leg [E] is announced.
   - **Who funds capex without diluting returns is the investment question.**
5. **Distribution and brand (carpenter loyalty) is the only durable moat.**
   - It explains Century's plywood margin of 15–17% against Greenpanel's ~4–5% in the same product, and Stylam's 26.6% ROCE against Greenlam's 8%.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Panel company EBITDA = Σ over panels [Volume (CBM or sheets) × Realisation (₹/unit) − Variable cost/unit × Volume] − Fixed costs − Brand & channel spend**, where:
- Volume = min(Demand share, Capacity × Utilisation) + Outsourced volume (plywood only).
- Realisation is set by brand/mix (plywood, laminates) or by industry utilisation and import parity (MDF, PB).
- Variable cost = timber/veneer + resins/papers + power + freight.

### C.1 Worked example 1: Century plywood, FY26 [V volumes/values; E split]
- Volume 485,403 CBM × ₹55,878/CBM = ₹2,712 Cr plywood net sales. Decoply and commercial veneer add ₹183 Cr, giving segment net turnover of ₹2,895 Cr and total income of ₹2,915 Cr [V].
- Gross profit ₹937 Cr (32.1% of total income) → EBITDA ex-forex **₹444 Cr (15.2%)** → EBITDA per CBM of plywood ≈ **₹9,150** [E: 444 Cr ÷ 485,403].
- Depreciation ₹42 Cr; segment profit ex-forex ₹431 Cr (14.8%) [V].
- Capital employed: not disclosed in the deck. At ~₹1,000–1,200 Cr [E, FLAG], pre-tax segment ROCE is ~35–45%.

### C.2 Worked example 2: Century MDF, FY26 vs Q1 FY27 [V; E for per-CBM]
- **FY26:** 430,713 CBM × ₹29,527 = ₹1,272 Cr; total income ₹1,284 Cr; gross profit 25.7%; EBITDA ex-forex ₹163 Cr (12.7%) → **₹3,784/CBM**; depreciation ₹60.6 Cr; segment EBIT ex-forex ₹108 Cr (8.4%).
- **Q1 FY27:** 102,951 CBM × ₹32,222 = ₹332 Cr; gross profit 21.3% (vs 29.2% a year earlier, the chemical squeeze); EBITDA ex-forex ₹23.5 Cr (7.0%) → **₹2,285/CBM**; segment EBIT ex-forex ₹9.5 Cr (2.8%).
- **FY22 (peak):** 177,936 CBM × ₹31,215 = ₹555 Cr; EBITDA ₹177.7 Cr (31.7%) → **₹9,989/CBM**.
- Interpretation: realisation is about the same as FY22 (₹32k vs ₹31k), but EBITDA/CBM is down ~77% [E], because cost per CBM rose by ~₹7,700 (timber + chemicals + the AP plant's fixed costs) [E].

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed

1. **Century is a plywood company with a capital-heavy board business attached.**
   - Per AR note 43, plywood earned **₹428 Cr of EBIT on ₹1,055 Cr of capital employed (~41% pre-tax)**.
   - MDF + PB earned **₹72 Cr on ₹2,226 Cr (~3%)**.
   - Every rupee of board capacity is a ~3× heavier capital commitment per rupee of sales than plywood.
   - The FY31 ₹12,000 Cr plan needs ~₹2,500 Cr more capex, and the UP/Odisha board projects (₹1,300 Cr of MDF/PB alone) are where ROCE is won or lost.
2. **The MDF glut is a supply-calendar story, not a demand story.**
   - Listed-peer domestic MDF volumes grew ~10–25% in Q1 FY27 ("mid-teens" for the industry).
   - But ~0.85–0.9 mn CBM of new lines (Action Tesa Vizag, Elixrr MP, Greenply line 2) on a ~4.2 mn base arrives by mid-2027, and ~1.5 mn CBM sits with Tier-2 lines that discount.
   - Price discipline failed in Q1: the 15% hike was rolled back by all but Rushil.
3. **Greenply, not Century, runs the most profitable MDF line.** Greenply's Vadodara MDF made **17.3% Core EBITDA** at ~₹33,500/CBM on 300k CBM, while Century made 7.0% and Greenpanel 10.3%. A single, full, West-India line with a 22% pre-lam mix beats bigger, emptier networks. The "scale wins" narrative needs this caveat.
4. **Greenpanel has blinked.** It has dropped its price premium to Century and now prices at par. Century out-sold it in domestic MDF in each of the last two quarters (analyst, unchallenged). The market leader by capacity is now a price-follower. Combined with **₹154 Cr of outstanding EPCG export obligations** and zero exports, that is a hidden balance-sheet risk.
5. **The chemical shock was bigger and shorter than "+40–45%" suggests.**
   - Methanol, the formaldehyde feedstock, was +~180% YoY in rupee terms by May-2026, then eased after the June US–Iran MoU. CRISIL sees FY27 CFR at US$380–420/t, vs ~US$300 pre-war.
   - Laminates carry ~35% chemical content and pass through ~7–8% net (Greenlam).
   - MDF could not pass it through; plywood (Century +7%) could.
6. **Laminate volumes are shrinking while value grows, and the reason is size mix, not weakness.**
   - Century: sheets −8.4% in Q1 (large formats); Greenlam: −6.4% (export timing); Stylam: −3% QoQ (export).
   - Realisations are up 13–29% YoY (Greenlam ₹1,240/sheet; Rushil ₹836 at 1 mm-equivalent).
   - Decor-paper ADD on China ($110–542/t, under sunset review to Mar-2027) is a cost headwind laminators rarely mention.
7. **Century's balance sheet is stronger on its own definitions than on ICRA's.**
   - Deck "total debt" ₹1,531 Cr vs ICRA ₹1,765 Cr. ICRA's leverage path is 2.6× → 2.2–2.3× (FY27) → 1.6–1.7× (FY28).
   - Contingent liabilities nearly doubled to ₹92 Cr, mostly new income-tax and GST demands. Small, but worth a line.
8. **Plywood formalisation now has a measured pace.** Century targets 15% of the total market in five years (from 9.5–10%), i.e. 12–15% a year against 5–7% industry growth. Greenply's own-manufactured share rose from 58% to 68% in a year. **The organised players are in-sourcing**, which lifts capital intensity but secures margins against the trade-and-outsourcing model.
