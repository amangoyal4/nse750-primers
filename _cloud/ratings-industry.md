## READ THIS FIRST: twelve definitional traps

1. **CY vs FY.**
   - CRISIL's "FY2026" (in its own filings) = **January–December 2026**. Its "Q3 FY26" = Jul–Sep 2026.
   - ICRA/CARE "FY26" = Apr-2025 to Mar-2026.
   - Never put CRISIL CY25 and ICRA FY26 in one column without a label. Part 3 does it only as a labelled "latest full year" comparison.
   - Screener labels CRISIL's years "Dec 2025" etc. [V].
2. **Standalone vs consolidated.**
   - CRISIL standalone (₹1,796.5 Cr revenue, ₹614.9 Cr PAT in CY25) holds the Indian research/analytics businesses and the Global Analytics Centre. It also books **dividends from subsidiaries** as other income (₹342.8 Cr in CY25).
   - **Crisil Ratings Ltd is a separate wholly-owned subsidiary**, so domestic ratings revenue is *not* in standalone.
   - Use consolidated everywhere: ₹3,649 Cr revenue, ₹766 Cr PAT in CY25 [V].
3. **"Ratings segment" ≠ Crisil Ratings Ltd ≠ "domestic ratings".** CRISIL's reported **Ratings services segment (₹1,078.7 Cr in CY25)** contains two things:
   - **(a) Crisil Ratings Ltd**, the SEBI-registered CRA: CY25 turnover **₹670.6 Cr**, PBT ₹398.8 Cr, PAT ₹297.4 Cr [V AR25 AOC-1].
   - **(b) the Global Analytics Centre (GAC)**, which does ratings-support analytics for **S&P Global Ratings** at a **transfer-pricing cost-plus margin** [V call 20-Apr-26]. GAC ≈ **₹408 Cr** [E: 1,078.7 − 670.6].
   - So the "44% Ratings margin" blends a ~59%-PBT-margin domestic CRA with a cost-plus captive service.
   - For **domestic market-share maths use Crisil Ratings Ltd (₹670.6 Cr)**, not the segment.
4. **"Research" means three different things.**
   - (i) CRISIL's reported **Research, Analytics & Solutions (RAS)** segment (₹2,572.4 Cr, CY25). It covers Crisil Integral IQ (formerly GR&RS), Crisil Coalition Greenwich (incl. PriceMetrix) and Crisil Intelligence (formerly MI&A / CRISIL Research). There is no sub-split. Management: "the two global businesses … Coalition Greenwich and Integral IQ … are the material parts … followed by the intelligence business in India" [V call].
   - (ii) ICRA's **"Research & Analytics" (renamed "Risk & Analytics" in Q1 FY27)**. This is ICRA Analytics: KnowTech (outsourcing for Moody's), BankTech (incl. Fintellix, D2K) and CapTech [V].
   - (iii) CARE's **"non-ratings"** segment: CareEdge Analytics & Advisory plus ESG. It is small (₹50 Cr).
   - Never compare (i) with (ii) or (iii) as if they were the same business.
5. **Rated debt vs number of ratings vs number of issuers vs fresh ratings. Every "market share" has a basis.**
   - **SEBI's annual-report data covers listed / to-be-listed securities only.** FY26: **725 fresh ratings**, of which CRISIL had **339 (46.8%)**, ICRA 20.1%, India Ratings 19.3%. Outstanding ratings on listed securities: **1,942** [V SEBI AR 2025-26].
   - **Bank-loan ratings (BLRs) are excluded from SEBI's chart but are the bulk of rated entities.** Crisil Ratings alone has **7,200+ companies** with active ratings and issued **1,280+ new BLRs in CY25** [V AR25].
   - **Revenue share** is a third basis. Domestic ratings revenue, latest year: Crisil Ratings ₹670.6 Cr (CY25), CARE standalone ₹387.7 Cr (FY26), ICRA ratings ₹336.5 Cr (FY26). That makes CRISIL **~48% of the listed three** [E, mixed years]. India Ratings, Acuité and Infomerics revenue is unsourced.
   - **The primer's ">60% share" has no basis we could verify.**
6. **BLR vs capital-market ratings.**
   - A **BLR** rates a bank facility (term loan, cash credit, LC/BG). Banks use it for Basel capital under RBI's ECAI rules.
   - **Capital-market ratings** cover NCDs/bonds, CP, securitisation, fixed deposits, MLDs and municipal bonds, and are *mandatory* for issuance under SEBI rules.
   - Pricing: "the yields in the bond market as far as rating fees are concerned are better than in the bank loan market … bank loan rating … is fairly commoditized" (CARE's Revati Kasture, 14-May-2026) [V].
   - **Bank credit can grow while bond issuance falls (2025–26), so ratings revenue still grows. The mix gets worse, but volume holds.**
7. **Initial (new-issue) revenue vs surveillance revenue.**
   - Surveillance (annual monitoring) fees on the outstanding book are an annuity.
   - Crisil Ratings: surveillance revenue "is driven by the new rating revenues performance in prior year … what you're going to see in '26 will be largely driven by the new revenues we did in 2025 and partly … '24" [V call].
   - So **this year's ratings revenue lags last year's issuance.** That is why ratings revenue grew ~25% in Q3 CY26 while 9M bond issuance fell 6%.
8. **Issuer-pays and "investor preference".**
   - The issuer pays, but investors choose which ratings they accept.
   - CRISIL attributes its share gains to "investor preference for best-in-class assessments" [V].
   - CRISIL's 46.8% share of fresh *listed* ratings versus its much smaller presence in the long tail of SME BLRs is the clearest expression of this two-tier market.
9. **"Default rate" has three different definitions.**
   - (i) SEBI's count of **defaults among listed-security ratings** (FY26: 11 across all CRAs; CRISIL 2) [V].
   - (ii) Each CRA's **cumulative default rate (CDR)** by grade and horizon (1/2/3-year), published on its own website under SEBI's format.
   - (iii) RBI's new **average one-year Observed Default Rate (ODR)** per rating grade, used from 1-Apr-2027 to *adjust bank risk weights* (Table 21: AAA ≤0.05%, AA ≤0.10%, A ≤0.20%, BBB ≤0.40%, BB ≤1.0%) [V RBI].
   - Never mix them.
10. **Brickwork was NOT finally de-licensed.**
    - SEBI cancelled Brickwork's registration on 6-Oct-2022, but the **SAT set the order aside in June 2023**. On 13-Sep-2023 SEBI replaced it with a six-month bar on new mandates, board reconstitution and an independent CEO [V-snip Moneylife/BoardStewardship].
    - Brickwork is still among the **8 SEBI-registered CRAs (31-Mar-2026)** and on **RBI's 2026 list of eligible domestic CRAs** [V SEBI AR; V RBI].
    - The brief's "SEBI cancelled licence 2022" is half the story. Write "cancelled in 2022, reinstated on appeal with curbs."
11. **Eight CRAs, not seven or nine.**
    - SEBI: eight registered at 31-Mar-2026: CRISIL Ratings, ICRA, India Ratings, CARE, Acuité, Infomerics, Brickwork and **Acer Credit Rating Pvt Ltd (registered 21-Aug-2025)** [V].
    - RBI's 2026 ECAI list has seven domestic agencies (no Acer) plus CareEdge Global IFSC for non-resident corporates [V].
    - Crisil Ratings' Jan-2026 note said "seven CRAs … another in the pipeline" [V].
    - The primer's "nine CRAs are registered in all" is wrong.
12. **ESG ratings are a separate SEBI licence.**
    - ESG Rating Providers (ERPs) are regulated *within* the SEBI (CRA) Regulations, 1999 (Chapter IVA, added Jul-2023) [K; V SEBI AR says "The regulatory framework for ESG Rating Providers (ERPs) is part of the SEBI (Credit Rating Agencies) Regulations, 1999"].
    - CRISIL's ERP is **Crisil ESG Ratings & Analytics Ltd** (a subsidiary of Crisil Ratings; CY25 turnover ₹4.0 Cr, loss ₹6.4 Cr) [V AOC-1]. CARE claims **58% share among Category-I ERPs** [V]. ICRA ESG Ratings Ltd is a Cat-I ERP (first rating Sept-2024) [V-snip screener].
    - The revenue is tiny. Do not over-weight it.

# PART 1 — INDUSTRY DOSSIER

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Two industries share one name.**
  - **(1) Indian credit ratings:** a SEBI-licensed, RBI-accredited, issuer-pays oligopoly. Revenue pool of the listed three ≈ **₹1,400 Cr** (Crisil Ratings ₹671 Cr CY25 + CARE ₹388 Cr FY26 standalone + ICRA ₹337 Cr FY26) [V/E]. Add India Ratings, Acuité, Infomerics, Brickwork and Acer, and the whole domestic pool is perhaps **₹1,900–2,200 Cr** [E; FLAG — unlisted revenues not sourced].
  - **(2) Financial research / analytics / benchmarking exported from India** to global banks, asset managers and the CRAs' own parents. CRISIL's RAS segment (₹2,572 Cr), GAC (~₹408 Cr) and ICRA's KnowTech/BankTech (₹266 Cr R&A in FY26) are listed proxies. Competitors include Acuity Knowledge Partners, Evalueserve, eClerx and the banks' own GCCs.
  - **CRISIL is ~70% industry (2) by revenue and ~50% by segment profit in 9M CY26** [V: Ratings 30.1% of 9M revenue, 50.0% of segment profit].
- **The 2026 paradox: bonds fall, raters boom.**
  - Corporate bond issuance (Prime Database, calendar): CY24 ₹11,195 bn → CY25 ₹11,245 bn (flat) → **9M CY26 ₹8,407 bn, −6%**. Q1 −12%, Q2 −26%, Q3 +36% on a −31.7% base [V CRISIL slides].
  - SEBI data: FY26 issuance **~₹9.1 lakh Cr, −8.4%, the first fall in four years** [V-snip]. CareEdge: Q1 FY27 issuance **₹2.5 lakh Cr, −29.3%** [V].
  - Yet rating revenues grew fast: Crisil Ratings segment **+23.6% in 9M CY26**; ICRA Ratings +14.2% in FY26 and +13% in Q1 FY27 [E: 83.8 vs 74.2]; CARE ratings segment +17% in FY26 and +19.1% in Q1 FY27 [V].
  - Three reasons: (a) **bank credit boomed**: **19.1% YoY in Aug-2026** vs 10.3% a year earlier; credit to large corporates +14.9% [V CRISIL]; CARE: 18.6% at end-Jun-2026 vs 9.5% [V]. That means more BLRs. (b) **Surveillance annuity** from the CY24–25 new-mandate surge. (c) **Pricing discipline / mix** ("we acquired a lot of new clients at good prices" — Crisil Ratings MD) [V].
- **The rate cycle has turned against bonds.** RBI hiked to 5.50% (Oct-2026), and the 10-year G-sec was ~7% in March 2026. Banks undercut bond yields for top corporates ("substitution of corporate bonds with bank loans") [V CRISIL call]. Higher rates "do not favour the bond market because yields further harden" [V]. **Near-term ratings growth rides on bank credit and surveillance, not bonds.**
- **RBI's new Standardised Approach (effective 1-Apr-2027) makes rating QUALITY a pricing variable for the first time.**
  - Corporate risk weights drop (AA 30→**20%**, BBB 100→**75%**, BB 150→**100%**). They are adjusted up a bucket if the **CRA's** one-year ODR for that grade breaches RBI's range [V RBI Directions 2026].
  - Unrated exposures above **₹500 Cr** (banking-system aggregate) carry **150%** [V].
  - Crisil Ratings estimated **~₹71,000 Cr** of bank capital relief on the draft, assuming every CRA qualifies [V].
  - **This is the single most important structural change to Indian CRA economics since Basel II (2008).** It rewards low-default CRAs and may concentrate BLR mandates in them.
- **CRISIL's global engine is slowing at the margin.** RAS growth fell from +34.9% (Q1 CY26) to +30.1% (Q2) to **+16.5% (Q3)**, and the margin fell to **17.2%** [V]. PriceMetrix (US$38m, from 7-Nov-2025) [V-snip] inflates CY26 growth. Coalition Greenwich pulled ~US$4.5m of renewals into Q1 [V]. FX gains vanished as hedges struck at ~₹88.8/$ rolled off. **The rupee at ~₹95 is a tailwind only after hedges roll.**
- **Valuation: CRISIL is priced as a global compounder; ICRA/CARE as domestic cyclicals** [screener 23-Oct-26]:
  - **CRISIL:** m-cap **₹34,745 Cr**; P/E **38.8×** TTM; P/B 10.6×; ROE 24.5%; EV/TTM op. profit ~**26.8×** [E].
  - **ICRA:** ₹4,316 Cr; P/E **22.0×**; P/B 3.7×; EV/EBITDA ~16.8× FY26 [E]. The stock is −32% from its 52-week high of ₹6,620.
  - **CARE:** ₹4,786 Cr; P/E **26.9×**; P/B 5.1×; EV/EBITDA ~20× [E].
  - **S&P Global:** P/E 27.4× (fwd 24.0×). **Moody's:** 29.5× (fwd 26.8×) [V stockanalysis 23-Oct-26].
  - **CRISIL trades at a premium to its own parent.** Explanations: scarcity, a low float, and India's growth.

### 1.2 The value chain (ratings)

1. **Trigger.** A borrower needs funding.
   - **Bond / CP route:** SEBI requires a rating from a registered CRA for public and listed privately placed debt [K: ILDS/NCS Regulations 2021]. Most institutional investors' mandates (insurers, pension funds, MFs) impose rating floors. CareEdge: "insurance companies and pension funds are permitted to invest only in instruments rated AA or above" [V].
   - **Bank route:** the bank prefers a rated facility because an external rating can cut its risk weight. Unrated large exposures (>₹500 Cr aggregate, from Apr-2027) carry 150% [V RBI].
2. **Mandate and fee.**
   - The issuer signs a rating agreement and pays an **initial fee**, usually scaled to the instrument/facility size with floors and caps [K; FLAG — no published fee schedule fetched].
   - A **surveillance fee** follows each year for the life of the instrument or facility.
   - CRAs also earn small fees from rating reviews and from structured/securitisation work (pool-level analysis, higher fees per ₹) [K].
3. **Analysis → rating committee → press release.**
   - The analyst team does management meetings, financial modelling, peer and industry benchmarking, and the rating committee.
   - The rating, outlook and rationale are published on the CRA website and stock exchanges. They are disclosed in the CRA's **monthly bulletin**, which RBI now requires for bank use [V RBI para 25].
4. **Surveillance.** The rating is reviewed at least annually. RBI requires a review within the previous 15 months for capital use [V]. Defaults must be recognised promptly (SEBI 90-day framework, 2017 onward [K]).
5. **Use.**
   - Investors use the rating to fix spreads and to meet investment limits.
   - Banks use it to compute risk-weighted assets. Insurers and pension funds use it for permitted investments. MF schemes use it for portfolio caps.
   - Regulators use it in rules: e.g., the large-corporate borrowing framework [K].
6. **Issuer-not-cooperating (INC) / withdrawal.**
   - If the issuer stops paying or sharing data, the CRA must still publish an "INC" rating.
   - From Apr-2027 an INC rating carries a **100% risk-weight floor for six months and 150% after** [V RBI para 31]. That is a strong incentive for borrowers to stay cooperative and keep paying.

### 1.3 The value chain (research/analytics export)
- **Buyers:** the global CIB and wealth/asset-management industry. CRISIL's slide cites a CIB revenue pool running to roughly US$600–800 bn on its chart [V Q3 PPT, chart only; scale ambiguous — FLAG].
- **Two buying motives:**
  - **(a) Cost/scale arbitrage:** offshore analysts for research, credit underwriting support and model validation.
  - **(b) Proprietary data / benchmarks** the bank cannot build itself: Coalition Index league tables, Greenwich client surveys, PriceMetrix advisor data.
- **Delivery models:**
  - A **captive GCC** (bank-owned India centre).
  - A **third-party KPO** (CRISIL Integral IQ, Acuity, Evalueserve).
  - A **hybrid**: build-operate-transfer, or a GCC topping up specialist work. CRISIL: "GCCs do need support whenever they set up their COE models or where there are regulatory remediations … GCCs have offered opportunities" [V call].
- **Pricing:** FTE/T&M, fixed-price projects and subscriptions (benchmarks, data). Revenue is billed in USD/GBP/EUR. CY25 revenue by geography: **North America 39.1%, Europe 20.4%, Rest of world 9.7%, India 30.9%** [V AR25 segment note]. Costs are ~88% India-based (88% of 5,081 permanent staff are in India) [V].

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

**Ratings demand = f(new instruments × fee per instrument) + surveillance on the stock.**

1. **Bank credit to rated-size borrowers.**
   - The dominant driver of *rated-entity count* and of BLR revenue. Bank credit growth: 15.9–16.1% in FY26; 18.6–19.1% by mid-2026 [V].
   - CARE: "The bank loan rating market was buoyant with 16.1% growth in bank credit to the segment that we look at as rating agencies. Last year … about 11.5%" [V].
   - ICRA expects bank credit "to drive bulk of our growth" in FY27 [V].
2. **Corporate bond and CP issuance.**
   - Higher fee yield per ₹ and per instrument than BLRs [V CARE]. Issuance is sensitive to the G-sec curve, bank pricing and regulation.
   - Examples: RBI's Nov-2023 hike in risk weights on bank loans to NBFCs pushed NBFCs to bonds in FY25; its **rollback in April 2025** pulled them back to banks in FY26 [V CareEdge].
3. **NBFC/HFC/MFI funding needs.** BFSI is ~2/3 of bond issuance and the core of CP and securitisation. NBFC securitisation grew 25% in FY26 [V ICRA].
4. **Infrastructure and InvIT/REIT debt; municipal bonds.** EL ratings were extended to municipal bonds [V SEBI]. Infra capex is "projected to double by fiscal 2030" (CRISIL) [V Q3 PPT].
5. **Regulatory use-cases.** RBI capital rules (new SA from Apr-2027), insurer/pension investment limits, MF portfolio limits, SEBI's large-corporate framework [K], and now ratings for instruments under other regulators [V].
6. **Surveillance stock.**
   - Every rating earns an annual fee until maturity/withdrawal.
   - Outstanding listed-security ratings: 1,942 at Mar-26 (SEBI) [V].
   - Crisil Ratings: 7,200+ companies with active ratings [V]. Across all CRAs the count of rated entities including BLRs runs to tens of thousands [K; FLAG — no consolidated SEBI figure fetched].
7. **Credit quality.** Benign credit (GNPA ~2%) means fewer defaults and more upgrades, a reputational tailwind. Stress means INC ratings, withdrawals and fee write-offs.

**Research/analytics demand:**
1. **Global bank revenue pools and cost-cutting.** "Despite record CIB revenues, margin pressures are driving greater scrutiny of discretionary spending … vendor consolidation and a shift towards outcome-led partnerships" [V CRISIL Q3 PPT]. "Banks are redirecting priorities toward credit quality, payments, and automation, while AI investments continue to crowd out other tech spends" [V].
2. **Regulatory intensity.** Basel III endgame / FRTB, PRA consultations (e.g. UK PRA CP9/26), IFRS 9/CECL and model-risk management → demand for model validation and risk analytics [V CRISIL thought leadership list].
3. **Private credit and private markets.** New coverage areas: CRISIL Integral IQ wins in "Fundamental Research and Credit Lending Solutions"; Integral IQ hosted a Private Markets Forum in New York [V].
4. **Wealth management.** The PriceMetrix acquisition (US/Canada wealth-advisor benchmarking) [V-snip].
5. **S&P Global delegation.** "Growth in delegation of surveillance support to S&P Global Ratings and expanded analytical and operational support to S&P in new areas" [V, every quarter of CY26].
6. **FX.**
   - A weaker rupee lifts INR revenue on ~69% non-India revenue. CRISIL: "Sharp INR appreciation against the USD will have a negative impact on margins" [V].
   - Hedges: USD forwards of **US$69.45m** at ₹88.84 for Jan–Dec 2026, plus GBP £2.41m at ₹118.58 and EUR €5.86m at ₹103.51 [V AR25].
   - So **CY26 realised rates lag the spot rupee (~₹95)**. CY27 hedges should be struck far higher [E].

### 7.1 SEBI — the CRA licence

  - **Brickwork** (2022 cancellation → 2023 reinstatement with curbs) [V-snip].

### 7.2 RBI — ECAI recognition and bank capital (the big 2026 change) [V RBI Directions 2026 unless tagged]
- **Title and timing:** "Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Capital Charge for Credit Risk – Standardised Approach) Directions, 2026", **dated 27-Apr-2026, effective 1-Apr-2027**. It replaces the 7-Oct-2025 draft [V taxmann; V RBI].
- **Eligible domestic CRAs (para 24(2)):** Acuité, Brickwork, CARE, CRISIL Ratings, ICRA, India Ratings, Infomerics.
- **International (24(3)):** CareEdge Global IFSC (non-resident corporates only), Fitch, Moody's, S&P.
- **Base corporate risk weights:**
  - Long-term: AAA 20%; **AA 20% (was 30%)**; A 50%; **BBB 75% (was 100%)**; **BB 100% (was 150%)**; below BB 150%; unrated 100%.
  - Short-term: A1+/A1 20%; A2 50%; A3 100%; A4/D 150%; unrated 100%.
- **ODR adjustment (para 12(3), 27(2), Table 21):** the base weights are "adjusted for the average one-year ODR for each rating category published by the respective ECRAs".

| Grade | ODR range (CRA's average one-year ODR must be within) | If breached |
|---|---|---|
| AAA | ≤0.05% | move one bucket up (AAA → 30%) |
| AA | 0.05–0.10% | AA → next bucket |
| A | 0.10–0.20% | → next |
| BBB | 0.20–0.40% | → next |
| BB | 0.40–1.0% | → next |
| B & below | >1% | — |

- **Unrated threshold:** unrated corporate/NBFC exposures above **₹500 Cr** aggregate banking-system exposure carry **150%**. Unrated MSME above the threshold also 150% (otherwise 75% regulatory retail / 85%).
- **Rating validity:**
  - The rating must be public and in the CRA's monthly bulletin, and reviewed within the last 15 months.
  - The press release must name the bank and the facility; otherwise the exposure counts as unrated (para 25(6)).
  - Unsolicited ratings are excluded, except international ratings of sovereigns.
- **Multiple ratings:** with two ratings, the higher RW applies. With three or more, take the two most favourable and apply the higher RW. **No cherry-picking.**
- **INC ratings:** 100% floor for six months, then 150% until withdrawn.
- **Economic impact (on the draft):** Crisil Ratings estimated capital relief of **~₹71,000 Cr on a base of ~₹4.35 lakh Cr**, if all CRAs qualify. A qualifying AA borrower could get up to **~50 bp/yr** lower pricing [V Crisil Ratings Jan-2026].
- **Industry implications [E]:**
  1. **Rating quality becomes a commercial differentiator.** A bank lending to an AA borrower rated by a CRA whose AA ODR breaches 0.10% loses the 20% weight. Banks will steer borrowers to "qualifying" CRAs. That favours CRISIL, ICRA and CARE, and hurts long-tail SME raters.
  2. **The ₹500 Cr unrated threshold and the INC penalties increase demand for (and stickiness of) BLRs.**
  3. CARE already claims its "observed default rates are well within the RBI prescribed benchmark" [V call]. Expect every CRA to market its ODRs. **FLAG:** we do not have each CRA's published one-year ODR by grade. The writer should not rank CRAs on ODR without fetching them.
- **History:** RBI's original ECAI accreditation came with Basel II (2007–08), when BLRs were born. CRISIL "was the first to rate bank loans in India" [V AR25]. The previous list circular was RBI/2024-25/50 [V-snip].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **Founding era (1987–1995).**
   - CRISIL was founded in 1987 by ICICI and UTI with other institutions [K; V AR "Founded in 1987 as India's first credit rating agency"]. ICRA followed in 1991 and CARE in 1993 [V screener/CARE].
   - Ratings were born with financial liberalisation, commercial paper (CP ratings were made mandatory by RBI in 1990 [K]) and the fixed-deposit market.
   - CRISIL listed in 1993 [primer; K: IPO 1992–93].
2. **Globalisation (1996–2014).**
   - Duff & Phelps → Fitch India (1996) → India Ratings (2013) [K].
   - **S&P took majority control of CRISIL in 2005** [K]. CRISIL bought Irevna (equity research outsourcing; subsidiaries dated 19-Oct-2004 in AOC-1) [V AOC-1; K].
   - Moody's moved up from a minority to ~50%+ of ICRA via its **2014** open offer [K].
   - Basel II (2007–08) created the BLR market. SMERA (2005) and Brickwork (2007) were born for SMEs [K].
   - CRISIL bought **Coalition (London) on 3-Jul-2012** [V AOC-1 date].
3. **Peak economics and the IL&FS shock (2015–2021).**
   - CARE's operating margins exceeded 60% in FY15–18 [V screener].
   - **IL&FS defaulted in Sep-2018 while carrying AAA/AA ratings.** Then came DHFL (2019), Reliance Capital/ADAG, Yes Bank AT1s and Altico [K].
   - SEBI penalised the raters, and CEOs at ICRA and CARE left [K].
   - CARE's revenue fell from ₹333 Cr (FY18) to ₹244 Cr (FY20) and its OPM from 63% to 33% [V screener]. ICRA's revenue stalled at ~₹300–330 Cr (FY15–21) [V].
   - CRISIL was largely untouched in ratings and kept growing via research. Revenue went from ₹1,748 Cr (CY18) to ₹1,982 Cr (CY20) [V]. It bought **Greenwich Associates (26-Feb-2020)** [V AOC-1].
4. **Recovery and re-rating (2021–2025).**
   - Post-COVID credit growth, NBFC bond issuance (aided by RBI's Nov-2023 risk-weight hike on bank loans to NBFCs, rolled back Apr-2025) and record bond issuance in FY25.
   - CARE: revenue +21% in FY25, OPM back to 39–42%. ICRA: +12–20%/yr. CRISIL: CY22 +20% revenue; CY24 flat (global bank budget cuts); CY25 +12% [V].
   - Rebrands: CRISIL → Crisil Ratings / Intelligence / Coalition Greenwich / Integral IQ (2024–25) [primer; V press release names]; CARE → CareEdge [V]; ICRA new identity (Jul-2026) [V].
5. **2026: the credit cycle turns — bonds down, bank credit up, rates up.**
   - The West Asia war (28-Feb-2026) pushed the 10-year G-sec to ~7% [V CRISIL]. The repo was hiked to 5.50% in Oct-2026.
   - Bond issuance fell (9M CY26 −6%; Q1 FY27 −29%), but bank credit hit 19%.
   - Raters still grew 15–29% on BLR volume and surveillance [V].
   - RBI finalised the ODR-linked risk-weight framework (Apr-2026, effective Apr-2027) [V]. SEBI opened non-SEBI instrument ratings (Jan-2026) [V].
   - ICRA bought Fintellix (consolidated from Oct-2025) and moved D2K and Fintellix to 100% [V]. CRISIL closed PriceMetrix (Nov-2025) [V]. CARE is selling 19.8% of its GIFT City arm to SBI and NSE IFSC (Oct-2026) [V].

**Cycle mechanics in one line:** ratings revenue = (new-mandate fees, which follow issuance and credit growth with ~0–2 quarters lag) + (surveillance on the stock, which follows *last* year's mandates). Reputation shocks (IL&FS) hurt *margins and pricing* for 3–4 years, more than volumes.

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert leads with)

1. **Bank credit vs bond market: the mix decides volumes AND fee yield.**
   - Indian ratings demand is driven by two credit channels with different fee yields. Bonds pay better per mandate; BLRs give count and stickiness.
   - In 2026 bank credit (19%) has displaced bonds (9M −6%, Q1 FY27 −29%). Raters still grew ~15–29% on BLRs and surveillance.
   - Rate hikes (5.50%) keep the bank channel favoured. The bond channel recovers when G-sec yields fall or bank liquidity tightens.
2. **The surveillance annuity and "investor preference" create a two-tier oligopoly.**
   - Surveillance revenue follows prior-year mandates, so revenue lags issuance by about a year. This smooths the cycle.
   - In listed bonds, investors effectively choose the CRA (CRISIL 46.8% of fresh ratings). In SME BLRs, issuers shop on price.
   - The top tier earns 45–60% margins. The tail fights over SME fees, and the default and penalty records cluster there (Acuité, Infomerics, Brickwork) [V SEBI defaults].
3. **Regulation now prices rating quality (RBI SA 2027) and widens the licence (SEBI 2026).**
   - RBI ties risk weights to each CRA's own observed default rates (AAA ≤0.05%, AA ≤0.10% …). The ₹500 Cr unrated threshold and the INC penalties make BLRs stickier.
   - SEBI's Jan-2026 amendment lets CRAs rate non-SEBI instruments.
   - Net: **higher volume, more concentration toward low-ODR incumbents, rising compliance cost for the tail** [E].
4. **For CRISIL specifically: an offshore analytics engine tied to S&P and global bank budgets.**
   - ~70% of revenue is RAS. ~69% of revenue is earned outside India. ~13% comes from S&P entities.
   - Its margin and growth depend on global CIB budgets, GenAI deflation, GCC competition, the rupee and hedges, and the parent's delegation.
   - The Q3 CY26 RAS margin of 17.2% is the number the market will watch.
5. **Reputation is the asset, and defaults are the existential risk.**
   - IL&FS (2018) cut CARE's margin from ~63% to ~32% for three years and cost two CEOs their jobs.
   - SEBI's PD-benchmark and default-recognition regimes, and now RBI's ODR test, make reputational damage measurable and immediately costly.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Ratings (domestic CRA):**
> Ratings revenue = (Number of new mandates × average initial fee) + (Outstanding rated book × surveillance-fee rate) + structured/other fees
> Ratings EBIT = Revenue − analyst headcount × cost per analyst − overheads

**Research/analytics (CRISIL RAS archetype):**
> RAS revenue (₹) = Billable FTEs × utilisation × rate per FTE (US$/£) × realised FX (hedged) + subscription/benchmark revenue (clients × ACV) + project revenue
> RAS profit = Revenue − (FTE cost in ₹ × total FTEs incl. bench) − data/tech/travel − acquired-business costs

**Group (CRISIL):**
> PAT = Ratings segment profit + RAS segment profit + unallocable other income − unallocable D&A − tax (~25–26%)

### C.1 Worked example 1 — Crisil Ratings Ltd CY25 [E reconstruction anchored on V]
- Rated companies: ~7,200 [V]. Average annual fee per rated company: ~₹9.3 lakh → revenue ₹670.6 Cr [V].
- Assume ~65% of revenue is surveillance (₹436 Cr) and ~35% initial/new (₹235 Cr) [E]. 1,280+ new BLRs plus bond mandates.
- Cost: assume ~1,000–1,200 analysts and support staff [E; FLAG — not disclosed] at ~₹20–25 lakh fully loaded ≈ ₹250–280 Cr [E].
- Other income: treasury on net assets of ₹226 Cr plus investments ₹250 Cr [V AOC-1: investments ₹250.6 Cr].
- **PBT ₹398.8 Cr (59.5% of turnover); PAT ₹297.4 Cr** [V].

### C.2 Worked example 2 — a ₹1,000 Cr AA bank loan under RBI's new SA (from Apr-2027) [E using V tables]

| Scenario | Risk weight | RWA | Capital @12% CRAR | Capital cost @15% ROE (pre-tax ~20%) | Rate impact on the loan |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rated AA by a CRA within the AA ODR range | 20% | ₹200 Cr | ₹24 Cr | ~₹4.8 Cr/yr | ~48 bp |
| Rated AA, but the CRA's AA ODR breaches 0.10% | 50% (next bucket) [V para 27(2) — "move up one bucket"; the next long-term bucket above AA's 20% is A's 50%] | ₹500 Cr | ₹60 Cr | ~₹12 Cr/yr | ~120 bp |
| Old regime (pre-2027) AA | 30% | ₹300 Cr | ₹36 Cr | ~₹7.2 Cr/yr | ~72 bp |
| Unrated (>₹500 Cr system exposure) | 150% | ₹1,500 Cr | ₹180 Cr | ~₹36 Cr/yr | ~360 bp |

- **Read:** a ₹10–15 lakh rating fee buys up to ~70 bp/yr of pricing benefit on a ₹1,000 Cr loan (₹7 Cr/yr). **The ROI on a rating is enormous, which is why the BLR is a near-mandatory purchase.**
- Crisil's own estimate: up to ~50 bp/yr for AA borrowers [V].
