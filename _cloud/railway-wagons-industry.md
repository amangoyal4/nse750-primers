  - **BOBRN** = bottom-discharge hopper.
  - **BVCM** = brake van.
  - **BCFC** = covered hopper for fly-ash/cement.

## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Titagarh's FY26 revenue is ₹3,186 Cr, not ₹3,868 Cr.**
   - Screener consolidated: FY24 ₹3,853 Cr, **FY25 ₹3,868 Cr, FY26 ₹3,186 Cr** [V screener].
   - The four FY26 quarters sum to ₹3,185 Cr (679 + 799 + 832 + 875) [V screener].
   - Company segment sums [V Arihant, ₹ mn → Cr]: freight ₹2,615 Cr + passenger ₹539 Cr + shipbuilding/bridges ₹32 Cr = ₹3,186 Cr.
   - **Our primer's headline "~₹3,868 Cr revenue (FY26)" is the FY25 number.** The primer's segment numbers (freight ~₹2,604 Cr, passenger ~₹539 Cr) are correct FY26 figures. They simply do not add up to its headline.
   - FY26 revenue therefore fell **−17.6%**, not "slipped".
2. **Titagarh FY26 PAT: reported annual ₹123 Cr vs a quarterly sum of ~₹166 Cr.**
   - Screener annual FY26 PAT is **₹123 Cr** (Upstox ₹122.8 Cr).
   - The four screener quarters sum to **₹167 Cr** (31 + 37 + 45 + 54). Company quarterlies per Arihant sum to ₹165.5 Cr (315 + 370 + 454 + 516 mn).
   - The ~₹43 Cr gap sits in annual "other income" (−₹15 Cr annual vs +₹39 Cr in the quarters) and PBT (₹194 Cr annual vs ₹237 Cr quarterly).
   - Most likely explanation: a year-end charge related to the Firema exit or discontinued operations booked only in the audited annual numbers [E; FLAG]. Anand Rathi shows ₹140 Cr — a third number [V-snip].
   - **Use ₹123 Cr (reported, audited) in tables.** Say that the run-rate is higher.
3. **Texmaco's ₹700 Cr provision did NOT go through the P&L.**
   - Management (Q4 FY26 call, 13-May-26) said the company "created a provision for contingencies of INR 700 crores from its free reserves" [V transcript]. They described it as a worst-case cushion against "geopolitical tension and trade uncertainties… energy supply, shipping, transportation, supply chain" and against large contracts that miss their expected outcomes. It has **no cash impact**.
   - "The statutory auditors have thus qualified their report… only on account of provision being adjusted against the free reserves instead of taking it from the profit and loss account" [V].
   - Moneylife reports it was funded as **₹494 Cr from general reserve + ₹206 Cr from retained earnings**. That exceeds the aggregate consolidated profit of the four preceding years (₹586 Cr) [V-snip].
   - Screener reserves fell from **₹2,757 Cr (Mar-25) to ₹2,333 Cr (Mar-26)** despite ₹194 Cr of PAT [V]. That is consistent with the −₹700 Cr, offset by the preferential issue (equity capital ₹40 → ₹41 Cr) and other items [E].
   - **Consequences:**
     - (a) FY26 reported PAT (₹194 Cr) is **not depressed** by the provision.
     - (b) Book value and ROE are distorted: equity is ₹700 Cr lower, so ROE is flattered going forward.
     - (c) Any future write-back of "savings out of these contingency provisions" (management's words) could flow to P&L or reserves. Watch the accounting.
     - (d) Our primer implies the provision is "a material charge" that flows through "reported versus underlying profit". That is wrong. Fix it.
4. **"EBITDA" is on three different bases across the three companies.**
   - **Texmaco's company EBITDA includes other income.**
     - FY26 company EBITDA ₹450 Cr = screener OP ₹391 Cr + other income ₹56 Cr ≈ ₹447 Cr.
     - Q1 FY27 standalone EBITDA ₹81 Cr ≈ OP ₹57 Cr + OI ₹23 Cr (screener consolidated) [V].
   - **Titagarh's and Jupiter's company EBITDA exclude other income** and match screener OP.
     - Titagarh Q1 FY27: company ₹94.9 Cr vs screener ₹94 Cr.
     - Jupiter Q1 FY27: ₹65 Cr vs ₹65 Cr [V].
   - **Use screener OP for every cross-company margin.** Texmaco's "10.3%/10.8% EBITDA margin" is ~8.9–9.0% on the like-for-like basis (FY26 OP 391/4,377 = 8.9%).
5. **Order book: Titagarh's "₹26,635 Cr" is half JV.**
   - At 30-Jun-26 [V call; V Arihant]:
     - Standalone ₹13,335 Cr = passenger ₹10,395 Cr + freight ₹2,470 Cr (5,300 wagons) + ships ~₹470 Cr.
     - **Plus JV orders ₹13,300 Cr**: the Titagarh share of the BHEL Vande Bharat consortium ₹7,000 Cr, and the 49%-owned Ramkrishna–Titagarh wheel JV's 20-year Indian Railways supply contract ₹6,300 Cr.
   - **The wheel-JV book will never be Titagarh revenue.** It is equity-accounted (share of JV profit).
   - The Vande Bharat **supply** is proportionate (consortium) and goes into revenue. The 35-year maintenance (AMC) sits in a JV and is equity-accounted [V call].
   - Book-to-bill on standalone = **4.2× FY26 revenue**; on the headline total = 8.4×.
   - **Primer issue:** the primer's "₹27,540 Cr (FY26 exit)" headline mixes these without saying so.
6. **"Wagons produced" (PIB) is not "wagons acquired by Indian Railways" (Budget).**
   - PIB (4-Apr-2025): national wagon **production** of **22,790 (FY23), 37,650 (FY24), 41,929 (FY25, record)** [V-sec, India Shipping News reproducing PIB]. This includes Indian Railways orders, private/GPWIS/LSFTO wagons, PSU and workshop output, and some exports.
   - The Union Budget **physical target "Wagons (vehicle units)"** counts Indian Railways' own budget-funded additions:
     - **FY25 target 38,000, achieved 29,889 (79%)**.
     - **FY26 BE 38,000 → RE 26,000 (68%)**.
     - **FY27 BE 32,000** [V PRS DfG 2026-27, Table 7, "corrected on February 20, 2026"].
   - The ~12,000 gap in FY25 (41,929 vs 29,889) is broadly the private, export and non-capex pool [E].
   - **Never compare a company's deliveries with the wrong denominator.**
7. **Indian Railways "free-supply" wheelsets: IR prices are not comparable to private prices.**
   - For wagons ordered by Indian Railways, **wheelsets are mandated to come from IR's own units** (Rail Wheel Factory, Bengaluru; Rail Wheel Plant, Bela). Texmaco: "we are dependent on the supply of Rail Wheel Factory. And that's what is the mandate of the government" [V Texmaco Q1 call]. They are typically supplied as "free-supply items" [K].
   - **The 2022 IR award prices therefore exclude most of the wheel cost.**
     - Titagarh: 24,177 wagons for ₹7,838 Cr ≈ ₹32.4 lakh/wagon.
     - Texmaco: 20,067 wagons for ₹6,450 Cr ≈ ₹32.1 lakh/wagon [V-snip; E].
   - **Private and export wagons include wheels and fetch more.**
     - Jupiter's average realisation was **₹41.5 lakh** in Q1 FY27 vs ₹38 lakh a year earlier [V call].
     - Titagarh's freight backlog is ₹2,470 Cr ÷ 5,300 wagons ≈ **₹46.6 lakh** [E].
   - The reported mega-tender (₹40,000 Cr for 1 lakh wagons) ≈ ₹40 lakh/wagon [E]. Whether wheels are free-supply in it is unknown [FLAG].
   - **Revenue-per-wagon comparisons must state the customer mix.**
8. **The shift to private and export orders is huge and recent, and it changes the economics.**
   - Texmaco: private + export share of the freight-car order book was **21% (FY25) → 79% (FY26) → 96.4% (Q1 FY27)** [V Texmaco Q1 call].
   - Jupiter: "close to 80% of our [wagon] order book is all private wagons" [V Jupiter call].
   - Titagarh: only 5,300 wagons left, mostly IR; it is "aggressively… booking private orders" [V].
   - Private orders mean more designs (prototype approvals cost Jupiter a quarter of volume in Q1 FY27), more customers, more realisation per wagon, and possibly less PVC protection [E].
   - **The 2022–25 boom was an IR-tender boom. FY26–27 is a private-order bridge.**
9. **Jupiter is not net cash at Mar-26.**
   - The primer says "net cash… net-debt-to-equity around −0.08".
   - **Screener Mar-26 [V]:** borrowings **₹996 Cr** (vs ₹502 Cr Mar-25); cash **₹442 Cr**; investments ₹236 Cr.
   - Net debt is **₹318 Cr** (₹554 Cr if investments are excluded) [E].
   - FY26 CFO was only ₹9 Cr, capex ~₹533 Cr, and FCF **−₹524 Cr** [V screener].
   - The −0.08 figure is probably an FY25 "average" ratio after the July-2024 ₹800 Cr QIP.
   - **The Odisha wheel plant (₹2,600 Cr) is debt-plus-partner funded from here.**
10. **"Capacity" claims are elastic, so quote them with the speaker.**
    - Titagarh: 1,000 wagons/month (12,000/yr), currently run at 600–650/month; 850+ passenger cars/yr by FY29–30 [V call].
    - Jupiter: wagon capacity "lifted from 6,000 toward 8,000–10,000" [primer]. Q1 FY27 actual was 1,141 wagons (~4,600/yr annualised) [V call].
    - Texmaco: "capacity is elastic, we can make 15,000, 16,000 wagons" of commodity types [V call]. FY26 actual was **8,372** [V Q4 call].
    - Industry: "theoretical capacity of 60,000 units per year but producing 35,000–40,000" [V The Core, Feb-26].
    - The binding constraint has been **wheelsets, not sheds**.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Three listed companies, one buyer, one bottleneck.**
  - Indian Railways (IR) buys most freight wagons through periodic mega-tenders. It also mandates the wheelsets on its own orders and supplies them.
  - The 2022 tender cycle (~90,000 wagons tendered for ~₹31,000 Cr; ~60,000 placed in June 2022 for ~₹23,500 Cr) drove national wagon production from **~12,500/yr (FY21–22) to 41,929 in FY25** [V-sec PIB; V-snip].
  - FY26 then broke:
    - IR's own wagon additions were cut to **RE 26,000 vs BE 38,000** [V PRS].
    - Procurement from private builders ran **~40% below target in Apr–Jul 2025** because of wheelset shortages [V-snip Business Standard].
    - All three members' revenue fell.
- **FY26 (screener consolidated, sum of three) [E: sums of V screener]:**
  - Revenue **₹10,479 Cr (−19.0% YoY from ₹12,938 Cr)**.
  - OP ₹1,076 Cr (10.3%, vs 10.6%).
  - PAT **₹483 Cr (−32.5% from ₹716 Cr)**.
  - The peak was FY25 for revenue and FY24 for margin (10.9%) and ROCE: Titagarh 25%, Jupiter 31%, Texmaco 11% (FY25 14%).
- **Q1 FY27 shows a bottom on low volume:**
  - Revenue ₹2,193 Cr (+7.0% YoY), OP ₹216 Cr (9.8%), PAT ₹129 Cr (+42% YoY on a weak Q1 FY26) [E: sums].
  - Sequentially it was down 22% on revenue vs Q4 FY26.
  - Wagon output in Q1 FY27: Titagarh 1,284, Jupiter 1,141, Texmaco 1,054 = **~3,480 wagons in the quarter**, ~13,900/yr annualised [V calls]. That is ~37–40% of the three companies' combined stated capacity (~35–38k/yr) [E].
- **The next IR mega-tender is the master variable, and it keeps slipping.**
  - Mint/Angel One (25-May-26): IR is "preparing a tender of ₹40,000 crore for 1,00,000 freight wagons" over 3–4 years (35–40k/yr) [V].
  - Arihant (May-26) expected it "within two quarters" [V].
  - Titagarh (14-Aug): "deferred by a few months or a couple of quarters, but not dropped… priority overall have shifted because of the Middle East war" [V].
  - Texmaco (May-26): near-term need of "no less than 1.5 lakh to 2 lakh wagons" [V].
  - **FY27 Budget wagon target: only 32,000 IR units** [V PRS].
- **The companies have diverged in strategy, and the market prices the divergence.**
  - **Titagarh** has become a passenger-rail company.
    - ~80% of its standalone backlog is metro and Vande Bharat.
    - Passenger revenue was 30% of Q1 FY27 revenue (₹230 Cr; +197% YoY) at a **14.7% segment EBIT margin** vs freight 12.2% [V Arihant].
    - Cars delivered: 12 (FY25) → 63 (FY26) → 30+ in Q1 FY27 alone [V].
  - **Jupiter** is a vertically integrated wagon-and-components play. Its add-ons are the forged-wheel plant (₹2,600 Cr, 1 lakh wheelsets/yr, 50% export), brakes (Stone India; JWL-DAKO), BESS (~₹500 Cr book) and e-LCVs [V call].
  - **Texmaco** is the biggest pure wagon builder (8,372 wagons FY26) plus rail EPC.
    - Electrification (Bright Power) revenue was ₹610 Cr (+66%) at a 10.8% EBIT margin [V].
    - Its order book jumped to **₹9,923 Cr** on a ~₹4,100 Cr **South Africa** wagons + 15-year maintenance order [V].
- **Valuations (screener, 23-Oct-26 closes) [V screener; E for EV]:**
  - **Titagarh:** m-cap ₹8,934 Cr, **P/E 46.0× TTM**, P/B 3.6×, EV/FY26 OP ~27×.
  - **Jupiter:** m-cap ₹7,410 Cr, **P/E 41.6×**, P/B 2.5×, EV/OP ~22×. The stock (₹173) sits at its **52-week low** (₹171); 1-year return −46%.
  - **Texmaco:** m-cap ₹4,008 Cr, **P/E 18.5×**, P/B 1.7× (on reserves already cut by ₹700 Cr), EV/OP ~11×.
  - **Group:** m-cap **₹20,352 Cr**; aggregate P/E **42× FY26 PAT / 36× TTM** [E].
  - All three have de-rated since the primers were written in Jul–Aug-26:
    - Titagarh ₹11,560 Cr → ₹8,934 Cr (−23%).
    - Jupiter ₹11,100 Cr → ₹7,410 Cr (−33%).
    - Texmaco ₹4,450 Cr → ₹4,008 Cr (−10%).
- **Wheels are being localised at scale for the first time.**
  - Ramkrishna Forgings–Titagarh (Chennai; 51:49) **2.20–2.28 lakh forged wheels/yr**, with a 20-year IR contract for 80,000/yr; sample production began Aug-26 [V].

### 1.2 The value chain (inputs → customer)

1. **Steel plate and sections** (SAIL, JSW, AM/NS; stainless for BOXNS/BCNHL from Jindal Stainless) [K] are the largest bought-in input, at ~45–55% of a wagon's ex-works cost with wheels excluded [E].
2. **Castings** (bogie side-frames, bolsters, couplers, knuckles) come from in-house foundries (Texmaco, Jupiter, Titagarh) or third parties.
   - Foundry tonnage is a key internal KPI: Texmaco **34,000 t in FY26; 5,148 t in Q1 FY27** [V].
3. **Wheelsets** come from IR units for IR orders, or from imports/JVs for private and export orders. Forged wheels are needed for higher speeds; cast wheels are limited to **~100 km/h** (Jupiter MD) [V].
4. **Brakes, bearings, springs, couplers** come from Wabtec, Knorr, Stone/DAKO, Timken/SKF/NBC (bearings), Amsted [K].
5. **Fabrication and assembly** happen in sheds sized in wagons/month. Prototype approval by **RDSO** (Research Designs & Standards Organisation, Lucknow) is needed for every new design. Each new private design costs months: Jupiter lost Q1 FY27 volume to it [V].
6. **Inspection and acceptance** (RITES/RDSO), then dispatch. **Revenue is recognised on delivery and acceptance.** A wagon missing wheelsets is pure inventory, which is why Jupiter's inventory days hit **188 in FY26** [V screener].
7. **Customer:** IR (zonal railways take delivery) or a private owner registered under a wagon scheme. Since Feb-2026 a wagon-maker can itself own and lease wagons (Titagarh registered as a Wagon Leasing Company) [V-snip; V Arihant].
8. **After-market:** maintenance contracts. Texmaco has 15-year maintenance in South Africa (~30–35% of that order's value) [V]. Titagarh–BHEL has 35-year Vande Bharat AMC (JV-accounted) [V].

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **Commodity IR wagons** earn the thinnest margin: ~**10–12% EBITDA / 11–12% EBIT** at Titagarh freight, "±0.5–1% depending on public vs private mix, price variation clause, commodity price movements" [V Arihant Q4 note].
  - Per wagon: **EBIT ~₹4.5–5 lakh** on ~₹39 lakh revenue (Titagarh Q1 FY27: ₹61.7 Cr EBIT ÷ 1,284 wagons = ₹4.8 lakh) [E from V].
- **Components and wheels** carry higher margins.
  - Wheel-plant targets: Jupiter **≥15% EBITDA** on ₹2,500–3,000 Cr revenue; Titagarh–RKFL "in line with a standard forging business" (RKFL itself runs ~20–22% EBITDA [K]) [V].
  - Brakes: Jupiter targets 15%+ from FY28 [V].
- **Passenger cars** earn more per unit of shed space: one ~₹10 Cr car ≈ 25 wagons of revenue.
  - Titagarh passenger EBIT was 11.1% → 19.0% across FY26 quarters (19% in Q4 due to "free-supply material" on Bangalore Metro), 14.3% for FY26 and **14.7% in Q1 FY27**.
  - Guided "~12% near term, 15% in 2–3 years" with propulsion backward integration [V Arihant].
- **Annuities** — Vande Bharat 35-year AMC, Texmaco's 15-year maintenance and leasing (Texmaco Touax; Titagarh's licence) — are the least cyclical slice. In each case it is small today [E].

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Freight loading targets.**
   - FY26 loading was **1,670 MT** (+3.25% vs 1,617 MT in FY25). Coal is ~50% and fell 0.51% [V-snip, multiple outlets].

3. **Dedicated Freight Corridors.**
   - **EDFC (1,337 km) fully commissioned.**

4. **Private wagon ownership.**
   - **640 rakes procured under wagon investment schemes** [V PRS].

     - Jupiter: ₹264 Cr from JSW Rail Logistics + Central Warehousing Corp; ₹211 Cr from JSW Port Logistics (7 BOSM rakes = 329 wagons) + Orissa Alloy Steel (150 wagons under LSFTO) [V].

5. **Passenger and urban transit.**
   - Metro city count to double; Vande Bharat sleeper (Titagarh–BHEL 80; Kinet 120; BEML/ICF first set flagged off 17-Jan-26) [V-snip].

## 7. Regulation and policy

1. **Procurement:** Railway Board centralised wagon tenders.
   - Historically the L1 price is matched by all qualified bidders, and quantity is allocated by capacity/past performance ("qualifying bidders would have to match the lowest rate") [V-snip maritimegateway, 2022].
   - RDSO approval is needed for designs and vendors [primer; K].
   - **GFR rule 144(xi)** (2020) restricts bidders from land-border countries (China) [K].
2. **Wheel mandate:** wheelsets for IR-ordered wagons are supplied by IR (RWF/RWP). Private orders may import [V Texmaco calls]. The policy on importing for IR orders is unclear and in flux [V-snip May-26].
3. **Private wagon schemes** [V PRS; K details]:
   - **GPWIS** (General Purpose Wagon Investment Scheme): customers own wagons and get a freight rebate (e.g. "10% on base freight for every booking" in an SWR–JSW agreement) [V-snip].
   - **LSFTO** (Liberalised Special Freight Train Operator): special-purpose wagons (e.g. Orissa Alloy Steel's 150 wagons, Jupiter) [V].
   - **AFTO** (Automobile Freight Train Operator) [V PRS].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert leads with)

1. **IR's wagon procurement calendar is the master variable.**
   - Production went from ~12.8k (FY22) to 41.9k (FY25), then to ~26–31k (FY26 [E]) and an IR BE of 32k (FY27).
   - Member revenue tracked it: +59%, +55%, +18%, −19% [V].
   - Everything else (margins, ROCE, stock prices) is downstream of this one buyer's tender timing. The next ₹40,000 Cr / 1 lakh tender is the swing factor for FY28–FY30.
2. **Wheelsets are the physical bottleneck, and their localisation is the cycle's structural change.**
   - IR's mandate that IR-ordered wagons use RWF/RWP wheels made FY26 a supply-constrained year despite full order books.
   - The ~3.3 lakh private forged-wheel capacity coming in FY27–FY28 (RKFL–Titagarh, JTRWF) would remove the constraint. It could also create a new profit pool (₹1 lakh/wheel; 15–20% margins) and, by FY29+, possibly an oversupply.
3. **Rail capex is high but plateauing, and wagons are a small slice of it.**
   - Capex was ₹2.69 lakh Cr (FY25) → ₹2.65 lakh Cr (FY26 RE, flat) → **₹2.93 lakh Cr (FY27 BE, +10.5%)** [V PRS].
   - Rolling stock is only ₹65,497 Cr (+3%). Wagons are ~₹10–13k Cr of that [E].
   - The operating ratio is ~98–99%, so capex is 95% GBS-funded. A fiscal squeeze shows up first in "deferrable" items like wagons [E].
4. **The private-order and passenger pivot is the companies' hedge, and it changes margin structure.**
   - Texmaco's book went from 21% to 96% private/export in five quarters. Jupiter's wagon book is ~80% private.
   - Titagarh's backlog is ~80% passenger at 14–15% EBIT.
   - This raises realisation and margins per unit but adds design/prototype risk and bespoke WC.
5. **Freight-share policy (NRP 45%, Mission 3000 MT, DFCs) is the long-run demand anchor.**
   - Loading grows only ~3–4%/yr (FY26 +3.25%, H1 FY27 +4.1%) and modal share is stuck at ~27–28%.
   - So the wagon "need" (1.5–2 lakh in management's view) depends on IR choosing to buy ahead of traffic.
   - Credibility of the policy matters more than its arithmetic.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Wagon maker:**
`Revenue = Σ (wagons delivered × realisation per wagon) + components/foundry sales + EPC/passenger revenue`
- Wagons delivered = min(order backlog release, shed capacity × utilisation, **wheelset availability**)
- Realisation = base bid price (IR: ex-wheels; private: with wheels) × (1 + PVC escalation)

`EBITDA = Revenue × contribution margin − fixed costs`. Contribution margin is roughly 18–22% on wagons [E].

`PAT = EBITDA − D&A − interest (incl. BG commission) − tax ± share of JV/associates`

**Passenger maker:** `Revenue = cars delivered × value per car × (consortium share)`. Titagarh's VB share is 51% of car value [V].
