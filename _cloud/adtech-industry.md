- "Adex" = advertising expenditure. "Digital" in industry reports may or may not include digital extensions of TV/print (CTV, e-paper), retail media and SME self-serve spend — the definitional trap in §READ THIS FIRST.
- CPCU = Cost Per Converted User (Affle's billing unit). "Average CPCU" = CPCU revenue ÷ conversions, in ₹ per conversion.
- "Inventory and data costs" = Affle's main cost line (ad-space bought from publishers/exchanges + third-party data). Gross margin here = 1 − inventory & data costs ÷ revenue (company basis; not a screener proxy).

## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **"India's ad market" has at least four sizes in 2025, and they are not contradictory — they define the pie differently.**
   - **FICCI-EY (Mar-2026):** total advertising grew **13.5%** in CY2025; **digital advertising ₹94,700 Cr (+26%)**, "nearly two-thirds" of ad revenue → total ad ≈ **₹1.43–1.45 lakh Cr** [V newsonair; E for the total].
   - **Pitch Madison Advertising Report 2026 (Feb-2026):** "expanded" definition **₹1,55,105 Cr (+12%)**, digital **₹93,156 Cr = 60%**; "legacy" definition **₹1,15,291 Cr (+7%)**, digital 46% [V-snip writtenlyhub]. The expanded number adds MSME self-serve digital (₹35,814 Cr) and e-commerce/retail media.
   - **dentsu-e4m Digital Advertising Report 2026:** digital **₹71,621 Cr = 59%** of the ad pie → total ≈ ₹1.21 lakh Cr [V-snip exchange4media; E].
   - **WPP Media TYNY (Feb/Jun-2026):** 2026E **₹2.0 lakh Cr** ($22.5 bn midyear; ₹2,01,891 Cr in Feb), digital **68.1%** "including digital extensions of TV, print, audio, OOH" [V wppmedia; V-snip]. WPP counts gross platform revenue (incl. SME/long-tail self-serve and commerce) — the widest definition.
   - **Rule for the writer:** quote one source per chart and name it; use FICCI-EY (₹94,700 Cr digital, CY2025) as the anchor for "digital ad market" and WPP for forecasts; never compute growth across two publishers.
2. **Digital overtook TV years ago; "digital share" ranges 46%–68% depending on definition.** On dentsu-e4m's series digital went from **12% (2016) to 59% (2025)** and is projected at **70% by 2027** [V-snip]. Under Pitch Madison's legacy definition it is only 46%. Linear TV ad revenue fell **5% to ₹32,855 Cr in 2025** (volumes −10%) [V-snip PMAR]; WPP sees total TV **−6.8% in 2026** [V wppmedia].
3. **Affle's "revenue" is gross of media cost, unlike an agency's.** Affle books the full CPCU bill as revenue and the ad-space/data it buys (~62–63% of revenue) as "inventory and data costs" [V]. Agencies (WPP/Publicis/Dentsu) report net revenue/fees; Google India reports a small "net advertising revenue" (₹2,694 Cr FY25) because it remits ~₹32,048 Cr to Google Asia Pacific [V-snip]. Compare **gross margin (~37–38%)** or **EBITDA/gross profit**, not revenue, across business models.
4. **"CPCU revenue" ≠ "revenue from operations".** FY26 CPCU revenue ₹2,687.3 Cr vs revenue ₹2,709.3 Cr (99.2% CPCU); H1 FY27 99.8% [V]. The primer's "~99.5%" is in between — fine as an approximation, but give the period.
5. **"Average CPCU" is a mix statistic, not a price.** It rises when the mix shifts to developed markets (DM pays more per conversion; "5x" more traffic per rupee is needed in EM, per the CEO) and falls with the rupee/EM currencies (Affle "passes some currency benefit to advertisers" to keep local-currency ROI constant) [V Q1 FY27 call]. The ₹56.2 (FY24) → ₹57.5 (FY25) → ₹58.9 (FY26) → ₹60.6 (Q2 FY27) drift is therefore partly FX and mix, not pricing power [V; E interpretation].
6. **Geographic split = "India & Emerging Markets" vs "Developed Markets", by billing entity — not "India vs abroad".** India alone is not disclosed in the presentation; EM includes SE Asia, MEA, LatAm. Q2 FY27: **India & EM 73.7% / DM 26.3%**; Q4 FY26: 71.6% / 28.4%; H1 FY27: 73.0% / 27.0% [V]. **The primer's "71.6% / 28.4% (FY26)" is actually the Q4 FY26 quarter split.** The metrics are "unaudited and derived for market-wise performance basis our billing entities" [V disclaimer].
7. **Perion will change every Affle ratio from ~Q4 FY27/Q1 FY28.** Perion (CY2025 revenue US$439.9 mn, net loss US$7.9 mn; 2023 revenue US$743.2 mn) is a search-advertising + display/CTV/retail-media business with a much lower gross margin profile and a history of a Microsoft Bing search-partnership shock (2024) [V-snip unite.ai; K for the Bing episode]. Post-close Affle will be **~60% DM by revenue, not ~27%** [E], CPCU share will fall below 99%, and gross margin will fall [E]. Affle guides a **FY2028 revenue run-rate >US$700 mn** (~₹6,650 Cr) and EPS accretion within the first year [V-snip].
8. **Cash: "reserves" are not cash.** The primer's "~₹15 Cr debt vs ~₹3,600 Cr reserves" compares debt with book equity. Company-reported **cash & liquid investments ≈ ₹1,792 Cr (Mar-26) and ≈ ₹2,117 Cr (Sep-26)**; goodwill & intangibles ≈ ₹1,518 Cr → ₹1,582 Cr; borrowings ₹11.9 Cr → ₹2.2 Cr [V Q4 FY26/Q2 FY27 presentations; bar-label mapping cross-checked against screener fixed assets of ₹1,394+131 Cr (Mar-26) and ₹1,589 Cr (Sep-26) — E]. Screener's "investments" (₹114–116 Cr) badly understates treasury because Affle holds liquid funds/deposits under other heads.
9. **The real-money-gaming (RMG) ad ban has two dates.** Parliament passed the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act in **Aug-2025** (assent 22-Aug-2025 [K]); RMG apps (Dream11 etc.) pulled advertising immediately [V-snip storyboard18]. But the Act and its Rules were **formally notified 22-Apr-2026 and came into force 1-May-2026** (the earlier "1-Oct-2025" date slipped) [V-snip Business Standard/mondaq]. For ad budgets the shock began in **Aug-2025 (Q2 FY26)**; Affle's Q1 FY27 still cited RMG as a YoY drag ("last year RMG was a contributor, this year it is not") [V].
10. **Privacy: three separate regimes, often conflated.** (a) **Apple ATT** (iOS 14.5, Apr-2021) — opt-in IDFA, in force [K]; (b) **Google Chrome third-party cookies** — deprecation **abandoned Apr-2025**, and most **Privacy Sandbox APIs retired 17-Oct-2025** (Topics, Protected Audience, Attribution Reporting incl. Android) [V-snip]; (c) **Android Advertising ID** — still available; the Android Privacy Sandbox ad APIs were part of the Oct-2025 retirement [V-snip; FLAG — confirm Android scope on Google's post]. Plus India's **DPDP Act 2023 + Rules (notified ~13/14-Nov-2025)**: consent-manager registration opens ~**14-Nov-2026 (i.e. tomorrow)**; the substantive consent/notice duties bite **~13/14-May-2027** [V-snip law firms]. The primer's privacy section predates (b).

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **India's ad market is a ~₹1.4–2.0 lakh Cr pie (CY2025–26) that has already gone digital; growth is now commerce, CTV and SME self-serve, not "digital vs TV".**
  - Digital ad revenue **₹94,700 Cr in 2025 (+26%)**, ~two-thirds of the total; M&E sector ₹2.78 lakh Cr (+~9%) [V FICCI-EY via newsonair].
  - E-commerce/retail media is the fastest pool: **₹10,257 Cr (+27%)** on PMAR's count, **₹17,601 Cr (+56%, 24.6% of digital)** on dentsu-e4m's, **₹22,000 Cr incl. point-of-sale (+50%)** on FICCI-EY's; quick-commerce ads alone **₹4,000 Cr (+202%)** → ₹6,000 Cr in 2026E [V-snip]. WPP sees commerce ads **+29% in 2026** to US$3.9 bn [V].
  - CTV ~**₹6,000 Cr in 2025 (~2x)**, ₹8,000 Cr 2026E [V-snip PMAR]; linear TV shrinking [V-snip].
- **The pie is owned by walled gardens.** Google India gross ad revenue **₹34,742 Cr (FY25, +11.3%)** and Meta India **~₹28,000–29,400 Cr (FY25)** = ~₹63,000 Cr ≈ **two-thirds of Indian digital adex** [V-snip RoC-based press; E share]. Add Amazon/Flipkart retail media and JioStar, and the "open" programmatic market that independent adtech (Affle, InMobi, Vertoz) competes in is ~₹30,000 Cr (dentsu-e4m programmatic, 42% of digital, +19%) — and much of that runs through Google's own DSP/exchange [V-snip; E].
- **Affle is a small, profitable, outcome-priced specialist inside that pie — and is about to become a global mid-cap adtech roll-up.**
  - FY26 revenue **₹2,709 Cr (+19.5%)**, EBITDA ₹610 Cr (22.5%), PAT ₹455 Cr; **456 mn conversions at ₹58.9** [V]. Q2 FY27 revenue ₹783.6 Cr (+21.2%), EBITDA margin 23.2% (record), PAT ₹129.8 Cr [V]. 15 consecutive quarters of sequential revenue growth [V-snip].
  - **Perion (US$420 mn equity, EV ~US$192 mn [E])** brings US$440 mn of CY25 revenue, US/Europe/Israel exposure, CTV/retail media/DOOH and ~600 DM staff [V-snip] — at **~0.44x EV/sales vs Affle's ~7.1x** [E]. This is the defining event for the report: Affle is buying scale in developed markets at a distressed-asset multiple.
- **Global adtech has split into an AI-winner (AppLovin), a stalling independent DSP (The Trade Desk), and turnaround mid-caps (Digital Turbine, Perion).**
  - AppLovin Q3 2026: revenue US$2.18 bn (+55%), adjusted EBITDA margin **84%**, net margin 66% [V-snip SEC 8-K/press]. Its AXON engine is the proof that model quality, not inventory, captures the economics in mobile performance ads.
  - The Trade Desk Q3 2026: revenue **US$736 mn, −0.5% YoY — first decline since its 2016 IPO** [V-snip] (Amazon DSP competition, agency principal-trading, CTV pricing).
  - Digital Turbine FY26 (Mar-26): revenue US$565 mn (+15%), adjusted EBITDA US$122.5 mn (21.7%) but a GAAP loss [V-snip].
- **Valuation:** Affle at **₹23,155 Cr m-cap, 46.6x TTM P/E, ~31x EV/TTM EBITDA [E]** — between AppLovin's premium and the low-single-digit EV/sales of Indian small-caps (Vertoz 11.2x P/E, Brightcom 1.8x P/E — a governance discount) [V screener].
- **Three Indian-specific shocks of 2025–26:** (1) the **RMG ad ban** (Aug-2025; Act in force 1-May-2026) removed a category that was spending an estimated ₹3,000–4,500 Cr/yr on digital (up to ₹7,000 Cr all media) [V-snip industry estimates]; (2) the **DPDP Rules** (Nov-2025) start a consent regime by May-2027; (3) the **equalisation levy on online ads (6%) was abolished from 1-Apr-2025** (and the 2% e-commerce levy from 1-Aug-2024) [K], removing a cost wedge that had favoured Indian-billed over offshore-billed ad spend.

## 1. What the sub-sector does: services, customers, value chain, where value is captured

### 1.1 The services (who sells what)

| Layer | What it does | Pricing | Examples (India-relevant) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Advertiser** | Brand, app, e-commerce or SME buying reach or outcomes | — | HUL, Amazon, Flipkart, Swiggy/Zomato, Dream11 (pre-ban), PhonePe, OTT apps |
| **Agency (media buying/planning)** | Plans and buys media for brands; increasingly "principal" trading of inventory | Fees/commission (~2–10% of billings) or principal margin | WPP Media (ex-GroupM), Publicis (Starcom, Zenith), Dentsu, Omnicom (merged with IPG Nov-2025 [K]), Madison, Havas [K] |
| **Walled gardens (O&O platforms)** | Sell their own audiences via self-serve auctions | CPM/CPC/CPA auction | Google (Search, YouTube), Meta (FB/IG/WhatsApp), Amazon Ads, Flipkart Ads, JioStar/JioHotstar, Blinkit/Zepto/Instamart |
| **DSP (demand-side platform)** | Buys open-web/app inventory programmatically for advertisers | % of spend (take rate ~15–20%) or CPM | The Trade Desk, Google DV360, Amazon DSP, Affle (Jampp, RevX, mediasmart), Vertoz |
| **SSP / exchange / ad network** | Sells publisher inventory to DSPs; mediation | % rev share | Google AdMob/AdX, AppLovin MAX, Unity/ironSource, InMobi Exchange, Digital Turbine (DT Exchange) |
| **Performance / outcome networks** | Take the conversion risk; bill per install/purchase/sign-up | CPI/CPA/CPCU | **Affle (CPCU)**, AppLovin (CPI/ROAS-based), Moloco, Liftoff, InMobi |
| **On-device / OEM** | Pre-installs, lock-screen and app-recommendation placements on handsets | Per install / rev share with OEM | Glance (InMobi; lock screen), Digital Turbine (Ignite), **Affle (Appnext, OEM/operator integrations)** |
| **Measurement / verification / fraud** | Attribution, viewability, invalid-traffic filtering | SaaS/CPM | AppsFlyer, Branch, Adjust, IAS, DoubleVerify; **Affle mFaaS** (in-house) |
| **Publisher** | Apps, sites, OTT, CTV, DOOH screens that own attention | Receive ~50–70% of media spend [K/E] | Gaming apps, news, OTT, OEM lock-screens |

### 1.2 Value chain — one rupee of programmatic performance spend
1. Advertiser (or its agency) sets a goal (install, first purchase) and a target cost.
2. A buy-side platform predicts which impression will convert and bids in real time (≈100 ms auctions) [K].
3. Exchange/SSP takes a fee and passes the impression from the publisher/app/OEM.
4. Attribution partner (AppsFlyer/Adjust/SKAdNetwork on iOS) confirms the conversion; fraud filters remove bots/click-spam.
5. **In CPCU, Affle buys the impressions on its own account (CPM) and sells the outcome (per conversion).** Its gross margin is the arbitrage between the two, earned only if its prediction beats the market [V primer; V company slide "CPCU model"].

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **Audience owners with first-party log-in data (Google, Meta, Amazon, Flipkart, JioStar)** capture most of the profit; they also control measurement and identifiers [K; E].
- **Among independents, the model owner wins:** AppLovin (84% EBITDA margin) vs TTD (~36% in Q3 2026) vs Digital Turbine (~22%) vs Affle (~23%) vs Vertoz (~17%) [V-snip/V].
- **Media agencies** keep shrinking fee pools; principal-based trading and retail-media partnerships are their response [K].
- Affle's slide frames its stack as "Direct to Advertisers ↔ ConvergeAI ↔ Direct to Supply-Side Platforms, OEMs & Operators, Walled Gardens" with mFaaS fraud prevention, vernacular intelligence, CTV & DOOH, agentic AI [V Q2 FY27 presentation, slide 17] — i.e., it sits on both buy and supply sides (OEM/operator inventory) to keep more of the margin.

## 6. Business-model archetypes and their economics

| Archetype | Revenue recognition | Gross margin | EBITDA margin | Capital | Who |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Outcome network (CPCU/CPA)** — takes conversion risk, buys media on own account | Gross (full billed amount) | ~37–40% (Affle) | ~20–23% (Affle) | Light; goodwill-heavy if acquisitive | **Affle**, Moloco/Liftoff (private), InMobi (partly) |
| **2. AI ad platform with captive supply** — owns mediation (MAX) + bidder (AXON) | Mostly net of publisher payouts | ~75–80% [K] | **84%** (Q3 2026) [V-snip] | Light | AppLovin |
| **3. Independent DSP (self-serve software)** — % of client spend | Net (take rate) | ~80% [K] | ~36–40% [V-snip] | Light; stock-comp heavy | The Trade Desk |
| **4. On-device / OEM preload + exchange** | Mixed gross/net | ~45–50% [K] | ~22% (DT FY26: US$122.5 mn on US$565 mn) [V-snip] | Revenue-share prepayments to OEMs/carriers | Digital Turbine, Glance, Affle (Appnext) |
| **5. Search/display intermediary** — sells search monetisation + display | Gross | ~35–45% [K] | low/negative recently | Light | Perion (CY2025 net loss US$7.9 mn) [V-snip], Brightcom (reported) |
| **6. Media agency** | Net fees / principal margin | n/a | ~15–20% of revenue [K] | Working-capital float | WPP Media, Publicis, Dentsu, Madison |
| **7. O&O walled garden** | Gross (ad sales) | ~55–80% | ~35–50% globally [K] | Massive capex (AI/data centres) | Google, Meta, Amazon |

**Economics in one line each:**
- Outcome networks win if prediction accuracy beats the auction; they lose when signals degrade (ATT) or when a better model (AXON) bids up the same inventory.
- AppLovin shows the end-state: once you own both supply (mediation) and the best model, margin goes from ~30% to ~80% (2022 → 2026) [K/V-snip].
- Affle sits between archetype 1 and 4 (OEM/operator supply via Appnext + AdColony SDK) and is now buying archetype 5 (Perion: Perion One platform, Outmax AI agent, retail media, CTV, DOOH) [V-snip].

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **AI model arms race (AXON effect).** AppLovin's gains came at the expense of other networks bidding for the same gaming inventory [K]. If AXON-class models move into e-commerce/web (AppLovin's 2025–26 e-commerce push [K]), Affle's EM moat narrows. Outcome-priced, first-party-integrated models are relatively resilient (100% of Affle's business has direct advertiser technology integration [V]).
3. **Walled-garden encroachment.** Google's Performance Max/App campaigns, Meta Advantage+, Amazon DSP and quick-commerce retail media capture conversion budgets with closed-loop measurement [K].
4. **Generative/agentic AI changes the ad surface.** AI assistants and AI-search answers may reduce app installs and search-ad clicks; Perion's search-monetisation business is directly exposed (Microsoft Bing partnership shock in 2024 [K]) [E].

7. **On-device OEM inventory** (Glance lock-screen, Digital Turbine Ignite, Affle/Appnext) faces regulatory scrutiny over pre-installs and user consent [K].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **India's ad market has gone digital, and digital has gone to the walled gardens and commerce platforms.** Digital is ~60–68% of adex (₹94,700 Cr in 2025, +26%) and Google + Meta take ~two-thirds of it; retail/quick-commerce media is the fastest pool (+27% to +202% depending on definition) [V/V-snip]. Independent adtech therefore grows by finding inventory and outcomes the gardens do not own (OEM/telco, open app supply, CTV/DOOH, vernacular EM audiences) — Affle's whole positioning [V].
2. **Outcome pricing + model quality = the margin.** CPCU makes Affle a risk-taker: it buys impressions and sells conversions, so gross margin (~37–39%) is a direct read of model accuracy and inventory cost. AppLovin's 84% EBITDA margin shows the prize when a model wins; TTD's first revenue decline shows the cost of being a pass-through when the gardens and Amazon compete [V/V-snip].
3. **Privacy and regulation set the signal supply.** ATT (2021) is the precedent; Chrome cookies survived (Sandbox retired Oct-2025); India's DPDP consent regime arrives May-2027; India banned the RMG ad category outright (Aug-2025/May-2026) [V-snip]. Each rule changes which models (first-party, contextual, outcome-based) win.
4. **Advertiser-cycle and FX exposure.** Performance budgets come from VC-funded app categories (e-commerce, fintech, gaming) and EM consumer companies; they swing with funding cycles and, for a USD-billing EM network like Affle, with EM currencies (FY26 gross-margin squeeze partly from USD strength; only ~20% of revenue is US dollar-to-dollar) [V Q1 call].
5. **Consolidation by acquisition.** Adtech is a serial-M&A industry: Affle (Vizury, RevX, mediasmart, Appnext, Jampp, YouAppi, AdColony assets, now Perion), Digital Turbine (AdColony, Fyber), AppLovin (MoPub, ironSource bid, MAX) [V/K]. Distressed mid-caps sell at <1x revenue (Perion EV ~0.44x [E]) while scaled winners trade at 7x+ sales (Affle) — the arbitrage Affle is now exploiting.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Outcome network (Affle):**
Revenue = Σ markets [Conversions × Average CPCU] (+ small non-CPCU)
Gross profit = Revenue − (Impressions bought × CPM ÷ 1,000) − data costs = Conversions × (CPCU − cost per conversion)
where cost per conversion = CPM ÷ (1,000 × conversion rate per impression)
EBITDA = Gross profit − employees − other opex
PAT = (EBITDA − D&A + other income − interest) × (1 − ETR)

### C.1 Worked example 1 — Affle FY26 (company numbers) [V; E arithmetic]
- Conversions 456.0 mn × CPCU ₹58.9 = CPCU revenue ₹2,687 Cr; + ₹22 Cr other = revenue ₹2,709 Cr.
- Inventory & data ₹1,680 Cr (62.0%) → **cost per conversion ≈ ₹36.8**; gross profit ₹1,030 Cr (38.0%) → ₹22.6/conversion.
- Employees ₹251 Cr + other ₹168 Cr = ₹419 Cr → **EBITDA ₹610 Cr (22.5%) = ₹13.4/conversion**.
- − D&A ₹124 Cr − interest ₹5 Cr + other income ₹78 Cr = PBT ₹559 Cr; tax ₹104 Cr (18.6%) → **PAT ₹455 Cr**; EPS ₹32.3.

### C.2 Worked example 2 — one illustrative CPCU campaign [E, illustrative]
- An Indian quick-commerce app pays **₹60 per first order**. Affle bids on impressions at an effective **₹40 CPM** (₹0.04/impression). If 1 in 1,100 impressions converts (0.09%), cost per conversion = ₹0.04 × 1,100 = **₹44** → gross profit ₹16 (27%).
- Improve the model so 1 in 1,600 → cost ₹64 — loss-making; 1 in 700 → cost ₹28 → gross profit ₹32 (53%). **A ±30% change in conversion rate swings gross margin from loss to >50%** — why model quality and fraud filtering are the business.
- Same campaign in a developed market: CPCU maybe ₹300+, CPM ₹200+, fewer impressions per conversion needed ("5x" less traffic per rupee in DM, per the CEO) [V qualitative; E numbers].

### C.3 Worked example 3 — Affle + Perion pro-forma (illustrative, FY28 run-rate) [E — heavy assumptions; FLAG]
- Affle organic FY28E revenue ≈ ₹2,709 Cr × 1.2² ≈ ₹3,900 Cr (~US$410 mn at ₹95).
- Perion ≈ US$300–350 mn (if decline continues from US$440 mn) → combined ≈ US$710–760 mn — consistent with Affle's ">US$700 mn run-rate" guidance [V-snip guidance; E].
- If Perion runs at ~10% EBITDA (US$30–35 mn ≈ ₹300 Cr) and Affle at 23% (₹900 Cr), combined EBITDA ≈ ₹1,200 Cr (~17% margin) — **margin dilution of ~6 pts** even as EBITDA grows [E].
- Interest on, say, US$200 mn Citibank debt at ~6% ≈ ₹115 Cr/yr; Perion's US$228 mn cash earns ~4% ≈ ₹85 Cr [E] — accretion depends on synergies.

### C.4 Worked example 4 — Google India's two revenue lines (why "revenue" misleads) [V-snip]
