## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Same-store growth (SSSG/LFL) means four different things here.**
   - **DMart "LFL"** = revenue growth of stores open **≥24 months** at period end [V presentation footnote]. Q2 FY27 **9.5%** (Q2 FY26 6.8%).
   - **VMM "SSSG"** = net-revenue growth of stores open **≥15 months at the start of each quarter**, excluding stores temporarily closed [V press release]. Q2 FY27 **5.4%**; management says ~9% "adjusting for the festival shift".
   - **V-Mart "SSSG"**: definition not stated in the update; quoted for V-Mart core and Unlimited separately. Q2 FY27 **3%** reported, **11%** "adjusting for festive timing shift" [V].
   - **V2 "SSSG"**: Q2 FY27 **−14.9% calendar**, **+0.5% "festival-normalised"** (each day compared with the matching day of last year's festival calendar) [V].
   - The festival-adjusted numbers are **management constructs**; the writer should always print the reported number first.
2. **Q2 FY27 is distorted by an 18–19-day festival shift.** Navratri began **11-Oct-2026 vs 22-Sep-2025** (V-Mart: "19-day shift"; VMM: "18 days") [V]. Durga Puja and Dussehra demand moved from Q2 into Q3. Apparel-heavy chains (V2, V-Mart) show the biggest hit, VMM a medium hit and DMart (57% food) almost none. **Do not read Q2 SSSG as a demand collapse, and do not read Q3 FY27 SSSG as a boom**: compare H1+Q3 combined, or Oct–Dec vs Sep–Dec.
3. **Ind AS 116 makes leased chains look more profitable than DMart at the EBITDA line.**
   - Since FY20, rent on leased stores is booked as depreciation of a "right-of-use" asset plus interest on a lease liability, so **reported EBITDA excludes rent**.
   - VMM FY26 reported EBITDA margin ~14.6% vs **operating (pre-Ind AS, pre-ESOP) ~10.2%** [primer]. Q2 FY27: reported 12.1% vs operating **7.7%**; rent ₹168.7 Cr in one quarter [V].
   - V2 FY26 reported 14.9% vs **pre-Ind AS 9.0%** [V]. V-Mart Q1 FY27 reported 14.8% vs **pre-Ind AS 7.6%** [V].
   - DMart owns most of its stores, so its **7.5% OP margin is nearly a pre-Ind AS number already**. On a like-for-like rent-paid basis DMart (~7.5–7.8%) and VMM (~10%) are much closer than 7.5% vs 14.9% suggests. **Use pre-Ind AS margins for cross-company comparison.**
4. **Screener "borrowings" are mostly lease liabilities for the leased chains.**
   - VMM: screener ₹1,988 Cr (Mar-26) is essentially lease liabilities; VMM has negligible bank debt and holds ₹1,119 Cr of investments (₹1,888 Cr Sep-26) [V screener; primer "debt-modest"].
   - V2: ₹995 Cr = lease liabilities ₹741 Cr + bank debt ₹254 Cr [V company balance sheet].
   - V-Mart: ₹958 Cr = leases ₹858 Cr + borrowings ₹100 Cr (Mar-26); bank borrowings fell to **₹1.2 Cr by Jun-26** [V].
   - DMart: ₹2,425 Cr (Mar-26) → ₹4,264 Cr (Sep-26), now **real borrowing for capex** plus small leases [V].
   - **Net debt for prose: VMM net cash (ex-leases), V-Mart ~debt-free ex-leases, V2 ~₹250 Cr bank debt, DMart ~₹4,000 Cr gross debt and rising.**
5. **DMart standalone vs consolidated.** Standalone = the DMart stores. Consolidated adds DMart Ready (Avenue E-Commerce) and small subsidiaries.
   - FY26: standalone PAT ₹3,224 Cr vs consolidated ₹2,970 Cr → **subsidiaries (mostly DMart Ready) lost ~₹254 Cr** [V/E].
   - H1 FY27: ₹1,739 Cr vs ₹1,603 Cr → **−₹136 Cr** [V/E].
   - "Revenue" in the press-release headline (₹19,206 Cr, +18.4%) is **standalone**; consolidated is ₹19,644 Cr (+17.8%). PAT growth: standalone +7.6%, consolidated +8.5%.
   - **Consolidated minus standalone revenue is NOT DMart Ready's revenue.** DMart Ready buys from DMart stores (intercompany sales sit in standalone revenue and are eliminated). The gap was ₹885 Cr in H1 FY26 and ₹889 Cr in H1 FY27, i.e. **flat**, while DMart Ready cut its cities from 19 to 11 [V/E].
6. **Store counts: gross vs net, and Mar vs Sep.**
   - VMM FY26: **105 gross openings, 6 closures, 99 net** (696 → 795) [V presentation]. Our primer says "105 net new stores" — wrong.
   - V2 FY26: 189 → 325 = **136 net** [primer; V Q1 PPT 381 = 325 + 57 − 1]. H1 FY27: **106 opened, 4 closed** → 427 [V].
   - V-Mart: **577 at Mar-26** (not "~600" as in the primer), 591 Jun-26, 613 Sep-26 [V].
   - DMart: 500 Mar-26, 518 Sep-26 (incl. one Navi Mumbai store closed for reconstruction) [V].
7. **Revenue per sq ft is quoted on three different clocks.**
   - DMart: **per quarter** in the presentation (₹8,741/sq ft Q2 FY27). Annualised that is ~₹35,000 [V/E].
   - V-Mart and V2: **per month** (V-Mart ₹767 Q1 FY27; V2 ₹700 Q2 FY27, ₹925 FY26). V-Mart also reports **annual** ₹8,735 (FY26) [V].
   - VMM does not publish it. FY26 implied ~₹9,500–10,000/sq ft/yr [E: ₹12,906 Cr ÷ ~13–13.5 mn sq ft average].
   - **Annualise before comparing**: DMart ~₹35k, V2 ~₹11k, VMM ~₹9.5–10k, V-Mart ~₹8.7k.
8. **"Private label" share uses different bases.**
   - VMM: own brands **73.9% of total revenue** (H1 FY27), 74.1% FY26 [V; primer].
   - V-Mart: **66%+ of apparel** revenue (not of total) [V presentation].
   - V2: "mix of own-designed and procured products", with a 60-person in-house design team; no share disclosed [V].
   - DMart: no private-label share disclosed; FMCG is largely third-party brands [K]. Do not compare VMM's 74% with an apparel-only figure.
9. **Category mix definitions differ.** DMart: Foods 56.90% / Non-Foods (FMCG) 19.48% / General Merchandise & Apparel 23.62% (H1 FY27) [V]. VMM: Apparel 44.1% / General Merchandise 28.4% / FMCG 27.3% (H1 FY27) [V]. V-Mart: Apparel 81% / Non-apparel 9% / FMCG 10% (Q1 FY27) [V]. V2: Men 42% / Ladies 28% / Kids 23% / Lifestyle 7% (Q1 FY27) — almost all apparel [V]. **"Grocery" is 77% of DMart (Foods + FMCG), 27% of VMM, ~10% of V-Mart and ~0% of V2.**
10. **DMart's FY23 PAT and RoNW include a one-off tax benefit of ₹138.8 Cr** (and ₹36 Cr in FY25) [V presentation footnote]. Screener FY23 PAT ₹2,378 Cr therefore overstates the trend; FY23 → FY24 growth looks flat (+6.6%) partly for this reason.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Four members, two business models, one customer.** All four sell "value" to India's middle and lower-middle class, but there are two economic engines:
  - **Grocery-led, owned-store hypermarket: DMart.** FY26 revenue ₹68,821 Cr, OP ₹5,189 Cr (7.5%), PAT ₹2,970 Cr [V screener]. That is **77.7% of the group's revenue and 79.5% of its market value** [E].
  - **Apparel/general-merchandise-led, leased value chains: VMM, V-Mart, V2.** FY26 revenue ₹19,762 Cr (+24.6%), OP ₹2,904 Cr (14.7% post-Ind AS; ~9–10% pre-Ind AS), PAT ₹1,125 Cr (+50%) [E: sums of screener].
  - **All four members: FY26 revenue ₹88,583 Cr (+17.8%), OP ₹8,093 Cr (9.1%), PAT ₹4,095 Cr (+18.5%); m-cap ₹2,59,024 Cr on 23-Oct-2026 (63× FY26 PAT)** [E: sums of screener 24-Oct-26].
  - Store base: **2,402 stores at Sep-2026** (DMart 518, VMM 844, V2 427, V-Mart 613), up from 1,797 at Mar-2025 [V].
- **October 2026 de-rating.** The market has cut multiples hard while revenue growth stays at 14–28%.
  - DMart **₹3,158** on 23-Oct vs **₹3,813 on 1-Oct** (−17% in three weeks) and a 52-week high of ₹4,644; m-cap **₹2.06 lakh Cr** vs "~₹2.8 lakh Cr" in our June primer; P/E 66× TTM [V screener; V-snip univest]. PL Capital cut it to **Reduce, target ₹3,214** on 11-Oct, citing quick commerce, "limited success of D'Mart Ready" and falling ROE [V-snip].
  - VMM **₹87.4** (52-week range ₹84.9–150), m-cap ₹40,922 Cr vs ~₹56,000 Cr in the primer; trading **below both block-deal prices (~₹115)** [V screener].
  - V2 **₹163** (52-week range ₹149–259); V-Mart ₹777 (52-week range ₹458–882) — V-Mart is the only member near its high [V].
- **Q2 FY27 in one line each** [V company filings]:
  - **DMart:** revenue +17.8%, LFL 9.5% (best in five quarters bar Q4 FY26), bills +14%, but EBITDA margin 7.1% (7.3%) and PAT +8.5%. "Wage inflation at entry level has led to a marginal increase in operating expenses."
  - **VMM:** revenue +14.0%, SSSG 5.4%, gross margin 28.0% (28.3%), operating EBITDA margin 7.7% (8.5%), PAT +3.7%. 25 net stores to 844.
  - **V2:** revenue +28.4% on a store base up ~1.8–1.9× YoY [E: 427 vs ~225–235 at Sep-25, interpolated between 189 (Mar-25) and 325 (Mar-26)]; same-store −14.9% calendar.
  - **V-Mart:** revenue +18%, SSSG 3% (V-Mart core 2%, Unlimited 11%).
- **Growth now comes from new stores, not old ones.** Openings are running at a record pace:
  - DMart +85 in FY26 (record) but only +18 in H1 FY27 [V].
  - VMM +105 gross in FY26, +53 gross in H1 FY27 [V].
  - V2 +136 net in FY26, **+106 opened in H1 FY27**; FY27 guidance 170–200 openings toward ~500 stores [primer; V].
  - V-Mart +80 net in FY26 (497 → 577), +36 net in H1 FY27 [V].
  - **Group store count +34% from Mar-25 to Sep-26** vs **Q2 FY27 group revenue +17.6%** [E]. New stores ramp slowly (J-curve), so revenue per sq ft is falling at V2 (monthly ₹960 → ₹886 in Q1) and flat at DMart.
- **Three macro shocks in twelve months.**
  - (1) **GST 2.0 from 22-Sep-2025**: apparel up to ₹2,500 at 5% (was 12% for ₹1,000–2,500), most FMCG staples to 5%/0% [V-snip]. Plus the **Feb-2025 income-tax cut** (zero tax up to ₹12 lakh under the new regime) [K]. Together a consumption tailwind in FY26 H2.

### 1.2 Value chain (inputs → shopper)

1. **Grocery and FMCG (DMart's 77%).** Manufacturers (HUL, ITC, Nestlé, P&G, local brands) and staples suppliers (millers, oil packers) → sometimes a distributor/C&F → retailer DC → store.
   - DMart buys direct from brand owners at scale and **pays suppliers in ~7 days** (days payable 6.8–7.2) [V presentation], vs a 30–60-day trade norm [K]. In return it gets cash discounts and better trade terms. This is the EDLC lever.
   - Retailer gross margin on branded FMCG is thin: DMart's whole-store gross margin is ~14–15% [primer; K].
2. **Private-label apparel and general merchandise (VMM, V-Mart, V2).**
   - In-house design/merchandising → contract manufacturers and job workers (largely domestic: Tiruppur, Ludhiana, Delhi NCR, Kolkata, Surat for fabrics [K]) → central DC (VMM: multiple; V-Mart: Palwal 6 lakh sq ft owned + Bengaluru; V2: Gurugram) → store.
   - The retailer captures the brand-owner's margin. Group gross margins: VMM **28.0–28.3%**, V2 **28.6–30.2%**, V-Mart **34.5–35.3%** (V-Mart includes LimeRoad commission income) [V].
   - V2 uses dedicated job workers for its own designs and its own vehicle fleet, refilling stores weekly [V presentation]. V-Mart has 600+ vendors, 190 mn+ pieces/year and fortnightly ("bi-weekly") fashion drops [V].
3. **Consignment / sale-or-return (VMM especially).** Part of VMM's third-party stock is held on consignment, which is why its inventory days (80, FY26) sit close to its payable days (64) [V screener; primer].

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **Retail is a spread business.** Value is captured by (a) buying cheaper than rivals (scale, payment terms, own brands) and (b) turning the store and the stock faster.
- **DMart captures value through turns, not margin.** Inventory turnover **12.8×** (FY26, standalone), fixed-asset turnover 3.2× including ROU assets [V]. ~7.8% standalone EBITDA margin × fast turns → ROCE 17.1%.
- **The leased chains capture value through own-brand gross margin.** ~28–35% gross margin, but they pay rent (VMM: ₹168.7 Cr in Q2 FY27 = ~5.0% of revenue [V/E]) and turn stock 3–4× a year (inventory days: VMM 80, V-Mart 145 on screener basis / 86 on company "days of sales" basis, V2 195 screener / NWC 74 company) [V].
- **Brand owners vs retailer.** In FMCG the brand owner keeps the margin (HUL gross margin ~50% [K]). In VMM's own-brand apparel the retailer keeps it. This is why VMM's gross margin is about double DMart's.
- **The platform layer** (quick commerce) monetises convenience through delivery/platform fees and ads. Blinkit's adjusted revenue was ₹15,664 Cr on ₹17,132 Cr NOV in Q1 FY27, mostly because it moved to an inventory-led (1P-like) model [V-snip]. It competes for the same FMCG basket that is DMart's traffic driver.

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Tier 2–4 formalisation.** The core thesis for all three leased chains.
   - VMM: **443 of 844 stores in Tier III towns (<2 lakh population)**; 582 cities [V; tier mapping E from the presentation layout].
   - V-Mart: **376 of 591 stores in Tier III/IV**; 335 cities. Tier 4 SPSF grew **+15% YoY** in Q1 FY27, the fastest of any tier [V].
   - V2: "Tier 2 and Tier 3 consumption markets"; >300 cities, 25 states [V].
   - Zudio: >80% of new Q1 FY27 stores in Tier II/III [V-snip Trent]. The competition is moving into the same towns.
2. **Income and tax.** Budget 2025-26 (1-Feb-2025) made income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free under the new regime (~₹1 lakh Cr of revenue forgone) [K]. GST 2.0 (22-Sep-2025) cut rates on most daily-use goods and on apparel ₹1,000–2,500 [V-snip]. Both raise disposable income for the salaried middle class — VMM/DMart's core customer more than V2/V-Mart's rural-semi-urban one.
3. **Food prices.** For DMart, food inflation lifts the ticket. CPI food fell through much of FY26 (deflation in vegetables), and DMart blamed mid-FY26 LFL softness partly on deflation [primer]. Food inflation of 7.74% in Sep-26 [V-snip] helps DMart's nominal LFL. For value-fashion chains it **crowds out discretionary apparel**, especially in rural areas (rural food 8.06%).
4. **Monsoon and rural income.** 2026 monsoon 87% of LPA; South Peninsula 76%, North-East 74% [V-snip]. Expect weaker rural kharif income in the south and north-east into Q3–Q4 FY27 [E]. Watch Unlimited's SSSG (south, 11–13% so far) and V-Mart/VMM's east (VMM east = largest region by revenue share, ~29–30% [V presentation, mapping E]).
5. **Seasonality** (see §B and §E). Apparel chains earn most of their profit in **Q3 (festive + winter + weddings)**:
   - V-Mart Q3 FY26 OP ₹210 Cr = **41% of FY26 OP** (₹514 Cr); Q2 FY26 OP ₹72 Cr and a **Q2 net loss of ₹9 Cr** [V screener].
   - V2 Q3 FY26 PAT ₹103 Cr = **64% of FY26 PAT** [V].
   - VMM Q3 FY26 PAT ₹313 Cr = 37% [V].
   - DMart Q3 ₹856 Cr = 29% — nearly flat across quarters [V].
6. **Weddings.** Per-wedding spend and wedding dates (muhurat) are a Q3/Q4 driver for ethnic wear and gifting [K]. Not quantified here.
7. **Population and households.** ~1.45 bn people, ~300 mn+ households [K]. VMM claims **18.1 crore registered loyalty customers**, with ~95% of revenue from loyalty members [V] — a registration count, not unique active households.

## 4. Supply side and cost structure

### 4.1 Key inputs and their price drivers
- **Merchandise cost (COGS):** 65–86% of revenue. DMart ~85–86% (gross margin 14–15%); VMM ~72%; V2 ~70–71%; V-Mart ~65% [V/E].
  - Apparel input = cotton/yarn/fabric + conversion. VMM's CEO (Q2 FY27): "renewed inflationary pressures across key raw material categories" [V]. V-Mart FY24's loss was blamed partly on cotton inflation [primer].
  - FMCG input = brand-owner list price less trade terms. GST cuts were passed through as lower MRPs from 22-Sep-2025 [V-snip], which deflates nominal revenue per unit.
- **Rent:** zero for DMart's owned stores (it carries land/building capex and depreciation instead). Leased chains pay **~5–7% of revenue**: VMM Q2 FY27 rent ₹168.7 Cr / ₹3,398.5 Cr = 5.0%; V-Mart Q1 FY27 rent reversal ₹77.4 Cr / ₹1,088.8 Cr = 7.1%; V2 FY26 rent ≈ pre/post-Ind AS EBITDA gap ₹178 Cr / ₹3,067 Cr = 5.8% [V/E].
- **Staff:** VMM 6.6% of revenue (Q2 FY27 ₹223 Cr); V2 8.0% (Q1 FY27 ₹80.3 Cr); V-Mart 10.2% (Q1 FY27 ₹111.2 Cr) [V/E]. DMart: "wage inflation at entry level" flagged in Q2 FY27 [V]; staff ~4% of revenue [K/E].
- **Power & fuel:** V-Mart Q1 FY27 ₹43.3 Cr = 4.0% of revenue [V]. Air-conditioned large stores make power a fixed cost.
- **Advertising:** V-Mart 1.3% of revenue [V]. DMart spends almost nothing (EDLP) [primer].

## 7. Regulation and policy

- **GST 2.0 (effective 22-Sep-2025)** [V-snip apparelresources; K for the structure]:
  - Two main slabs, 5% and 18%, plus 40% on sin/luxury goods [K].
  - **Apparel and footwear ≤₹2,500: 5%** (earlier 5% <₹1,000, 12% for ₹1,000–2,500). **>₹2,500: 18%** (from 12%).
  - Many FMCG items (soaps, shampoos, toothpaste, packaged foods, ghee, butter, namkeen) moved from 12–18% to 5%; some staples to 0% [K].
  - V-Mart: 75–80% of its apparel was already <₹1,000; the gain is in the ₹1,000–2,500 band [V-snip].
  - CRISIL: +200 bp to organised apparel retail growth in FY26; premium wear (~35% of organised apparel) hurt by the 18% rate [V-snip].
  - **Value retailers' price points sit almost entirely below ₹2,500**, so they are pure winners. Premium players (Vedant/Manyavar) lost.
- **Income tax (Budget 2025-26):** rebate raised so income up to ₹12 lakh pays no tax under the new regime (₹12.75 lakh for salaried with the standard deduction) [K].
- **FDI:** 100% FDI allowed in single-brand retail (automatic route) [K]. Multi-brand retail capped at 51% with conditions, and e-commerce FDI is marketplace-only (no inventory model for foreign-owned platforms) [K]. This shapes quick commerce: Blinkit's move to inventory ownership required Eternal to become an "Indian-owned and controlled company" (2025) [K; FLAG verify].
- **Shops & Establishments Acts / 24×7 trading:** state-level; store hours and staffing [K].
- **Legal Metrology / FSSAI:** labelling and food safety. Blinkit faced warehouse raids by state FDAs over food safety (2026) [V-snip sahi].
- **SEBI minimum public shareholding (MPS):** listed companies must keep ≥25% public float [K]. **DMart's promoter group at 74.48% (Sep-26)** is compliant. The family came down from 82.2% (2017) to 74.99% (Mar-2020) through a QIP and has drifted to 74.48% since [V screener]. **There is no live MPS issue.** VMM's PE promoter is far below 75%.
- **Ind AS 116 (leases)** applies from FY20 [K]; see trap 3.
- **Competition Commission / quick commerce:** trader bodies (AICPDF) have complained to CCI about deep discounting by quick-commerce platforms (2024–25) [K]. Status unverified.

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

- **2000s boom and bust.** Pantaloon/Big Bazaar (Future Group), Subhiksha, Vishal Retail, Spencer's, More and Reliance Fresh expanded on debt in the 2005–08 boom [K]. Vishal Retail collapsed in 2008–11 (sold to TPG/Shriram) [V screener V2 profile; K]. Subhiksha shut in 2009 [K].
- **DMart's patient model.** First store Powai 2002; ~29 stores by 2010; IPO Mar-2017 at ₹299, subscribed ~105× [primer]. It compounded at ~22–23% revenue CAGR for a decade [V screener: 10-year sales CAGR 23%].
- **Future Group collapse (2020–22).** Big Bazaar's ~1,500 stores went dark after the Amazon–Reliance fight; Reliance took over many leases in Feb-2022 [K]. A large share of organised value retail space was handed to Reliance and the survivors.
- **COVID (FY21).** DMart revenue −3% (₹24,870 Cr → ₹24,143 Cr), V-Mart −35% (₹1,662 Cr → ₹1,075 Cr) [V screener]. Apparel was hit hardest.
- **Acquisitions.** V-Mart bought Unlimited from Arvind Fashions (74 stores, ~₹150 Cr, FY22) and LimeRoad (distress, ~₹31 Cr upfront, FY23) [V presentation; primer]. FY23–FY24 integration pain → **FY24 net loss ₹97 Cr** [V screener].
- **VMM IPO (Dec-2024).** ₹8,000 Cr, 100% OFS at ₹78; listed 18-Dec-2024 [primer]. PE sell-down in Jun-2025 and Feb-2026 [V-snip; V screener].
- **Quick-commerce explosion (2023–26).** Blinkit from a few hundred dark stores to 2,443 (Jun-26) [V-snip]. DMart LFL dipped to 5.5–6.8% in several FY26–27 quarters [V]. DMart Ready retrenched from 24 to 19 cities (Oct-2025, exiting Amritsar, Belagavi, Bhilai, Chandigarh, Ghaziabad) and then to **11** (by Sep-2026) [primer; V-snip; V presentation].
- **FY26 consumption stimulus** (tax cut + GST 2.0) → record store openings at all four.
- **Oct-2026 de-rating:** food inflation, a weak monsoon, a festival-shift-distorted Q2 and an RBI hike → DMart −17% in October; VMM near its 52-week low [V screener; V-snip].

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Quick commerce** (the main one; §B1). It attacks the top-up FMCG basket and impulse GM, i.e. DMart's high-frequency traffic driver. Blinkit's NOV equals ~91% of DMart's quarterly revenue [E: ₹17,132 Cr ÷ ₹18,795 Cr, Q1 FY27].
   - Counter-trend: Instamart reached contribution break-even (May-2026) by **raising monetisation per order** (₹97 → ₹108) [V-snip]. Price competitiveness of quick commerce vs DMart may erode as subsidies fall.

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Store count is the growth engine; SSSG is the health check.**
   - FY26 group revenue +17.8% came mostly from space: group stores 1,797 (Mar-25) → 2,197 (Mar-26), +22% [V].
   - SSSG ran 5–11% (DMart LFL 5.5–10.8% by quarter; VMM ~11%; V2 ~8.6%) [V/primer].
   - The test of every value chain is whether new stores mature (J-curve) and whether old stores keep growing while the chain cannibalises itself and competitors move into the same towns.
2. **Rent vs own is the deepest structural fork.**
   - DMart owns, pays no rent and earns ~7.8% EBITDA on a ~14.5% gross margin. It grows slower and is capex-heavy: FCF negative in FY25–26, now borrowing (₹4,079 Cr, Sep-26) [V].
   - The leased chains earn ~28–35% gross, pay ~5–7% of revenue in rent and end at the same ~8–9% pre-Ind AS EBITDA, but can add 100–200 stores a year cheaply.
   - Ind AS 116 hides this fork in reported EBITDA (trap 3).
3. **Quick commerce vs the big basket.** Blinkit's quarterly order value (₹17,132 Cr) now equals DMart's quarterly revenue (₹18,795 Cr) [V-snip/V]. DMart Ready has shrunk to 11 cities [V].
   - Bull case: the planned monthly basket is defended by price.
   - Bear case: metro top-up trips and the higher-margin impulse GM are lost.
   - Evidence so far: DMart LFL **9.5%** in Q2 FY27 with bills +14% [V] — still healthy. But EBITDA margin is slipping (7.1% vs 7.3%) and the market is de-rating the stock.
4. **Own brands set the margin.** VMM's 73.9% own-brand share gives a ~28% gross margin on a basket that is 27% FMCG [V]. V-Mart's 66%+ apparel private label gives ~34.5% [V]. Own brands also make these chains less exposed to quick commerce, which sells mostly national FMCG brands [E].
5. **The value customer's wallet is macro-sensitive.**
   - Tailwinds: GST 2.0 and the tax cut (FY26 H2).
   - Headwinds: food inflation 7.74%, rural food 8.06%, monsoon 87% of LPA, repo hike [V-snip/V-sib].
   - Apparel is discretionary and seasonal (Q3 = 40–65% of annual profit for V-Mart/V2) [V/E]; grocery is not.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Revenue = average retail area (sq ft) × revenue per sq ft**, where revenue per sq ft = footfall × conversion × average bill value ÷ area. Equivalently, **(mature-store base × (1 + SSSG)) + (new stores × ramp factor × mature productivity)**.

**Profit (pre-Ind AS EBITDA) = revenue × gross margin − (staff + rent + power + logistics + marketing + head office)**; then − depreciation − interest − tax.

**Return on a store = store EBITDA ÷ (capex + working capital)**; for owned stores add land, which retains value.

### C.1 Worked example 1 — one mature DMart store, FY26 [E composite]
- Area ~41,000 sq ft × ~₹34,000–35,000/sq ft/yr → **~₹140 Cr revenue**. Check: ₹66,968 Cr SA ÷ ~480 average stores = ₹139.5 Cr [E].
- Gross margin ~14.5% → ₹20.3 Cr. Store opex (staff ~₹5.6 Cr, power/maintenance ~₹2.5 Cr, other ~₹1.3 Cr) ~₹9.4 Cr → store EBITDA ~₹11 Cr (~7.8%).
- Capex owned ~₹45–50 Cr (land ~₹20–25 Cr, building + fit-out ~₹25 Cr) + inventory ~₹11 Cr (33 days of COGS) [E].
- **Pre-tax cash return ~19–21% on capital; ~4.5–5-year payback, with the land kept.**
- A leased version would pay ~₹6–8 Cr of rent (4.5–5.5% of sales) and earn ~₹3–5 Cr, but need only ~₹15 Cr of capital [E].

### C.2 Worked example 2 — one VMM store, FY26 [E composite]
- ~16,700 sq ft × ~₹9,800/sq ft → **~₹16.4 Cr revenue**. Check: ₹12,906 Cr ÷ ~745 average stores = ₹17.3 Cr [E].
- Gross margin 28.3% → ₹4.6 Cr. Rent ~5% (₹0.8 Cr), staff ~6% (₹1.0 Cr), other store costs ~7% (₹1.1 Cr) → store EBITDA ~₹1.7 Cr (~10%).
- Fit-out ~₹3–4 Cr + net working capital ~₹1 Cr (consignment, payables offset) → **~35–40% pre-tax return; ~2.5–3-year payback** [E].

### C.3 Worked example 3 — one V2 store, ramp year vs mature [E composite]
- ~10,800 sq ft. Mature at ~₹960/sq ft/month (Q1 FY26 level) = ₹12.4 Cr/yr; in the ramp year at ~₹700–800/month = ₹9–10 Cr.
- Gross margin ~29% → ₹2.6–3.6 Cr. Rent ~6% (₹0.6–0.7 Cr), staff ~8% (₹0.8–1.0 Cr), other ~6% → EBITDA pre-Ind AS ₹0.5–1.1 Cr (5–9%).
- Capital: fit-out ~₹2–3 Cr + **inventory ~₹3.5 Cr** (V2 inventory ₹1,142 Cr ÷ 325 stores [V/E]).
- **Inventory is V2's real capex.** Payback ~5–7 years if the store stays at ramp productivity, ~3 years at mature [E].

### C.4 Worked example 4 — V-Mart Q1 FY27 actual bridge (₹ Cr) [V]
- Revenue 1,088.8 − COGS 713.6 = GP 375.2 (34.5%).
- − employee 111.2 − other (pre-Ind AS, incl. rent) 180.7 = **pre-Ind AS EBITDA 83.2 (7.6%)**.
- Add back rent 77.4 → **reported EBITDA 160.6 (14.8%)**.
- Post-Ind AS: − D&A 84.0 − finance 19.8 + other income 2.7 = PBT 59.5 → **PAT 47.2**.
- Pre-Ind AS PAT 42.3. The Ind AS PAT is higher in Q1 because of the timing of lease interest/depreciation and a ₹0.4 Cr profit on lease termination.

## 4.2 Company deep dives (primary documents)

### 4.2.1 Avenue Supermarts (DMart · DMART · 540376)

**Sources:** [T1] annual analyst meet transcript (call 28-Jul-2026), the only management Q&A since FY26 because DMart holds no quarterly calls; Q2 FY27 press release and H1 FY27 deck (10-Oct-2026, already [V] in Part 2); [R1] Q2 results with the Sep-26 balance sheet; [A1] FY26 annual report; [SC].

**A. Management-guidance digest (paraphrased; [T1] unless noted)**
1. **Store additions.**
   - The internal benchmark is to add **~15% of the store base a year**: a few points higher or lower in a given year, depending on real-estate timing. On a 500-store base that implies ~75 a year, against 85 (17%) in FY26.
   - Management would not commit to repeating FY26's ~20% net growth. 100–150 stores a year will come "at some point", not at the current base.
   - The pipeline holds 2–3 years of projects. Land-to-store takes 2–3 years: longer for double-basement, multi-storey stores.
   - Only **18 stores were added in H1 FY27** [V deck], so H2 must deliver ~57 to hit ~15%. **Read: FY27 openings are at risk of landing below 15%.**
2. **Ownership model.**
   - The company is now "more open" to **long-term leases** where clean land is not available. **68 of ~500 stores are leased; 15 of the 85 FY26 openings were leased** (53 before FY26).
   - The CFO says lease costs sit below EBITDA under Ind AS 116, so **PBT is the fair comparison**.
3. **Capex and funding.**
   - FY26 capex was "upwards of ₹4,000 Cr".
   - "Actual debt" was ~₹965 Cr at Mar-26: commercial paper ~₹800 Cr, plus a **₹1,000 Cr NCD programme** approved to lock in longer tenor and fixed cost.
   - **Borrowing could reach ~₹2,000 Cr by FY27-end, all for capex.** By Sep-26 standalone borrowings were already **₹2,628.66 Cr** [R1], above that guide.
   - FY26 capital commitments: **₹5,238.65 Cr** (₹4,274.09 Cr in FY25) [A1].
4. **Margins.**
   - Gross margin "has been broadly 14–15%" for 7–8 years and will stay there. **Long-run net margin ~5%** (FY26 standalone 4.8%).
   - FY26 standalone: gross margin +16 bp; **employee cost +27 bp**; other expenses −9 bp; EBITDA flat at ~7.85%.
   - Employee cost rose **~33% in FY26** (standalone), partly the **wage code implemented from Dec-2025** and partly capability-building "ahead of the curve". Management would not say the build is over.
   - **Q2 FY27 press release:** "wage inflation at entry level has led to a marginal increase in operating expenses" [V Part 2]. Standalone EBITDA margin 7.3% vs 7.6%.
5. **LFL / same-store.**
   - FY26 LFL (stores >2 years old) **8.1%**.
   - Older metro stores in Mumbai and Bengaluru are near **capacity saturation**, and new stores nearby cannibalise them. Management also cites **competition**, including quick commerce for "lower basket sizes".
   - **SSSG "would possibly be hovering more in the range that we are seeing today"**; no return to double digits expected, all else equal.
   - Tier-1 and Tier-2 stores opened in the last two years have **SSG "much better than the company average"**.
   - Quarterly revenue growth swings explained:
     - Q3 FY26 +13%: staples deflation.
     - Q4 FY26 +19%: March panic buying.
     - Q1 FY27 +15%: spill-over of the March buying.
6. **Quick-commerce commentary.**
   - Management "suspect[s]" the quick-commerce impact "is not going to change over the next few years".
   - It will not change its store-location strategy to avoid quick commerce.
   - FMCG vendors are "doubling down" on DMart, which is the **No. 1 retailer** for several of them.
   - Brands that scale online (including D2C brands) are pulled into stores.
   - The bulk monthly basket remains the value proposition. On a ~₹1,600 average bill the saving is "significantly more than 10%".
7. **DMart Ready.**
   - The business has been "double down" focused on **11 cities**, which give "the vast majority" of online sales. Three priorities: online-specific assortment, **delivery within ~6 hours (slotted)**, and app user experience.
   - Management has seen "a lot of erosion in profitability" and says the model must be proven profitable in the 11 cities before any expansion.
   - **165 pick-up points.** FY26 contract-labour cost +24% and transport +34% (analyst's reading of the annual report, not contested).
8. **Private label.** FMCG private label follows the "20-20-20" rule: a 20% price advantage, 20% better margin than the benchmark, and a shot at a 20% volume share. There is no push for more shelf space. Garments, general merchandise and DMart-packed staples are not counted as "private label".
9. **Capability.** CEO priorities: management bandwidth, a modernised technology and data stack, and execution rigour. Five new states were entered in FY26; FY27 focus is density in those states.

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed

1. **"Owner vs tenant" is less clean than the dossier says.**
   - DMart now has **68 leased stores (~14% of the base; 18% of FY26 openings)**, lease liabilities up ~2× in a year (₹693 Cr → ₹1,451 Cr by Sep-26), and an explicit policy shift toward long leases.
   - Its rent-paid EBITDA margin is **~7.5%, not ~7.8%**, which narrows the gap with VMM's ~10.2% operating EBITDA.
   - Combine this with borrowings of ₹2,629 Cr (above the "~₹2,000 Cr by year-end" guide) and capital commitments of ₹5,239 Cr: the "fortress, rent-free" narrative needs a 2026 update.
2. **DMart Ready is a ₹4,094 Cr business losing ₹307 Cr (−7.5% net margin).** The loss widened 24% even as cities were cut.
   - 63% of its turnover is goods bought from DMart.
   - Its auditor-flagged ₹1,488 Cr carrying value is now a **key audit matter**.
   - The new six-hour slotted model is a deliberate refusal to fight 10-minute delivery.
   - **Subsidiary drag on DMart's consolidated PAT has doubled since FY22 (₹124 Cr → ₹254 Cr).**
3. **Quick commerce's per-sq-ft productivity is ~2× DMart's.**
   - Instamart GOV per dark-store sq ft is ~₹17.7k a quarter vs DMart's ₹8.7k.
   - Blinkit is guiding to >3,500 stores by Mar-27 and ~5,000 by Mar-28 (from 2,751), with small-city baskets at 85–90% of metro.
   - **DMart management itself now expects the quick-commerce impact on small baskets to persist "over the next few years" and SSSG to stay near current levels.**
   - **That is the most important sentence for the DMart bear case, and it comes from the company.**
4. **The festival calendar cuts both ways, and Q3 FY27 laps soft bases.**
   - 2025's early Navratri (22-Sep) pulled demand into Q2 FY26: V2 +86.6%, V-Mart +22.1%, VMM +22.4%.
   - That depressed Q3 FY26 (V-Mart +9.6%, VMM +17.0%).
   - **Q3 FY27 compares against the weakest Q3 base in three years.** Headline Q3 growth will overstate the trend. Use Sep–Dec or H2 comparisons.
5. **V2's FY26 profit includes a judgement-driven lease re-estimate.**
   - The ₹27.7 Cr exceptional gain came from shortening store lease terms on 1-Oct-2025 (ROU −₹484 Cr).
   - It also **lowers future depreciation and lease interest**: depreciation fell quarter-on-quarter in Q1 FY27 despite ~56 new stores.
   - The auditor's emphasis of matter (a 2019-era ₹12.9 Cr BCCL advance) and a prior-year barcode/inventory mismatch are small, but they **point to controls that are still catching up with 2× annual growth.**
6. **Labour is the new cost line for everyone in 2026.**
   - DMart: wage code from Dec-2025; standalone employee cost +33%.
   - VMM: employee cost per sq ft +13% (minimum-wage increases in four states).
   - V-Mart: minimum-wage increases; Karnataka under stay.
   - Plus ~10% apparel raw-material inflation (crude-linked polyester).
   - The value chains can pass on only 3–5% ASP. **Gross margin holds, but operating leverage now depends on SSSG ≥3–4%** (V-Mart's own rule of thumb).
7. **VMM's PE overhang has a regulatory twist.**
   - The company has proactively capped foreign ownership at 49.99% to stay "Indian-owned and controlled" (foreign ~20% now).
   - Domestic funds absorbed both blocks (Feb-26 buyers included HDFC MF, alongside GIC and MAS), and DIIs now hold 36.45%.
   - The remaining ~40% (Partners Group ~27%) must again find mostly domestic buyers, now **at ~₹87 vs ₹117 in Feb-2026.**
8. **V-Mart's "turnaround" has people risk.** The COO left (Jul-26) and the President-Merchandising resigned (eff. 9-Oct-26) just before the festive quarter. Conversion has fallen from the mid-40s% to 38–39% as competition rises, even with footfall +39%.
