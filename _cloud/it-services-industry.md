### 0.10 H-1B: $100,000 payment proclamation, extension, weighted lottery, litigation [V]
Primary sources fetched:
- White House, Proclamation 10973 "Restriction on Entry of Certain Nonimmigrant Workers", 19 Sep 2025. https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/09/restriction-on-entry-of-certain-nonimmigrant-workers/
- Federal Register public inspection 2026-19554, Proclamation extending the restriction (signed Sep 2026; publication 23 Sep 2026). https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-19554.pdf
- Litigation status: immihelp (2 Sep 2026) https://www.immihelp.com/h1b-100000-fee-first-circuit-ruling-2026/ and Univ. of Colorado ISSS (7 Oct 2026) https://www.colorado.edu/isss/2026/10/07/100000-h-1b-fee-current-status

Facts:
- **2025 Proclamation**: entry of H-1B workers restricted unless petition "accompanied or supplemented by a payment of **$100,000**"; effective **12:01 a.m. EDT 21 Sep 2025**; 12-month duration absent extension; applies to petitions for beneficiaries **outside the US** (consular), not to existing visa holders, extensions or in-country change of status; national-interest exemptions; directs DOL to raise prevailing wage levels and DHS to prioritise high-paid workers.
- Proclamation's own claims: IT share of H-1B approvals grew from **32% (FY2003) to >65%** (5-yr avg); cites a 36% wage discount for "entry-level" H-1B positions.
- **DHS weighted-selection final rule** published **29 Dec 2025** (90 FR 60864), in effect for the FY2027 cap season (registration 4–19 Mar 2026): Level IV wage = 4 entries, III = 3, II = 2, I = 1 (Miller Canfield summary, search [S]).
- **DOL NPRM** on raising prevailing wages: **27 Mar 2026** (91 FR 15454) — not yet final.
- **Extension proclamation (Sep 2026)**: extends to **21 Sep 2027**. Key data disclosed by the US government itself:
  - $100,000 payment made for **"over 700 petitions"** since 21 Sep 2025 — i.e., the fee has functioned as a near-prohibition, not a revenue tool.
  - **"The largest IT staffing and outsourcing firms reduc[ed] their combined H-1B registrations from 24,946 to 2,055, a 92 percent decrease."**
  - Consular processing requests down **~97%** from FY2025 to FY2027 cap seasons.
  - Registrants with ≥US master's: **45.1% (FY26) → 66.1% (FY27)**; top-two wage levels = **46.3%** of FY27 selections; lowest level only **17.8%**.
- **Litigation**: Chamber of Commerce v. DHS (D.D.C.) — fee upheld, appealed. **8 Jun 2026**: D. Mass. vacated the policy (unauthorised tax / no notice-and-comment); briefly stayed pending appeal (fee collected for several weeks). **24 Jul 2026**: First Circuit denied government's stay → USCIS stopped collecting. Separate State of California v. Mullin (20 states). As of 7 Oct 2026 the payment is **"currently blocked by the court order"**; extension "has no practical effect" while the injunction stands; merits appeal pending. [S] A separate DHS proposal for a ~$103,265 fee on certain cap petitions is not in effect.
- **Industry read**: Indian vendors had already cut H-1B dependence (local hiring, offshore shift since 2017) — management commentary through FY26 said the fee was "not material" because new filings were small. The real cost is (i) onsite delivery flexibility for new ramp-ups, (ii) a shift to more expensive local hires/subcontractors in the US (raising onsite cost), (iii) accelerating clients' preference for offshore/GCC models. The 92% registration collapse means the vendors' US pipeline of rotational Indian staff has effectively closed for FY27 hires.

### 0.11 US "HIRE Act" (outsourcing excise tax) [S — Moreno Senate PDF located but not fetched; status from law-firm summaries]
- Halting International Relocation of Employment Act, Sen. Bernie Moreno (R-OH), introduced Sep 2025 (S.2976, referred to Senate Finance): **25% excise tax** (new §5000E) on payments by US persons to foreign persons for services benefitting US consumers + **denial of deduction** for such payments; proceeds to a Domestic Workforce Fund. Unanimous-consent attempt blocked 17 Sep 2025. As of Feb 2026: stalled in committee, no new co-sponsors (BDO/Withum/Cullen LLP summaries; search). Would hit vendors **and GCCs** alike (captive payments to Indian subsidiaries). Effective cost of a $100 offshore invoice to a US client: ~$125 + lost deduction vs ~$79 after-tax today (one law-firm calc, +58%). Tail risk, low probability, high impact.

# PART I — INDUSTRY ANALYSIS (§2 checklist)

## 1. What the sub-sector does, the value chain, and where value is captured

**Definition.** "IT || Computers - Software & Consulting" on NSE/BSE classification = companies that sell *labour-based technology services* to enterprises: application development & maintenance (ADM), infrastructure & cloud managed services, enterprise-package implementation (SAP, Oracle, Salesforce, ServiceNow), testing/QA, data & analytics, cybersecurity, digital engineering/ER&D, and adjacent business-process services. Revenue is overwhelmingly export (USA ~54–56%, Europe ~28–32%) and dollar-invoiced (USD 72.6% of India's software exports, RBI FY26 [V, 0.22]).

**Value chain (client dollar → Indian vendor):**
1. *Client technology budget* (CIO/CTO + business units). Split broadly into "run" (keep-the-lights-on: maintenance, support, infra ops — ~60–70% of enterprise IT spend [K, Gartner/Infosys commentary over the years]) and "change/build" (projects, transformation — the *discretionary* part).
2. *Strategy/consulting layer* (Accenture, Big-4, McKinsey/BCG) — shapes programmes; highest bill rates ($150–400/hr onsite [K]); Indian tier-1 capture little here.
3. *Platform/hyperscaler & software layer* (Microsoft, AWS, Google, Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow, now OpenAI/Anthropic) — captures licence/consumption dollars; vendors are resellers/partners. Gartner 2026: software $1.44 trn growing 15%, data-centre systems +56%, versus labour services growing low single digits ex-IaaS [V, 0.9/0.18].
4. *System integration & managed services layer* — **where the listed Indian companies sit**: global delivery model (GDM) with ~75–80% of effort offshore in India (Infosys onsite mix 22.8% Q4 FY26 [V]), long-tenure (3–7 yr) managed-services contracts, plus project work.
5. *Sub-contractors/staffing* — second-tier vendors, freelancers, local US/EU contractors (13–24% of revenue at many firms [V, 0.27]).
6. *Captives/GCCs* — the client's own India centre (2,117 GCCs, $98.4 bn, 2.36 mn people [V/S, 0.23]); both competitor and customer.

**Where value is captured.** The labour-arbitrage spread — billing an offshore engineer at ~$25–35/hr while paying ~₹8–20 lakh a year — produced 20–28% EBIT margins and 40–60% ROCE for 25 years (TCS ROCE 63%, Infosys 40%, HCL 30% [V, 0.12]). Value capture is now migrating (a) upward to platforms/LLM vendors (who sell "digital labour" directly) and (b) inward to GCCs (which keep strategic work in-house). The defensible pockets for vendors are: integration complexity (legacy + cloud + AI), accountability (outcome SLAs), regulated industries (BFSI, healthcare, public sector), engineering domain depth (ER&D, semis, automotive), and the ability to fund transitions (vendor-financed transformation deals).

## 3. Demand drivers (with numbers)

1. **Enterprise tech budgets & the AI capex crowd-out.** Gartner 2026 total IT spend $6.31 trn (+13.5%) but the growth is in data-centre systems (+55.8%) and software (+15.1%); labour-based services grow ~3–4% ex-IaaS (Kotak via Gartner: 3.1% 2025 → 4.2% 2026) [V, 0.9/0.18]. CIOs are funding GPU/LLM/software by squeezing run-budgets → the very pool Indian vendors live off.
2. **US discretionary spend.** North America is 54% of India's software exports (RBI FY26) [V]; HCLTech USA 56% of services revenue [V]. Discretionary project spend has been weak for "four years" (analyst on HCL call [V]); deal mix is "cost take-out, vendor consolidation and modernization" (HCL), "structural cost optimization" (Wipro) [V]. Accenture's consulting book-to-bill of 1.0 vs managed services 1.4 (Q4 FY26) [V] is the global confirmation: buyers sign multi-year run contracts, not discretionary projects.
3. **BFSI.** Largest vertical (~30–35% of tier-1 revenue [K]; HCL FS 22.1% [V]). BFSI is the 2026 bright spot: Cognizant FS +11.7% cc [V], TCS BFSI +2.5% QoQ cc in Q2 FY27 [V], Accenture FS +6% LC [V]; US banks also the most aggressive GCC builders (JPMorgan, Citi, BofA, Truist BOT [V]).
4. **Europe.** 28–33% of revenue; growing faster than US in FY25 (+14.3% vs +4.9%, RBI) [V] thanks to mega-deals (UK retail/telecom; TCS–M&S, TCS–Porsche/MHP) but weakening on autos/manufacturing and geopolitics in 2026 (HCL "impact already hurting the growth outlook in Europe" [V]).
5. **Vendor consolidation & cost take-out.** Clients consolidating 6–10 vendors into 2–3 creates large TCVs for winners (record bookings at TCS $40.7 bn FY26, Infosys $14.9 bn FY26 [V/S]) but net-zero for the industry and ~20% lower price points.
6. **AI services demand (offset).** TCS annualised AI revenue $1.8 bn (Q3 FY26) → >$3.1 bn (Q2 FY27, >10% of revenue) [V]; HCL Advanced AI $620 mn run-rate (Q4 FY26) [V]; Infosys AI-related revenue 8.2% of revenue (Q1 FY27) [V]; NASSCOM industry AI revenue $10–12 bn FY26 [V]. HCL sizes "AI-native" services at 5% of the market growing 30% [V].
7. **Currency.** ₹ depreciation from ₹84.8 (FY25 avg) to ₹89.6 (FY26) to ~₹95.5 (FY27 YTD) [V] mechanically adds ~6–7% to ₹ revenue in FY27 and ~150–250 bps of gross margin before reinvestment/pass-through [E].

## 4. Supply side, cost structure, capacity, utilisation

- **Capacity = people.** 5.95 mn direct employees (NASSCOM FY26) [V], of which ~2.36 mn in GCCs [S/V] and ~1.4–1.5 mn in the listed top-5 [E: TCS 598k, Infosys 328k, HCL 224k, Wipro ~230k [K], TechM ~150k [K]]. Annual engineering graduate supply ~1.5 mn [K]; fresher intake by top-5 has collapsed from ~200k+ (FY22) to <60k (FY25–26) [K/E].
- **Utilisation.** Infosys FY26 84.4% excl. trainees / 81.1% incl. [V]; tier-1 operate in an 82–88% band; pre-COVID 80–84%, FY22 peak 87–89% [K]. Higher utilisation with fewer fresher batches is how margins were defended in FY24–26 — but it leaves less bench for ramp-ups.
- **Pyramid.** Typical tier-1: ~45–55% of staff with 0–3 yrs experience, 25–30% 3–8 yrs, 15–20% senior [K]. Fresher hiring freeze (FY24–25) has *inverted* the pyramid ("diamond"), raising average cost per head; GenAI threatens the bottom of the pyramid (junior coding/testing) and thus the training pipeline.
- **Subcontracting** is 13–24% of revenue at most firms [V, 0.27]; HCL 14.8% (Q1 FY27, up from 13.6%) [V].
- **Wage inflation.** Offshore increments 4.5–7% (TCS Sep-25), Infosys/Wipro deferred; mid-tiers 9–11%; GCCs double-digit [S]. Each annual cycle costs ~150 bps of margin at TCS [S] before offsets. New Labour Codes added one-time ₹4,400+ cr charges across top-4 and ~20–40 bps recurring [V/E, 0.14].
- **Attrition.** Peaked Q1–Q2 FY23 (Infosys 28.4%, HCL 23.8%, Wipro 23.3%, TCS LTM 21.5%) [S]; now 12.6–13.9% (Infosys 13.0%, HCL 12.7%, TCS 13.3%, Wipro 13.9%, Cognizant 13.0%) [V/S] — normal-to-low.
- **Revenue per employee** (USD/yr): HCL $65.5K [V]; Infosys ≈ $61K (FY26 $20.2 bn ÷ ~326k avg) [E]; TCS ≈ $50K (₹2.67 lakh cr ÷ 89.6 ÷ ~596k) [E]; Accenture ≈ $95–100K [K]. Rising RPE with flat headcount is the signature of the "non-linear" (AI-assisted) model NASSCOM highlights (revenue +6.1% vs jobs +2.3%) [V].

## 9. Native KPIs — definitions and how to read them (summary; full glossary in §F)
- **Constant-currency (cc) growth** = current-period volumes × prior-period FX rates vs prior period (Wipro definition [V]). The only like-for-like growth measure; ₹ growth is distorted by the rupee (FY27: ₹ growth ≈ cc + 8–10 pts).
- **TCV / bookings / book-to-bill** — total contract value signed (new + renewal, or "net new" only — definitions differ: Infosys reports *large deals* ≥$50 mn; Wipro *large deals* ≥$30 mn and *total bookings*; HCL *net new*; TCS *total order book*). Book-to-bill = TCV ÷ trailing revenue.
- **EBIT margin bridge** — QoQ walk: currency, wage hikes, utilisation, pyramid, subcon, pricing/realisation, visas, acquisitions (amortisation), restructuring (Infosys Q4 FY26 walk [V, 0.26]).

# PART II — SYNTHESIS (items A–H)

## A. The defining forces (5)

### Force 1 — GenAI deflation of the "run" book: the volume-price equation is breaking
*Reasoning.* For 25 years Indian IT revenue ≈ headcount × utilisation × rate, with productivity gains shared slowly (renewals historically re-priced 10–15% over a deal's life ≈ 3–5%/yr [V, HCL call]) and offset by new scope. GenAI changes the slope: HCL sizes 40% of the market as "AI-disrupted" and shrinking 3–5% a year, which is **incremental** to the old baseline (CEO: "this is the incremental impact") and "very little has really played out in already reported numbers. We expect this to happen in FY'27 and onwards" [V]. Evidence is already visible in leading indicators: (i) a $100 mn scope now prices at ~$80 mn and needs 25–30% more sales effort [V]; (ii) Infosys FY26 grew on *price/realisation* while volumes were "flattish" [V]; (iii) NASSCOM jobs +2.3% vs revenue +6.1% [V]; (iv) HCL headcount −3,292 in Q1 FY27 while bookings were a record [V]; (v) RBI: ITES/BPO exports grew ~2% vs computer services ~11% in FY26 [V]. *Why it defines the sector:* it converts the industry from a volume-growth model into a "productivity-sharing" model in which revenue per unit of scope falls every year. The winners are those who (a) own the productivity (fixed-price/outcome contracts, platforms) and (b) refill the funnel with AI-native work (TCS AI run-rate >$3.1 bn; HCL Advanced AI +62% YoY [V]). Kotak's base case of 3.5% industry pricing erosion, surfacing at renewals [V], implies **tier-1 USD growth of 0–4% for FY27–28** unless AI-native work scales faster.

### Force 2 — The demand pool is being re-allocated from labour to capital (AI capex crowd-out) and the US discretionary cycle is in its fourth soft year
*Reasoning.* Gartner's 2026 forecast (+13.5% total IT spend) is entirely driven by data-centre systems (+55.8%) and software (+15.1%); services ex-IaaS grow ~3–4% [V]. CIO budgets are capped, so every GPU dollar is partly funded by squeezing run-services spend — exactly the managed-services pool Indian vendors dominate. Simultaneously US discretionary projects (the high-margin, short-cycle "change" work) have been weak since mid-2022; deal mix is cost take-out/vendor consolidation [V]. *Consequence:* growth has converged downward to global-peer levels (Indian tier-1 1–3% cc vs Accenture ~5%, Cognizant 4%) [V]; the sector's historical growth premium has disappeared, and valuations have reset (tier-1 P/E 13–19x; Nifty IT −31% from Nov-24 peak) [V]. Kotak: current prices discount only 3–4% perpetual USD growth [V] — a floor, not a catalyst.

### Force 3 — Protectionism + insourcing: H-1B $100k/weighted lottery, outsourcing-tax threats, and the GCC wave
*Reasoning.* The September 2025 proclamation and the weighted-lottery rule have, by the US government's own data, collapsed big IT outsourcers' H-1B registrations by 92% (24,946 → 2,055) and consular processing by ~97% [V]. Even though collection of the $100k fee is blocked by courts since Jul-2026 [V], the extension to Sep-2027 [V] and DOL wage rule [V] make the rotational-onsite model structurally dead. Direct revenue impact is modest because onsite (Mode 4) is only ~8% of exports [V] and onsite mix is already ~22% [V], but costs shift to US local hires and subcontractors (subcon ratios rising [V]), and ramp-up agility declines. In parallel, clients are bringing strategic work in-house through GCCs: 2,117 centres, $98.4 bn, 2.36 mn people, >100 new per year [V/S] — a captive sector now **as large as the 18 listed companies combined** [E]. The HIRE Act tail-risk (25% excise + non-deductibility) would hit vendors and GCCs alike [S]. *Net:* vendors' role shifts from staffing onsite + offshore delivery to BOT/advisory/managed services *for* GCCs (Infosys–Truist $500 mn BOT [V]) — a smaller, more transient revenue pool.

### Force 4 — Margins held up by the rupee, the pyramid and cost programmes — but the cushion is being spent
*Reasoning.* Despite weak growth, margins have been remarkably stable (TCS OPM 26–27% for a decade; Infosys 24% [V screener]). The levers: (i) ₹ depreciation from 74 (FY21) to ~95.5 (FY27 YTD) [V] — each 1% ≈ 20–30 bps EBIT [E/K]; (ii) utilisation pushed to ~84–85% ex-trainees [V]; (iii) fresher-hiring freeze/pyramid "diamond-isation" reversal; (iv) programmes like Infosys "Project Maximus" (+30 bps in Q4 FY26) [V]; (v) lower travel/visa (travel ~1% of revenue, HCL [V]). Offsets now intensifying: wage hikes (Wipro −130 bps in Q1 FY27 [V]), Labour Codes (one-time ₹4,400 cr+ and recurring 20–40 bps [V/E]), restructuring (HCL 55–122 bps/qtr [V]), acquisition amortisation (Infosys −50 bps [V]), AI investments and price pass-through. Managements explicitly say currency gains are being **reinvested** [V]. *Implication:* reported ₹ margins are flattered; in constant currency, the bridge is negative. If the rupee stabilises or appreciates, the deflation shows up in margins.

### Force 5 — Scale vs. agility: tier-1 vs mid-tier divergence, and M&A substituting for organic growth
*Reasoning.* Mid/small (13 cos) grew revenue at 17.7% CAGR FY20–26 vs tier-1 9.7% [V], and PAT 19.2% vs ~7.5% [V/E]; the market pays 29x vs 16x [V]. Persistent (+23.5% FY26 ₹), Coforge (+35.9%, with Cigniti), LTM (+11.3%), Mphasis (+11.6%), Hexaware (+12.2%) have outgrown TCS (+4.6%) and Wipro (+4.0%) [V]. Drivers: ability to win share in vendor consolidations (mid-tiers are the "challenger" in a 2-3-vendor consolidation), product-engineering/AI-native skew (Persistent), and acquisitions (Coforge–Encora $2.35 bn; LTM–Randstad unit; Indian IT M&A ~$5 bn in 2025, $2.1 bn 2026 YTD) [V]. But mid-tiers carry higher client concentration, subcon (20–24%) [V], and now leverage. Tier-1s respond with M&A too (TCS–Coastal Cloud $700 mn, TCS–MHP $373 mn [S]). *Implication:* industry structure is consolidating at the top (vendor consolidation) and polarising — scale players defend margins and cash returns; agile mid-tiers capture growth at richer multiples; small undifferentiated firms (P/E 12–14x: Zensar, Birlasoft, Sonata, Mastek [V]) are squeezed.

## B. Deep-dive themes

### B1. GenAI deflation: how big, where, and when
**Mechanics.** Three channels: (1) *Renewal re-pricing* of managed-services contracts (productivity commitments now 3–5% p.a. on top of historical; clients "embedding productivity concessions into renewals" — Kotak [V]); (2) *New-deal sizing* — scope priced 20% lower ($100 mn → $80 mn) [V]; (3) *Volume substitution* in T&M work (coding/testing/L1 support) and BPM (customer support). Offsets: (a) AI-native services (AI factories, data engineering, agent build/ops, AI governance) growing 25–30% [V]; (b) legacy modernisation unlocked by AI economics ("60% lower" cost makes previously uneconomic migrations viable [V]) — a Jevons-type effect; (c) vendor consolidation share gains.
**Sizing [E].** Using HCL's split: 0.40 × (−4%) + 0.55 × (+10%) + 0.05 × (+30%) = −1.6 + 5.5 + 1.5 = **+5.4% market growth** *if* the amplified segment really grows 10%. But Indian tier-1 portfolios are over-weight the disrupted bucket (~50–60% of revenue in ADM/infra/BPO [E]) and under-weight AI-native: 0.55 × (−4%) + 0.40 × (+8%) + 0.05 × (+30%) = −2.2 + 3.2 + 1.5 = **+2.5%** — consistent with FY27 guidance of 1–4% cc (HCL 1–4%, Infosys 1.5–3.0%) [V].
**Timing.** Little visible in FY26 reported numbers (HCL CEO) [V]; FY27–FY28 is when FY21–22 mega-deals renew. Pricing pressure so far is masked by the rupee (Kotak) [V].
**Evidence to track:** revenue per employee rising while headcount flat (HCL RPE +3.3% YoY [V]); fixed-price share; renewal TCV vs original; AI revenue share (TCS >10%, Infosys 8.2% [V]); NASSCOM non-linearity (jobs/revenue growth ratio 0.38 in FY26 [V/E]).
**Precedent.** The 2014–2019 cloud/SaaS transition (legacy infra/ADM cannibalised, "digital" grew 25–30%) produced 6–10% industry growth, not a collapse — the AI transition looks faster and deeper because it substitutes labour directly rather than shifting it.

### B2. Visas, protectionism and GCCs — the end of the onsite-offshore arbitrage as we knew it
**Facts.** Proclamation 10973 ($100k, from 21 Sep 2025) → extended to 21 Sep 2027 → collection blocked by courts (D. Mass. 8 Jun 2026; First Circuit stay denied 24 Jul 2026) [V]. Only "over 700" payments ever made [V]. FY27 cap season: top IT outsourcers' registrations −92%; master's-degree registrants 45.1% → 66.1%; top-two wage levels 46.3% of selections vs lowest 17.8% [V]. DOL prevailing-wage NPRM (Mar 2026) pending [V].
**What it means for vendors.** (i) Onsite headcount must be sourced locally (US hires + subcontractors) at 1.5–2x the cost of an Indian H-1B; (ii) more work pushed offshore (Infosys onsite mix 23.1% → 22.8% [V]; Mode-4 exports only $18.4 bn of $221 bn [V]) — positive for margins, negative for revenue (lower onsite billing; Infosys: lower onsite mix cut FY26 growth ~70 bps [V]); (iii) client preference for nearshore + GCC models rises.
**GCCs as competitor.** 2,117 GCCs; $98.4 bn; +100 new in FY26; North American firms two-thirds of new set-ups; even AI labs (Anthropic) setting up [V]. GCC pay premium 20–40% drains vendor mid-level talent [S].
**GCCs as customer.** BOT (Infosys–Truist >$500 mn, 5-yr then transfer [V]), set-up advisory, managed services for sub-scale GCCs, staff augmentation; Everest sizes the vendor opportunity at >$25 bn growing ~25% [S].
**Policy tail risks.** HIRE Act (25% excise) [S]; state offshoring restrictions; L-1 tightening; a future reinstatement of the $100k fee if the First Circuit reverses.

## C. The revenue/profit equation with a worked ₹ example

**Equation.**
Revenue = Σ_segments [Billable FTEs × billable hours × realised rate] + fixed-price/managed-services revenue (≈ effort × price, with productivity retained by vendor until renewal) + pass-through (third-party software/hardware).
Billable FTEs = Total headcount × utilisation (excl. trainees × trainee share).
EBIT = Revenue − (employee cost + subcon + travel + facilities + SG&A + D&A).
Key ratios: EBIT margin ≈ 1 − (avg cost per head ÷ RPE) − non-people costs.

**Worked example — a 10,000-person delivery unit (₹, FY27 rates) [E]:**
Assumptions: utilisation 85% → 8,500 billable; onsite 22% (1,870 billable), offshore 78% (6,630 billable); 1,800 billable hours/yr; blended rates offshore $28/hr, onsite $95/hr; ₹95/$.
- Offshore revenue = 6,630 × 1,800 × $28 = $334 mn; onsite = 1,870 × 1,800 × $95 = $320 mn → **$654 mn = ₹6,213 cr** (RPE $65.4K — matches HCL's $65.5K [V]).
- Employee cost: offshore 7,800 people × ₹12.0 lakh avg CTC = ₹936 cr; onsite 2,200 × $110K × ₹95 = ₹2,299 cr → **₹3,235 cr (52.1%)**.
- Subcontractors 12% = ₹746 cr; travel/visa 1.5% = ₹93 cr; facilities/IT/communication 3% = ₹186 cr; S&M/G&A/other 4% = ₹249 cr; D&A 3% = ₹186 cr.
- **EBIT = ₹6,213 − 4,695 = ₹1,518 cr = 24.4%** (TCS-like). PAT ≈ (1,518 + other income 1.5% ₹93 cr) × 0.75 ≈ **₹1,208 cr (19.4%)**.
**Sensitivities (each vs base):**
| Shock | Δ Revenue | Δ EBIT | Δ EBIT margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| ₹ depreciates 1% (₹95 → ₹95.95) | +₹62 cr | +₹62 − ₹30 (USD costs: onsite pay + ~half of subcon) = +₹32 cr | **+~30 bps** |
| Utilisation +1 pt (85 → 86%) | +₹73 cr | +₹73 cr (no extra cost) | **+~90 bps** |
| Offshore wage hike 7% (onsite 3%) | — | −₹66 − ₹69 = −₹135 cr | **−~220 bps** (before pass-through/pyramid) |
| AI deflation: 3% price cut on 50% of revenue, no headcount change | −₹93 cr | −₹93 cr | **−~110 bps** |
| Same deflation with matching 3% fewer people on that work | −₹93 cr | −₹93 + ₹49 (lower people cost) ≈ −₹44 cr | **−~40 bps** (Kotak: margin-neutral only if delivery-cost cut matches) |
| Onsite mix 22% → 20% (H-1B squeeze, work moved offshore) | −₹190 cr | −₹190 + ₹209 (onsite pay) − ₹30 (extra offshore staff) ≈ −₹11 cr | **+~60 bps on lower revenue** |
| Subcon +2 pts of revenue (local US contractors replacing H-1Bs) | — | −₹124 cr | **−200 bps** |
The example shows why the rupee, utilisation and onsite-offshore mix have kept margins steady while revenue growth faded, and why AI deflation hurts unless companies cut headcount on the affected work, which in turn weakens the jobs story.
