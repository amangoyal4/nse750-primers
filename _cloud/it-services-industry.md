### 0.10 H-1B: $100,000 payment proclamation, extension, weighted lottery, litigation [V]
Primary sources fetched:
- White House, Proclamation 10973 "Restriction on Entry of Certain Nonimmigrant Workers", 19 Sep 2025. https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/09/restriction-on-entry-of-certain-nonimmigrant-workers/
- Federal Register public inspection 2026-19554, Proclamation extending the restriction (signed Sep 2026; publication 23 Sep 2026). https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-19554.pdf
- Litigation status: immihelp (2 Sep 2026) https://www.immihelp.com/h1b-100000-fee-first-circuit-ruling-2026/ and Univ. of Colorado ISSS (7 Oct 2026) https://www.colorado.edu/isss/2026/10/07/100000-h-1b-fee-current-status

Facts:
- **2025 Proclamation**: entry of H-1B workers restricted unless petition "accompanied or supplemented by a payment of **$100,000**"; effective **12:01 a.m. EDT 21 Sep 2025**; 12-month duration absent extension; applies to petitions for beneficiaries **outside the US** (consular), not to existing visa holders, extensions or in-country change of status; national-interest exemptions; directs DOL to raise prevailing wage levels and DHS to prioritise high-paid workers.
- Proclamation's own claims: IT share of H-1B approvals grew from **32% (FY2003) to >65%** (5-yr avg); cites a 36% wage discount for "entry-level" H-1B positions.
- **DHS weighted-selection final rule** published **29 Dec 2025** (90 FR 60864), in effect for the FY2027 cap season (registration 4–19 Mar 2026): Level IV wage = 4 entries, III = 3, II = 2, I = 1 (Miller Canfield summary, search [S]).
- **DOL NPRM** on raising prevailing wages: **27 Mar 2026** (91 FR 15454) — not yet final.
- **Extension proclamation (Sep 2026)**: extends to **21 Sep 2027**. Key data disclosed by the US government itself:
  - $100,000 payment made for **"over 700 petitions"** since 21 Sep 2025 — i.e., the fee has functioned as a near-prohibition, not a revenue tool.
  - **"The largest IT staffing and outsourcing firms reduc[ed] their combined H-1B registrations from 24,946 to 2,055, a 92 percent decrease."**
  - Consular processing requests down **~97%** from FY2025 to FY2027 cap seasons.
  - Registrants with ≥US master's: **45.1% (FY26) → 66.1% (FY27)**; top-two wage levels = **46.3%** of FY27 selections; lowest level only **17.8%**.
- **Litigation**: Chamber of Commerce v. DHS (D.D.C.) — fee upheld, appealed. **8 Jun 2026**: D. Mass. vacated the policy (unauthorised tax / no notice-and-comment); briefly stayed pending appeal (fee collected for several weeks). **24 Jul 2026**: First Circuit denied government's stay → USCIS stopped collecting. Separate State of California v. Mullin (20 states). As of 7 Oct 2026 the payment is **"currently blocked by the court order"**; extension "has no practical effect" while the injunction stands; merits appeal pending. [S] A separate DHS proposal for a ~$103,265 fee on certain cap petitions is not in effect.
- **Industry read**: Indian vendors had already cut H-1B dependence (local hiring, offshore shift since 2017) — management commentary through FY26 said the fee was "not material" because new filings were small. The real cost is (i) onsite delivery flexibility for new ramp-ups, (ii) a shift to more expensive local hires/subcontractors in the US (raising onsite cost), (iii) accelerating clients' preference for offshore/GCC models. The 92% registration collapse means the vendors' US pipeline of rotational Indian staff has effectively closed for FY27 hires.

### 0.11 US "HIRE Act" (outsourcing excise tax) [S — Moreno Senate PDF located but not fetched; status from law-firm summaries]
- Halting International Relocation of Employment Act, Sen. Bernie Moreno (R-OH), introduced Sep 2025 (S.2976, referred to Senate Finance): **25% excise tax** (new §5000E) on payments by US persons to foreign persons for services benefitting US consumers + **denial of deduction** for such payments; proceeds to a Domestic Workforce Fund. Unanimous-consent attempt blocked 17 Sep 2025. As of Feb 2026: stalled in committee, no new co-sponsors (BDO/Withum/Cullen LLP summaries; search). Would hit vendors **and GCCs** alike (captive payments to Indian subsidiaries). Effective cost of a $100 offshore invoice to a US client: ~$125 + lost deduction vs ~$79 after-tax today (one law-firm calc, +58%). Tail risk, low probability, high impact.
