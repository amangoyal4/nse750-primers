## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Cupid's market cap (₹43,641 Cr) is three times Poly Medicure's (₹14,692 Cr) on one-fifth of the revenue.** Any "sector" valuation aggregate is therefore a Cupid number.
   - Cupid trades at **P/E 318× TTM**, **P/B 97×** and **~96× TTM sales** [V screener 1-Nov-26; E].
   - The 52-week range is **₹46 – ₹370**: a ~7× move in a year, after a **4:1 bonus** in FY26 [V screener; CU-Q4PPT].
   - On 15-May-2026 the stock was ₹121.23 and the m-cap ₹16,301 Cr [V CU-Q4PPT]. It has risen **~2.7× since the FY26 results**.
   - Shareholders rose from 2.10 lakh (Mar-26) to **4.70 lakh (Sep-26)** [V screener]. That is a retail-driven re-rating.
   - Show every aggregate both **with and without Cupid**.
2. **Cupid's FY26 revenue is ₹357.7 Cr, not ₹379 Cr.**

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Two members, two different businesses, both export-led "single-use" manufacturers:**
  - **Poly Medicure** is India's largest listed maker of hospital disposables. It calls itself the "2nd largest medical devices player in India | largest exporter of medical consumables" and the "3rd largest IV cannula manufacturer globally" [V PM-Q1PPT].
    - FY26: revenue **₹1,875 Cr** (+12.3%), Operating EBITDA ₹458 Cr (24.4%), PAT **₹321 Cr (−5.3%)** [V PM-Q4PPT; screener].
  - **Cupid** is a condom maker (male + female, WHO/UNFPA prequalified) turning itself into a branded personal-care company.
    - FY26: revenue **₹358 Cr** (+94.9%), EBITDA ₹117 Cr (32.6%), PAT **₹108 Cr (+165%)** [V CU-Q4PPT].
  - **Together (straight sums, screener consolidated):**
    - FY26 revenue **₹2,233 Cr** (+20.6% YoY), OP ₹565 Cr (25.3%), PAT ₹429 Cr.
    - TTM (to Jun-26) revenue ₹2,450 Cr, PAT ₹450 Cr.
    - **M-cap ₹58,333 Cr (30-Oct-2026 close), of which Cupid is 74.8%** [E: sums of screener].
- **India imports most of its medical devices, and that is the policy story.**
  - FY25: exports **US$4.1 bn**, imports **~US$8.6 bn**. **70–80% of domestic demand** is met by imports, mostly advanced/electronic devices [V Rubix Industry Insights via ETV Bharat, Feb-2026].
  - AiMeD puts dependence at 80–85% [V-snip Jun-2026]. It cited total imports of **~₹76,000 Cr**, of which **₹48,000 Cr was medical electronics** [V-snip Feb-2026].
  - FY26 (one report): **exports ₹28,106 Cr vs imports ₹94,664 Cr** [V-snip indiaseatradenews, May-2026; basis unclear, FLAG].
  - **Consumables are India's export strength:** ~47% of device exports (H1 FY25). **Electro-medical equipment is ~60% of imports** [V Rubix].
  - Poly Medicure sits on the strong side of that split: low/mid-tech disposables, exported.
- **China is both the import threat and the export opportunity.**
  - Imports from China rose from **$622 mn (FY20) to $1.3 bn (FY24)** [V-snip]. AiMeD flags Chinese goods routed through Hong Kong, Malaysia and Singapore [V-snip Angel One].
  - Poly Medicure's renal business shrank 3.8% in Q1 FY27 on "continued pricing pressure from Chinese players" importing duty-free under the ASEAN FTA. **At Polymed's request, the government has opened an anti-dumping investigation into dialyzers from China and Malaysia** (claimed injury margin 20%) [V PM-Q1PPT, PM-Q1call].
  - Abroad, the US has put **100% Section 301 tariffs on Chinese syringes and needles (from 27-Sep-2024)** and **100% on Chinese rubber medical/surgical gloves (from 1-Jan-2026)** [V-snip law-firm summaries of the USTR final action]. That is China+1 demand for Indian disposables.
  - But **Polymed's US sales are only ~US$3.5–4 mn**. India now pays a 10% Sec 301 duty on top of MFN [V PM-Q1call; macro V-sib].
- **Regulation is the moat, and it cuts both ways.**
  - Polymed holds 399 patents. Its device registrations cover 100+ countries, with CE/FDA/CFDA/ANVISA certificates at subsidiaries [V PM-Q1PPT, PM-Q4PPT].
  - Cupid is "the first company in the world to receive WHO/UNFPA prequalification for both male and female condoms" [V CU-PR 30-Sep].
  - Constraints: India's Medical Device Rules 2017 have licensed every class (A–D) since 1-Oct-2023 [K]. EU MDR transition deadlines are **31-Dec-2027** (Class III and IIb implantables) and **31-Dec-2028** (other classes) [K, Reg (EU) 2023/607]. These deadlines matter for Citieffe's trauma implants and PendraCare's catheters.
- **Price controls touch the edges of this sub-sector, not its core.**
  - NPPA ceiling prices apply to **coronary stents** (BMS ₹10,692.69; DES ₹38,933.14, ex-GST) and **knee implants** (trade-margin cap since Aug-2017, extended to **15-Nov-2026**) [V Medical Dialogues, Apr-2026].
  - Every other device faces the DPCO rule of **≤10% MRP increase a year** [V].
  - Polymed's **DES (launched in India in 2025)** is directly under the stent ceiling [V PM-Q1PPT]. Its cannulae, infusion sets and dialysis lines are under the 10% rule only.
  - **Cupid's condoms are "drugs" under the Drugs & Cosmetics Act** [K]. Its retail prices are set by the market, not NPPA.
- **Condoms are a donor-funded market in shock.**
  - **USAID's 2025 cuts** removed a large part of global public-sector condom procurement.
  - Karex (Malaysia, the world's largest condom maker, >5 bn pcs/yr) saw **FY6/2026 revenue fall 7.2% to RM462.4 mn** (~₹1,045 Cr), "mainly in the tender market". It made a net profit of only **RM0.29 mn** [V The Star, 27-Aug-2026].
  - Yet Karex says commercial demand rose ~30% and stockpiles are depleted. It raised prices **20–30%** in Apr-2026 [V The Star, 22-Apr-2026].
  - **Cupid's FY26 doubling therefore came against a shrinking donor market.** It rested on the South Africa national tender (₹115 Cr/yr; 59% of female-condom volume), PFSCM/Global Fund/UNFPA orders and the domestic FMCG push [V CU-SA PR; CU-Q4PPT].
- **Valuations price two very different things** (screener 1-Nov-26):
  - **Poly Medicure:** **45.9× P/E**, 4.7× book, EV/EBITDA ~31× FY26 [E].
    - That is 6.3× FY27-guided sales (₹2,300–2,400 Cr) [E].
    - The stock is 31% below its 52-week high (₹2,094). The market is waiting for ROCE to recover.
  - **Cupid:** **318× P/E**, 97× book, EV/EBITDA ~365× FY26 / ~270× TTM [E].
    - Even on the raised FY27 guidance (₹250 Cr PAT) it is **~175× forward P/E**.
    - On ₹800 Cr FY27 revenue it is **~55× forward sales** [E].

### 1.2 Value chains (inputs → customer)

1. **Plastic single-use hospital disposables (Poly Medicure model)**
   - **Inputs:**
     - Medical-grade polymers: PVC (plasticised, DEHP or non-DEHP), polypropylene, polycarbonate, ABS, polyurethane/FEP/PTFE for catheter tubing, silicone, latex-free elastomers.
     - Stainless-steel cannula tubing for needles.
     - Packaging (medical paper, blister film).
     - Sterilisation (EtO gas, gamma).
   - **Process:** extrusion (tubing) → injection moulding (hubs, connectors, chambers) → needle grinding/siliconisation → automated assembly in ISO 7/8 clean rooms → packaging → EtO sterilisation and aeration → batch release.
   - **Polymed's integration:** "significant investment in backward integration, automation and sterilization" [V PM-Q1PPT].
   - **Scale:** **~1.9 bn units a year** across **15 plants in 5 countries** (FY26), vs ~1.0 bn units across 8 plants in FY20 [V PM-Q1PPT]. Indian sites: Faridabad (5), Haridwar (2), Jaipur SEZ (2). Abroad: Italy (Amaro, Bologna), Netherlands (Leek), Egypt (Assiut), China (Laiyang-Qingdao) [V PM-Q4PPT].
   - **Route to customer (exports):**
     - Distributors (1,000+ globally).
     - OEM/private-label for global majors (PendraCare supplies "2 large OEMs"; P1H is "sole approved supplier for one of the largest OEMs for Class III implants").
     - Direct sales in India, US, Italy and Mexico (590+ reps) [V PM-Q1PPT].
   - **Route to customer (India):** hospital chains, government tenders (GeM, state medical corporations such as TNMSC/RMSCL/BMSICL), distributors, and the PMNDP dialysis PPP operators [K].
2. **Implants and interventional devices (Citieffe / PendraCare / DES)**
   - Titanium/stainless bar stock and polymers → CNC machining / catheter braiding and coating → clinical evidence (Polymed's **PACIFIER DES registry**, 2,000 patients, 840+ enrolled by Q1 FY27) → CE/FDA → hospital tenders and surgeon (KOL) relationships [V PM-Q1PPT].
   - The value chain is longer and more evidence-driven. Margins are higher; volumes are lower.
3. **Latex dipping (Cupid model)**
   - **Inputs:**
     - Centrifuged **natural rubber latex** (60% DRC), compounded with sulphur, accelerators (ZDEC/ZDBC), zinc oxide and stabilisers.
     - Silicone oil (lubrication), aluminium-foil laminate (wrapper) and cartons. Nitrile/synthetic latex for the new female condom.
   - **Process:** compounding → maturation → **dipping on glass formers** (double dip) → beading → curing/vulcanisation → leaching → stripping → **100% electronic pinhole testing** → batch tests (burst volume/pressure, freedom from holes, package seal) to ISO 4074 / WHO-UNFPA specification → foiling, packing.
   - **Route to customer:**
     - Institutional: UNFPA long-term agreements, PFSCM (Global Fund's procurement agent), national tenders such as South Africa's RT75-2025, NGOs (MSI, PSI), IDA Foundation [V CU-Q4PPT].
     - OEM: private label for brands.
     - Domestic retail (Cupid brand), plus the new FMCG range through Baazar Style's 260+ stores [V CU-Q4PPT].
4. **IVD rapid tests (Cupid's diversification)**
   - Antigens/antibodies (largely imported), nitrocellulose membranes, plastic cassettes → lateral-flow assembly → CE/IVDR, WHO PQ for tender access.
   - Cupid received **CE IVDR** for HIV 1&2 antibody and HBsAg tests, and CE for syphilis and pregnancy tests, in FY26 [V CU-Q4PPT].

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **The regulatory file and clinical trust, not the plastic.** Polymed's gross margin is **68.2%** (FY26 consolidated). It rose to **73.4%** in Q1 FY27 after Citieffe/PendraCare were consolidated [V PM-Q1PPT]. A two-rupee cannula is mostly paid for its approval stack, sterility assurance and its patents (399).
- **Within disposables, value rises with complexity.**
  - Commodity: basic IV sets, urine bags, Indian-tender syringes, priced near Chinese levels.
  - Mid-tier: safety cannulae, needle-free connectors, closed systems.
  - Premium: PICC/ports, dialyzers, DES, trauma implants.
  - Citieffe's US prices are "**4–5× Europe**" for trauma implants [V PM-Q1PPT]. That explains the push into ortho and the US.
- **In condoms, value sits in prequalification and tender allocation, not in volume.**
  - Karex makes >5 bn pcs a year and earned RM0.29 mn in FY26 [V The Star].
  - Cupid made ~₹108 Cr of PAT on roughly 0.4–0.5 bn pieces [E]. The difference is female-condom scarcity, the South Africa allocation, mix and a weak rupee.
- **Distribution is the next layer of capture.** Both members are moving "go-direct":
  - Polymed: Brazil (Medyneo), US (subsidiary + GPO contracts), Italy and Mexico (Citieffe direct).
  - Cupid: domestic retail through Baazar Style and 1.5 lakh outlets.
- **Global majors keep the hospital relationship.** BD, B. Braun and Terumo own the hospital contract (GPO) relationships in the US and EU. Indian makers often enter as OEM/private-label suppliers, capturing the manufacturing margin but not the brand margin [K].

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Procedure volumes (Polymed).** Each IV therapy uses a cannula plus a set; each dialysis session uses a fistula-needle pair, bloodlines and a dialyzer (if not reused).
   - India: Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY insurance coverage and hospital-bed additions by listed chains (see the hospitals dossier) push procedure counts up.
   - The MD: "as insurance penetrates deeper into the hospital sector, Ayushman Bharat … hospitals will look at more quality-oriented" products [V PM-Q1call].
2. **Dialysis (renal).** The **PM National Dialysis Programme** (launched 2016) runs **1,856 haemodialysis centres with 13,535 machines across 751 districts** (30-Jun-2026) [V-snip Deccan Chronicle, parliamentary reply]. That compares with 1,704 centres in Jun-2025 [V-snip].
   - Earlier replies cite ~28.5 lakh patients served and ~355 lakh sessions cumulatively [V-snip].
   - Polymed has **~1,100 dialysis machines installed** (Jul-2026). It sold **450 machines in FY26** [V PM-Q1PPT, PM-Q4PPT]. Each machine pulls through consumables.
   - Pricing pressure: Chinese dialyzers enter at zero duty via the ASEAN FTA. An anti-dumping case has been initiated [V PM-Q1call].
3. **Export replacement of China (China+1).**
   - US Section 301 at 100% on Chinese syringes/needles (2024) and gloves (2026) [V-snip].
   - EU buyers are diversifying suppliers after COVID.
   - Polymed's Europe revenue grew **+17.6% organically in Q1 FY27** after new-customer wins [V PM-Q1call].
4. **Up-trading in India.**
   - Safety cannulae: a needlestick-prevention push; India has no legal mandate (unlike the US Needlestick Safety Act, 2000) [K].
   - Closed systems, PICC.
   - "Most of the newer products we are developing today are import substitution products" [V PM-Q1call].
   - Domestic revenue grew **19.6% in FY26** and **16.2% in Q1 FY27**; standalone domestic is guided at **>20%** for FY27 [V].
5. **Cardiology and orthopaedics (new TAM).**
   - India's DES market sits under NPPA ceiling prices. Polymed has deployed ~13,000 stents cumulatively (Jul-2026) [V PM-Q1PPT].
   - Ortho: Citieffe ranks #2 in Italy and Mexico (~12% share), and "Plates" are to be added to "double the TAM" [V PM-Q1PPT].
6. **Condoms — public sector.**
   - Donor funding: USAID (dismantled in 2025), Global Fund (replenishment shortfall), UNFPA (lost US funding in 2025) [K]. The 2026 Karex comment: "global condom stockpiles have fallen after deep foreign aid cuts" [V The Star].
   - **National self-procurement** (South Africa's RT75, Indian NACO/state tenders) partially substitutes for donor buying [V CU-SA PR; K].
7. **Condoms — commercial.**
   - Karex: commercial demand "resilient", +30% in 2026 [V The Star].
   - India retail: ~$300–400 mn, growing ~8% a year [V-snip].
   - Cupid's own brand is small. Its FMCG growth is mostly non-condom products [V CU-Q4PPT].
8. **IVD.** Programme-driven (HIV, hepatitis B, syphilis screening), plus retail pregnancy tests. CE IVDR opens EU tenders [V CU-Q4PPT].

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 India device regulation (CDSCO)
- **Medical Devices Rules, 2017** (effective 1-Jan-2018). Risk classes A (low) → D (high) [K].
  - **All medical devices were notified as "drugs" from 1-Apr-2020.** Registration was phased in.
  - **Licensing became mandatory for Class A & B from 1-Oct-2022 and for Class C & D from 1-Oct-2023** [K]. Since Oct-2023 every device sold in India needs a CDSCO manufacturing (MD-5/MD-9) or import (MD-15) licence. That raised the cost of compliance for MSMEs and cut the long tail of unlicensed importers [K].
- **Condoms** are long-regulated under the Drugs & Cosmetics Act (Schedule R; BIS IS 6200 / ISO 4074) [K].
- **IVD kits:** licensed under MDR 2017 as IVD medical devices [K].
- **Quality Control Orders / BIS** for certain devices; and the **Uniform Code for Marketing Practices in Medical Devices (UCMPMD) 2024** (marketing ethics) [K].

### 7.2 Price controls (NPPA, DPCO 2013)
- **Coronary stents (Feb-2017, Schedule I via NLEM):** ceiling **BMS ₹10,692.69; DES ₹38,933.14** (current, ex-GST). Revised annually by WPI; a revision notification was reported dated 27-Mar-2026 [V Medical Dialogues; V-snip compliance guide]. Hospitals must itemise stent brand/batch/cost on bills [V].
  - **Relevance:** Polymed's DES and PTCA balloons. Balloons are not under the ceiling (only the 10% rule) [K].
- **Orthopaedic knee implants (Aug-2017):** a trade-margin/ceiling cap, **extended to 15-Nov-2026 "or until further orders"**. Industry sought a 10% hike (NPPA was weighing it, Nov-2025). AdvaMed is opposed to the extension [V Medical Dialogues; V-snip Business Standard].
  - **Relevance:** Citieffe makes trauma and extremity implants (fixators, nails), not knee replacements. So **the knee cap does not directly apply**, but it signals the risk for ortho pricing in India [E].
  - **Watch 15-Nov-2026.**
- **Trade Margin Rationalisation (TMR) on devices:** In 2021 (COVID) NPPA capped trade margins on **pulse oximeters, BP monitors, nebulisers, digital thermometers and glucometers** (and oxygen concentrators) [V Medical Dialogues; K]. The famous **Feb-2019 TMR on "42 drugs"** was on **non-scheduled anti-cancer medicines, not devices**. **The queue's "42 devices 2019" is a conflation — correct it** [K].
- **All other devices (non-scheduled):** MRP increase capped at **10% in any 12 months** [V].
  - **Relevance:** this is the binding constraint on Polymed's Indian price hikes. Its 4–5% Q1 FY27 hike sits within it.
- **No "One Nation, One Price"** proposal is under consideration (Rajya Sabha reply, Apr-2026) [V].

### 7.3 Industrial policy and incentives
- **PLI Scheme for Medical Devices** (notified 2020): **outlay ₹3,420 Cr**. It covers (a) cancer care/radiotherapy, (b) radiology & imaging, (c) anaesthetics, cardio-respiratory & **renal care**, and (d) implants [V indianpharmapost, 28-Jul-2026].
  - **27 applications approved (14 MSMEs); 23 commissioned; actual investment ₹1,153.07 Cr; incentives released ₹266.64 Cr till FY25** (Lok Sabha written reply, 24-Jul-2026, Minister J.P. Nadda) [V].
  - An earlier reply (Jun-2025) said 31 approved and ₹133.95 Cr disbursed to 7 applicants [V-snip]. The approval count fell from 31 to 27 (withdrawals/cancellations, presumably).
  - Beneficiaries named: Wipro GE, Siemens Healthcare, Philips, **Nipro India (renal)**, Meril. **Neither member is a named PLI beneficiary** [V; E].
- **Medical Device Parks scheme:** four parks approved (Himachal Pradesh – Nalagarh; Madhya Pradesh – Ujjain; Tamil Nadu – Kancheepuram; Uttar Pradesh – YEIDA/Greater Noida), with up to **₹100 Cr grant each (₹400 Cr total)** for common infrastructure [K].
- **National Medical Devices Policy 2023** (Cabinet 26-Apr-2023): $50 bn market by 2030, 10–12% global share in 25 years, and six strategies (regulatory streamlining, infrastructure, R&D, investment, HR, brand positioning) [K].
- **Scheme for Strengthening the Medical Device Industry (Nov-2024): ₹500 Cr** over three years. Five sub-schemes: common facilities for clusters, marginal investment for components/raw materials, skilling, clinical studies support, and promotion [K]. Polymed's **₹3.3 Cr government grant for clinical studies** (Q1 FY27) is likely from this [V PM-Q1call; link E].
- **Export Promotion Council for Medical Devices (EPCMD)** under the Department of Commerce (2022) [K]. Further 2025–26 export-promotion schemes: **not verified** this session (FLAG).
- **Customs/trade remedies:**
  - Basic customs duty on most devices is 7.5–10% plus a health cess [K].
  - AiMeD lobbies for withdrawal of duty exemptions and for 7.5% on ASEAN-routed goods [V-snip].
  - **Anti-dumping investigation on dialyzers (China PR, Malaysia), initiated 2026 on Polymed's application** [V PM-Q1PPT].
- **Public procurement:** Make-in-India preference orders and GeM. State medical services corporations favour local suppliers (Class-I local content ≥50%) [K].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

- **1990s founding wave:** Cupid incorporated 1993 (Nashik), listed 1995. Poly Medicure founded 1995 (Faridabad), IPO 1996 for **₹55 lakh** [V PM-Q1PPT; primer].
- **2000s exports:** Polymed built CE-marked IV cannula exports: Egypt JV 2003, China plant 2009 [V PM-Q1PPT]. Cupid's female condom later obtained WHO/UNFPA PQ (primer: "early 2010s").
- **2016–2018: India's price-control shock.**
  - **Stent ceilings (Feb-2017) cut DES prices ~85%**; knee implants followed (Aug-2017) [K].
  - MNCs threatened to withdraw premium stents.
  - This established that India's state would cap device prices where it sees "profiteering".
- **2017–2023: regulation catches up.** MDR 2017 → all devices as drugs (2020) → full licensing (Oct-2023) [K].
- **2020–2021: COVID.** A demand spike for disposables, gloves, PPE and diagnostics; TMR on oximeters etc.; the PLI scheme launched.
  - Polymed raised **₹400 Cr QIP (2021)** [V PM-Q1PPT].
  - Cupid earned record COVID-era revenue (FY20 ₹160 Cr standalone), then fell to ₹133 Cr by FY22 [V screener].
- **2023: Cupid changes hands.** The Garg founders sold a ~41.8% block to the **Halwasiya group (Kolkata)** at ₹285/share (~₹159 Cr) [primer; PRNewswire cited in primer]. Aditya Kumar Halwasiya is now Chairman & MD [V CU-Q4PPT].
- **2024: Polymed's ₹1,000 Cr QIP (Aug-2024)** [V PM-Q1PPT]. Promoter holding went 53% → 66% (Jun-24) → 62.4% [V screener]. That pattern suggests promoter warrant conversion before the QIP dilution (FLAG: mechanism not verified). The US raised Sec 301 on Chinese syringes/needles to 100% (Sep-2024).
- **2025: two shocks for condoms and one acquisition spree.**
  - USAID cuts.
  - Polymed buys PendraCare (Sep-2025) and Citieffe (Nov-2025), then Medyneo (Q4 FY26). Polymed's FY26 PAT falls 5% as costs of acquisitions, plants and labour codes land [V].
  - Cupid wins the South Africa RT75 allocation (Nov-2025) and launches FMCG [V].
- **2026:**
  - West Asia war (Feb–Apr) → polymer and freight spike; Polymed's Middle East sales stall.
  - IEEPA struck down; India pays 10% Sec 301 from 24-Jul.
  - Cupid's stock re-rates from ~₹46 to ₹370 (52-week high), and Cupid joins BSE Group A, the Nifty Smallcap 250 and the FTSE EM All Cap [V CU-PR 30-Sep; screener].
  - Polymed's stock de-rates from ₹2,094 to ~₹1,450 [V screener].

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Safety-engineered and needle-free devices.** Mandates (US, EU Directive 2010/32/EU) shift demand from plain to safety cannulae. Polymed has patents here; laggards lose share [K; primer].
2. **DEHP-free PVC and PVC-free materials.** The EU MDR restricts CMR/endocrine-disrupting substances (DEHP >0.1% w/w requires justification). Re-formulating tubing and bags is a compliance cost and a moat for early movers [K].
3. **EtO sterilisation restrictions** (US EPA 2024 rule) could push sterilisation to gamma/e-beam, adding capex [K].
4. **Dialysis technology:** home haemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis and dialyzer reuse policy. India allows reuse, which lowers consumables per session [K].
5. **Drug-coated balloons and intravascular lithotripsy (IVL)** in cardiology. Polymed has a DEB (ex-vivo preclinical data obtained) and IVL "run on machine" [V PM-Q1PPT, PM-Q1call].
6. **Condoms:** synthetic (polyisoprene, nitrile, polyurethane) condoms are gaining over latex. Karex is "scaling up the synthetic condom business" [V The Star]. Cupid's dual-polymer Palava lines respond to this [V CU-Q4PPT].
   - **PrEP (oral and long-acting injectable lenacapavir, approved 2025)** may reduce condom procurement priority in HIV programmes [K]. A structural headwind for donor condom budgets.
7. **IVD:** a shift from rapid lateral-flow tests to molecular point-of-care and self-tests [K].
8. **AI/connected devices** are irrelevant for most disposables. They are relevant for Polymed's dialysis machines.

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Import dependence vs export strength: India imports the machines and exports the tubes.**
   - Imports are ~US$8.6 bn (FY25; ~₹76,000–95,000 Cr), 60% of them electro-medical. Exports are ~US$4.1 bn, ~47% of them consumables [V Rubix; V-snip AiMeD].
   - Poly Medicure is the listed embodiment of the export-strength side. Policy (PLI, parks, NMDP 2023, the ₹500 Cr strengthening scheme) targets the import side, and **neither member is a PLI beneficiary**.
   - The policy tailwind for these two is indirect: procurement preference, trade remedies (the dialyzer anti-dumping case) and clinical-study grants.
2. **China, both ways.**
   - Chinese dialyzers and disposables undercut Indian makers at home, through the ASEAN FTA at zero duty. Polymed's renal revenue fell 3.8% in Q1 FY27 [V].
   - The US 100% Sec 301 tariffs on Chinese syringes/needles (2024) and gloves (2026) open the US to Indian disposables [V-snip]. But India's own 10% Sec 301 (since 24-Jul-2026) and the tiny US base (Polymed ~$3.5–4 mn) mean the China+1 dividend is **still prospective** [V PM-Q1call].
   - The rupee at ₹94.9/$ is the more immediate tailwind.
3. **Regulation as moat and as cost.**
   - Polymed: CE/MDR, FDA 510(k), 399 patents. Cupid: WHO/UNFPA PQ and CE IVDR. These certificates are what turn commodity plastics/latex into 58–73% gross margins [V].
   - The same regimes impose MDR re-certification by 2027/28 and CDSCO licensing for all classes since Oct-2023. They also create single-point-of-failure risk: a failed audit or recall loses the market.
4. **Capital-allocation cycles, not demand cycles, drive the numbers.**
   - Polymed's FY26 PAT fell 5% in a +12% revenue year, and screener ROCE fell 24% → 12% over FY24–26.
   - Causes: ₹1,400 Cr of institutional equity raised, ₹308 Cr capex, ~€50 mn of acquisitions and new plants not yet earning [V].
   - Cupid's doubling came with ₹332 Cr committed to a retailer's equity, a South African JV and a US$5 mn fund investment [V].
   - **The question for both is the return on newly deployed capital.**
5. **The donor-funded condom market is shrinking while national and commercial demand rises.**
   - USAID cuts drove Karex's tender sales down; Karex earned ~0% net margin in FY6/26 [V].
   - Cupid grew by winning national allocations (South Africa 59% of FC) and pivoting to FMCG [V].
   - The supply side is consolidating ("global manufacturing capacity is being rationalised"), and prices rose 20–30% in 2026 [V The Star]. That favours prequalified survivors.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

- **Disposables maker:** Revenue = Σ (units sold × realisation per unit) across ~225 products. Units ≈ capacity (1.9 bn) × utilisation.
  - EBITDA = gross margin (≈ 1 − polymer/needle/packaging cost per unit ÷ price) − people − other opex (sterilisation, freight, selling, regulatory).
  - PAT = EBITDA + treasury income − D&A − interest − tax.
- **Condom maker:** Revenue = Σ tender allocations (pcs × $/pc × ₹/$) + OEM + retail/FMCG.
  - EBITDA = revenue − latex/nitrile/foil (~41% of sales) − people (~9%) − other (~17%).

### C.1 Worked example 1 — Poly Medicure FY26 consolidated, from units to PAT [E composite on V]
- Units: ~1.9 bn capacity. If revenue of ₹1,875 Cr is spread over ~1.5 bn units sold [E, ~80% utilisation, assumed], **average realisation ≈ ₹12.5 per unit**.
  - This is a blended figure. A basic IV set sells for ~₹10–20; a safety cannula ₹25–60; a PICC or DES ₹thousands [K; FLAG].
- Gross margin 68.2% → gross profit ≈ **₹8.5 per unit**; COGS ≈ ₹4.0 per unit.
- People ₹2.7 + other ₹2.6 + R&D ₹0.2 per unit → **Operating EBITDA ≈ ₹3.05 per unit (24.4%)**.
- + treasury ₹0.8 − D&A ₹0.77 − interest ₹0.12 − one-offs ₹0.11 → PBT ₹2.85 → **PAT ≈ ₹2.14 per unit (17.1%)**.
- Check: 1.5 bn × ₹2.14 = ₹321 Cr ✓.

### C.2 Worked example 2 — Poly Medicure FY27 guidance bridge [E on V]
- Standalone ₹1,950 Cr (mid) × 26% EBITDA ≈ ₹507 Cr.
- Consolidated ₹2,350 Cr (mid) × 24% ≈ **₹564 Cr EBITDA**, implying subsidiaries ≈ ₹400 Cr revenue at ~14% margin [E].
- D&A ~₹150 Cr (Q1 run-rate ₹36 Cr × 4); interest ~₹25 Cr; other income ~₹110 Cr (cash ₹855 Cr at ~7% + export incentives) → PBT ≈ ₹500 Cr; tax 26% → **PAT ≈ ₹370 Cr** (+15% vs FY26) [E].

- Q1 FY27 PAT was ₹85 Cr (run-rate ₹340 Cr), so the guidance needs H2 acceleration.

## 4.2 Company deep-dives

### 4.2.1 Poly Medicure (POLYMED · 531768)

**(a) Management-guidance digest — Q1 FY27 earnings call, 10-Aug-2026** (speakers: MD Himanshu Baid; President Strategy & Corporate Development Rahul Gautam). Paraphrased. [V PM-Q1call]

1. **"PolyMed 3.0 / Ascent": double revenue by FY30** (from the FY26 base of ₹1,875 Cr), i.e. ≈18% CAGR over four years.
   - The mix "would continue at a similar run rate" of **~80% organic / ~20% inorganic**. An analyst framed that as ~14–15% organic plus 3–4% from acquisitions, and the MD agreed it is "the trajectory".
   - The MD framed 1.0 → 2.0 → 3.0 as: bootstrapped to ~₹700 Cr; the 2021–26 institutional raise (revenue ×2.75 to ₹1,875 Cr); then "Ascent" via high-tech verticals and direct access.
2. **FY27 guidance maintained after Q1:**
   - Consolidated ₹2,300–2,400 Cr (including full-year PendraCare and Citieffe), EBITDA 23–25%.
   - Standalone ₹1,900–2,000 Cr (domestic >20%, international >15%), EBITDA 25–27%. Gautam expects to "close this year with upper end of that guidance range".
   - Capex ₹200–225 Cr.
   - Q1 is seasonally the weakest quarter. The price hike taken at the start of the year slowed early-quarter sales, and "we should be able to get to much better numbers in Q2 onwards".
3. **Margins.**
   - Q1 standalone gross margin of 71.5% was flattered by price hikes, mix and **inventory gains**. The guide is to "normalise" to **67–69% standalone and ~71–72% consolidated** (the acquired units run higher gross margins).
   - The EBITDA percentage should hold because revenue rises.
   - **Long-term margin stance: "keep the margins in the similar range"** while reinvesting for growth; not lower.
4. **Raw materials and pricing.** Price increases were taken in Q1 to pass through polymer costs. "As crude stays where it is, … we have almost seen that peak off."
5. **Middle East.** Demand is intact, but "ships are not calling"; product is piling up at ports. When normality returns, the MD expects ME growth of possibly ">20–25%". Logistics costs are up "2, 3 times in the last 6 months" and "will have to come down for India to remain competitive in exports".
6. **Renal.**
   - Q1 −3.8% on Chinese duty-free imports via the ASEAN FTA.
   - The anti-dumping probe (20% injury-margin claim) was raised directly with Health Minister J.P. Nadda; the MD expects action "between now and end of the year".
   - New renal head: Abhimanyu Hooda.
   - **FY27 renal growth guide 15–18%, "maybe close to 20%"**. That needs Q2–Q4 renal growth of roughly 21–25% after a −3.8% Q1 [E: FY26 renal ₹187 Cr, Q1 FY26 ₹44.9 Cr].
7. **Exports.**
   - Europe is back to the planned 17–18% organic growth on new customer wins; Southeast Asia is strong.
   - "~25 new products in pipeline for CE marking" within 3–4 months.
   - US exports only US$3.5–4 mn. On tariffs: "If we have 100% tariffs tomorrow or today night, I don't know."
8. **Acquisitions.**
   - Integration is on track. Moving parts of PendraCare and Citieffe production to India "is at the regulatory approval stage".
   - Cross-selling synergies are not expected before **FY28**, because CDSCO approval is needed to bring EU products into India.
   - PendraCare is "not growing; in fact … de-growth", owing to its ~20% Middle East exposure. Citieffe grows "mid- to high single digits".
   - The aspiration is mid-teens growth for both with synergies. No margin-expansion guidance was given.
9. **Capital deployment of ~₹855 Cr cash:** organic and inorganic; Brazil direct sales (Medyneo, "a very small acquisition"; board funds allocated); technologies in cardiology, oncology and orthopaedics; and the two plants under construction. Asset turns are expected to stay **1.2–1.4×**.
10. **Plants.** Faridabad–Palwal (orthopaedics, transfusion, extra infusion capacity) "by March '27"; Noida (cardiology) commercial in **Q1 FY28**.
11. **Debt.** The only standalone debt is a ~₹250 Cr revolving working-capital line. PendraCare has no long-term debt. Citieffe has a ~€9 mn loan, repaying €1.5–2 mn a year.
12. **Working capital.** "Cash conversion is still in the same level … March 140 days … currently similar range". There is no guidance on improvement, because hard-currency shortages in export markets and longer transit times delay collections.
13. **Portfolio.** "Our overdependence … in the vascular segment … is now only 50%". The company is adding electronics (dialysis machines, IVL "run on machine"). It is "not ready to launch anything new substantially in the next few years" outside the current therapies. Uncovered TAM includes peripheral vascular, neurology, urology, endosurgery and GI.
14. **Product claims.** 399 patents; "global patents"; "nothing works on price"; sold in 125 countries.

3. **The China+1 dividend shows up in syringes, not cannulae.** India's syringe exports to the US rose six-fold (US$2.05 mn → US$12.75 mn) in the first full year of the 100% US Sec 301 tariff on Chinese syringes. India's catheter/cannula exports (9018.39, Polymed's core heading) were **flat at US$512 mn**, with **UAE −20%** (West Asia war), matching Polymed's Middle East −32%.
4. **Polymed is a large share of its heading** [E]. Standalone international revenue of ~₹1,080 Cr in FY26 is ≈US$123 mn at ~₹88/$. Not all of it falls under 9018.39, but **Polymed is plausibly 15–25% of India's 9018.39 exports**. Its US exports (US$3.5–4 mn) are ~1% of India's US$316 mn of 9018 exports to the US.
5. **Dialyzer imports from the two subject countries were ~US$37 mn in FY26** (China + Malaysia; ~US$43 mn with Hong Kong). That is the market an anti-dumping duty would reprice. Malaysian volumes rose 37.5% and Hong Kong's 7×, consistent with AiMeD's "routing" complaint. Germany (Fresenius, B. Braun) is the largest source and is **not** covered by the case.

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed, and new primer issues

### 4.5.1 Insights (what an expert would add)

1. **Polymed's anti-dumping case protects a loss-making line, not the Renal segment's margin.**
   - DGTR's initiation records that the domestic dialyzer industry (Polymed, plus Nipro as a 100% EOU) "incurred financial losses, cash losses and negative return on capital employed" in CY2025.
   - Dialyzer imports from China and Malaysia are only ~US$37 mn a year. Germany, the largest source at US$46 mn, is outside the case.
   - So a duty helps Polymed's dialyzer volumes but is **not a group-level margin event**. Expect findings around mid-2027.
2. **The biggest related-party flow at Polymed is ₹87 Cr of job work given to Vitromed Healthcare**, an enterprise controlled by KMP and relatives. That is ~84% of all standalone job-work charges and ~5% of revenue. Add the ₹47 Cr of KMP pay (~14% of standalone PAT) and the family trust receiving ~half of CSR.
   - None of this is unusual for an Indian family-run mid-cap, but **Parts 1–3 described Polymed as governance-clean by omission.** The writer should present it neutrally in the governance box.
3. **Polymed's free float was smaller than it looked before Jun-2024.** A 12.88% holder classified as "public" (Ezekiel Global) turned out, after an NCLT-sanctioned amalgamation, to sit inside the promoter group. Today the public plus retail float is only ~16%. FIIs have fallen from 14.1% to 5.4% since Dec-23. That combination (thin float, falling FII ownership, rising DII) helps explain the stock's derating and volatility.
4. **About half of Polymed's ₹1,000 Cr QIP (₹484 Cr) was still parked in funds and deposits at Mar-26.** The 12% headline ROCE is a capital-deployment-timing story more than an operating story.
   - CRISIL (AA-/Positive) sets the bar: revenue >₹2,200 Cr at a 22–24% margin.
   - Watch Q2 FY27 for whether the parked cash moves into M&A. The MD named cardiology, oncology and orthopaedics technologies, plus Brazil.
5. **China+1 is visible in India's trade data, but in syringes, not in Polymed's cannulae.**
   - India's syringe exports to the US went ×6 (US$2 mn → US$12.8 mn) in FY26.
   - India's catheter/cannula exports (HS 9018.39) were flat at US$512 mn, with the UAE −20%.
   - Polymed sells IV cannulae, not commodity syringes. Its own US base is tiny, so it is **not the listed beneficiary of the syringe tariff**. HMD (unlisted) and BD India are.
6. **The condom export V-shape explains the Cupid–Karex divergence.**
   - India's condom exports fell 27% in FY25 (the USAID freeze) and rebounded 33% in FY26 to above the FY24 level, led by South Africa, Tanzania and Kenya (national/Global Fund buying).
   - USAID's FY2023 condom suppliers were Thai and two other Indian plants (Bangalore; Puducherry/Virudhunagar), plus the Malaysian FC2 plant for female condoms. **Cupid was not among them.**
   - So Cupid lost little in the freeze and gained as buyers diversified. Karex, a tender-heavy supplier, lost.
7. **Cupid's FY27 growth must come from price and non-dipping products until Palava runs.**
   - Management says the existing plant runs at "full capacity utilisation", and Palava has slipped from "Q2 FY27" to "imminent".
   - So the ~₹445 Cr needed in H2 FY27 for the ₹800 Cr guide relies on:
     - the ≥10% export price increase;
     - FMCG and personal-care products, whose manufacturing source is undisclosed (likely contract manufacturing);
     - the Baazar Style channel, its own investee.
   - That raises the **arm's-length / channel-stuffing question** for revenue booked through Baazar Style. The writer should ask it, not assert it.
8. **Cupid's capital outside the core (~₹440 Cr committed) now roughly equals its FY26 net worth (₹451 Cr).**
   - Baazar Style ₹331.5 Cr, of which ~₹194 Cr paid by mid-Oct.
   - GII Healthcare ₹60.8 Cr plus US$5 mn (~₹47 Cr).
   - The 2024 preferential issue earmarked ₹245 Cr for *foreign* acquisitions and JVs; Baazar is domestic and not a stated object.
   - Treasury income fell from ₹11–12 Cr a quarter to ₹2 Cr in Q1 FY27. **From FY27, Cupid's P&L loses its other-income cushion** (24% of FY26 PBT). Its balance sheet carries a listed-retailer equity stake whose mark-to-market (FVOCI or FVTPL) can swing reported equity.
