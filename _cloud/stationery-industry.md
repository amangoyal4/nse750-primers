## READ THIS FIRST: twelve definitional traps

1. **The Q2 FY27 print changes the story the primer tells.** The primer (18-Jul-2026) is built on FY26 (EBITDA 17.3%). Q1 FY27 and Q2 FY27 both printed **EBITDA ~12.3–12.4%** and **gross margin 38.2% / 39.1%** vs 43.8% a year earlier [V]. The cause is raw-material inflation linked to the **West Asia war** (management: average RM +20% in Q1; price hikes only 4–5%) [V Q1 call], plus ESOP cost (~₹10 Cr in FY27), new-plant pre-operating cost and Reynolds team costs before production [V]. Management calls it "a temporary blip rather than a structural deterioration" and targets "about 16-odd percent" EBITDA in **FY28** [V Q1 call]. The writer must present FY26 as the "normal" year and H1 FY27 as the shock, not average them silently.
2. **Consolidated ≠ stationery.** Since 16-Sep-2024 DOMS consolidates **Uniclan Healthcare (51.77%, baby diapers/wipes)**. FY26 segment revenue: Stationery ₹2,123.6 Cr, **Hygiene ₹203.1 Cr (8.7%)** [V AR note 41]. Segment operating profit (pre-D&A): Stationery **19.0%**, Hygiene **8.6%** [V]. So the consolidated 17.3% understates the stationery margin by ~1.7 pts. In Q2 FY27 the stationery segment earned **13.7%** (vs 19.6% in Q2 FY26) and hygiene 6.6% [V Q2 outcome]. Use the segment numbers when comparing with pure stationery peers.
3. **Revenue vs gross product sales (GPS).** All DOMS mix percentages (category, brand, channel, region, export) are on GPS (FY26 ₹2,420.6 Cr; Q2 FY27 ₹690.2 Cr), not on revenue (₹2,326.4 Cr; ₹667.8 Cr) [V]. Export share is therefore **12.5% of GPS** but **13.0% of revenue** by the statutory geography note (₹301.9 Cr outside India) [V AR]. Quote the basis.
4. **"Market share" has at least four bases.** Technopak (RHP, FY23): DOMS **~12% of the branded stationery & art market**, **~29% of the overall pencil market by value**, **~30% of mathematical instrument boxes** [V Technopak]. But Technopak's own numbers do not reconcile: branded market ₹13,850 Cr and DOMS revenue ₹1,232 Cr (₹974 Cr domestic) give **8.9% (7.0% domestic)**, not 12% [E arithmetic]. ITC's "20%" likewise exceeds ₹2,010 Cr / ₹13,850 Cr = 14.5%. The exhibit's shares appear to be computed on a smaller base (~₹10,000–10,300 Cr). Our primer's "roughly a third of the **branded** pencil market" is a paraphrase of the 29%-of-**all**-pencils figure. Use "~29% of India's pencil market by value (FY23, Technopak)" and "~12% of branded stationery per Technopak, ~7–9% on Technopak's own arithmetic".
5. **Two incompatible market-size series.** Technopak: **₹38,500 Cr (FY23, domestic, excluding exports)** → ₹71,600 Cr FY28P (~13% CAGR) [V]. DOMS's FY26 annual report instead cites Fortune Business Insights: "India stationery market projected to reach **USD 18.08 billion by 2026**" (≈ ₹1.6 lakh Cr) [V AR, footnote to fortunebusinessinsights.com]. The second is a desk-research global-report figure with a different scope; **do not mix them**. Use Technopak as the base series and flag the FBI number as an outlier.
6. **GST 2.0 made core school items nil-rated — which is not the same as zero cost.** From **22-Sep-2025**, pencils, crayons, pastels, drawing charcoal (9609), pencil sharpeners (8214), exercise books/notebooks/graph books (4820), maps/charts/globes moved **12% → nil**; erasers **5% → nil**; mathematical/geometry/colour boxes **12% → 5%** [V notification 9/2025-CT(Rate) Sch. I S.No. 410 for boxes; V-snip for the nil list, which sits in the exemption notification]. Pens (9608) stayed at **18%** [V-sib]. A nil-rated (exempt) supply **does not allow input-tax credit**: makers must reverse ITC on polymer, packaging, graphite and services used for those items [K: CGST Act s.17(2)]. So the cut lowers shelf prices but **raises the manufacturer's effective input cost** by the blocked ITC [E]. We did not find management quantifying this; flag as a gap (§H).
7. **FILA's stake has moved twice; the primer conflates them.** Post-IPO **30.58%** → **26.01%** (Dec-2024 block, ~₹798 Cr) → **19.01%** (17-Jun-2026 block, ~₹934.7 Cr) [V/V-snip]. Promoter group: 74.95% (Dec-23) → 70.38% (Dec-24) → **63.38% (Jun-26 and Sep-26)** [V screener]. Our primer dates the 19% trim to "2025–26"/"FY26"; it happened in **Q1 FY27 (June 2026)**.
8. **FILA "exports" ≠ all exports, and the FILA share is shrinking.** Channel mix of GPS: exports to FILA group **15.4% (FY22) → 12.9% → 10.0% → 8.4% → 7.3% (FY26)**; third-party exports 8.4% → 5.2% [V AR FY26 channel chart]. In Q2 FY27: FILA 7.4%, third-party 6.0% [V Q2 PPT]. Related-party sales to FILA entities in the standalone accounts were **~₹133 Cr in FY26 vs ~₹122 Cr in FY25**, of which Dixon Ticonderoga (US) ₹75.4 Cr [V AR note 43, sum E]. The GPS-based 7.3% (~₹177 Cr) and the RPT ₹133 Cr differ — likely GPS vs net and timing [E]; flag. Some exports also leave via **merchant exporters in Mumbai/Gujarat** and are booked as **domestic West-region sales** [V Q1 call]; "West" sales fell in Q1 FY27 for that reason.
9. **Net debt: three numbers.** Company FY26 net debt (total debt ex-leases − cash & bank) = **₹27.8 Cr** [V AR financial highlights: "2,781 lakh"]; FY25 was **net cash ₹72.3 Cr**. Screener "borrowings" ₹141 Cr (Mar-26) **includes ₹51.6 Cr lease liabilities** [V]. By **Sep-26**, borrowings ex-leases were **₹207.5 Cr** (NC ₹97.5 + current ₹110.0) against cash & bank ₹48.2 Cr → **net debt ~₹159 Cr** [V Q2 balance sheet; E arithmetic]; screener shows ₹260 Cr (incl. leases). Capex is now debt-assisted. CRISIL counts ₹38.0 Cr unsecured promoter loans as debt (gearing 0.08×) [V].
10. **EBITDA basis.** Company EBITDA FY26 ₹402.6 Cr (excludes other income) = screener operating profit ₹403 Cr [V]. CRISIL uses **₹412.0 Cr** (its own adjustments) [V]. Use ₹402.6 Cr.
11. **School enrolment is falling while DOMS volumes grow — both are true.** UDISE+ total enrolment: **25.18 cr (2022-23) → 24.80 cr (2023-24) → 24.68 cr (2024-25)**, a seven-year low; primary (I–V) **10.78 → 10.44 cr** in one year [V ThePrint 28-Aug-2025 citing UDISE+]. The 2021-22 → 2022-23 drop (~26.5 → 25.2 cr) is partly a **methodology change** (student-level data) [K]. Private-school enrolment **rose 9.00 → 9.59 cr** while government fell 12.75 → 12.16 cr [V]. Stationery growth therefore comes from **per-student spend, branded share, private-school mix and categories beyond school**, not headcount.
12. **Capacity and share figures for Hindustan Pencils are thin.** The only hard numbers: FY25 revenue **₹1,374.74 Cr** (PrivateCircle, MCA-derived, 22-Jun-2026) [V]; FY23 stationery revenue ~₹1,100 Cr and capacity **8 million pencils/day** (Technopak, from company website) [V]. Blog claims of "70% of India's pencil market" are unsourced — **do not use** [V-snip markhub24 = unsourced].

# PART 1 — INDUSTRY DOSSIER

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **A ₹40–55k Cr domestic market (FY23 ₹38,500 Cr; ~₹55,500 Cr FY26 if Technopak's 13% CAGR held [E]) that is ~64% unbranded, distributed through ~3–3.5 lakh stationery shops, and taxed differently item by item** [V Technopak; V DOMS Q4 FY26 call for the store universe].
  - Split (FY23): paper stationery **₹16,200 Cr (42%)**; writing instruments **₹13,350 Cr**; office supplies **₹4,700 Cr**; art & craft **₹1,120 Cr**; "others" (technical instruments, erasers, sharpeners, adhesives, computer stationery, calculators) **₹3,130 Cr** [V Technopak exhibit 3.2].
  - Writing instruments: pens **₹10,700 Cr (80%)**, pencils **₹1,650 Cr (12%)**, markers/highlighters **₹1,000 Cr (8%)**; by volume ~**1,000 crore pens, ~400 crore pencils, ~50 crore markers** a year [V Technopak]. India uses ~**3 pencils per capita** vs ~6 in the US [V Technopak].
  - Branded share **29% (FY17) → 35% (FY22) → 36% (FY23)**, projected 43% by FY28 [V Technopak]. Writing instruments are already **78% branded**; paper stationery and art are the least branded.
- **The listed universe is small and lopsided; the unlisted is large.** DOMS (₹2,326 Cr FY26) is the only scaled listed "full-basket" maker. Listed specialists: Flair (pens, ₹1,250 Cr), Navneet (notebooks + publishing, ₹1,721 Cr, of which stationery is a part), Kokuyo Camlin (art, ₹806 Cr), Linc (pens, ₹543 Cr), Cello World's pens (₹369 Cr) [V screener; V-sib]. Unlisted: **ITC Classmate (₹2,456 Cr stationery revenue FY23 per Technopak — the largest player)**, **Hindustan Pencils ₹1,375 Cr (FY25)**, Luxor ₹449 Cr, Kangaro ₹402 Cr, Unomax ₹221 Cr, plus Rorito, Elkos, Win, Omega and others [V Technopak; V PrivateCircle].
- **Pencils are a near-duopoly; pens are a crowded oligopoly; notebooks are fragmented.**
  - Pencils: DOMS **~29%** by value (FY23) [V Technopak]; Hindustan Pencils (Nataraj/Apsara) likely **~35–40%** [E: ~60% of ~₹1,100 Cr revenue from its largest category ≈ ₹660 Cr vs a ₹1,650 Cr market]. Together **~65–70%** [E].
  - Mass pencils (≤₹5) are **85% of pencil value**; super-premium (>₹20) is <1% (Faber-Castell, Staedtler) [V Technopak].
  - Pens: mass (≤₹15) is **80% of pen value**; Linc, Flair, Cello, Reynolds, Luxor, Hamilton, Unomax, Rorito, Elkos, Win [V Technopak; V-sib].
- **DOMS is a volume compounder whose margin just broke.**
  - Revenue FY22 ₹683.6 Cr → FY26 ₹2,326.4 Cr (**36% CAGR**); EBITDA margin 10.2% → 15.4% → 17.7% → **18.2% (FY25 peak)** → 17.3% (FY26) [V company 5-yr tables].
  - H1 FY27: revenue **+18.4%** (₹1,338.3 Cr) but EBITDA **−16.6%** (12.4% margin) and PAT **−30.3%** [V]. FY27 revenue guidance **18–20%** reiterated on 2-Nov; no margin guidance ("visibility … remains limited") [V].
- **Capacity-led growth with a doubling of footprint underway.**
  - Existing operations: **18 facilities, 5 locations (Umbergaon, Jammu, Jalandhar, Jaipur, Siliguri), 2.0+ mn sq ft, 55+ acres** [V Q2 PPT; AR].
  - New: **~50+ acre greenfield at Umbergaon + 11+ acres adjoining**; total project capex **₹850–1,000 Cr**; first building (~300,000 sq ft) **commissioned in Q2 FY27**, dedicated to **Reynolds pens**, revenue from Q3 FY27 [V]. Wooden-pencil capacity **5.8 mn/day → ~8 mn/day** when fully built [V Q4 FY26 call].
  - Capex: FY23 ₹136 Cr, FY24 ₹154 Cr, FY25 ₹213 Cr, **FY26 ₹293 Cr**, **H1 FY27 ₹211 Cr**; FY27 guide **₹250–275 Cr** [V].
- **Valuation still prices a franchise.** DOMS **₹11,530 Cr m-cap, P/E 53.0 (TTM), P/B ~9.0, EV/EBITDA ~29× FY26 / ~32× TTM** [V screener 3-Nov-26; E]. The share price (₹1,900) sits near its **52-week low ₹1,796** and well below the ₹2,200 FILA block price of June [V]. Reference peers trade at **9–15× EV/EBITDA** (Flair, Linc, Navneet, Camlin, Cello) [E from screener].

### 1.2 Value chains (inputs → shelf)

1. **Wood-cased pencil (DOMS/Hindustan Pencils model; the pivotal product).**
   - **Wood:** poplar logs/blocks (Indian farm-forestry poplar from Punjab/Haryana/UP/J&K) → sawn, treated, **slats** → grooved. Poplar block price ₹365/unit (Dec-21) → ₹450 (Jan-22) → ₹375 (Apr-22) [V Technopak exhibit 3.30]. DOMS's Jammu and Jalandhar plants sit in the poplar belt [E inference from locations]. Hindustan Pencils has a wood unit in Kashmir (Bijbehara) [K].
   - **Lead (core):** natural graphite + clay (+ polymer binder for "polymer pencils") extruded and fired; DOMS makes leads in-house ("in-house manufacturing of leads and erasers" — CRISIL) [V CRISIL Aug-2026].
   - **Assembly:** lead laid in grooved slat, second slat glued (sandwich), shaped (hexagonal, round, DOMS's "zoom" triangular since 2010 [V Technopak]), lacquered/painted, tipped, eraser-ended, printed, packed (DOMS makes tin boxes and labels in-house via Micro Wood 75%) [V PPT slide 18; AR].
   - Output: India ~400 crore (4 bn) pencils/yr domestic + exports; DOMS ~5.8 mn/day; Hindustan Pencils ~8 mn/day [V].
2. **Pen (Linc/Flair/Reynolds/Cello model).** Polymer (PP, PS, ABS, PC) moulded into barrel, cap, plug; **tip** (brass/nickel-silver/stainless with tungsten-carbide ball, Swiss machines — historically imported or bought from CRI Ltd, Newell's Indian tip unit and others); **ink** (oil-based or gel). DOMS: "from sketch pen caps to ink & reservoir to packaging — all manufactured in-house"; **first tip-making plant (Swiss) being installed in 2026**, aiming to make **30–40% of tip needs** in-house over CY26–27 [V Q2 PPT; Q1 FY27 call]. CRI Ltd (tips) had ₹234 Cr revenue in FY25 [V PrivateCircle].
3. **Crayons/colours.** Paraffin wax + pigments (crayons); PVA/gum + pigments (water/poster colours); FILA know-how on formulations [V PPT "access to FILA Group's established product formulation"].
4. **Notebook.** Maplitho/creamwove writing paper (from mills: ITC, JK Paper, West Coast, Andhra Paper, Tamil Nadu Newsprint [K]) → ruling, printing, binding, cover. Paper is ~60–70% of a notebook's cost [K/E]. DOMS buys notebooks from Pioneer (₹103.9 Cr intra-group purchases FY26) [V AR RPT].
5. **Route to market (all categories).** Maker → super-stockist → distributor → retailer (stationery shop, general store, school-supply store) → household. **Distributor buys at ~30–40% off MRP and keeps ~10%; retailer keeps ~25–30%; institutional (school) sales get ~40–45% off** [V Technopak]. DOMS: 140+ super-stockists, 6,300+ distributors, 1,45,000+ retail outlets, 1,200+ sales staff (Sep-26) [V Q2 PPT].

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **At the brand + distribution layer.** A maker realises only ~60–70% of MRP (before GST) [V Technopak trade margins]; the trade keeps ~30–40%. A brand that can *also* make cheaply keeps the manufacturing margin. DOMS's gross margin (43.6% FY26) is in the band of FMCG staples; Flair's ~46% and Navneet's 50% (FY23, Technopak) [V].
- **At backward integration in the commodity core.** In mass pencils (≤₹5) and mass pens (≤₹15) price points are fixed by MRP habits (₹5, ₹10 packs); cost — wood, graphite, polymer, tips — decides margin. Technopak: "In volume driven category … the corresponding increase in raw material costs is primarily absorbed by the manufacturers" [V].
- **At the export/OEM layer for low-cost makers.** India exported **~₹5,732 Cr** of stationery in FY23 (notebooks 36%, pens 34%, painting items 15%, pencils 7%; US 28%) [V Technopak, TradeStat]. DOMS manufactures OEM for FILA (Dixon Ticonderoga, Lyra, Canson, Daler-Rowney entities) [V AR RPT].
- **Not at retail.** Organised retail is only ~12% of stationery retail sales (FY23), projected 19% by FY28 [V Technopak]. General trade (~70%) remains the gate.

### 9.2 What drives each KPI
- **Gross margin**: input prices (polymer 40% direct crude link; 30% indirect) × pricing lag × mix (art, kits, Reynolds premium pens lift; hygiene, paper stationery and exports dilute [E]) × integration depth (tips, leads, wood).
- **EBITDA margin**: gross margin minus operating leverage on a heavy employee base (14–15% of revenue) and new-plant fixed costs; Uniclan (~8.6% margin) dilutes by ~0.8–1.0 pt [E].
- **Outlets/revenue per outlet**: DOMS's stated policy is *depth over breadth* — "maximum shelf space" in a store before adding the next store [V Q4 call]. Directly serviceable universe ~2.25 lakh stores of 3.0–3.5 lakh stationery stores; plus kirana/general-merchant outlets via Uniclan's diaper distribution for cross-selling [V].

### 9.3 Price points that anchor the economics [V Technopak; E]
- Pencil: mass ≤₹5 (sold in ₹10/₹20/₹50 packs); premium ₹5–20; super >₹20.
- Pen: mass ≤₹15 (₹5/₹10 ball pens dominate); premium ₹15–400; super >₹400.
- Management example (May-2026): "assume a 10% hike, the revised MRP could be ₹6 and ₹11" — i.e. **price points of ₹5 and ₹10 are sticky**; a hike means a jump to the next round number or grammage/count changes [V Q4 call].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Formalisation from a 64%-unbranded base — the real growth engine.** Branded share rose 29% → 36% (FY17–FY23) and is projected at 43% by FY28 [V Technopak]. Outside writing instruments only ~14% is branded [E]. A branded maker can grow 2× the market for a decade without any demand help. DOMS grew 36% CAGR FY22–26 [V] while the market (per Technopak's own FY17–23 history) grew ~3%/yr — the gap *is* share gain. Risk: GST 2.0 removed the 12% tax advantage that unregistered pencil/notebook makers had (nil for everyone), which may *slow* formalisation in exactly the categories DOMS leads [E — test it].
2. **The pencil duopoly and the "wedge".** Pencils are only ~₹1,650 Cr (12% of writing instruments, FY23) [V], but they are the first item every child buys and the product that wins the shop counter. DOMS (~29%) and Hindustan Pencils (~35–40% [E]) own ~two-thirds; DOMS overtook HP on total revenue (₹1,913 vs ₹1,375 Cr in FY25) [V]. Whoever owns the pencil shelf sells the eraser, sharpener, scale, maths box and crayons beside it.
3. **Backward integration vs input-cost shocks.** Polymer/graphite/wood are the bulk of cost [V CRISIL]; ~70% of DOMS's RM basket is crude-linked [V]. Integration (leads, slats, erasers, tins, now tips) widens the cost gap against importers but does not hedge commodity prices: gross margin fell ~470 bps YoY in Q2 FY27 [V]. The industry's sticky ₹5/₹10 price points mean recovery comes through pack/grammage changes and slow hikes over 2–4 quarters [V calls].
4. **Capacity-led growth and its absorption.** DOMS is doubling its built-up footprint (~2 → ~4 mn sq ft) with ₹850–1,000 Cr of capex; it targets ₹3 of sales per ₹1 of fixed assets and 18–24 months to fill a plant [V]. With school enrolment falling [V UDISE+], absorption depends on share gains (HP, unbranded), new categories (Reynolds pens, hobby, BTS) and exports (FILA). Debt reappeared in H1 FY27 (₹207.5 Cr ex-leases) [V].

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Manufacturer-brand form:**
Revenue = Σ_category [ outlets reached × SKUs stocked per outlet × units sold per SKU × net realisation per unit ] + exports (FILA OEM + third party) + adjacencies.
EBITDA = Revenue × gross margin (1 − consumption %) − employee cost − other costs.
Capacity form (for absorption): Revenue ≈ gross fixed assets × asset turn (target 3×); new plant reaches full turn in 18–24 months.

**Worked example 1 — one ₹10-MRP pack of two mass pencils, before and after GST 2.0 [E composite, illustrative]**

| Step | Before (12% GST) | After (nil, MRP held at ₹10) | After (nil, MRP cut to ₹9) |
|---|---|---|---|
| MRP (incl. GST) | ₹10.00 | ₹10.00 | ₹9.00 |
| Retailer margin ~27% of MRP | ₹2.70 | ₹2.70 | ₹2.43 |
| Distributor margin ~10% of its buying price ≈ 6.5% of MRP | ₹0.65 | ₹0.65 | ₹0.59 |
| Maker invoice (incl. GST) | ₹6.65 | ₹6.65 | ₹5.98 |
| GST in invoice | ₹0.71 | ₹0 | ₹0 |
| **Maker net realisation** | **₹5.94** | **₹6.65 (+12%)** | **₹5.98 (+0.7%)** |
| Input cost (materials ~56% of pre-change realisation) | ₹3.33 | ₹3.33 + blocked ITC ~₹0.40 [E] | ₹3.73 |
| **Gross profit per pack** | **₹2.61 (44%)** | **₹2.92 (44%)** | **₹2.25 (38%)** |

Reading: if the trade cut MRP fully, the maker *loses* gross margin because of ITC reversal; if MRP held, the maker gains. The truth for FY26–27 is in between and unknown (gap). Retail/distributor margins from Technopak (distributor buys at 30–40% off MRP; retailer 25–30%) [V].

**Worked example 2 — DOMS FY26 from capacity to PAT [V inputs; E arithmetic]**
- Gross fixed assets ≈ revenue ÷ 2.7 = ₹2,326.4 ÷ 2.7 ≈ **₹862 Cr** [E; company states GFA turn 2.7×].
- Revenue ₹2,326.4 Cr = domestic ₹2,024.4 Cr (87.0%) + outside India ₹301.9 Cr (13.0%) [V AR geography].
- Domestic GT revenue per outlet ≈ (₹2,420.6 Cr GPS × 74.2% GT) ÷ ~1.40 lakh outlets ≈ **₹1.28 lakh per outlet per year (~₹10,700 a month)** [E].
- Gross profit ₹1,015.1 Cr (43.6%) − employees ₹328.9 Cr − other ₹283.6 Cr = **EBITDA ₹402.6 Cr (17.3%)** [V].
- − D&A ₹88.1 Cr − finance ₹10.8 Cr + other income ₹18.6 Cr = PBT ₹322.3 Cr; tax ₹82.7 Cr (25.7%) → **PAT ₹239.6 Cr** [V].
- ROCE 23.9%, ROE 20.1% [V].

**Worked example 3 — the Q2 FY27 margin bridge (YoY) [V inputs; E arithmetic]**
- Q2 FY26 EBITDA margin 17.5% → gross margin −4.7 pts (43.8% → 39.1%) → employee cost −0.7 pt (14.4% → 15.1%) → other expenses +0.3 pt (11.9% → 11.6%) → **Q2 FY27 12.4%**.
- In rupees: revenue +₹99.9 Cr; gross profit only +₹12.2 Cr; employee +₹19.4 Cr; other +₹9.5 Cr → **EBITDA −₹16.7 Cr** [V].
- Sensitivity: each 1 pt of gross margin ≈ ₹27 Cr of annual EBITDA at the H1 FY27 run-rate (₹2,677 Cr annualised) [E].

**Worked example 4 — new-plant economics [V targets; E arithmetic]**
- ₹1,000 Cr project × 3× asset turn at maturity ≈ **₹3,000 Cr of incremental revenue** (more than FY26's whole revenue) — but only at full utilisation after phased construction (3+ years of capex) [V: "next 3 years … high capex cycle"].
- At 16% EBITDA, ₹3,000 Cr → ₹480 Cr EBITDA; pre-tax ROCE ≈ (480 − D&A ~₹60 Cr) ÷ (₹1,000 Cr + WC ~₹530 Cr at 65 days) ≈ **~27%** [E] — attractive *if* filled. At 2× turns and 13% margin: ~₹260 Cr EBITDA, ROCE ~13% [E].

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed, and new primer issues

**Insights an expert would add**

1. **DOMS's core grew only 3% in FY26.**
   - Scholastic Stationery (pencils, erasers, sharpeners, maths boxes) rose just **+3.2%** to ₹762.5 Cr.
   - Group growth of 22% came from Office Supplies/pens (+59%), Kits (+32%), Hygiene (+80%), Hobby (+194%) and SAM (+11%).
   - Three causes stacked in the core: the GST transition destock (Sep–Oct 2025), the **US 50% tariff on pencils sold to Dixon Ticonderoga**, and capacity limits (pencils ~5.5 mn/day).
   - **So the FY27–28 thesis rests on the new pencil capacity and Reynolds, not on the existing core.** [V AR; Q2/Q3 FY26 calls]

2. **The duopoly is tilting.**
   - Hindustan Pencils' FY25 revenue was flat (−1.1%), with EBITDA −59% and PAT −78%, while DOMS grew 24% at 18% EBITDA. DOMS claims 35%+ of pencils, rising toward ~45%.
   - The rival is losing money momentum just as DOMS adds ~40% more pencil capacity.
   - This is the best evidence for "share gain, not market growth" — but it rests on MCA-derived secondary data. [V-sec; V]

3. **GST 2.0 did not hurt DOMS's margin, by its own account, because DOMS did not pass on the full cut.**
   - MRPs fell ~3% against a 12% tax removal; primary prices rose 3–4%; the ITC loss was priced in.
   - Consumers saw only a token cut, and that weakens the "GST makes branded cheaper" demand argument. [V Q2 FY26 call; E]

4. **FILA-group sales are bigger than the first pass thought (₹175.6 Cr).** Subsidiaries — notebooks via Pioneer — also sell to Dixon. With US school spending cuts (FILA North America −8.7% in 2025), the **US is DOMS's main external demand risk, not China**. [V AR; FILA PR]

5. **FILA's incentive to keep selling is arithmetic.** Its 19% DOMS stake (~€199m) is ~43% of FILA's own market cap at ~6× EBITDA. The parent is effectively a holding company trading at a discount to its Indian asset. **Expect further blocks; the June 2026 block at ₹2,200 is the market's reference price.** [E on V]

6. **Peers price harder than DOMS.**
   - Flair took selective hikes and trimmed schemes, but still lost ~390 bp of EBITDA margin. Linc passed on ~50% of its RM rise. Navneet's paper costs rose only ~2%.
   - DOMS's deliberate under-pricing is a **share bet whose payback must show up as volume in H2 FY27**, against the 18–20% guide (needs ~18–22% H2 growth).

7. **Japanese strategic shareholders run through the listed sector:** Kokuyo (74% of Camlin), Mitsubishi Pencil (13.5% of Linc; uni-Linc JV) and Pilot (growing its own India subsidiary +11%). Add **Flair's Flomaxe pencil and sharpener JV** and the pencil/creatives space is getting more contested, not less.

8. **Enrolment has stopped falling in aggregate (24.72 cr in 2025-26), but primary keeps shrinking (−2.4%/yr).** The pencil wedge loses ~2–3% of children a year. Private unaided schools gained 2.5 million students in one year, and they are the premium-pencil buyer.
