## 1. Archetype map: what this "sub-sector" actually is

"Other Electrical Equipment" is an exchange classification bucket, not one industry. Its 13 listed members fall into four business models with different demand drivers, cost structures, cycles and valuations. This report is organised around them. FY26 figures are from screener.in consolidated pages fetched 13-Oct-2026 [V]; ABB India reports on a calendar year, so its CY25 is used.

**Why the archetypes matter.** The table shows a split valuation. The two industrial-electrical MNC-style franchises trade near 100x earnings. The solar and wind manufacturers trade near 20x even though their ROCE is higher (Waaree 38%, Premier 33%, Emmvee 45%, Suzlon 34%, against ABB 30% and CG 27%) [V]. The market is pricing durability. It treats solar margins as policy rents that will fade as capacity floods in, and wind as a cyclical, working-capital-heavy business. It treats ABB and CG as compounding on a multi-year grid, data-centre and industrial capex cycle. Most of this report is about the solar archetype, because that is where the policy machinery (PLI, ALMM, BCD, DCR, US trade) and the overcapacity risk sit.

## 2. What the sub-sector does: value chains and where value is captured

### 2.1 Solar PV value chain
Polysilicon → ingot → wafer → cell → module → (inverter, BOS, EPC) → IPP or rooftop owner.

- **Polysilicon, ingot and wafer.** India has almost none. PLI-commissioned ingot-wafer capacity was only 2.2 GW at Jun-2025 [V pv magazine India, Aug-2025]. China holds >90% of global polysilicon and wafer capacity and >80–85% of cells and modules [V ICRA Dec-2024 / Nov-2025]. Indian cell makers import wafers, for example N-type M10 wafers at RMB 1.00 per piece (~₹12) in Sep-2026 [V TrendForce].
- **Cells.** These are the scarce, high-value step in India. Domestic cell capacity is ~36.6 GW, against ~262 GW of module capacity (Jun-2026) [V Mercom via pv magazine]. ALMM List-II (cells) is mandatory from 1-Jun-2026 for most government-linked and utility projects [V Energetica]. Any compliant ("DCR") cell therefore commands a large scarcity premium. Premier Energies made 844 MW of cells and 953 MW of modules in Q1 FY27 and earned EBITDA of ₹759 cr. That is roughly ₹8–9 per W of cell output [E derived from V], against a China cell *price* of RMB 0.30/W (~₹3.6/W) [V TrendForce]. **Value capture is concentrated at the cell step.**
- **Modules.** This step is commoditised and over-built. ALMM List-I has 228.5 GW enlisted (Sep-2026) [V], compared with ~45 GW of annual domestic installations (FY26 44.6 GW) [V JMK]. A non-DCR module made from imported cells earns a thin conversion margin. A DCR module earns most of the cell premium if the maker owns cells. If it buys DCR cells, the cell maker keeps the rent.
- **Distributed and rooftop (Fujiyama, Waaree retail).** Here the product is a system: modules, inverters, batteries and the installer channel. Value is captured through brand and channel and the PM Surya Ghar subsidy flow, not through manufacturing scale. Waaree's retail revenue grew 130% YoY to ₹2,289 cr in Q1 FY27 [V].

### 2.2 Wind value chain
Components (blades, towers, gearboxes, generators, bearings, castings, power electronics) → WTG OEM (nacelle assembly, blades in-house for Suzlon/Inox) → EPC/turnkey (land, evacuation, civil) → IPP/C&I offtaker → 20–25 years of O&M.

- OEMs capture value through technology (own IP for Suzlon; licensed platforms historically for Inox), in-house blades, and the turnkey/EPC wrap. Suzlon's EPC share of its order book was 32% in Q1 FY27 [V].
- O&M is an annuity stream (Inox Green ~13.3 GW book [search-snippet]; Suzlon services). It is lower-growth but high-visibility and often bundled with turnkey awards.
- Per-MW ticket sizes (from fetched order data): Suzlon's AP projects were 1,325 MW for ~₹10,500 cr (~₹7.9 cr/MW). Inox–NLC was 200 MW turnkey for ~₹1,600 cr (₹8.0 cr/MW). Inox–IOC was 100 MW for ₹755 cr (₹7.6 cr/MW) [V GreentechLead]. Suzlon's FY26 revenue/MW delivered was ₹16,732 cr ÷ 2,456 MW ≈ ₹6.8 cr/MW, which blends supply-only, EPC and services [E from V].

### 2.3 Industrial electricals value chain
Copper, steel (CRGO/CRNO electrical steel), aluminium, silicon power devices and resins → motors, drives, transformers, switchgear and RMUs, traction electricals, automation → utilities (transmission and distribution), industry, data centres, railways, buildings, exports.

- Value comes from engineering content, product breadth, an installed-base service business, and the ability to deliver long-lead items (EHV transformers and breakers have 11–24 month lead times [V Outlook Business/MoP]).
- Material is 58–69% of sales (ABB 58% CY25, CG 69% FY26) [V screener schedules]. Gross margin is driven by pricing power against copper and steel.

### 2.4 Batteries, defence and rail electronics (HBL)
Nickel-cadmium and specialised batteries (aviation, defence, rail), electronic fuzes, and Kavach (Indian Railways' indigenous Automatic Train Protection) on-board and track-side equipment. Value is captured through qualification barriers: RDSO approval for Kavach, defence product qualification. Payment and order lumpiness are high. HBL's OPM swung from 44% (Q2 FY26) to 12% (Q4 FY26) to 23% (Q1 FY27) [V screener].

## 3. Size and growth

### 3.1 Solar
- **Annual installations (India, AC/DC as reported by MNRE)** [K MNRE/CEA for FY15–FY21; V for FY25–FY27]:
  FY22 ~13.9 GW → FY23 ~12.8 GW → FY24 ~15.0 GW → FY25 23.8 GW → **FY26 44.6 GW** (+87%) → **Q1 FY27 11.9 GW** [V Energetica/MNRE]. CY2025 was 45.7 GW, making India the world's #2 solar market ahead of the US [V SolarPower Europe/pv magazine].
- **Cumulative:** 162.15 GW at 30-Jun-2026 (ground-mounted 121.25, rooftop 30.11, hybrid 4.36, off-grid 6.43) [V].
- **India's global share:** 45.7/664 GW ≈ **6.9% of 2025 global installations** [E from V]. On manufacturing, India's ~262 GW module nameplate is roughly 15–20% of global module capacity (China alone >1,100 GW globally in 2024 per ICRA) [E]. India's cell share is far smaller, about 2–3% [E].
- **Market value of domestic module demand** [E]: 45 GW × ~₹15–20/W blended (non-DCR ~₹13.6, DCR ~₹22–25) ≈ ₹70,000–90,000 cr per year (~US$8–10 bn). Exports add more: India shipped 2.3 GW (US$793 mn) to the US in 2024 [V Commerce data via Energetica], and the US took 92% of India's solar exports in H1-2026 [V Mercom].
- **Listed solar archetype revenue** grew from ~₹11,000 cr in FY23 to ₹52,391 cr in FY26 (Waaree ₹6,751 cr → ₹26,537 cr; Premier ₹1,429 cr → ₹7,824 cr) [V screener]. That is a ~65–70% CAGR [E], driven by volume, the DCR premium and backward integration.

### 3.2 Wind
- **Annual installations** [K MNRE for FY15–FY24; V FY25–FY27]: FY15 2.3, FY16 3.4, FY17 5.5 (the old peak), FY18 1.8, FY19 1.6, FY20 2.1, FY21 1.5, FY22 1.1, FY23 2.3, FY24 3.3, FY25 ~4.15, **FY26 6.06 GW (record)**, Q1 FY27 1.35 GW [V].
- **Cumulative:** 57.44 GW (Jun-2026), 58.14 GW (Jul-2026) [V]. Target is 100 GW by 2030, which implies ~10 GW per year from here, against a 6 GW record [E].
- India was 6.3 of 165 GW added globally in CY2025 (~3.8%), and #3–4 on cumulative capacity [V GWEC].
- Domestic turbine manufacturing capacity is ~24 GW per year [V GreentechLead/MNRE], about 4x the installation run-rate. That makes India an export-capable hub, but OEM utilisation is low across the board.

### 3.3 Industrial electricals
- IEEMA put industry production at ~₹2.5–2.6 lakh cr (~US$30–33 bn) in FY23 [search-snippet, IEEMA]. With the FY24–FY26 growth index running at 9–22% YoY [V IEEMA], FY26 production is plausibly ~₹3.3–3.6 lakh cr (~US$38–41 bn) [E]. McKinsey (via snippet) puts 2025 consumption at US$59 bn, with import dependence rising from 22% to 33% [unverified].
- Fastest-growing lines (Q3 FY26 YoY, IEEMA [V]): energy meters +53.6% (smart-meter rollout), power transformers +26.6%, insulators +29.6%, HV switchgear +9.5% (after +30–38% in H1). The laggards are rotating machines (+0.8%) and LV switchgear (+5%). Motors, the core of CG Industrial Systems and ABB Motion, slowed sharply in FY26 even as grid products boomed.
- Trade (Apr–Dec FY26, ₹ cr) [V IEEMA/DGCIS]: motor imports 8,386 (+28%) against exports 4,707; LT switchgear exports 14,672 (+85%); power electronics imports 15,113. India is a net exporter of cables, towers, transformers and HV switchgear, and a net importer of motors, LT switchgear and power electronics.

### 3.4 Batteries, storage and rail safety
- Cumulative BESS commissioned was 9.6 GWh (Aug-2026) [V JMK/IEEFA]. Storage tenders were the largest technology bucket in FY26 Q3–Q4 (7.6 and 8.9 GW tendered) [V IWTMA].
- Kavach 4.0 was commissioned on 2,633 route-km by Jul-2026, with track-side work under way on ~21,800 rkm [search-snippet]. Indian Railways' BG network is ~68,000 rkm [K Indian Railways Year Book], so the multi-year rollout pipeline runs into tens of thousands of crores [E].

## 4. Demand drivers (with numbers)

1. **The 500 GW non-fossil target by 2030.** Non-fossil capacity was 297.37 GW at Jun-2026 [V]. Reaching 500 GW by Mar-2030 needs ~200 GW in 3.75 years, about **54 GW per year** of non-fossil additions [E]. That compares with 55.3 GW achieved in FY26 [V JMK headline]. Solar is likely to be ~40–45 GW per year of this and wind 6–10 GW per year [E].
2. **Auction pipeline, which is now the binding constraint.** Since FY18 ~427.6 GW of RE has been tendered [V IWTMA]. But 46.65 GW of REIA-awarded capacity has no signed PPA/PSA [V IWTMA], and quarterly tendering fell from 15–18 GW per quarter in FY26 to 11.7 GW in Q1 FY27 and only 4.6 GW in Jul-2026 [V]. S&P puts FY26 awards down 45% to ~24 GW [snippet]. **The demand pipeline for 2027–28 is thinner than the FY26 installation boom suggests.** Installations lag awards by ~18–30 months, so FY26 installations reflect FY23–FY25 awards.
3. **The procurement mix is shifting** from plain solar to hybrid, FDRE and storage. Storage was 8.9 GW of 17.2 GW tendered in Q4 FY26, and hybrid was ~60% of Jul-2026 tenders [V IWTMA]. This helps wind (needed for evening and night firmness) and BESS makers (Waaree 5.15 GWh, Fujiyama, Premier). It reduces module GW per tender.
4. **C&I open access and the ISTS-waiver cliff.** The ISTS waiver deadline of 30-Jun-2025 pulled projects into H1 FY26 [V JMK]. The graded waiver now runs to Jun-2028 [snippet]. C&I and PSU are 84% of Suzlon's order book [V].
5. **Rooftop (PM Surya Ghar).** The ₹75,021 cr outlay targets 1 crore homes. ~26 lakh systems and ~9.56 GW were installed by Mar-2026 [snippet, Lok Sabha], rising to ~50 lakh households and ~14.8 GW by Jul-2026 [snippet, MNRE statement; unverified]. Rooftop solar added 8.7 GW in FY26 (+69%) and 6.4 GW in H1-2026 (+104%) [V JMK/DTE]. This is **100% DCR demand**, so it absorbs scarce domestic cells. At ~3 kW per home, 1 crore homes ≈ 30 GW of DCR modules over the scheme life [E].
6. **PM-KUSUM (DCR).** Only 27% of 34.8 GW achieved by Dec-2025 [V Mercom]. This is a slow but DCR-heavy demand bucket.
7. **US exports, which are now impaired.** 2.3 GW in 2024 [V]. Final AD 123.04% plus CVD 126.09% on Indian cells "whether or not assembled into modules" (Sep-2026) [V], and Section 232 (15% plus minimum import prices from 4-Dec-2026) [V headline]. Indian-cell-based modules are effectively shut out of the US. Indian modules using non-Indian, non-Chinese cells, and US-located plants (Waaree's Texas facility [K company]), are the remaining routes.
8. **Grid capex.** NEP-Transmission needs >₹9.15 lakh cr over 2022–32. FY27 transformation target is 1,58,339 MVA (+40% vs FY26 actual) [V]. ICRA expects ₹5–6 lakh cr of transmission capex over FY27–FY32 and says supplier order books doubled from FY22 to FY26 [V]. This drives CG Power Systems (backlog +59%) and ABB Electrification.
9. **Data centres.** 2.2 GW (2025) → 12 GW (2030) per WoodMac, or a more conservative +3.9 GW per Cushman [V]. Each MW of IT load needs MV/LV switchgear, RMUs, UPS, transformers and busways. ABB's Q2 CY26 orders were +50%, with data-centre switchgear and RMU orders named [V/snippet].
10. **Railways.** Kavach rollout; Vande Bharat and traction electricals (CG, ABB, HBL). CG booked a ₹20 cr railway provision in Q1 FY27 [V], a reminder that rail traction margins are thin.

The policy sequence (List-I in 2021, List-II in 2026, List-III in 2028) moves the bottleneck up the chain one step at a time. **Each time a list goes live, the step just upstream earns scarcity rents until capacity catches up, and then margins converge to import parity plus duty.** Modules went through this in FY23–FY25. Cells are in it now, FY26–FY28. Wafers come next, from 2028.

**China's anti-involution campaign has not fixed the chain.** Polysilicon rallied from RMB 30,400/t (futures, 28-Jun-2025) to RMB 61,985/t (18-Dec-2025) on the campaign [V SunSirs]. The regulator then halted the RMB 3 bn Beijing Guanghe Qiancheng buy-and-shut JV in Jan-2026 [V Caixin]. Spot polysilicon is back at ~RMB 33.5/kg (Sep-2026) [V TrendForce]. Listed Chinese solar firms lost >RMB 50 bn in 2025 at 40–50% utilisation [V Caixin]. China removed its 9% export VAT rebate in Apr-2026 [V pv magazine], which lifted FOB module prices from ~US$0.09 to ~US$0.116–0.12/W. **This is the external price anchor for Indian non-DCR modules.** ~₹10.2/W FOB plus ~40% duty (20% BCD + 20% AIDC) gives ~₹14.3/W landed for a Chinese module [E]. That is very close to the ₹13.6/W Indian non-DCR price, so Indian non-DCR modules already sell at import parity.

Inside a module maker's material line, an engineering composite applies when cells are bought in [E, from China price chain above plus K industry BOM]: cells ~55–65% of module cost; glass ~8–10%; aluminium frame ~7–9%; EVA/POE encapsulant ~4–6%; backsheet ~2–3%; ribbon, junction box and others ~5%. Silver paste and wafers dominate a cell maker's cost. Cell material ratios (Websol 42%) are much lower than module ratios, which is the scarcity rent showing up in the P&L.

### 5.4 Wind OEM cost and capacity
- Material is 55–65% of sales (Suzlon 65%, Inox 55%), plus a higher manufacturing share (Inox 10%) because of blade and tower production and the EPC wrap [V].
- Working capital is the swing factor. Inventory days are Suzlon 152 and Inox Wind 268. Cash conversion cycle is Suzlon 117 days and Inox 443 days (FY26) [V screener]. Inox's ROCE of 11% against Suzlon's 34% is mostly a working-capital story.
- Capacity: Suzlon ~4.5 GW nacelle [K company]; Jaisalmer blades 1,260 MW after doubling [V]. Inox ~2.5 GW, Envision ~3 GW, Adani Wind ~2.25 GW (→5 GW) [V]. Industry ~24 GW against ~6 GW installed → **~25% utilisation industry-wide** [E].

### 5.5 Industrial electricals
- Capacity constraints are in EHV transformers and breakers. CG added 80% EHV breaker capacity at Nashik [V], and lead times for 765 kV transformers are 20–24 months [V].
- Margins: CG Power Systems PBIT is 23.1% while Industrial Systems is lower (motor price competition, railway provisions) [V]. ABB's operational EBITA is 13.0% [V], down from 19% OPM in CY24 [V screener]. That reflects mix, competition and forex, with orders strong.

## 6. Industry structure, concentration, MNCs/unlisted, imports and China

### 6.1 Solar
- **Fragmented at the module step, concentrating at the cell step.** ALMM-I has hundreds of entities. The Sep-2026 revision alone added 12 new names (Atal Solar 1,440 MW, Solberry 1,038 MW, Nelson 1,001 MW, etc.) [V]. Cells are concentrated: ALMM-II had ~14 makers (31.8 GW) at one revision [V].
- **Largest unlisted or conglomerate players:** Adani (Mundra Solar; integrated poly→module, also the main named respondent in the US AD/CVD case) [V]; Tata Power Solar (4 GW PLI cell+module) [V PIB]; ReNew (4.8 GW PLI); Avaada (3 GW PLI; Avaada Electro DRHP lists 3 GW TOPCon cells at Nagpur) [snippet]; Reliance (6 GW PLI, giga-complex Jamnagar); Indosol (Shirdi Sai; 6 GW PLI); First Solar (3.4 GW thin-film, Chennai); Grew, Gautam, Rayzon, Goldi, Insolation, Navitas, Rays. Several (Avaada, Rayzon, Goldi, Juniper Green) are in or near the IPO pipeline [K/snippet].
- **Listed members' module share:** Waaree is the largest. 3.6 GW sold in Q1 FY27 [V] annualises to ~14 GW, roughly a quarter of domestic installations [E]. Premier, Emmvee, Vikram and Saatvik are each 3–10 GW nameplate.
- **China:** It is the price-setter for every input. India imports cells (81% of H1-2026 solar imports by value [V Mercom]) and wafers. Solar module import value fell from US$3.36 bn (FY22) to US$2.15 bn (FY25) [V], while cell imports rose.

### 6.2 Wind
- Domestic OEMs held an estimated **~49% of FY26 installations**, up from 31% in FY24, and Chinese OEMs (Envision, Sany) ~33% (Deven Choksey estimate) [snippet, unverified]. On deliveries, Suzlon's 2,456 MW is ~41% of FY26's 6.06 GW [V].
- Others: Envision (~3 GW per year India capacity), Adani Wind (captive for Adani Green), Siemens Gamesa India (sold Dec-2025 to a TPG/MAVCO consortium as "Vayona Energy" [snippet]), GE Vernova (>5 GW installed base; 3.8 MW/154 m), Senvion India (4.2M160 RLMM approval Jan-2026 [snippet]), Sany, Vestas.
- **ALMM-Wind domestic-content rules** (Jul-2025, bearings relief Feb-2026) target Chinese OEMs' import-heavy supply chains. They favour Suzlon and Inox, which have in-house blades and Indian vendor bases.

### 6.3 Industrial electricals
- An MNC-dominated oligopoly per product: ABB, Siemens/Siemens Energy India, Schneider, Hitachi Energy India, GE Vernova T&D India. Indian challengers include CG Power (Murugappa), BHEL, Transformers & Rectifiers, Bharat Bijlee, Kirloskar Electric, and Crompton (fans and pumps). Chinese imports are significant in LT switchgear and power electronics [V IEEMA trade table].
- Most comparable peers sit in other exchange buckets (Heavy Electrical Equipment), so this sub-sector's industrial peer set is thin (ABB, CG).

### 6.4 HBL
Kavach is supplied by a handful of RDSO-approved vendors: HBL, Kernex Microsystems, Medha Servo Drives (unlisted), and Siemens/Quadrant via JV routes [K/snippet]. HBL reportedly missed a major Railways order in Jan-2026 (stock −13%) [snippet], then won a ~₹1,714 cr CLW loco-equipment order (May-2026) [snippet]. The share structure is lumpy.

The central point is that **the solar archetype's margins are a function of where a firm sits relative to the current ALMM bottleneck**. Websol and Premier, the cell-heavy players, earned 30–44% EBITDA margins. Saatvik, a mostly module-only player, fell to 6.6% OPM in Q1 FY27, and Vikram fell to 8% [V screener]. That is the module price war showing through even as the DCR cell premium persists.

**Reading the stack.** ALMM-I created module capacity but no technology moat. India moved from <20 GW (2022) to >70 GW (Dec-2024) to 228 GW (Sep-2026) [V ICRA/Energetica]. ALMM-II is now doing the same for cells. ICRA expects ~100 GW of cells by Dec-2027 [V], so the cell scarcity premium has perhaps 18–30 months to run. PLI money is small relative to the DCR rent. ₹18,500 cr spread over 48 GW and five years is about ₹0.8/W per year [E]. The DCR-to-non-DCR spread is ~US$0.13/W ≈ ₹11/W [V SMM].

- **ALMM (Wind)** replaced RLMM in Jul-2025. ## 9. History and cycles (dates)

**Solar: three cycles.**
1. **2008–2013, the first boom and bust.** Moser Baer Solar, Indosolar, Tata BP Solar and XL Energy built cell/module capacity, mostly for European export. JNNSM launched in Jan-2010 with a DCR only for crystalline-Si, so developers used thin-film imports from the US. Chinese overcapacity then crushed prices: modules fell ~75% in 2008–2012 [K]. Indian cell lines sat at 10–20% utilisation. The DGAD anti-dumping recommendation in 2014 was never notified. Moser Baer went into liquidation (2018). Indosolar went through IBC and was acquired by Waaree (2022) [K].
2. **2018–2022, protection by duty.** Safeguard duty from Jul-2018 (25%→20%→15%, extended to Jul-2021) [snippet], ALMM order Jan-2019 (effective 2021), BCD 40%/25% from Apr-2022 and PLI (2021–23). Capacity grew but stayed mostly module assembly on imported cells.
3. **2023–2026, the policy-rent super-cycle.** Three things coincided: US demand for non-Chinese modules after the UFLPA (2022) and SE-Asia AD/CVD, ALMM-I reinstated Apr-2024 with DCR rooftop demand, and record domestic installations (FY25 23.8 GW → FY26 44.6 GW). Margins expanded from ~4% (FY22) to 20–35% (FY25–FY26) [V screener: Waaree OPM 3.7% → 22%, Premier 4% → 30%, Emmvee 19% → 34%]. An **IPO wave** followed [K exchange records]: Premier Energies (Sep-2024, ~₹2,830 cr), Waaree Energies (Oct-2024, ~₹4,321 cr), Vikram Solar (Aug-2025, ~₹2,079 cr), Saatvik Green (Sep-2025, ~₹900 cr), Emmvee (Nov-2025, ~₹2,900 cr), Fujiyama Power (Nov-2025, ~₹828 cr). Websol is an older listed name that pivoted back to cells.
4. **2026–?, the shake-out.** Module overcapacity (228 GW ALMM against ~45 GW demand), the US door closing (AD/CVD + Sec 232), and falling tender issuance. Q1 FY27 module-heavy margins collapsed: Saatvik 6.6%, Vikram 8% OPM [V], and Waaree's margin was 18.15% against 22.5% a year earlier [V]. Cell-integrated players are holding up for now (Premier 29–30%, Emmvee 35%).

**Wind: boom, bust, restructuring, revival.**
- 2000s–2012: Suzlon is a global top-5 OEM. It acquires REpower (2007, ~€1.2 bn) and Hansen [K]. Over-leveraged when the 2008 crisis hits.
- 2012–2015: FCCB defaults (2012), CDR of ~₹11,000+ cr debt (2013), Dilip Shanghvi group investment (2015), sale of Senvion (2015) [K].
- FY17: 5.5 GW peak on feed-in tariffs plus accelerated depreciation. FY18: auctions start → tariffs fall to ₹2.43–2.64/kWh → installs collapse to 1.1–2.3 GW (FY18–FY23) [K MNRE/SECI].
- 2020–2023: Suzlon debt resolution (2020), rights issue (Oct-2022, ₹1,200 cr), QIP (2023, ₹2,000 cr) [K]. Borrowings fell from ₹6,925 cr (FY21) to ₹150 cr (FY24) [V screener]. Inox Wind goes through parallel losses (OPM −48% FY22) and is recapitalised by the INOXGFL group; Inox Green demerged and IPO'd (Nov-2022) [K].
- FY24–FY27: Revival. FY26 is a record 6.06 GW. Suzlon revenue goes from ₹6,529 cr (FY24) to ₹16,732 cr (FY26), PAT ₹3,163 cr [V]. Margins are now normalising (Suzlon Q1 FY27 15.6% vs 19.2%) [V].

**Industrial electricals.** ABB India's parent restructured into four businesses (Electrification, Motion, Process Automation, Robotics). ABB sold its global robotics division to SoftBank (announced Oct-2025) [K], and ABB India's ₹90/sh special dividend reflects the local robotics divestment [V]. CG Power: 2019 fraud by the Thapar promoters → Murugappa/Tube Investments takeover (Nov-2020) → turnaround (OPM 0.6% FY20 → 13% FY26; ROCE peaked at 61% in FY23) [V screener]. CG Semi OSAT JV with Renesas and Stars (approved Feb-2024) → commercial production 4-Jul-2026 [V].

## 11. Global lens

- **Solar:** 664 GW installed globally in 2025 (+12%), with China 382 GW (57%) and India 45.7 GW (#2) [V]. 2026 is forecast at 612 GW (−8%), the first decline in 20+ years, driven by China −24% [V SolarPower Europe]. China's module capacity of >1,100 GW against <700 GW of demand [V ICRA] guarantees a structurally low global price. **The global price anchor of ~US$0.10–0.12/W will not rise much**, so India's domestic premium depends on regulation, not on cost advantage. Global peers: Chinese majors (Longi, Jinko, Trina, JA, Tongwei) are loss-making in 2025 [V Caixin]. First Solar (US, thin-film, IRA 45X credits) trades at a premium because of the US policy moat. Indian makers' 20–35% EBITDA margins are an outlier globally and are explained by the ALMM/DCR moat.
- **Wind:** 165 GW installed globally in 2025 (+40%), China 120.5 GW [V GWEC]. Western OEMs (Vestas, Siemens Gamesa, GE Vernova, Nordex) run at mid-single-digit EBIT margins. Chinese OEMs (Goldwind, Envision, Mingyang, Sany) dominate on price. Suzlon's ~18% EBITDA margin [V] is strong by global standards and reflects a protected domestic market with ALMM-Wind and domestic-only execution.
- **Electrical equipment:** There is a global grid and data-centre supercycle. Global peers (Siemens Energy, Hitachi Energy, Schneider, Eaton, GE Vernova) re-rated in 2024–26 on transformer and switchgear shortages [K]. ABB India and CG's ~100x P/E is consistent with India being a scarce growth market for that theme.
- **Battery/storage:** China LFP cells ~US$53.6/kWh. India landed price is 14–23% higher [V JMK/IEEFA]. India's BESS manufacturing is at the module/container stage.

## 12. Technology and disruption

- **Cell technology migration.** Mono-PERC (≤23% cell efficiency) → TOPCon (n-type, 24.5–25.5%) → HJT (25–26%) and back-contact (BC; 26%+) → perovskite-silicon tandem (lab 33%+) [K]. ALMM module capacity is 80% TOPCon, 4% PERC, 3% HJT, 2% thin-film [V Mercom]. Risk: India's 2025–27 TOPCon cell build-out could be partly obsolete by 2029–30 if BC/tandem scale in China. The OPIS-reported narrowing of the TOPCon efficiency premium as BC suppliers cut prices [V] is an early sign. Waaree's first 125 MW HJT order (US) [V] shows some hedging.
- **Wafer formats:** M10 (182) → G12R (210R) → G12 (210). Indian lines must keep up or lose BOM competitiveness.
- **Module power** is moving to 620–650 W+ (few can supply >640–650 W at scale [V OPIS]), with bifacial glass-glass dominant.
- **Wind:** Platforms are moving to 4–5 MW+ onshore with 160–185 m rotors (Suzlon S175 5 MW, Adani 5.2 MW, Envision 5 MW, Sany 4 MW/168 m, Senvion 4.2M160, Inox 4.X MW coming) [V GreentechLead/Energetica]. This raises PLF and cuts land per MW, but brings new-platform reliability risk and blade-logistics limits. Hybrid lattice towers (160 m) open low-wind sites.
- **Storage:** BESS is replacing pure solar in tenders (FDRE/assured-peak at ₹5.99/kWh) [V]. This is a revenue opportunity for integrated players (Waaree, Premier, Fujiyama) but makes demand less module-intensive.
- **Industrials:** SiC/GaN power electronics, digital substations, GIS, data-centre liquid cooling and power density. The OSAT move by CG is a long-dated option.

## A. The defining forces (5)

1. **Policy-manufactured scarcity that migrates up the chain.** ALMM-I (2021/2024) → ALMM-II (Jun-2026) → ALMM-III (Jun-2028). Each step creates a 2–3 year window of supernormal margins at the newly protected stage, and then a capacity glut. *Reasoning:* Indian solar margins (20–44%) are 3–5x global peers' while India is a price-taker on every input. The gap is regulatory, not cost-based. Evidence: Websol's 44% and Premier's 30% EBITDA margins sit beside Saatvik's 6.6% Q1 FY27 margin [V].
2. **Module overcapacity against a softening demand pipeline.** 228 GW ALMM-I [V] against ~45–55 GW per year of installations. Tendering is down (FY26 awards −45% per S&P [snippet]; Q2 FY27 tenders 3.4 vs 17.9 GW YoY [V]), and 46.65 GW of awarded RE has no PPA [V]. *Reasoning:* FY26–FY27 installations are running off the FY23–FY25 award boom. Without new PPAs, FY28–FY29 demand plateaus just as cell and module capacity doubles again.
3. **China's price chain as a ceiling, and the US door closing as a floor-remover.** China's FOB TOPCon is ~US$0.116/W and polysilicon RMB 33.5/kg [V]. The US is shutting Indian cells out (AD 123% + CVD 126%) and adding Section 232 [V]. *Reasoning:* Exports were the release valve for Indian overcapacity in FY24–FY25 (US 92% of solar exports [V]). With that valve closing, capacity turns inward and module prices converge to Chinese landed cost plus duty (~₹14/W), which is already the non-DCR price.
4. **Grid, data-centre and storage capex as a multi-year electrical supercycle.** ₹9.15 lakh cr NEP-T, FY27 MVA target +40%, data centres 2.2 → 12 GW, storage tenders now the largest category [V]. *Reasoning:* This favours the industrial-electricals archetype (CG Power Systems backlog +59% [V]) and gives solar majors a diversification path (Waaree into transformers and BESS; Premier into Transcon transformers [V]).
5. **Wind's localisation-protected revival with execution and working-capital risk.** A record 6.06 GW [V] and ALMM-Wind local-content rules favour Suzlon and Inox. Growth needs ~10 GW per year for the 100 GW 2030 target [E] against only 11.82 GW of wind awards still awaiting PPA [V]. *Reasoning:* Wind economics hinge on converting orders to cash. Inox's 443-day cash cycle against Suzlon's 117 days [V] is the difference between ROCE of 11% and 34%.

## B. Deep-dive themes

### B1. The cell bottleneck: how long do DCR rents last?
- **Supply:** ALMM-II has 35.5 GW (Aug-2026), against ~9.7 GW added in H1-2026 [V]. ICRA projects ~100 GW of cells by Dec-2027 [V], and CRISIL (2024) projected 43–47 GW by mid-2026 [snippet]. Actual delivery (36.6 GW) is ~20% behind plan. Cell lines take 12–18 months to build and 3–6 months to stabilise, and Mercom notes nameplate overstates availability [V].
- **Demand for DCR cells** [E]: rooftop ~10–12 GW per year (PM Surya Ghar pace of 5 lakh installs per month × 3 kW ≈ 18 GW per year at peak), KUSUM ~2–4 GW, CPSU and List-II-covered utility projects ~15–25 GW per year once the 45–50 GW exempt pipeline [V ICRA] is exhausted. Total is ~30–45 GW per year by FY28.
- **Economics:** A DCR module at ~₹25/W against non-DCR ~₹13.6/W [V SMM, ₹88/$] gives a ~₹11/W spread. Making the cell costs ~₹5–6/W (wafer ~₹1.5/W [V TrendForce] + paste, chemicals, power, labour ~₹3–4/W [E]), against an imported cell landed at ~₹5.6/W (US$0.05 × 1.275 duty × ₹88 [E]). The DCR rent is therefore ~₹8–10/W, which matches Premier's ~₹9/W EBITDA per cell-W [E from V].
- **When does it fade?** Once List-II capacity exceeds DCR-required demand by ~20–30%, which is plausibly 2027–28 on ICRA's numbers [E]. List-III (wafers, Jun-2028) then shifts the rent upstream to the few wafer makers. Cell-only margins converge to import parity (~10–15%), unless a cell maker integrates into wafers.
- **Implication by member:** Websol (cell-heavy, 44% OPM) and Premier (30%) have the most earnings at risk from cell-rent normalisation and the most to gain from executing wafer integration first. Waaree (planning ingot-wafer, >₹30,000 cr capex plan [V]) is positioned for List-III. Saatvik and Vikram, with less cell integration, are already in the price war [V Q1 FY27 margins].

### B2. The export door closes: US AD/CVD + Section 232 + tariffs
- Indian module exports to the US went from 232 MW (2022) to 2.05 GW (2023) to 2.30 GW (2024) [V]. The US was 92% of exports in H1-2026 [V]. Waaree's overseas revenue was ₹1,609 cr in Q1 FY27 (~20% of sales) [V].
- Final rates: AD 123.04% (AFA, cash deposit 107.17%) + CVD 126.09% on India-origin cells, "whether or not assembled into modules" [V]. **Indian-cell modules become uneconomic in the US.** Indian modules with third-country (non-India, non-China) cells may still enter but face Section 232 (15% plus MIPs) from Dec-2026 [V headline].
- Second-order effect: export-oriented module capacity, especially the plants built for US non-DCR supply, redirects to India and adds to the domestic glut (ICRA's Nov-2025 warning [V]).
- Mitigants: US-sited manufacturing (Waaree's Texas module plant [K]; Premier/Vikram US plans [K]) captures US 45X credits, and EU and Middle East markets remain open.

### B3. Wind 2.0: localisation moat versus pipeline gap
- **Supply side:** ~24 GW of turbine capacity against ~6 GW installed [V] gives low industry utilisation. ALMM-Wind (Jul-2025) forces vendor localisation, which squeezes Chinese OEMs (~33% FY26 share [snippet]) and favours Suzlon (own IP, blades) and Inox.
- **Demand side:** Wind tenders were 0.8–1.5 GW per quarter in FY26, 2.35 GW in Q1 FY27 and 0 in Jul-2026 [V IWTMA]. Hybrid and FDRE are the real wind channels; FDRE assured-peak cleared at ₹5.99/kWh [V]. C&I drives ~84% of Suzlon's book [V].
- **Economics:** About ₹7–8 cr/MW turnkey and ~₹6–7 cr/MW blended [V/E]. EBITDA of 15–20% gives ~₹1.0–1.3 cr per MW. On Suzlon's 2.5 GW, that is ₹2,500–3,300 cr of EBITDA, consistent with FY26's ₹3,025 cr [V].
- **Risk:** The record FY26 was helped by the ISTS waiver rush. H1-2026 wind was −16% YoY [V DTE]. FY27 guidance assumes H2-loaded delivery (Inox 70–75% in H2 [snippet]). Margin compression is visible: Suzlon Q1 15.6% vs 19.2%, Inox OPM 16–19% vs 20–23% [V].

### B4 (short). The grid supercycle: industrial electricals' valuation premium
CG Power Systems backlog +59% to ₹14,434 cr at a 23.1% PBIT margin [V]. ABB orders +50% in Q2 CY26 [V]. IEEMA power transformer production +21–38% YoY for seven straight quarters [V]. Transmission orders doubled FY22 → FY26 [V ICRA]. Against this, motors are flat (IEEMA rotating machines +0.8% in Q3 FY26 [V]), ABB's EBITA margin compressed to 13%, and CG's semiconductor losses weigh. The ~100x P/E prices in the order-book acceleration converting at higher margins.

## C. Revenue and profit equation, with worked ₹ examples

### C1. The equations
- **Solar module maker:** Revenue = GW sold × ₹/Wp. EBITDA = GW × (₹/Wp − cell cost/Wp − non-cell BOM/Wp − conversion/Wp). Here cell cost = imported landed (US$/W × FX × (1 + BCD + AIDC)) or captive cell cost.
- **Integrated cell+module:** EBITDA/W ≈ (DCR module price − non-DCR price), i.e. the policy rent, + module conversion margin + (import-parity cell price − captive cell cost).
- **Wind OEM:** Revenue = MW delivered × ₹ cr/MW (supply) + EPC + O&M. EBITDA = revenue × margin − LD/warranty provisions. Cash = EBITDA − Δ(net working capital), and the NWC term usually dominates.
- **Industrial electricals:** Revenue_t ≈ opening backlog × burn rate + book-and-bill flow. EBITDA = revenue × (1 − material% − conversion%), where material% tracks copper and CRGO with a 1–2 quarter lag.

### C2. Worked example 1 — a 1 GW standalone module line (imported or bought-in cells) [E]
Assumptions: ₹88/US$; capex ₹120 cr/GW (module line only; [E], consistent with integrated ₹750–850 cr/GW cell+module disclosures [V/snippet] less ~₹600–700 cr/GW for cells); utilisation 70%, so 700 MW sold; non-DCR realisation ₹13.6/W [V SMM US$0.155].
| Line (per W) | ₹/W | ₹ cr on 700 MW |
|---|---|---|
| Realisation | 13.60 | 952 |
| Imported TOPCon cell landed: US$0.050 × 1.275 × 88 [V InfoLink price; duty per Budget-2025] | 5.61 | 393 |
| Non-cell BOM: glass, frame, EVA/POE, backsheet, JB, ribbon [E ~1.1–1.2x China's ~RMB 0.35–0.40/W non-cell content] | 5.40 | 378 |
| Conversion: power, labour, consumables | 0.60 | 42 |
| Freight, warranty provision, SG&A | 0.90 | 63 |
| **EBITDA** | **1.09 (8%)** | **76** |
| Depreciation (₹120 cr / 8 yrs) | 0.21 | 15 |
| EBIT | 0.88 | 62 |
Payback on ₹120 cr capex plus ~₹100 cr working capital ≈ 3 years at this margin. At ₹12.8/W (a 6% price fall) EBITDA goes to ~₹0.3/W and payback exceeds 10 years. **This is why module-only makers' margins (Saatvik 6.6%, Vikram 8% in Q1 FY27 [V]) collapse when prices slip.** And after 1-Jun-2026, most domestic utility demand needs List-II cells, so this line only sells into exempt, export or open-access channels.

| Freight, warranty, SG&A, R&D | 1.50 | 128 |

| Depreciation (₹800 cr / 7 yrs; cell tooling shorter-lived) | 1.34 | 114 |
| EBIT | 8.16 | 694 |
Payback ≈ 1.2–1.5 years at today's DCR premium. Cross-check: Premier's Q1 FY27 EBITDA of ₹759 cr on 844 MW cells and 953 MW modules [V] ≈ ₹8–9/W, and Websol's FY26 OPM is 44% [V]. **Sensitivity:** if the DCR premium halves (realisation ₹17.5/W), EBITDA is ~₹5/W (29%), payback ~2 years. If cells reach import parity (₹14/W), EBITDA is ~₹1.5/W (11%), payback 5+ years. A 1 GW wafer add-on at ~₹400–1,700 cr/GW [snippet; Premier ₹1,700 cr ingot-wafer project] is the next rent pool after List-III.

### C4. Worked example 3 — wind OEM, 1 GW per year of deliveries [E from V]
Realisation ₹6.8 cr/MW blended (Suzlon FY26: ₹16,732 cr ÷ 2,456 MW [V]) gives revenue ₹6,800 cr. Material 65% [V], manufacturing 4%, employees 7%, other 7% gives EBITDA ~17–18%, about ₹1,190 cr (₹1.2 cr/MW). NWC at 117 days (Suzlon) ties up ~₹2,200 cr. At Inox's 443 days it would be ~₹8,250 cr, which is why Inox's ROCE is ~11% against Suzlon's ~34% [V].
