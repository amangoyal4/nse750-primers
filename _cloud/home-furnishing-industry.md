## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Redseer's "organised" is a CHANNEL definition, not a BRAND definition.** This is the single most important trap.
   - In the RHP, "Organized B&M" means sales through "branded, structured retailers and national chains … **with a minimum store area of 2,500 sq. ft**"; "Online" means brand websites plus marketplaces. "Unorganized" means "local vendors, small shops, and unbranded manufacturers" [V RHP p.159].
   - On that basis the **organised mattress share is ~20% (CY19) → ~30% (CY24) → ~45% (CY30P)**, and online is **~4% → ~8% → ~13%** [V RHP].
   - A Sleepwell sold through a 600 sq ft dealer counter is probably "unorganised" in Redseer's split but "organised/branded" in Sheela's or CRISIL's language.
   - CRISIL says unorganised players are "**more than 50%** of the mattress market" and that Sleepwell + Kurlon hold "**29% of the modern mattress market**" [V CRISIL Apr-26]. Sheela's AR claims "**~30% share in the Indian branded mattress segment**" [V AR FY26]. Screener's company note cites "30% market share in India's mattress segment" [V screener].
   - The writer must name the basis every time and never mix "30% organised (Redseer)" with "30% share of branded (Sheela)".
2. **"Mattress market" size depends on the source.** Redseer: **₹145–160 bn (₹14,500–16,000 Cr) in CY2024**, 28–32 mn units, growing 7–9% CAGR since CY19 and 10–12% to **₹270–300 bn by CY2030**. Cotton is ~60% of value, foam ~23% [V RHP]. Older press: "₹20,000 Cr in 2022, organised 40%" [V-snip, unattributed]. Redseer's definition is presumably retail value, but it is not stated whether this includes GST or B2B. **Use Redseer, cite it as "Redseer for Wakefit's RHP", and flag that it was paid for by an interested party.**
3. **Wakefit has three "EBITDA"s; Sheela has two.**
   - Wakefit FY26 has three bases [V Q1 FY27 PPT]:
     - **Reported EBITDA (Ind AS, ex-other income): ₹182.0 Cr (12.2%)**. Screener's "operating profit" is ₹186 Cr (12%).
     - **Reported incl. other income: ₹227.4 Cr.**
     - **"Operating EBITDA" (pre-Ind AS 116 rent, pre-ESOP, pre-one-offs): ₹112.3 Cr (7.5%).** The rent adjustment alone is ₹80.4 Cr.
     - Q1 FY27: reported ₹56.4 Cr (13.9%) vs operating ₹36.8 Cr (9.1%).
     - **For a store-led retailer the pre-Ind AS 116 number is the economically honest one.** Management guides on it: "aspire" to ~7.5% in FY27 [V transcript].
   - Sheela [V Q4 FY26 PPT]:
     - "Core EBITDA" (before forex MTM) FY26 **₹414 Cr (10.8%)** vs reported **₹393 Cr**; screener OP is ₹395 Cr.
     - FY25 core EBITDA **₹283 Cr includes ₹35 Cr paid by KCPL** (Kurlon's former owner) as compensation for inventory/quality issues, booked in other income. Ex that, FY25 core is ~₹248 Cr (7.2%) [E].
     - **From Q1 FY27 the hedge was closed, so core = reported** [V Q1 call].
4. **Wakefit's FY26 PAT of ₹189 Cr includes a ₹98.1 Cr deferred-tax credit.**
   - FY26 PBT was ₹91.1 Cr, after a ₹3.8 Cr exceptional charge for labour-code gratuity/leave. Tax was **−₹98.07 Cr (DTA recognition)** [V PPT].
   - In Q1 FY27 the DTA began to unwind: deferred tax charge ₹7.3 Cr and current tax ₹5.6 Cr. Reported PAT ₹23.4 Cr; **"PAT excluding deferred tax" ₹30.7 Cr (7.6%)** [V].
   - **Screener's P/E of 22.8 is therefore flattered.** TTM PAT ₹193 Cr includes Q4 FY26's ₹122 Cr quarter. Removing the ₹98 Cr DTA credit and adding back the Q1 FY27 deferred-tax charge of ₹7.3 Cr gives an ex-DTA TTM PAT of **~₹102 Cr**, so the P/E on that basis is **~44×** [E].
5. **Sheela's PAT includes Furlenco's equity-accounted profit, itself DTA-boosted.** FY26 JV share was **₹21.4 Cr**: Q4 FY26 ₹14 Cr and Q1 FY27 ₹3 Cr [V AR; PPT]. Roughly ₹9–10 Cr of it is Sheela's share of Furlenco's DTA gain [E: 35% × ₹27 Cr]. The FY26 consolidated PAT of ₹160.85 Cr also benefits from a **tax-expense line inflated by deferred tax ₹44.7 Cr**. So the "cash PAT" Sheela advertises (₹384 Cr) = PAT + deferred tax + depreciation [V PPT].
6. **Sheela "standalone" now means SFL + Kurlon (KEL) merged; "consolidated" adds Joyce (Australia), Interplasp (Spain), Staqo (IT) and the Furlenco JV share.**
   - FY26 standalone revenue ₹2,962 Cr vs consolidated ₹3,821 Cr. The ~₹859 Cr gap = Australia ₹422 Cr + Spain ₹391 Cr + Staqo ₹70 Cr, less eliminations [V PPT].
   - The mattress volume and value KPIs (3,692K units, ₹1,497 Cr) are **standalone India only** [V].
7. **Volume vs value in Q1 FY27.** Sheela's foam revenue rose 26% on 4% volume. Mattress revenue rose 15% on 6% volume. That is TDI/polyol-driven price inflation after the West Asia shock [V].
   - The CFO says gross margin fell 405 bp (consolidated) / 688 bp (standalone). The cause was "the sharp fall in raw material prices during the quarter": prices rose in March, fell from April and rose again from July. High-cost inventory was sold into falling prices [V call].
   - Wakefit's mattress growth (+27.3%) was ~two-thirds volume and one-third price [V call].
8. **Store counts mean different things.**
   - Sheela: "~5,500 brand outlets (SFL+KEL)" = exclusive dealer-owned outlets. "~11,000 dealer touchpoints". "8,400+ U2O dealers" (FY26) → "nearly 10,000" (Q1 FY27). **~42 COCO stores** in Q1 FY27, target 50. "Added 600 net new showrooms in FY26" [V AR; call]. CRISIL's "600 retail showrooms till Dec-2025, target 700 in FY26" is about additions [V CRISIL].
   - Wakefit: **165 COCO stores** (Jun-26; 100 cities), **2,250 MBOs** (701 cities) [V PPT].
   - **"Stores" for Sheela are mostly franchise/dealer outlets; for Wakefit they are company-owned and company-operated, with rent on its own P&L.** Never compare the two counts directly.
9. **Wakefit's channel splits overlap.**
   - "Own channels" (website + COCO) were **72.3%** of Q1 FY27 revenue; "external" (marketplaces + MBOs) **27.7%**.
   - The same quarter split **online 52.7% / offline 47.3%** [V call].
   - Online = own website + marketplaces; offline = COCO + MBOs. Do not add the two splits together.
10. **The Redseer market is calendar-year and in a ₹ range; company data are fiscal and point estimates.** Any "share" computed by this dossier is [E] and carries ±10–15% uncertainty from the range alone.

# PART 1 — INDUSTRY DOSSIER

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Two listed companies, two opposite models, one chemistry.**
  - Sheela Foam is a **petrochemical converter with brands on top**. It is India's largest flexible slabstock PU-foam maker: **1,39,000 MT India capacity, 12 units**; Australia 17,000 MT; Spain 22,000 MT [V AR FY26; CRISIL].
    - About half its India revenue is mattresses (₹1,497 Cr); the rest is B2B/B2C foam (₹1,352 Cr) [V].
    - It sells through dealers: 83% of standalone sales go to dealers/distributors [V AR BRSR].
  - Wakefit is a **vertically integrated D2C home brand**: own factories (5 facilities, 6.99 lakh sq ft), own website, own COCO stores.
    - About 61% of revenue is mattresses, ~29% furniture, ~9% furnishings [V PPT FY26].
    - Its gross margin is **~56–57%** vs Sheela's **~44%**, but it spends the difference on stores (rent), marketing (~7.5% of revenue) and logistics.
  - **Both buy the same two crude-linked chemicals, TDI and polyol.**
    - Sheela: TDI + polyol are **65–70% of COGS** [V CFO, Q1 FY27 call].
    - Wakefit: TDI + polyol are **60–65% of mattress raw material cost**, or **~55% of the mattress selling price** [V CFO/ED, Q1 FY27 call; the two statements differ in base].
- **The combined listed sub-sector is small** (sums of screener rows) [E]:

  |---|---|
  | FY26 revenue | ₹5,310 Cr (+12.7% YoY) |
  | FY26 OP | ₹581 Cr (10.9%) |
  | FY26 PAT | ₹350 Cr (₹252 Cr ex Wakefit DTA) |
  | Q1 FY27 revenue | ₹1,437 Cr (+23.0%) |

  | Q1 FY27 PAT | ₹85 Cr |

  - Both stocks are well off their highs. Sheela trades at ₹543 against a 52-week range of ₹832–457; its 5-year price CAGR is −16% [V screener]. Wakefit trades at ₹135 against an IPO price of ₹195, **−31%** [V].
- **The market is huge, mostly unbranded and growing ~10%.**
  - Redseer: home & furnishings **₹2.8–3.0 trillion (CY24) → ₹5.2–5.9 trillion (CY30), 11–13% CAGR** [V RHP]:

    | Segment | CY24 size | CY30P size | CAGR |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Furniture | ₹1.8–2.0 tn | ₹3.2–3.9 tn | 10–12% |
    | Furnishings & décor | ₹0.79–0.86 tn | ₹1.63–1.81 tn | 12–14% |

  - Organised share (Redseer channel basis): H&F ~29% → ~35% (CY30); mattress ~30% → ~45%; furniture ~26% → ~30% [V].
  - **Per-capita H&F spend is US$24–25, vs ~US$95 in China, ~US$475 in the UK and ~US$600 in the US** [V RHP].
- **2026 was a raw-material shock year, absorbed better than expected.**
  - The West Asia war (from 28-Feb-2026) spiked TDI/polyol in March. Prices eased from April and rose again from about July [V SFL call].
  - Sheela took "commensurate price increases": foam value +26% on volume +4% in Q1 FY27. Its consolidated gross margin still fell 405 bp to 40.6% [V].
  - Wakefit raised prices in the last week of March 2026, cut them nominally in June/July, and **lifted** gross margin to 57.1% (+130 bp) on mix (mattress share 60% → 66%) [V].
  - Sheela's management says the volatility hurts the **unorganised** sector most, because it "produces foam only when it is opportune" [V]. That is the formalisation argument in one line.
- **Sheela's story is now deleveraging plus margin recovery; Wakefit's is store rollout plus earnings quality.**
  - **Sheela:**
    - Net debt fell from ₹699 Cr to **₹543 Cr** (1.3× EBITDA) by Mar-26 [V AR].
    - CRISIL expects the residual Kurlon NCD (₹361 Cr) to be retired by **Oct-2026**. CRISIL rates Sheela **AA-/Positive** (outlook raised Apr-26); India Ratings has **IND AA/Stable** (affirmed Oct-26) [V].
    - Targets: **15% revenue growth and 15% EBITDA margin for FY28**; FY27 guidance 11–12%; ROCE 20–25% in three years [V call].
  - **Wakefit:**
    - Added **27 COCO stores in Q1** to reach 165, targeting ~80 in FY27 [V].
    - Capex ₹100–120 Cr, 80% of it retail and mostly for **two "jumbo" stores in Bengaluru, opening Jun–Sep 2027** [V].
    - Targets **20–25% growth**, holds operating EBITDA ~7.5%, and keeps A&P at **7–8%** [V].

### 1.2 Value chains

1. **Petrochemical → foam (Sheela's base layer).**
   - Toluene → TDI (toluene di-isocyanate, 80:20 isomer grade for flexible foam). Propylene oxide → polyether polyol.
   - TDI + polyol + water/blowing agent + catalysts + silicone surfactant → "slabstock" foam blocks on continuous lines. Variable-pressure foaming (VPF) is used by Sheela, Joyce and Interplasp [V CRISIL].
   - **Indian TDI supply**: GNFC (Gujarat Narmada Valley Fertilizers & Chemicals) is the main domestic producer [K; it was the ADD applicant, V]. Imports come from China (Wanhua, Covestro China), Japan, Korea, the EU (Covestro, BorsodChem) and Saudi Arabia (Sadara) [V ADD notification].
   - **Polyols** are largely imported (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Singapore, China) or made by a few domestic players (Manali Petrochemicals, propylene-oxide/polyol route [K]).
2. **Foam → mattress (both).**
   - Blocks are cut into layers, laminated with coir/spring/latex where needed, covered with quilted fabric (ticking) and edge-taped, then either compressed and roll-packed (D2C) or shipped flat (dealer).
   - The mattress is ~55% TDI/polyol by selling-price value at Wakefit [V call]. The rest is fabric, adhesive, springs, packaging, labour, freight and margin.
3. **Distribution: two archetypes.**
   - **Dealer model (Sheela):** factory → distributor (230+ for U2O) → dealer (~11,000 touchpoints; 8,400–10,000 U2O dealers) → household.
     - Redseer says intermediaries take **20–25% of the end price** [V RHP].
     - Sheela's own disclosures: 83% of standalone sales are to dealers/distributors; the top-10 take 29% of dealer sales [V AR BRSR].
   - **D2C model (Wakefit):** own factory → mother warehouse (Hosur, 1.6 lakh sq ft) → 10 inventory holding points → 24 points of delivery → household. Sales go via website (≈ online own), 165 COCO stores, marketplaces (Amazon, Flipkart, Pepperfry, quick-commerce) and 2,250 MBOs [V PPT].
4. **Furniture.**
   - Engineered-wood (particle board/MDF + laminate) flat-pack lines (Wakefit Hosur, 3.45 lakh sq ft) vs carpenter-built solid wood (unorganised).
   - Links to **plywood-laminates.html** (board and laminate inputs) and **paints.html** (finishes; Asian Paints owns White Teak and has a home-décor push [V RHP mention]).

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **Brand + distribution (Sheela) vs brand + vertical integration + owned channel (Wakefit).** The chemistry is common and globally priced, so the moat is not the foam.
- **Gross margin captures the integration** [V/E]:

  | Business | Gross margin | Period / source |
  |---|---|---|
  | Wakefit (owns the retail margin) | 55.8% | FY26 [V] |
  | Sheela consolidated (dealer margin goes to dealers) | 43.7% | FY26 [V] |
  | Joyce (Australia, foam) | 55% | FY26 [V] |
  | Interplasp (Spain, foam to mattress makers) | 32% | FY26 [V] |
  | Pure B2B foam converter | ~25–35% | [E] |

- **EBITDA margin converges**: Sheela core 10.8%, Wakefit reported 12.2% / operating 7.5% (FY26). Wakefit's extra gross margin goes into rent (~₹80 Cr Ind AS 116 adjustment), A&P (~7.5%), last-mile delivery and installation.
- **Capital intensity differs.**
  - Sheela: fixed assets ₹3,198 Cr, of which ~₹1,612 Cr is goodwill and brands (Kurlon) [V PPT cash-ROCE slide]. Operating capital employed is only ₹1,556 Cr, so the "cash ROCE" of **18%** (EBIT ₹277 Cr) compares with a reported ROCE of 9% [V].
  - Wakefit: net working capital **2.2 days** in FY26 (inventory 109 days offset by payables 108 days, receivables ~1 day; screener) [V]. Its asset base is right-of-use (store leases ₹239 Cr) plus PP&E ₹149 Cr [V PPT BS].

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Replacement-led household demand.** A mattress is replaced every ~8–10 years [primer, K]. With ~30 mn units a year across ~300+ mn households, the effective replacement cycle is longer still (~10+ years) [E].
   - Drivers: income (Redseer: middle-income households 144 mn (46%) CY19 → 174 mn (51%) CY24 → 219 mn (58%) CY30), nuclearisation (~30 mn new nuclear families CY19–24; ~60% of households nuclear, 67–70% by CY30), urbanisation (37% → ~40% by CY30) and female work participation [V RHP].
2. **Housing completions and sales.** New homes are the strongest trigger for a full-home basket (bed + mattress + wardrobe + sofa).
   - Redseer: residential sales 0.23 mn units (CY21) → 0.31 mn (CY22) → 0.35 mn (CY24); the top 8 cities are ~51% [V RHP].
   - **Anarock Q3 2026 (top-7 cities):** sales **~1,00,220 units (+3% YoY, +10% QoQ)**, value ~₹1.55 lakh Cr (+2%), launches **1,14,320 (+18%)**, unsold inventory **6,30,590 (+12%)**, average price **₹9,714/sq ft (+7%)** [V BusinessToday 29-Sep-2026]. The 9M-2026 total and completions data were not found (§H).
   - A housing sale converts into furnishing demand at **possession, ~2–4 years later** [K]. Completions lag sales, so the 2022–24 sales boom is flowing into 2025–27 possessions [E]. The **RBI hike to 5.50% (7-Oct-2026)** is a headwind for 2028–29 possessions, not for today [E].
   - Cross-link: **realty.html**.
3. **Rental and migration.** Over 20% of urban homes are rented [V RHP]. This is Furlenco's market (subscribers +36% YoY in Q1 FY27 [V SFL call]) and that of low-ticket roll-pack mattresses.
4. **Hospitality/institutional B2B.** Hotels refurbish every ~5–7 years. Redseer cites branded hotel openings +8% and new keys +17% (CY19–24) [V]. Mattress B2B is ~18% of value [V].
5. **Formalisation (U2O = "unorganised to organised").** Sheela's U2O network (5- and 6-inch foam mattresses at **~₹2,000–2,100/unit realisation**, analyst estimate on the call) grew **+111% value, +65% volume in FY26** and **+81% value, +19% volume in Q1 FY27** [V AR; call]. Cotton-to-foam substitution in Tier-2+ is the volume pool (cotton ~60% of value) [V RHP].
6. **Premiumisation and sleep-wellness.**
   - Masstige rises 20% → 23%; premium and luxury grow at 15–20% CAGR [V RHP].
   - Wakefit: in offline stores **~half of mattresses sold are premium**, vs **15–20% company-wide** [V call]. COCO AOV is **78.8% higher than website AOV** (H1 FY26) [V RHP].
   - Sheela: "ASP has marginally gone up … some premiumisation" [V call].
7. **Festive seasonality.** **Q3 (Oct–Dec) is the best quarter** for both, and Q4 the next best [V both calls]. Sheela's Q3 FY26 mattress volume was 1,151K vs 914K in Q4 and 859K in Q1 FY27 [V].
8. **Channel shifts.** Online share rises from ~8% to 13% of mattresses by CY30 [V RHP]. Sheela's e-com grew **+49% volume / +52% value in FY26**, brand.com +136%, marketplaces +39% [V AR]. Quick commerce is a channel for both (Wakefit lists "quick commerce platforms" as an external channel [V RHP]). Its share is undisclosed (§H).

**Economics that separate A from B [E]**
- On a ₹10,000 retail mattress, the dealer model passes ~₹2,000–2,500 to the channel. Sheela invoices ~₹7,500–8,000 ex-GST and earns ~44% gross margin on that.

- Pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA ends up similar: **Sheela ~10–11%, Wakefit ~7.5–9%**.
- Wakefit's pitch is that **jumbo stores, rising mattress mix and operating leverage** take it to 14–17% reported (≈ 10–12% operating) in "two to three years" [V call].

Earlier revocations covered acetic acid, methanol and aniline (23-Jul-2025) and fatty acids (22-Oct-2025) [V-snip].
- **TDI and polyol-specific QCOs:** no evidence of a separate TDI or polyol QCO, in force or revoked, was found. Whether TDI/polyol fell within the PU QCO scope is unverified (§H).
- **Net effect:** the Nov-2025 QCO roll-back (a DPIIT/Department of Chemicals push to ease input-cost inflation for MSMEs [K]) **removed a non-tariff barrier on PU imports**. That marginally helps converters (Sheela, Wakefit, Duroflex) and hurts no member.
- **Finished-goods QCOs:** BIS standards exist for furniture (e.g. IS 17631/17632/17633/17635 for chairs, tables and storage), and a Furniture QCO was notified ~2022–23 with phased deadlines [K; FLAG — status unverified]. No mattress QCO was found [E].

### 7.4 Other rules

- **Legal Metrology** (MRP labelling for packaged goods incl. roll-pack mattresses) and **e-commerce rules** (country-of-origin display, dark-pattern guidelines 2023) [K].
- **EPR for plastic packaging**: Sheela reports EPR for polythene [V AR BRSR]. Wakefit uses PU rollers and reusable covers [V PPT].
- **New labour codes**: Wakefit took a **₹3.8 Cr exceptional charge** for gratuity/leave liability "on account of changes in Labour laws" (FY26) [V PPT]. Sheela booked labour-code gratuity charges of ₹4.86 Cr (standalone) / ₹6.15 Cr (consolidated) [V AR notes]. The labour codes took effect in Nov-2025 [K].
- **Coir Board** (Ministry of MSME) promotes coir and coir-mattress exports [K]. This is relevant to Kurlon and Duroflex heritage.
- **Ind AS 116 (leases)** is not a regulation of the sector, but it drives the reported vs operating EBITDA gap for COCO-heavy models (trap 3).

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; timeline in §G)

1. **Pre-2000: cotton and coir.** The market was almost wholly unorganised (cotton gaddas, rubberised coir). **Kurl-on** (Manipal, 1962 [K]) and **Duroflex** (Alappuzha, 1963 [K]) built coir brands in the south.
2. **1971–2000s: the foam era.** Sheela Foam started PU foam production in **Sahibabad (UP) in 1971** [V CRISIL] and built **Sleepwell** into India's best-known foam mattress brand [K]. It became the largest slabstock foam maker.
3. **2005–2019: internationalisation.** Sheela bought **Joyce Foam (Australia) in 2005** [V CRISIL] and **Interplasp (Spain) in Oct-2019** [V CRISIL]. It listed in **Dec-2016**; promoters diluted 14.32% via Polyflex Marketing OFS [V CRISIL].
4. **2016–2021: the D2C insurgency.** **Wakefit** (Bengaluru, 2016), The Sleep Company (2019 [K]), Sleepycat and Duroflex's Sleepyhead built "bed-in-a-box" roll-pack mattresses with 100-night trials. Wakefit was "the first organised player to offer a 100-day free trial" [V RHP]. COVID (2020–21) accelerated online home buying.
5. **FY22–FY25: margin squeeze, then the input spike.** TDI and polyol spiked in **FY22** (post-COVID shortages). Sheela's OPM fell from 15% (FY21) to 11% (FY22) and **7% (FY25)** [V screener]. Wakefit lost ₹107–146 Cr a year in FY22–23 [V].
6. **FY24: consolidation.** Sheela raised ~₹1,200 Cr by QIP [K; equity capital 49 → 54 Cr, V screener]. It acquired **~94.66% of Kurlon for ~₹2,000 Cr** [primer; K] and **17.70% of Furlenco (29-Aug-2023)**, raised to 43.89% in FY25 [V AR]. Borrowings jumped from ₹571 Cr to ₹1,493 Cr [V screener].
7. **FY25: integration year.** Kurlon was a lower-margin business (CRISIL: consolidated OPM 7.3%). Interest was ₹121 Cr. **PAT fell to ₹90 Cr** [V].
8. **FY26: recovery.** Sheela: core EBITDA +46%, PAT +78%, **net debt down to ₹543 Cr**, first dividend [V]. Wakefit **IPO 8–10 Dec 2025 at ₹195** (₹1,289 Cr; 70% OFS) [V], listed 15-Dec-2025 and turned profitable (reported). GST 2.0 (22-Sep-2025) was neutral for the category. The QCO roll-back came on 12-Nov-2025.
9. **FY27 to date: the West Asia shock.** TDI/polyol spiked (Mar-26), eased (Apr–Jun) and re-spiked (Jul) [V]. Both members passed costs through. Sheela's Q1 gross margin fell 405 bp; Wakefit's rose 130 bp on mix. RBI hiked to 5.50% (7-Oct-26).
10. **Cycle signature:** mattress makers are **"spread" businesses with ~1-quarter pricing lags**. Margins compress in input-up quarters and expand in input-down quarters. Inventory-holding firms gain on the way up and lose on the way down (Sheela's international units gained in Q1 FY27 for this reason [V call]).

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Roll-pack and compressible foam** turned mattresses into parcels and broke the dealer monopoly [V RHP]. It is now table stakes: Sheela sells e-com-exclusive SKUs [V Q1 PPT].
2. **Sleep-tech:** Wakefit's "Zense" ecosystem includes Regul8 (temperature regulation) and Track8 (contactless tracker) [V RHP]. The global precedent (Sleep Number "smart beds") shows that tech alone does not protect margins.
3. **Materials:** bio-based polyols, soy foams, polyolefin foams, natural latex and recycled polyol are emerging substitutes. CRISIL lists them as a threat [V]. Sheela is doing R&D on recycled-polyol for shoes [V AR].
4. **Manufacturing automation:** Wakefit uses robotic arms, roller belts, CNC and "Every Part Every Interval" (EPEI) [V PPT]. Sheela uses VPF foaming for yield [V CRISIL].
5. **AI marketing:** Sleepwell ran AI-generated personalised ads for 2,000+ dealers (Q1 FY27) [V PPT].
6. **Channel disruption:** quick commerce (10-minute delivery of pillows and small furnishings), marketplace pricing cycles (Wakefit's external channels fell 15–20% for two quarters, then grew 7.6% in Q1 FY27 [V]) and rental/subscription (Furlenco).
7. **Mattress toppers** as a cheap substitute: Wakefit's CEO says they never got traction because opening-price mattresses cost ~₹5,000 single size [V call].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert leads with)

1. **Formalisation of a mostly unbranded market.** Cotton gaddas are ~60% of mattress value; Redseer-"organised" is only ~30% and heading to ~45% by CY30. Every point of share moving from local makers to brands is ~₹150 Cr of mattress revenue today [E]. Sheela's U2O network (+111% value FY26) and Wakefit's ₹5,000 opening-price roll-packs attack this pool from opposite ends.
2. **D2C vs dealer, converging to omnichannel.** Wakefit is going offline: 165 COCO stores, 2,250 MBOs, 47% of revenue offline. Sheela is going online (brand.com +136%) and opening COCO stores (42 → 50). The winner is whoever builds the cheaper omnichannel. The dealer moat is dense but leaks margin (20–25% to intermediaries); the D2C model keeps the margin but pays rent and CAC.
3. **Crude-linked spread (TDI + polyol ≈ 2/3 of COGS).** The West Asia shock (28-Feb-2026) and its July re-spike are the live test. Sheela's gross margin fell 405 bp in Q1 FY27; Wakefit's rose on mix. The member with the higher gross margin and faster repricing (D2C) is structurally less exposed per rupee of revenue.
4. **Trade policy on inputs, not outputs.** Anti-dumping duty on TDI (EU/Saudi continued to 2031; China/Japan/Korea under review) and the Nov-2025 revocation of the PU QCO decide landed input cost. Members lobby **against** protection, the reverse of most chemical sub-sectors.
5. **Online and quick-commerce channels.** Online is ~8% of mattresses, heading to 13% [V RHP]. Marketplaces run their own profit/growth cycles (Wakefit external −15–20% for two quarters, then +7.6%). Brand.com is where margin lives.
6. **Housing completions and rates.** New homes trigger whole-home baskets. Anarock Q3-2026 sales +3% and launches +18%, but unsold inventory is up 12% and the repo was hiked to 5.50%.
7. **Store rollouts and A&P intensity.** Wakefit spends A&P ~7.5% and targets ~80 stores a year. Sheela spends ~5.2% A&P and adds 600 dealer showrooms a year. A&P is a competitive arms race whenever a new brand raises money (Wakefit CEO [V]).
8. **Kurlon integration and Sheela's margin climb.** About 80% of synergies have been realised (CRISIL). Core margin went 7.2% (ex-KCPL) → 10.8% → 10.6% (Q1). The target is 15% in FY28. The Kurlon NCDs are being retired by Oct-2026.
9. **Wakefit's path to profit vs its IPO valuation.** The IPO valued it at ₹6,373 Cr (post-issue). It now sits at ₹4,478 Cr (−30%). Operating EBITDA is 7.5–9%, and PAT ex-DTA is ~₹102 Cr TTM. The lock-ins have expired (Jun-26) and FIIs have sold (19.5% → 14.4%).
10. **GST neutrality.** GST 2.0 cut durables, not mattresses or furniture (18% stays), so the category gained only indirectly.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Mattress maker:**

> Revenue = Units × Realisation (ASP after channel margin)
> Gross profit = Revenue − [foam kg/unit × (TDI share × TDI price + polyol share × polyol price + additives)] − fabric/springs/packaging
> EBITDA = Gross profit − channel cost (dealer schemes and A&P, **or** rent + A&P + logistics) − employee − overhead

**D2C variant:**

> Revenue = Stores × Revenue/store + Online orders × AOV
> Store contribution = Store revenue × gross margin − rent − staff − local A&P

#### (a) Management-guidance digest — Q1 FY27 call, 5-Aug-2026 (S2) [V; paraphrased]

Participants: Rahul Gautam (CMD), Tushaar Gautam (Vice-Chairman & Jt MD), Rakesh Chahar (Deputy MD), Amit Kumar Gupta (Group CFO). Host: Dolat Capital.

**Guidance and targets**
- **FY27 revenue growth ">15%"** (Q1 standalone was +20%, helped by extra foam sales while weaker players lacked raw material). Double-digit volume growth in **both** mattresses and foam for the full year; Q3 (festive) best, Q4 next.
- **FY27 EBITDA margin 11–12%.** The CFO explicitly denied ever guiding 12–13% for FY27. "Even if [gross margin] goes up by 2-odd percent, we would be there … it is simply volatility to go away."
- **FY28: 15% EBITDA margin** with ~15% growth ("we have a target of 15% EBITDA margin for the next year"). **ROCE 20–25% within ~3 years** (currently "~10%-odd").
- **Volatility horizon:** the CMD expects raw-material volatility "till October or even November" 2026, and for a couple of months after the war ends, because Suez/Hormuz disruptions broke supply chains. The CFO said raw-material prices swung **+40% to −20% within the quarter**.
- **Balance sheet:** FY27 free cash flow of **₹150–200 Cr** goes to debt. India debt "a little above ₹300 Cr", overseas ~₹350 Cr (self-liquidating over 5 years). India will need "another 1 year" to be debt-free; the balance sheet will **not** be debt-free at end-FY27.
- **Kurlon synergy:** ₹40 Cr still pending; the new machine is installed; "full impact … in quarter 3". Integration "96–97%" complete; operating units cut **from 21 to 12**; synergies "75–80%" realised.
- **International:** FY26 international revenue ~₹800–850 Cr; **FY27 ">₹1,000 Cr" with EBITDA "around ₹120-odd Cr"**. Long-run: ~5% local-currency growth, 10–12% margins. Q1 combined margin ~13–13.5% was inflated by cheaper inventory and slower price changes abroad; it normalises in Q2. Spain did **EUR 12 mn** in Q1 vs a normal EUR 9–10 mn.

**Top analyst Q&A points**
1. *Mattress volume +6% only:* the CMD said "sub-10% would be happiness … the industry, according to us, has grown closer to 5%". The CFO blamed the price hikes: "if the price had been like 10–15% … the volume growth would have been double digit".
2. *U2O price/mix:* launched at too low a price; channel margins were thin, so prices were raised and a second, higher model added. The analyst estimated **~₹2,000–2,100 per U2O mattress**; management did not confirm and will not split volumes by sub-category. U2O now has **~10,000 dealer points**.
3. *Gross-margin hit by segment:* foam was hit harder than mattresses because foam uses proportionally more TDI/polyol. TDI + polyol = **65–70% of COGS**.
4. *Inventory policy:* India carries **15–30 days** of raw material; Australia and Spain carry longer inventory, which helped in Q1. Q4 FY26 employee-cost spike = incentive provisions trued up in Q4.
5. *Furlenco:* operating PAT ~₹9 Cr a quarter; Sheela books ~35% of it; DTA recognition, if any, comes in Q4. Furlenco is "premium" vs RentoMojo (which filed a DRHP) "mass/utility".
6. *COCO stores:* 22 legacy stores upgraded + new → **~42 operating, target 50**. **Capex ₹27 lakh + working capital ₹20–22 lakh ≈ ₹50 lakh per COCO store**; ~2,000 sq ft (1,800–3,000); 50 stores ≈ ₹25 Cr. Franchise stores cost Sheela very little.
7. *Market share:* "if we look at the organised mattress market, we would have a combined share of close to 20%" (CMD). No new mattress sub-segments planned; growth to come from luxury, pillows/toppers/bedding accessories and Furlenco-designed furniture (sofa beds launched under Sleepwell and Kurlon). Furlenco shop-in-shops in 40–50 stores, first target 100; "3,500 exclusive brand outlets" (CFO).
8. *Premiumisation:* "ASP has marginally gone up … some premiumisation".

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed

1. **The 2026 shock was a TDI supply squeeze layered on crude.** GNFC's own TDI–toluene spread doubled (₹81 → ₹167/kg) as GNFC curtailed output on gas economics and Bharuch TDI went down in July, while imports were disrupted; India imports ~58% of its TDI (FY26: 79.6k t, China 37%). Converters were simultaneously paying ADD on four of the five main import origins, and DGTR has just (30-Sep-2026) opened a review that could extend the China/Japan/Korea duty to 2032. Expect Sheela, Duroflex and Wakefit to file as opposing interested parties again.
2. **FY26 was a deflation year for inputs, not just an integration year.** Sheela's TDI cost fell from ₹201 to ₹172/kg and polyol from ₹126 to ₹107/kg between Q2 FY25 and Q2 FY26; TDI import unit values fell ~28% from FY23 to FY26. A large part of Sheela's +261 bp core-margin gain and Wakefit's swing to profit rode that tailwind; FY27 reverses it. The writer should not present FY26 margins as fully "structural".
3. **Wakefit is far cash-richer than Part 2 assumed and has not yet spent its IPO money.** ₹958.6 Cr of investable cash against ₹4,478 Cr m-cap (21%), with only ₹10 Cr of IPO proceeds used by Mar-26. Treasury income (~₹50 Cr TTM) is ~45% of TTM PBT; "core" P/E ex-DTA ex-treasury is ~70×. The IPO-funded ₹108 Cr marketing and ₹161 Cr rent budgets start hitting the P&L in FY27–28, which is why management guides operating margin flat at ~7.5% despite Q1's 9.1%.
4. **Store economics are now disclosed and look good — but on mattress-first mini stores.** ~10–11-month payback, ₹4–5 lakh inventory per store and a 2.7–3× town-level uplift explain the 80-a-year rollout. The risk is mix: ~60 of 165 stores are bedding-first minis; furniture growth (the TAM story) now depends on two 1 lakh-sq-ft jumbo stores in Bengaluru (mid-2027) with an 18-month break-even target.
5. **Sheela's reported quarters carry non-recurring help.** Q4 FY26's ₹92 Cr PAT included a ₹16 Cr exceptional gain and ~₹14 Cr of JV share (mostly Furlenco's DTA); Q1 FY27 included a ₹6 Cr exceptional gain. FY26's lower depreciation (₹179 Cr vs guided ₹140–145 Cr in FY27) also reflects a policy shift to useful-life depreciation, not just asset roll-off. Underlying quarterly PAT run-rate is ~₹55–60 Cr.
6. **Sheela is a seller of its international arm, and Furlenco is a monetisation option.** The CMD says Sheela is "open" to selling Joyce/Interplasp (~₹800–1,000 Cr revenue, ~10–12% EBITDA), and the Furlenco IPO decision is pending (~43% fully diluted; ₹441.6 Cr carrying value; FY27 revenue plan ₹500 Cr). Either would accelerate debt-freedom and lift reported ROCE toward the 20–25% target.
7. **The two managements' own market-share estimates sum to only ~30% of "organised"** (Sheela ~20%, Wakefit ~10%), well below Part 1's 45–50% of Redseer-organised. Their "organised" is broader (it includes dealer counters and regional brands), and Sheela's CFO calls ~85% of the market unorganised. The writer should present the market as **fragmented even at the organised end**, contrary to Part 1's "top-3 oligopoly" framing, or show both readings with their bases.
8. **Price discipline is industry-wide and fast.** Major brands' consumer price increases in Mar–Apr 2026 landed within 1–3% of each other (Wakefit ED), and both companies cut nominally in June. The new competitive pressure comes from ₹100–300 Cr marketplace labels, not from the listed peers.
