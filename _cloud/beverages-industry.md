## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Calendar year, not March year.** VBL's "FY2025" or "CY2025" is Jan–Dec 2025. Its Q2 is the Indian peak season (Apr–Jun), which is everyone else's Q1 FY. **VBL Q2 CY26 ≈ HCCB/Tata Consumer/RCPL Q1 FY27.** Never compare VBL CY25 with an Indian peer's FY25 without saying the periods are offset by a quarter. VBL calls its year "Financial Year 2026" in filings — the writer should keep "CY" labels to avoid confusion.
2. **Seasonality is extreme; never annualise a quarter.** Q2 (Apr–Jun) carried **32.1% of CY25 volume and 39% of CY25 operating profit** (389.7 of 1,213.1 mn cases; ₹1,998 Cr of ₹5,070 Cr OP) [E from V]. Q4 (Oct–Dec) is the trough: OPM 15% vs 28% in Q2 [V screener]. Year-on-year comparisons are the only meaningful quarterly comparisons, and even they are hostage to the weather (see force A1).
3. **Unit case = 5.678 L of finished beverage, counted in 8-oz equivalents.** Pack upsizing (e.g. ₹20 pack moved from 250 ml to 400 ml in 2025–26 in response to Campa [V call]) **raises reported case volume without any more transactions.** Management: "everything is tracked in 8-ounce basis … if our realization has not come down per 8-ounce case … that is immaterial" [V call]. So CY26 volume growth flatters "number of bottles sold"; realisation per case falls in India (−1.5% Q1, −0.6% Q2 CY26) [V]. Water cases (cheap per litre) and Sting cases (expensive per litre) count the same.
4. **Net revenue is net of excise duty and GST.** VBL reports "Revenue from operations" (gross of excise but net of GST), then "Net Revenues" after deducting excise (₹540 Cr in CY25; ₹199 Cr in Q2 CY26 — mostly African excise/sugar levies) [V]. Screener "Sales" = net revenue (₹21,685 Cr CY25) [V]. The AR geographic split uses **gross** revenue (₹22,226 Cr) [V]. RCPL's Campa "₹4,700 Cr" is **gross sales** (likely incl. GST and/or before trade schemes) — **not comparable** to VBL's net revenue [V indianretailer].
5. **Consolidated vs standalone = Group vs India (almost).** Standalone VBL ≈ the India business (plus Indian investment holdings). CY25: standalone revenue ₹14,557 Cr (67%), OP ₹3,765 Cr (25.9%), PAT ₹2,677 Cr; consolidated ₹21,685 / ₹5,070 (23.4%) / ₹3,062 Cr [V screener]. **International ≈ consolidated − standalone: revenue ~₹7,128 Cr, OP ~₹1,305 Cr (~18%)** [E; ignores small eliminations and Indian subsidiaries]. The AR's gross geographic split is India ₹14,449 Cr / outside ₹7,776 Cr (65/35) [V].
6. **Acquisitions inflate growth: BevCo (CY24), Twizza (from 18-Mar-2026), Kenya (pending).**
   - **BevCo** (South Africa, Lesotho, Eswatini) was consolidated during **CY24** — international volume jumped **176 → 303 mn cases (+72%)** in CY24 [V volume chart]. CY24's consolidated +23% volume and +25% revenue are mostly inorganic [E]. Primer dates BevCo to "2023" — the agreement may be 2023 but consolidation is CY24 (see §3.4).
   - **Twizza** (South Africa; 3 plants) consolidated from **18-Mar-2026** at EV **ZAR 2,053 mn** (₹1,131 Cr inorganic capex) [V]. It added **11.8 mn cases in Q2 CY26** [V]: international volume +38.4% reported, **~+25% ex-Twizza** [E]. Twizza runs at "lower margins" and cut group EBITDA margin by 76 bp in Q2 [V].
   - **Kenya (Devyani Food Industries, US$32 mn)** — signed 6-Jul-2026, not yet in numbers [V].
   - **CY26 consolidated growth through Q4 CY26 will include ~3 quarters of Twizza** [E]. Always quote India (standalone) growth alongside.
7. **"Net debt free" is true for India, not for the group (since Mar-2026).** India free cash ~₹1,494 Cr (Jun-26) vs **consolidated net debt ~₹373 Cr** (Jun-26; Dec-25: ₹25.6 Cr) [V]. Screener's borrowings (₹2,508 Cr, Dec-25) exclude lease liabilities (₹484 Cr Dec-25; ₹542 Cr Jun-26) [V]. Use the company's net debt in prose.
8. **Realisation per case has TWO company bases — check which one before comparing.**
   - The **annual and Q4** figures VBL quotes in its press text are **total net revenue ÷ cases**: CY25 ₹178.8 = ₹21,685 Cr ÷ 1,213.1 mn; Q4 CY25 ₹177.3 = ₹4,204 Cr ÷ 237.1 mn [V arithmetic matches exactly].
   - The **quarterly table "Realization per case – Beverages"** (Q1/Q2 presentations) uses **beverage revenue only**: Q2 CY26 ₹175.3 = ₹8,181.9 Cr ÷ 466.7 mn; Q2 CY25 ₹173.2; Q1 CY26 ₹174.1; Q1 CY25 ₹171.4 [V]. On the total-revenue basis Q2 CY26 would be ~₹181 [E].
   - So CY25 beverage-only realisation is ~₹170–171 [E: (₹21,685 Cr − ~₹1,000 Cr non-beverage) ÷ 1,213 mn], not ₹178.8. **Our primer's "₹178.8/case" is the total-revenue basis** — fine, but never put it next to the quarterly ₹175.3.
   - **India realisation (~₹168–170/case, beverage basis) is below international (~₹190/case)** [V/E] — a mix shift to Africa lifts consolidated realisation without any price increase.
9. **Gross margin = net revenue − (materials + traded goods ± inventory change).** VBL's 55.0–55.5% gross margin [V] **includes the concentrate it buys from PepsiCo**. A brand owner (PepsiCo/KO) reports 55–60% gross margins on concentrate; a bottler's 55% is on finished drinks. Not comparable across the two archetypes (see §6).
10. **"Market share" claims are unaudited and basis-less.** There is no Nielsen-equivalent public series. VBL "does not go into market share" [V call]; PepsiCo says it "held or gained" India share [V-snip]; Campa claims "double-digit share in key markets" and "India's #4 CSD brand" [V]; Coca-Cola has historically claimed ~55–60% of Indian CSD by value (Thums Up + Coke + Sprite + Limca + Fanta) [K]. Present every share as "company claim" or as a range [E].

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **India is a low-consumption, high-growth, weather-hostage soft-drink market that has just turned into a three-way price war.**
  - Per-capita consumption of packaged RTD beverages is **15–20 litres/year in India vs 100–120 in the US, 70–80 in China and 60–70 in the UK** (Redseer, Apr-2026) [V IANS]. India's RTD market was **~$20 bn in 2025 and is expected to double to ~$40 bn by 2030** (Redseer; implies ~15% CAGR) [V]. Other consultancies put "non-alcoholic beverages" at **$22–35 bn (2025)** with 7–8% CAGRs (IMARC $34.7 bn; Kenresearch $35.5 bn; Nexdigm $22.5 bn) [V-snip] — the spread is definitional (whether loose tea/coffee, dairy, bulk water are in).
  - **Three organised CSD systems now**: PepsiCo (≈90% bottled by VBL), Coca-Cola (HCCB + ~10 franchise bottlers after 2024–25 refranchising), and **Reliance's Campa** (relaunched 2023; **FY26 gross sales >₹4,700 Cr; Q1 FY27 ₹2,900 Cr**; claims #4 CSD brand) [V]. VBL's chairman estimated **industry growth "~20% or so" in H1 2026** because VBL grew ~15%, Campa "50% or doubling", and Coke also grew [V call].
- **VBL is the scale operator of the PepsiCo system, now with a 23-year (to 2049) India licence and an African second leg.**
  - **CY25:** 1,213 mn cases (+7.9%), net revenue **₹21,685 Cr (+8.4%)**, EBITDA **₹5,049 Cr (23.3%)**, PAT **₹3,062 Cr (+16.2%)**, gross margin 55.2% [V]. India volume grew only **+2.2% (821 → 839 mn cases)** in a rain-hit year; international **+23.4% (303 → 374)** [V].
  - **H1 CY26:** 830 mn cases (India 607, +14.4%; international 223, +~30% incl. Twizza (H1 CY25: 171.7)), net revenue ₹15,025 Cr (+19.4%), EBITDA ₹3,872 Cr (25.8%), PAT ₹2,404 Cr (+16.9%) [V].
  - **Q2 CY26:** 466.7 mn cases (+19.8%; India +14.4%, international +38.4%), net revenue ₹8,451 Cr (+20.4%), EBITDA ₹2,343 Cr (27.7%, −76 bp), PAT ₹1,525 Cr (+15.1%) [V]. India: April "about flat" (rain), March and May–July "healthy twenties" [V call].
- **The stock has de-rated hard even as the business accelerated.** Price **₹393** (52-week range ₹381–556), m-cap **₹1,32,702 Cr**, **P/E 39.2× TTM** (₹3,409 Cr TTM PAT), P/B 6.8×, EV/EBITDA ~23.6× TTM [V screener; E]. Our primer (Jul-26) had ₹1.72 L Cr and ~54×. 1-year price −15%, 3-year CAGR +2% [V screener]. FIIs have cut from **26.6% (Dec-23) to 17.5% (Sep-26)**; DIIs rose from 3.6% to 16.3% [V]. The market is pricing (i) Campa's price war and the ₹10 tier VBL refuses to fight, (ii) the West Asia input-cost shock, (iii) the FSSAI "energy" label order against Sting, (iv) African currency/consolidation risk, and (v) a promoter-group related-party pattern (Devyani Kenya; alcobev talk).
- **The forces, in order of how much they move the numbers:** weather (±10–15 pts of India volume growth in a season); competitive intensity (Campa, Coke's refranchised FBOs); input costs (sugar, PET, energy, freight; partly offset by VBL's backward integration and low/no-sugar mix of ~73%); capacity and cold-chain reach (53 plants; visi-coolers); Africa (scale, currencies, sugar taxes); PepsiCo terms (fixed to 2049; concentrate pricing); and tax/regulation (GST 40% on CSD/caffeinated; FSSAI labelling).

### 1.2 Value chain (inputs → shelf)

1. **Brand owner / concentrate maker** (PepsiCo India; Coca-Cola India Pvt Ltd; Reliance for Campa): owns trademarks, recipes, national advertising (ATL); sells concentrate (or "beverage base") to bottlers. Coca-Cola India Pvt Ltd's FY25 revenue was **₹5,043 Cr (+7%) and PAT ₹615 Cr (+46%)** [V-snip RoC via press] — that is roughly the size of the Indian concentrate "toll" on the Coke system.
2. **Bottler** (VBL; HCCB; Kandhari, Moon, SLMG, Ludhiana Beverages, Brindavan etc. [K]; Reliance's contract bottlers and own plants): buys concentrate, sugar, water treatment, CO₂, PET resin/preforms, closures, labels, cans, glass, corrugate; runs filling lines; owns trucks and visi-coolers; manages distributors and outlets (BTL). VBL describes itself as "demand delivery" vs PepsiCo's "demand creation" [V].
3. **Backward integration (bottler-owned packaging):** preforms, closures, shrink film, crowns, corrugated boxes, pallets, and now **visi-coolers** (VBL bought 50% of Everest Industrial Lanka and formed JV White Peak Refrigeration in India, CY25) [V]. VBL has **3 exclusive + 19 integrated backward-integration plants** (Jun-26; was 3 + 16 in Dec-25) [V]. rPET: ~22,000 t used in CY25; Indorama JV for recycled PET [V].
4. **Distribution:** bottler → (owned depots) → primary distributors/super-stockists → retailers (kirana, paan shops, eateries, modern trade, HoReCa) and increasingly **quick commerce** (RTD sales on Q-comm grew ">100%", Redseer) [V]. Cold chain at outlet = branded visi-cooler (bottler capex, placed free with the retailer).
5. **Retail:** price points dominate (₹10, ₹20, ₹40/₹45, ₹90–100 for 2.25 L), and the retailer's margin per pack decides shelf/cooler space — Campa's entry weapon (§5).

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **Brand owner:** highest return on capital (concentrate is asset-light; KO's operating margin **33.4% in 2025**) [cmc]. In India, PepsiCo's and KO's local brand companies earn a toll on every case plus their own marketing economics.
- **Bottler:** volume × capacity utilisation business with 15–28% EBITDA margins; returns depend on plant utilisation in a 4-month peak, backward integration and route-to-market density. VBL ROCE **27–29% in CY22–23, 25% CY24, 20% CY25** [V screener] — high for a bottler (global bottlers typically 10–15% ROIC [K]).
- **Retail/trade:** captures 10–25% of the shelf price [E]; the war since 2023 is largely a war over trade margins.
- **Government:** GST of **40% on CSD/caffeinated/sugared drinks** (unchanged in effective terms after GST 2.0: 28% + 12% cess was also 40%) is the single largest slice of the consumer rupee on a cola [V/K] — see §7.

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Weather (the dominant short-run driver).** Summer months contribute **"more than half" of annual soft-drink volume** (industry, 2014 report) [V medianews]; for VBL, Q2 alone is ~32–35% and Q1+Q2 ~58% [V/E]. **May used to be ~15% of VBL's annual business (2024 and before), but in 2025 and 2026 May has not contributed 15%** because of rain [V call, Varun Jaipuria]. 2026: April "heavily rained", flat; March, May, June, July "20%+" [V]. Forecasts called for a hot, dry El Niño year (US CPC 61% El Niño probability May–Jul 2026 [V-snip]); by late June all-India monsoon rainfall was **42% below normal** with multi-day heat waves (>45 °C) across central, north-west and east India [V Drishti, 23-Jun-2026]; Skymet put 1-Jun to 31-Jul rainfall **13% below normal** [V-snip]. **A dry, hot monsoon is the setup for a strong Q3 CY26 (Jul–Sep) print on 30-Oct.**
2. **Price points and affordability.** ₹10 (Campa 200 ml → now 150 ml; Coke 200 ml cut from ₹15 to ₹10 in 2023 in Campa states), ₹20 (VBL upsized 250 → 400 ml; Campa 500 ml at ₹20), ₹40–45 (600–750 ml), 1.25–2.25 L take-home [V call; V-snip]. Chairman: "when 2 people are sharing a Rs. 20 pack it effectively works out to Rs. 10 per person" [V].
3. **Distribution reach and chilling.** Outlet count and visi-coolers drive impulse sales; electrification is the rural constraint (VBL AR) [V]. VBL spent ~₹400 Cr on "market infrastructure" (visi-coolers, glass bottles, pallets, vehicles) in H1 CY26 alone [V]. RCPL claims **>3 mn outlets and >5,000 distributors** [V]. Our primer: VBL ~4 mn outlets, ~1.1 mn visi-coolers, ~2,800 distributors, ~12,000 trucks [primer; not re-verified].
4. **Rural and small-town penetration.** Rural is under-penetrated and price-sensitive; it is where ₹10 packs and regional B-brands live, and where Campa recruits "new people … in the category" [V call].
5. **Health and sugar.** The shift to low/no-sugar recipes (VBL 73% of volume, H1 CY26) [V] is both a regulatory hedge and a cost lever (sugar is the largest non-packaging input). Demand for juices, hydration (Nimbooz +30%), VAD (+40%) and functional drinks is growing "3x–4x" the company average [V call].
6. **Youth and on-the-go consumption.** Energy drinks were the fastest-growing CSD sub-segment 2022–25 (Sting), until the FSSAI "energy" label order (1-Jul-2026) caused a temporary dip in June–July [V call].
7. **Channel shift:** quick commerce RTD sales +100%; Q-comm grocery ~$4 bn → ~$25 bn by 2030 (Redseer) [V]. Favours chilled single-serve and premium SKUs.
8. **Income and urbanisation:** standard; VBL AR cites rising women's workforce participation and urban on-the-go formats [V].
9. **Africa (for VBL):** population growth, low per-capita, sugar taxes (South Africa's Health Promotion Levy [K]; Zimbabwe's sugar tax hit VBL in 2025 [V call]), currency volatility.

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **1977–1993 — the protected years.** Coca-Cola leaves India in 1977 (FERA, formula disclosure demand); Parle's Thums Up, Limca, Gold Spot dominate; Campa Cola (Pure Drinks) is the national cola [K].
2. **1988–1993 — liberalisation.** PepsiCo enters (1988, JV with Punjab Agro and Voltas; "Lehar Pepsi"); Coca-Cola returns in 1993 and buys Parle's brands (Thums Up, Limca, Gold Spot, Maaza, Citra) [K]. Campa fades.
3. **1990s — bottler consolidation.** Ravi Jaipuria's group, a Coke bottler, switches to PepsiCo in the 1990s; **VBL incorporated 1995** [K]. Coca-Cola consolidates bottlers into HCCB (1997) [K].
4. **2003–2006 — the pesticide controversy** (CSE reports) hit volumes; Kerala's Plachimada plant closure (2004) [K].
5. **2015–2019 — PepsiCo refranchises to VBL.** VBL IPO **Nov-2016** [V DRHP link Nov-2016]; PepsiCo hands VBL North/East territories (2017–18) and **South & West India (2019)**, making VBL ~90% of PepsiCo India volume [primer; V "90%+"].
6. **2017 — GST** at 28% + 12% cess on aerated drinks (40%) [K].
7. **2018–2020 — Africa:** Morocco, Zambia, Zimbabwe acquisitions [primer].
8. **2020 — COVID**; VBL volume falls to 425 mn cases [V].
9. **2021–2024 — the Sting boom and super-cycle:** VBL volume 425 → 1,124 mn cases; EBITDA ₹1,215 → ₹4,818 Cr [V]; bonus issues, ₹7,500 Cr QIP (Nov-2024) used to repay debt [V AR "QIP"; amount K]; DRC greenfield; **BevCo (South Africa) consolidated in CY24** [V volume jump].
10. **2022–2023 — Reliance buys and relaunches Campa** (Aug-2022 / Mar-2023) at ₹10; Coke cuts prices in Campa states [V-snip; K].
11. **2024–2025 — Coca-Cola refranchises** (Rajasthan, Bihar, NE/WB, Jharkhand, Gujarat) and sells 40% of HCCB's parent to Jubilant Bhartia (completed Jul-2025) [V].
12. **2025 — the rain year:** VBL India volume +2.2% [V]; GST 2.0 (22-Sep-2025) [V]; CRISIL upgrade to AAA [V].
13. **2026 — the war year:** West Asia war (from 28-Feb) spikes crude, PET, PP, freight [V]; Campa scales (₹2,900 Cr in a quarter) [V]; VBL's PepsiCo India EBA extended to 2049 and freed from the SPV clause (21-May) [V]; Twizza consolidated (18-Mar) [V]; CALPIS alliance (18-Jun) [V]; FSSAI "energy" order (1-Jul) [V]; Kenya deal (6-Jul) [V]; COO exit (24-Sep) [V]; VBL stock near its 52-week low (₹381) [V].

**Cycle shape:** the Indian soft-drink "cycle" is not a price cycle but a **weather + competitive-intensity** cycle, layered on structural per-capita growth. Good years (2022, 2024) see +25–40% India volume; bad years (2025) +2%. Margins are steadier (VBL EBITDA margin 18.6–23.5% 2020–25) [V].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **A1. Weather is the swing factor; structure is the trend.** India soft-drink volumes swing between +2% (2025) and +25–40% (2022, 2024) on the summer alone [V VBL volumes]. In 2026, April rain flattened VBL India volume for the month while March and May–July grew 20%+ [V call]; late-June monsoon rain was 42% below normal with heat waves [V]. Seasonality itself is moving (May's share falling) [V]. An analyst models volume as "trend per-capita growth × weather index", and never reads one quarter as a trend.
2. **A2. The Campa price war (Reliance as the new third system).** Reliance relaunched Campa at ₹10 with fat retailer margins; by Q1 FY27 Campa sold ₹2,900 Cr in a quarter (vs >₹4,700 Cr gross in all of FY26) and claims to be India's #4 CSD brand [V]. Incumbents responded with value-per-rupee (VBL upsized its ₹20 pack from 250 ml to 400 ml) rather than chasing ₹10 ("a non-profitable category for us") [V]. Campa has itself started to take price (₹10 pack shrunk to 150 ml) [V]. The war compresses India realisation (VBL India −1.5% Q1, −0.6% Q2 CY26) but so far not VBL's India margin (+112 bp Q1, +38 bp Q2) [V].
3. **A3. The franchise-bottler model is winning — Coca-Cola is copying PepsiCo.** PepsiCo handed ~90% of India to VBL by 2019; Coca-Cola refranchised Rajasthan, Bihar, NE/WB, Jharkhand and Gujarat to Kandhari/SLMG/Moon in 2024–25 and sold 40% of HCCB's parent to Jubilant Bhartia (Jul-2025) [V]. Family-owned FBOs are hungrier locally. HCCB's 13.2% EBITDA margin vs VBL India's ~26% shows the prize from execution [V]. VBL's PepsiCo India licence now runs to **2049** with the SPV-only clause removed (May-2026) [V].
4. **A4. Input costs vs backward integration and de-sugaring.** Sugar, PET, closures, glass, freight and energy all rose in 2026 (PET +20%, PP +30%, LPG +65%, crude basket $112 at peak) [V]. VBL's 22 backward-integration plants, pre-season stock-up, average-cost inventory accounting and ~73% low/no-sugar mix kept gross margin at 55.0% in Q2 CY26 [V]. The remaining cost wave lands in Q3 CY26 [V call].
5. **A5. Africa = growth and currency/consolidation risk.** International is ~35% of VBL's gross revenue (CY25) and grew +23% (gross) [V AR]; Q2 CY26 international volume +38% (Twizza) and ~+25% organic [V/E]. Africa realises more per case (~₹190 vs India ~₹170) but earns less EBITDA per case (~₹35 vs ~₹45) [E]; South Africa carries the group's debt (net debt ₹373 Cr Jun-26) and FX translation adds noise (Q1 CY26 realisation helped by "favourable currency movement") [V].

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Bottler revenue = Unit cases × Net realisation per case (+ non-beverage revenue).**
**Bottler EBITDA = Cases × (Realisation − Materials/case − Variable opex/case) − Fixed costs.**
Cases = Outlets reached × Cases per outlet per year (a function of coolers, price points, weather).
PAT = EBITDA − Depreciation (≈ capex intensity) − Interest + Other income − Tax (~24%).

### C.1 Worked example 1 — VBL CY25, consolidated [V inputs; E arithmetic]
- 1,213.1 mn cases × ₹178.8 (total-revenue basis) = **₹21,685 Cr** net revenue [V]; of which beverages ≈ 1,213.1 × ~₹170.5 ≈ ₹20,700 Cr and non-beverage (snacks, dairy, preforms, closures, scrap) ≈ ₹1,000 Cr [E].
- Gross margin 55.2% → gross profit ≈ ₹11,970 Cr ≈ **₹98.7 per case**.
- Opex (employees ₹2,201 Cr + other ₹4,720 Cr) ≈ ₹6,921 Cr ≈ ₹57.1 per case.
- **EBITDA ₹5,049 Cr ≈ ₹41.6 per case** (23.3%).
- Depreciation ₹1,216 Cr (₹10.0/case); finance cost ₹170 Cr; other income ₹352 Cr; PBT ₹4,010 Cr; tax 23.6% → **PAT ₹3,062 Cr ≈ ₹25.2 per case**.

### C.2 Worked example 2 — VBL India, Q2 CY26 (peak quarter) [V inputs; E arithmetic]
- 346.5 mn cases × ₹169.6 = **₹5,877 Cr** beverage revenue (+ ₹119 Cr non-beverage = ₹5,996 Cr) [V].
- Standalone OP ₹1,878 Cr (31%) [V screener] → **~₹54 per case**.
- Interpretation: in the peak quarter India earns ~₹54/case; across CY25 ~₹45/case (OP ₹3,765 Cr ÷ 839 mn) [E].

### C.3 Worked example 3 — a ₹20 bottle, shelf to bottler [E composite]
- Shelf ₹20 → GST (40%) ₹5.71 → trade (retailer + distributor) ~₹3.3 → bottler net ~₹11.0 per 400 ml.
- Per 5.678 L case: ~14.2 bottles × ₹11.0 ≈ ₹156/case; plus higher-value SKUs → VBL India average ₹169.6 [V].
- Bottler cost per 400 ml: concentrate ~₹3.0; sugar ~₹0.6 (low-sugar) to ₹1.2 (regular); PET + closure + label ~₹2.6; other materials ~₹0.4 → materials ~₹6.6–7.2 (≈60–65% of net — above VBL's 45% average because a 400 ml PET single-serve is packaging-heavy) [E].
- Opex ~₹2.5 → EBITDA ~₹1.5–2.0 per bottle (14–18%) on this SKU; larger packs, cans, glass returnables and Sting carry the group average to ~26% in India [E].

### C.4 Worked example 4 — Campa ₹10, 150 ml vs 200 ml [E composite]
- Shelf ₹10 → GST ₹2.86 → trade at Campa's richer terms ~₹2.0 (vs ~₹1.4 for incumbents) [E; FLAG — margins not disclosed] → brand/bottler net ~₹5.1 per pack.
- Per case: at 200 ml, 28.4 packs → ₹146/case; at 150 ml, 37.9 packs → **₹194/case** — the pack cut raised Campa's realisation per litre by a third [E]. The ₹10 tier becomes economic only at 150 ml.

### C.5 Worked example 5 — Africa vs India EBITDA per case (CY25) [E]
- International: revenue ~₹7,128 Cr ÷ 374 mn cases ≈ **₹191/case**; OP ~₹1,305 Cr → **₹35/case (18%)**.
- India: ₹14,557 Cr ÷ 839 mn ≈ **₹174/case**; OP ₹3,765 Cr → **₹45/case (26%)**.
- If Africa converged to India's 26% margin at today's realisation, international EBITDA would rise by ~₹550 Cr (+11% of group EBITDA) [E].
