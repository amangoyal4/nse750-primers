## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Mined metal ≠ refined metal ≠ "~1.1 Mt".**
   - FY26: **mined metal 1,114 kt** (record); **refined metal 1,048 kt** = refined zinc **851 kt** + refined lead **197 kt**; **silver 627 t** [V co. Q4 deck].
   - Refined capacity is **1,129 ktpa** (zinc 919 + lead 210) [V co.]. Our primer says "roughly one million tonnes".
   - "Vision 2.0 / 2x" is framed by the company as **2.0 Mtpa refined metal capacity (zinc 1,590 + lead 410) plus 1,500 tpa silver by ~FY30**. The approved first step is **+250 ktpa at Debari (1,129 → 1,379 ktpa refined; mined metal 1,180 → 1,510 ktpa)** at ~₹12,000 Cr by **Q2 FY29** [V co. Q4 FY26 and Q2 FY27 decks]. The primer frames it as "mined metal ~1.1 → ~2.0 MT".
   - Since FY26, HZL has also **sold lead concentrate**: 24 kt in FY26 and 20 kt in H1 FY27, with silver and lead "equivalents" sitting outside the refined numbers (trap 6).
2. **Silver share: revenue vs profit, and "precious metal contribution".**
   - **Revenue share** [V co.]: FY25 **18%** (₹6,130 / ₹34,083 Cr); FY26 **24%** (₹9,841 / ₹40,844 Cr); Q2 FY27 **27%** (₹4,111 / ₹15,093 Cr); H1 FY27 **28%** (₹7,950 / ₹28,840 Cr).
   - **Profit share**: the company says silver ("precious metal") contributed "**c.45% to overall profitability**" in FY26 and again in H1 FY27 [V co.]. The basis is undisclosed (probably segment-level EBITDA before common costs).
   - Our primer's line "**the zinc engine ~85% of revenue**" is **wrong**: zinc was **58% of FY26 revenue** (₹23,771 Cr), lead 10%, silver 24%, others (sulphuric acid, alloys, wind power, concentrate) 8%.
3. **CoP excludes royalty AND silver, and includes acid credits.**
   - Q2 FY27 CoP **$703/t ex-royalty** vs **$1,228/t with royalty**. Royalty (+ DMF + NMET) therefore cost **$525/t ≈ 13.7% of the $3,825 LME price** [E from V co.].
   - CoP is helped by **sulphuric-acid by-product credits**. Acid revenue was "around ₹1,000–1,200 Cr" in FY26, and acid prices spiked in 2026 (Hormuz closure, China's acid-export ban). The CFO said geopolitical cost inflation of ~$50/t was offset by acid [V call]. **Part of the CoP fall is an acid windfall, not efficiency.**
   - A weaker rupee lowers dollar CoP mechanically (≈80% of costs are in rupees [E]). ₹ CoP fell from ₹86,800/t (Q2 FY26) to ₹67,141/t (Q2 FY27): **−23% in rupees vs −29% in dollars** [V co.].
4. **Ownership: four numbers float around. Use 60.71% / 27.92%.**
   - Vedanta (promoter): 64.92% from 2003 to Jun-2024 → **63.42%** (Aug-2024 OFS, floor ₹486) → **61.84%** (Jun-2025 OFS, ~1.59%) → **60.71%** (Jan–Mar 2026 quarter) [V screener; V-snip BS for floors].
   - GoI: **29.54%** (incl. tiny rounding variants of 29.58–29.59% in 2018–22) → **27.92%** after the **Nov-2024 OFS** (2.5% offered at floor ₹505 = 1.25% base + 1.25% green-shoe; ~1.62% sold) [V screener; V-snip Business Standard 5-Nov-2024].
   - Public 11.37% (FII 2.37%, DII 4.84%, others 4.15%) [V co. Sep-26 deck; V screener].
   - The primer's "~29.5% sometimes cited **including related holdings**" is a mis-explanation: 29.54% was simply the **pre-Nov-2024** stake. **The Vedanta primer's "~63% stake" is stale** (Sep-2024 to Mar-2025 level).
5. **Consolidated vs standalone are almost identical; screener's FY26 consolidated payout of "0%" is an artefact.**
   - FY26 consolidated revenue ₹40,844 / PAT ₹13,832 Cr vs standalone ₹40,658 / ₹13,712 Cr [V screener]. The only material subsidiary is Hindustan Zinc Alloys Pvt Ltd (HZAPL).
   - Screener's consolidated "Dividend payout FY26 0%" is wrong. Standalone shows **31%** = the ₹10/share (₹4,225 Cr) paid for FY26 [V screener standalone; V co.]. The ₹11 declared in Apr-2026 is the **FY27** first interim.
6. **Lead-concentrate sales distort volume and silver comparisons from FY26.**
   - FY26: 24 kt of lead concentrate sold, containing **37 t silver-equivalent** and 11 kt lead-equivalent, *on top of* 627 t refined silver [V co.].
   - Q2 FY27: 10 kt concentrate (10 t silver, 6 kt lead) → revenue ~₹300 Cr, profit ~₹155 Cr [V call].
   - Management: "H1 we have done 340 [t silver] you can assume" (321 t refined + ~19–20 t in concentrate) [V call]. **Quote refined silver (321 t) as the reported figure** and mention concentrate separately.
7. **"Realised" silver ≠ LBMA average. Quarterly revenue lags or leads the price.**
   - Implied realisation = silver revenue ÷ ounces sold: Q2 FY27 ₹4,111 Cr ÷ 5.56 Moz ÷ ₹95.57 ≈ **$77/oz vs LBMA average $62.6**. Q1 FY27 ≈ $85 vs $73.2. Q4 FY26 ≈ $78 vs $84.3. FY26 ≈ $55 vs $53.1 [E from V co.].
   - Plausible reasons: provisional/quotational-period pricing, Indian domestic silver premiums (import-parity pricing incl. customs duty) and the timing of sales. **Flag it; do not present $77 as the market price.** The sibling report's "~US$78 realised" is the same arithmetic.
8. **Silver has crashed since the quarter; Q2 FY27 is a peak print.**
   - LBMA averages [V co.]: FY25 $30.4; FY26 **$53.1**; **Q4 FY26 $84.3** (all-time high **$118.45/oz** during Jan–Mar 2026); Q1 FY27 $73.2; **Q2 FY27 $62.6**.
   - Spot on **9-Nov-2026 ≈ $48.9/oz** (indicative; a nearby print was $52.1) [V Skillings/MetalCharts]. Zinc ~**$3,711/t**, lead ~$1,884/t [V same].
   - On HZL's sensitivity (₹200–225 Cr of annual EBITDA per $1/oz), silver at ~$50 vs the Q2 average of $62.6 removes **~₹2,500–2,800 Cr of annualised EBITDA (~₹650–700 Cr a quarter)** [E].
9. **Market share: "74%" is of PRIMARY zinc, not of total consumption.**
   - Company: "c.74% domestic primary zinc market share", "~92% primary lead market share" (FY26) [V co.].
   - India's total zinc use (~1.0–1.15 Mt) also includes imported ingot (~0.2 Mt), secondary/recycled zinc and zinc scrap imports (82 kt in FY25) [V-snip BigMint]. HZL's share of all zinc used in India is therefore probably ~60–70% [E].
   - Lead: India's lead market is ~60%+ secondary (recycled), so HZL's 92% "primary" share is a minority of total lead [K; gravita primer].
10. **ROCE: company ~67% vs screener 70% vs "69.5%".**
    - Company FY26 ROCE **c.67%** (EBIT ÷ average capital employed) and ROE c.77% [V co.].
    - Screener FY26 ROCE **70%**; the TTM headline is **69.5%**, ROE **76.8%** [V screener].
    - Returns are flattered by the **FY23 special dividend (₹75.5/share total, 303% payout)**, which shrank reserves from ₹33,436 Cr to ₹12,087 Cr. Use the company 67% in prose and screener's numbers in tables.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **One listed company, but a global sub-sector.** In India, "zinc" on the NSE means Hindustan Zinc: the only integrated primary zinc-lead producer and India's only primary silver producer [V co.]. Everything else is either:
  - imported metal (~0.2 Mt/yr of high-grade ingot, mostly from Korea) [V-snip BigMint], or
  - secondary metal: lead recyclers (Gravita, Pondy Oxides, unlisted others), zinc-dross/ash recyclers, and the galvanisers who consume zinc.
- **HZL is a price-taker with a world-class cost position.**
  - Q2 FY27: zinc CoP **$703/t ex-royalty** (FY27 guidance revised down to **$825–850/t** from $975–1,000) against LME zinc of **$3,825/t** → margin before royalty ~$3,100/t; after royalty ~**$2,600/t** [V co.].
  - That is **first-decile** on the global cost curve [co. claim, Woodmac source]. Reference: Vedanta's own Zinc International ran at **$1,898/t** in Q1 FY27 [V-sib].
- **2025–26 was a "miners win, smelters lose" market.**
  - Mine supply lagged smelter capacity, so concentrate treatment charges (TCs) collapsed. The annual benchmark was **$80/dmt in 2025** (lowest in 50+ years) and **$85 in 2026**, against $165 in 2024 and $274 in 2023 [V-snip SMM/Fastmarkets; K for 2023].
  - Spot TCs went **negative**: −$71/dmt in Jun-2026 (SMM) and a record **−$115 to −$150/t on 9-Oct-2026** (Fastmarkets) [V].
  - Custom smelters are in distress. Nyrstar put its **300 ktpa Budel smelter under strategic review (24-Sep-2026)** [V Fastmarkets].
  - An integrated producer like HZL **does not pay TCs**. It captures the full metal value. This is the structural reason HZL's margin rose to 62% while Korea Zinc / Nyrstar-type custom smelters suffered.
- **Silver turned a zinc miner into a precious-metals proxy, and has now reversed.**
  - LBMA silver averaged **$30.4/oz in FY25 → $53.1 in FY26 → $84.3 in Q4 FY26** (ATH $118.45) → **$73.2 in Q1 FY27 → $62.6 in Q2 FY27** → **~$49 on 9-Nov-2026** [V co.; V Skillings].
  - Silver was **c.45% of HZL's profitability** in FY26 and H1 FY27 [V co.].
  - The stock (₹555 on 13-Nov-2026) is **24% below its 52-week high of ₹733** and the m-cap has fallen from a peak of ~₹3.1 lakh Cr to ₹2.34 lakh Cr [V screener; V co. "peak marketcap ₹310k crores"].
- **Records on every line in Q2 FY27, with a trough-to-peak swing in two years** [V screener/co.]:
  - Quarterly OPM rose from **46% (Sep-2023)** to **61–62% (Sep-2026)**.
  - TTM revenue **₹53,364 Cr**, TTM PAT **₹20,830 Cr** (+97% TTM).
  - Net cash **₹11,268 Cr** (30-Sep-2026), from ~₹4,000 Cr net debt equivalent at Mar-2025 [E: screener borrowings 10,967 vs investments 9,972 + cash].
- **The governance triangle is the valuation discount.**
  - Vedanta 60.71% (pledged/encumbered up the chain; VRL's Jul-2026 US$1 bn facility placed a negative lien on 99.99% of VRL's Vedanta shares [V-sib]).
  - GoI 27.92% (a check, and a seller in waiting). Public 11.37%.
  - Cash moves up through dividends (FY25 ₹29/share = ₹12,253 Cr, 118% payout; FY26 only ₹10 = 31%; FY27 first interim ₹11 = ₹4,648 Cr) and a **brand fee of 3% of turnover (2% until Mar-2025): ₹1,171 Cr in FY26 vs ₹658 Cr** [V co. for dividends; V-sib for fees].
  - The new **₹30,000 Cr Section 186 limit** (postal ballot to 3-Dec-2026) widens the scope for inter-corporate loans/investments. **Watch it as a related-party channel** [V notice; E interpretation].
- **Valuation (13-Nov-2026)** [V screener; E for EV]:
  - M-cap **₹2,34,442 Cr**; P/E **11.3× TTM**; P/B **7.8×**; dividend yield 1.98%.
  - EV ≈ ₹2,23,174 Cr (company net cash ₹11,268 Cr) → **EV/EBITDA ~7.2× TTM** (TTM EBITDA ₹31,193 Cr company basis) and ~10.1× FY26.

### 1.2 Value chain (ore → customer)

1. **Exploration & mining** (underground at all five HZL Rajasthan mines since the Rampura Agucha open-pit-to-UG transition) [K/V co.].
   - Ore grade ~**7.6% Zn+Pb in Q2 FY27** [V call]. HZL's R&R mine grade is "~7%" vs a global average reserve grade that has fallen from **6.9% (1990s) to 2.8% now** [V co. Q2 deck, Woodmac].
2. **Concentration** (crush → grind → froth flotation) → zinc concentrate (~50–55% Zn) and lead concentrate (~55–65% Pb, silver-bearing) [K].
3. **Smelting/refining**:
   - **Hydrometallurgical** zinc: roast → leach → purify → electrowin (Chanderiya hydro, Dariba hydro, Debari).
   - **Pyrometallurgical** zinc-lead ISP/Ausmelt (Chanderiya pyro: a record 164 kt in FY26).
   - **Lead smelters** (Dariba, Chanderiya), where silver is recovered from the lead bullion.
   - By-products: sulphuric acid, cadmium, jarosite residue. A **Hot Acid Leaching** plant at Dariba will recover silver and lead from jarosite: **27 tpa silver + 6 ktpa lead** [V co.].
4. **Power**: captive coal power plants (CPPs; domestic coal **53% of CPP use in FY26 and 32% of usage in Q2 FY27**, the balance imported) plus renewables at **27% of power in Q2 FY27 (target 35% near-term, 70% by FY28)** [V co./call].
5. **Sales**: galvanisers, continuous galvanising lines (steel majors such as Tata Steel, JSW, AM/NS), alloy and die-casters, battery makers (lead), exports.
   - Domestic prices are LME-linked plus a premium (import-parity incl. 5% basic customs duty [K]). Implied realisations [E]: Q2 FY27 zinc ~$4,020/t vs LME $3,825 (+5%); lead ~$2,320/t vs $1,857.

**Secondary route (recyclers):** collect scrap (used lead-acid batteries; zinc dross/ash/skimmings; brass) → smelt/refine → sell at LME-linked prices. They earn a **conversion spread**, not a metal margin.
- Gravita FY26 OPM 10%; Pondy Oxides 7% [V screener].
- The Hazardous & Other Wastes (Management & Transboundary Movement) Amendment Rules 2025 (G.S.R. 438(E), 1-Jul-2025) brought **EPR for aluminium, copper and zinc scrap from 1-Apr-2026**, with recycling targets of 10% in 2026-27 rising to 75% by 2032-33 [V-sib].

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **The mine captures the metal rent.** At Q2 FY27 prices, of each **~$3,825/t of zinc** [E from V co.]:
  - royalty/DMF/NMET take **~$525** (state of Rajasthan + DMF + NMET);
  - cash costs take **~$703**;
  - the remaining **~$2,600** is HZL margin.
- **Custom smelters capture only the TC + free metal + by-products.** With benchmark TCs at $80–85/dmt and spot negative, a standalone smelter's processing margin is near zero or negative before acid and premiums [V-snip/V Fastmarkets; E].
  - This is why **integration** is the moat in 2025–26. In 2015–2019, with TCs at $150–300/dmt, smelters did well and miners had to "give away" more of the price [K].
- **Silver is near-pure rent.** It rides in ore mined for zinc-lead; after royalty (7% of LBMA [K]) and refining, almost all silver revenue becomes EBITDA. H1 FY27 silver revenue was ₹7,950 Cr and "precious metal contribution" ≈ 45% of the ₹17,359 Cr EBITDA ≈ ₹7,800 Cr [E from V co.].
- **The state captures a large share.** HZL's contribution to the exchequer was **c.₹19,000 Cr in FY26** (vs PAT ₹13,832 Cr), of which **>₹6,000 Cr went to Rajasthan incl. royalties**. In H1 FY27 it was **₹13,027 Cr, of which ₹3,724 Cr to Rajasthan** [V co., unaudited].
  - Adding GoI's 27.92% of dividends, **the Indian state takes more out of HZL than any shareholder** [E].
- **Downstream users** (galvanisers, battery makers) earn conversion margins. They are price-takers on zinc and lead and pass metal through with a lag [K].

## 2. Size and growth

### 2.1 Global zinc

- **Refined zinc market ≈ 13.9–14.2 Mt/yr.**

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Galvanised steel (≈72% of Indian zinc demand)** [V-snip BigMint; HZL deck; sibling Iron & Steel report].
   - Indian crude steel: ~152 Mt (FY25) → 168 Mt (FY26e) → targeted **300 Mtpa by 2030** [V co. deck citing CMIE; National Steel Policy target].
   - Galvanised/coated flat products (GP/GC, colour-coated, galvalume) are the fastest-growing grades: roofing, solar module structures, transmission towers, highway crash barriers, metro/railway electrification, pre-engineered buildings, appliances and autos.
   - Rule of thumb: ~**30–40 kg zinc per tonne of hot-dip galvanised structural steel** and ~**8–10 kg per tonne of continuous galvanised sheet** [K; indicative].
   - Government capex is up **2.6× in six years**; FY27 effective capex is budgeted at **~4.4% of GDP** [V co./call].
2. **Autos.** Only 20–25% of Indian vehicles use galvanised body steel vs 90–95% globally (IZA). Rising export-grade and EV platforms push corrosion warranties up [V-snip]. Zinc die-casting goes into locks, handles and carburettor parts.
3. **Renewables and the energy transition.**
   - Solar mounting structures and wind towers are galvanised.
   - Zinc-based batteries are an emerging storage chemistry (HZL deck pitch) [V co.].
   - Silver: PV ~17% of demand; EVs and electronics [V co.].
4. **Lead-acid batteries** (~80–85% of lead): auto SLI (replacement-driven), inverter/UPS, telecom towers, e-rickshaws. Lithium-ion is substituting in two-/three-wheelers and storage over time [K].
5. **Brass and chemicals:** brass (Jamnagar cluster); zinc oxide for tyres (rubber vulcanisation), ceramics, pharma and feed [K].
6. **Fertiliser (zinc sulphate micronutrient; sulphuric acid → phosphoric acid → DAP).** India imports most of its DAP. HZL's 510 ktpa plant targets this using acid it already produces [V co.].
7. **Global macro:** Chinese property (the largest single swing factor in zinc demand), global manufacturing PMI, US/EU infrastructure, and the dollar [K]. HZL deck: "manufacturing strengthened in Japan, US and EU, while China held steady" (Sep-2026) [V co.].

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Mining law and leases
- The **MMDR Act 1957 (amended 2015, 2021, 2023)** sets royalties in its Second Schedule and creates the District Mineral Foundation (DMF: 30% of royalty for leases granted before 12-Jan-2015, 10% after) and the National Mineral Exploration Trust (NMET 2%) [K].
- HZL's legacy leases were **extended to 2030** under the 2015 amendment (deemed extension of captive/non-captive leases granted before 2015). New leases thereafter must be **auctioned** [V-sib; K].
  - **This is a material long-dated risk**: post-2030 auction premiums could add a % of value-of-dispatch on top of royalty. Several large non-captive leases lapsed in Mar-2020 and were re-auctioned at 80–150% premiums for iron ore [K].

### 7.2 Royalty and the Supreme Court's mineral-tax ruling
- **Royalty rates [K, MMDR Second Schedule]:** zinc **9.5%** of the LME zinc price on metal in ore produced; lead **8.5%**; silver (by-product) **7%** of the LBMA price. The 2013 SC ruling (State of Rajasthan v. HZL) confirmed royalty applies to metal in ore produced, not tailings [V-snip].
- **Mineral Area Development Authority v. SAIL (nine-judge bench, 25-Jul-2024, 8:1, Justice Nagarathna dissenting):** royalty is **not a tax**, and **states can tax mineral rights and mineral-bearing land** [V-snip SCC Online/JSA]. A 14-Aug-2024 order allowed states to recover arrears **from 1-Apr-2005**, in instalments over **12 years starting 1-Apr-2026**, with interest and penalty waived [K].
  - **Rajasthan:** no Rajasthan mineral-rights tax law affecting HZL was found this session [negative result]. The sibling report notes "any Rajasthan levy would fall directly on HZL's costs" [V-sib]. Odisha, Jharkhand and others have moved on such cesses [K].
  - **Sensitivity [E]:** a 1%-of-value cess on HZL's FY26 revenue = ~₹400 Cr/yr (~1.8% of EBITDA). A 5% levy = ~₹2,000 Cr. Back-dated arrears would add a one-off.
- **State capture is already high:** c.₹19,000 Cr to the exchequer in FY26, >₹6,000 Cr of it to Rajasthan [V co.].

### 7.3 Trade
- Zinc ingot basic customs duty ~5%; Korea CEPA/APTA concessional [K; V-snip]. **No anti-dumping duty** on zinc ingot found. Silver import duty was cut in Jul-2024 (to ~6% total) and is a swing factor for domestic silver premiums [K].
- **US tariffs:** Section 232 is 50% on steel, aluminium and copper derivatives. Refined zinc is not a 232 product (to our knowledge [K]), but **galvanised steel exports to the US are hit by the 50% steel tariff**. The 10% Section 301-type tariff on India affects HZL's (small) US exports. HZL's export destinations are mainly Asia/Middle East [K; FLAG].
- **EU CBAM** (definitive phase from Jan-2026) covers steel and aluminium, not zinc ingot. But galvanised steel exporters need low-carbon inputs, which supports **EcoZen** (~75% lower carbon footprint) and the Tata Steel partnership [V co.; K].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

- **State era (1966–2002).** HZL was carved out of Metal Corporation of India in 1966, with Zawar (mined since antiquity; modern operations 1940s) and the Debari smelter (1968) [K].
  - **Rampura Agucha**, discovered in 1977 and commissioned around 1991, transformed the company with one of the world's richest zinc orebodies. The Chanderiya smelter came in 1991–92 [K; V co. "Chanderiya pyro since 1992"].
- **Privatisation (2002–03).** Sterlite (Vedanta) took control (26% + open offer; 18.92% call option, Nov-2003) → 64.92% [K].
  - **The 2003–2008 China super-cycle**: zinc peaked at ~$4,500/t in Nov-2006 [K]. HZL expanded smelting (Chanderiya hydro, Dariba) and its cash pile ballooned. By FY15, investments were ₹27,254 Cr with zero debt [V screener standalone].
- **Treasury era and the UG transition (2012–2020).** Rampura Agucha moved from open pit to underground (open pit closed ~2018) [K]. Sindesar Khurd became a world-class silver mine. Cash was returned through large dividends (FY16–FY17 payouts ~144–149%; FY21 113%) [V screener]. Zinc fell to ~$1,500/t in early 2016 after Glencore's 500 kt supply cut [K].
- **Mine-closure boom (2017–18).** Century (Australia) and Lisheen (Ireland) closed in 2015–16 → refined deficit, LME zinc ~$3,500/t+ in early 2018. TCs fell to ~$25/dmt spot in 2017 [K].
- **Energy-crisis spike (2021–22).** European smelters (Nyrstar, Glencore Portovesme) curtailed on power prices. LME zinc hit an all-time high of ~**$4,896/t (Apr-2022)** [K]. HZL FY23 revenue peaked at ₹34,098 Cr and paid a **special dividend (FY23 total ₹75.5/share; 303% payout)** that cut reserves from ₹33,436 Cr to ₹12,087 Cr [V screener; K for DPS].
- **Trough (FY24).** LME zinc ~$2,450–2,500/t; HZL revenue −15%, OPM 47%, PAT ₹7,759 Cr [V screener]. The Zinc International acquisition (Jan-2023) was blocked by GoI [K]. The demerger plan (Sep-2023) was rejected (Mar-2024) [V-snip].
- **Sell-downs (2024–26).** Vedanta OFS Aug-2024 (floor ₹486), GoI OFS Nov-2024 (floor ₹505), Vedanta OFS Jun-2025, further Vedanta sale in Jan–Mar 2026 [V screener; V-snip].
- **The "TC collapse + silver boom" super-period (2025–26).** The 2025 benchmark TC fell to $80/dmt (50-year low). Silver went from ~$30 to $118 (ATH, Q1 CY26). LME zinc rose to $4,020 (Sep-2026 monthly average).
  - HZL posted records: FY26 revenue ₹40,844 Cr; Q2 FY27 EBITDA ₹9,285 Cr (62%); TTM PAT ₹20,830 Cr [V co./screener].
  - The parent demerged (1-May-2026). Silver halved from its peak by Nov-2026 [V Skillings].

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

- **Zinc demand substitutes:** aluminium-zinc (galvalume) coatings use ~55% Al, *less* zinc per m² than pure galvanising. Paint systems. Stainless and weathering steel for some infrastructure [K].
- **Lead:** lithium-ion is displacing lead-acid in e-rickshaws, 2W, telecom backup and ESS. That is a slow-burn risk to HZL's lead and to the recyclers [K].
- **Silver thrifting in PV:** cells use less silver per watt each year (copper metallisation and silver-coated copper are in trials). The 2025–26 price spike accelerates thrifting [K]. HZL's deck pitches the opposite (PV demand growth) [V co.].
- **Zinc batteries** (zinc-air, zinc-bromine, zinc-ion for long-duration storage) are a possible upside. HZL is promoting them [V co.].
- **Mining tech:** HZL's automation (tele-remote raise-boring, BEV fleets, AI froth-flotation, digital twins) is a cost lever [V co.]. Fine-particle flotation (CSIR-NML) and silver-recovery research (Virginia Tech) could lift recoveries [V co.].

- **Depletion:** reserves are only ~12.5 years of output [E]. The 25+ year life relies on converting resources to reserves (target 30 Mt reserves).

### B1. "Why the smelters are dying and the miner is minting": TCs, integration and the 2025–26 squeeze
- Explain the TC mechanism: a smelter buys concentrate at LME × payable (~85%) − TC; it earns TC + free metal + by-products.
- Chart the TC benchmark 2015–2026: ~$245 (2015), $203 (2016), $172 (2017), $147 (2018), $245 (2019), $300 (2020), $159 (2021), $230 (2022), $274 (2023), $165 (2024), **$80 (2025)**, **$85 (2026)** [K for 2015–2023; V-snip 2024–2026]. Add the spot path to −$150 (Oct-2026).

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**HZL EBITDA ≈**
- **Zinc:** refined zinc sold (t) × [LME zinc × (1 + domestic premium) − zinc CoP ex-royalty − royalty/DMF/NMET per t] × ₹/$
- **+ Silver:** silver sold (oz) × [LBMA (± realisation lag/premium) × (1 − ~9.4% royalty incl. DMF/NMET)] × ₹/$
- **+ Lead:** refined lead sold (t) × [LME lead × (1 + premium) − lead cost incl. royalty] × ₹/$
- **+ concentrate-sale margin + other operating income − overheads (incl. brand fee at 3% of turnover, CSR, corporate)**

(CoP is already net of sulphuric-acid and other non-silver by-product credits, so "others" revenue is largely already inside CoP.)

   - First-decile cost from high-grade underground ore (7.6%), integration (no TCs in a negative-TC world), captive power moving to RE, and by-product acid.

   - ~74% of India's primary zinc market at import-parity prices [V co.].

   - **Silver mean reversion**: spot ~$49 vs the $62.6 Q2 average, at ₹200–225 Cr per $1/oz.

   - **Royalty / state-tax creep** and 2030 lease auctions.
   - Reserves of only ~12.5 years [E].
4. **Growth:**
   - Debari +250 ktpa (₹12,000 Cr, Q2 FY29); tailings 10 Mtpa (₹3,823 Cr, Q4 FY28).
