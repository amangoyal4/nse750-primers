## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **IRB "consolidated" is no longer IRB's road portfolio.**
   - Since the Private InvIT was set up (2021–23) and IRB moved to 51% (with joint control alongside GIC 25% and Cintra 24% [V CRISIL]), the Private InvIT is a **joint venture accounted under the equity method**, not consolidated. The public IRB InvIT (~16–17%) is an associate/investment.
   - **Consolidated revenue (₹7,648 Cr FY26) contains only:**
     - the toll of the **two wholly owned concessions** (Mumbai–Pune Expressway/NH4 and Ahmedabad–Vadodara) plus two under-construction HAMs;
     - **construction/O&M revenue billed to the InvIT SPVs**;
     - "**InvIT & related assets**" income (project-management fees, interest and distributions from the trusts).
   - **Group toll on all 24 tolling SPVs is ~4× the toll inside consolidated revenue.** Q1 FY27 group toll ₹2,444 Cr; wholly owned only ₹738 Cr [V presentation].
   - Never compare IRB's consolidated revenue with NHAI toll market shares. Use **group gross toll** for share; use **consolidated** for P&L ratios.
2. **The FY25 "PAT ₹6,481 Cr" is a one-off.**
   - Screener FY25 PAT ₹6,481 Cr includes an **exceptional gain of ₹5,804 Cr (₹58,041 mn)**, booked in Dec-2024 (Q3 FY25 other income ₹5,869 Cr on screener). It relates to the Private InvIT restructuring and deconsolidation [V presentation; V screener].
   - **Pre-exceptional PAT: FY25 ₹677 Cr → FY26 ₹893 Cr (+32%)** [V presentation]. **Reported FY26 PAT ₹850 Cr** is after a −₹42.6 Cr exceptional item [V].
   - The primer's "₹645 Cr → ₹850 Cr (+32%)" mixes bases: +32% is the pre-exceptional growth (677→893), while 850 is reported. Use **pre-exceptional 677 → 893** in growth charts and **reported 850** in the peer table, and say so.
3. **Three EBITDA numbers for IRB FY26.**
   - Screener OP **₹4,008 Cr (52.4%)**.
   - Company EBITDA **₹4,188 Cr** (₹41,877 mn; margin 53.3% on total income ₹7,854 Cr) [V].
   - Segment EBITDA (Ind AS 108) is larger again, because unallocable items sit outside it. Q1 FY27 segment EBITDA ₹1,216 Cr vs company ₹1,188 Cr vs screener ₹1,153 Cr [V].
   - Tables use screener; prose may cite the company figure with its basis.
4. **IRB net debt: screener says ~₹700 Cr; the company says ~₹11,000 Cr.**
   - Screener: borrowings ₹20,027 Cr − investments ₹19,328 Cr = **₹699 Cr**. That is wrong for prose: the "investments" are mostly the **equity-accounted 51% Private InvIT stake and ~16% IRB InvIT units** — strategic, illiquid holdings, not treasury cash.
   - **Company net debt/equity 0.53× at FY26 and 0.51× at Q1 FY27** [V presentation]. On net worth ~₹20,900 Cr (screener) that is **~₹11,100 Cr (FY26)** and **~₹10,700 Cr (Jun-26)** [E derived].
   - **USD-bond covenant group** ("Business Segment II" = IRB standalone + MPEW + AV + 2 HAMs): gross debt **₹6,900 Cr**, EBITDA ₹2,500 Cr, **gross leverage 2.8×** (cap 4.0×) [V presentation].
   - Use company ~₹11,100 Cr for net debt/EBITDA (~2.7× FY26 company EBITDA [E]). Value the InvIT stakes separately in an SOTP.
5. **Construction revenue is mostly self-generated.**
   - IRB's construction segment (₹967 Cr in Q1 FY27; ~57% of segment revenue a year ago, ~42% now [V call]) is mostly EPC and **O&M done for its own InvIT SPVs** under fixed-price contracts.
   - The **₹44,100 Cr order book is ~96% O&M** (O&M ₹42,400 Cr; EPC ₹1,700 Cr) [V call/presentation]. It is long-dated O&M for the trusts, not third-party EPC as at PNC/KNR/HG.
   - Order-book-to-revenue comparisons with EPC peers are invalid.
6. **IFRIC-12 / Ind AS 115 intangible accounting (BOT/TOT).**
   - A toll concession is recognised as an **intangible asset (right to charge users)** and amortised, usually on a **revenue/traffic-units basis** (Ind AS 38 permits revenue-based amortisation for toll roads under Schedule II of the Companies Act) [K].
   - During construction the concessionaire books **construction revenue = cost + margin** against the intangible (Appendix D/IFRIC-12) [K]. Developer revenue in build years is therefore inflated by "construction revenue" that is really capex.
   - **TOT upfront fees are capitalised as intangibles** (e.g. TOT-17 ₹9,270 Cr) and amortised over 20 years. **Amortisation, not traffic, is why InvIT PAT is near zero** (Cube FY26 PAT ₹217 Cr on EBITDA ₹3,117 Cr) [V screener].
7. **HAM is a financial asset, not an intangible.**
   - Under HAM, NHAI pays **40% of the bid project cost during construction** (5 milestone tranches) and the remaining **60% as 30 half-yearly annuities over 15 years**, plus interest on the reducing balance (at **bank rate + 3%**) and O&M payments [K; V PRS annexure for 40/60 and 15 years].
   - The concessionaire books a **financial receivable (Ind AS 109)** and interest income. There is no traffic risk; toll is collected by NHAI.
   - HAM revenue is **inflation-linked through a price index multiple** on the bid project cost [K], not toll indexation.
   - Do not apply toll KPIs (traffic, rate hikes) to HAM assets.
8. **Toll "revenue" has five definitions.**
   - (a) **Gross toll collected at the plaza** — IRB monthly disclosures, ₹773 Cr for Sep-2026 across 24 SPVs.
   - (b) **Gross toll of consolidated entities only**.
   - (c) **IRB's economic share** (100% of WOS + 51% of Pvt InvIT + ~16% of IRB InvIT).
   - (d) **NHAI "user fee"** (on fee-plaza projects NHAI collects, and on PPP projects it reports concessionaire collection) — ₹68,483 Cr FY26.
   - (e) **NHAI user fee + annual-pass receipts** — **₹70,278 Cr FY26** [V-snip Swarajya, Rajya Sabha reply].
   - IRB's "~10% of India's toll" is (a) ÷ (e). IRB's economic share is far smaller [E: ~₹4,600 Cr of ~₹9,800 Cr annualised group toll].
   - **FASTag/NETC totals (₹20,682 Cr in Q1 FY26, +19.6%) include state highways and state expressways,** so they differ again [V-snip].
9. **InvIT "yield" on screener is wrong.**
   - Screener's "dividend yield" for InvITs counts only the **dividend component** of distributions. Cube's **2.58%** ignores interest (₹2.10/unit in Q1) and SPV loan repayment (₹0.76). **Cube's true DPU run-rate is ₹14.50 (FY27 guidance) → ~9.5% on ₹153** [V press release; E].
   - IRB InvIT happens to show 10.8%, close to the true **₹6.5 annualised ÷ ₹61 = 10.7%** [V/E].
   - NHIT quotes **2.12% per quarter on Mar-26 NAV** (~8.5% annualised), not on a market price [V].
   - Always quote **DPU ÷ price** and split it into interest / dividend / return of capital. The tax treatment differs, and return of capital is not income.
10. **Lane-km, km, assets: three different counts.**
    - IRB says **~17,355 lane-km** (16,575 operational) across 27 revenue assets, ₹94,000 Cr AUM, in 13 states [V presentation/press release]. The primer says "~15,500 lane-km, ~36 projects, 12 states" (stale).
    - NHIT reports **route km (~2,655 km)**; Cube reported **8,819 lane-km / 2,021 km** at Jun-2025 [V-snip].
    - IRB's "c.21,000 lane-km across 41 projects" counts **projects managed, including concessions already handed back** [V presentation].
    - Always say lane-km vs km, and owned vs managed.

# PART 1 — INDUSTRY DOSSIER

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **India's toll pool doubled in four years and is still growing ~10% a year.**
  - **NH and expressway toll receipts reached ₹70,278 Cr in FY26** (user fee ₹68,483 Cr + annual pass ₹1,795 Cr), vs ₹61,408 Cr in FY25 and **₹33,929 Cr in FY22** (Gadkari, Rajya Sabha reply, reported 30-Jul-2026) [V-snip Swarajya; FY22–FY25 also V PRS Table 5/11].
  - Fee plazas rose from **733 to 1,209** over the same period [V-snip].
  - ICRA: FY26 toll growth **~10% (traffic ~6%)**; **FY27e 7–9%** (traffic 4.5–5.5%, rate 3.4–4%); **FY28e 10–12%**, because the WPI spike lifts the 1-Apr-2027 rate revision to **6.2–6.4% for newer concessions (December WPI) and 4.5–5.5% for older ones (March WPI)** [V autocarpro/ICRA, 31-Aug-2026].
- **The private developer share of new road building has collapsed, but the private OWNERSHIP of operating toll roads has exploded.** This is the defining paradox of the sector.
  - Private sector financing of NH development fell from **51% (FY15) to 15% (FY24)**. Budgetary support went from 40% to 75%; monetisation supplied 10%; NHAI market borrowing is zero since FY23 [V PRS].
  - Meanwhile the government has sold the toll rights to built roads: **₹1,71,065 Cr monetised cumulatively to Mar-2026** (TOT ₹58,265 Cr, InvIT ₹43,638 Cr and SPV securitisation ₹50,125 Cr as of Jul-2025 = ₹1,52,028 Cr) [V IRB presentation citing MoRTH; V PRS Table 7].
  - **FY26 monetisation ₹28,078 Cr** via TOT and public/private InvITs (ICRA) [V]. **NMP 2.0 targets ₹4.42 lakh Cr from roads over FY26–FY30**, and MoRTH aims at **>₹30,000 Cr in FY27** [V IRB presentation].
  - Buyers are road InvITs (Cube ~₹36,700 Cr AUM, NHIT ₹58,604 Cr EV, IRB's two trusts ~₹87,000 Cr EV, plus Vertis/Interise/Sekura/Maple/Adani/Oriental) backed by **CPPIB, OTPP, KKR, GIC, Ferrovial, ADIA, Mubadala, CDPQ** [V presentations; K for ADIA/Mubadala].
- **The award cycle is in its third weak year.**
  - MoRTH awards were **~7,000 km in FY26 vs 7,538 km in FY25** (ICRA) [V-snip], well below the FY21–FY23 peak (12,000+ km [K]). **NHAI alone awarded 3,124 km in FY26 (−22% YoY) vs a ~4,500 km target** (Nuvama) [V-snip].
  - Execution is following: FY26 construction **~9,380 km**; ICRA sees **9,000–9,500 km in FY27** [V ICRA 31-Mar-26; V-snip].
  - Pace fell from **37 km/day (FY21) to 29 km/day (FY25)** [V PRS fig 14] and ~26 km/day in FY26 [E: 9,380/365].
  - The EPC/HAM developers felt it first: the six reference developers' FY26 revenue fell **15.7%** (₹45,327 → ₹38,204 Cr), and their m-caps sit near 52-week lows at **~0.66× aggregate book** [E: screener sums].
- **IRB has repositioned from "BOT developer" to "sponsor + O&M platform".**
  - All new assets go into the Private InvIT. IRB earns distributions (51% / ~16%), project-management fees, fixed-price O&M and recycling gains [V presentation, "Strategic Transition"].
  - The machine: **bid TOTs at 15–16% equity IRR → stabilise 3–4 years → transfer to the public InvIT at 12–12.5% IRR**. ₹8,400 Cr of EV has been transferred so far, releasing ₹4,900 Cr of equity, recycled into **₹14,000 Cr of new TOTs** [V presentation].
  - Targets by FY30: **net debt 0.6× → 0.0×; cash ROE 8% → 14%+; PAT CAGR 25%; AUM ₹80,000 Cr → ~₹1,40,000 Cr** [V presentation].
- **The asset base is concentrated in time as well as place.**
  - **Mumbai–Pune Expressway (MPEW) concession ends April 2030** [V presentation "Concession End Date (incl. extensions)"]. MPEW is IRB's single largest asset: **₹1,844 Cr toll in FY26; 21% of September-2026 group toll** (₹162 Cr of ₹773 Cr) [V].
  - **The primer never mentions the 2030 end date.** It is the biggest single "BOT concession-end risk" in the story; see §B2.
- **Interest rates have turned against the asset class just as the sector re-levers through InvITs.**
  - Repo 5.50% (hiked 7-Oct-2026); 10-year G-sec ~7.2–7.4%.
  - Road-InvIT cash yields: IRB InvIT ~10.7%, Cube ~9.5% (FY27 DPU guide), NHIT ~8.5% on NAV [V/E] → **spreads of ~120–340 bp over the 10-year**.
  - IRB's FY26 refinancings (MPEW ₹3,654 Cr at −65 bp; Private InvIT ₹11,000 Cr at −160 bp) were done just before the turn [V].

## 1. What the sub-sector does: assets, value chain, where value is captured

### 1.1 The product: a right to charge (or be paid) for road use

| Asset type | What the owner holds | Who pays | Risk carried | Accounting | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **BOT-Toll** (Build-Operate-Transfer) | Concession (typ. 20–30 yrs, extendable under floor/cap) to build, then toll | Road users (toll), revised 1-Apr each year by formula | Construction + **traffic** + financing | Intangible (IFRIC-12) | IRB Ahmedabad–Vadodara (to Mar-2043), Ganga Expressway Pkg-1 (to Oct-2058), Samakhiyali–Santalpur [V] |

### 1.2 The value chain (government → concession → developer → InvIT → unitholder)

1. **Policy and planning:** MoRTH sets the plan (Bharatmala 34,800 km, launched 2017, 82% of awarded length completed by Dec-2025, deadline now FY28 [V PRS]); Vision 2047 (high-speed corridor network) [K].
2. **Procurement:** NHAI (60% of the MoRTH budget), NHIDCL and state PWDs award projects by mode. Bids are on: lowest project cost (HAM/EPC); highest premium or lowest grant (BOT); highest upfront concession fee (TOT).
3. **Land and clearances:** 80% land typically required before the appointed date [K]. Land cost rose from ~₹0.8 Cr/ha pre-2015 to ~₹3.6 Cr/ha after the RFCTLARR Act 2013; NHAI spent ~₹1.7 lakh Cr on land acquisition over 2018–23 [V PRS].
4. **Construction (EPC):** the developer's EPC arm, or a third-party EPC (L&T, DBL, GR, PNC, KNR, HG, Ashoka). Key inputs: bitumen (crude-linked), cement, steel, aggregates, diesel, labour, equipment [K].
5. **Financing:** project finance from banks/NBFCs (roads ≈ **24% of infra bank credit, ₹13.64 lakh Cr outstanding Mar-2025** [V PRS]); typical 70:30 debt:equity at financial close [K]; refinancing after COD at lower spreads (IRB: 200–300 bp compression from construction to stabilisation [V presentation]).
6. **Tolling and O&M:** FASTag RFID (>98% of NH toll [V-snip Gadkari]; ~97% across IRB [V]); NPCI's NETC switch; IHMCL (Indian Highways Management Co.) runs the NHAI tolling stack; ANPR/MLFF pilots.
7. **Monetisation:** NHAI sells TOT bundles and InvIT rounds; developers sell stabilised SPVs to InvITs (Pvt→Pub at IRB; Ashoka/KNR/PNC HAM sales to Indus/Capital/Cube/Bharat Highways).
8. **Unitholders:** pension/sovereign funds, insurers, MFs, family offices, and retail (public InvITs).

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **At bidding:** the equity IRR is set by the bid. IRB targets **15–16% equity IRR on TOT bids** [V presentation]. HAM equity IRR is typically ~12–16% depending on bid aggression and leverage [E/K].
- **At de-risking:** value jumps between construction and stabilisation (IRB: "improvement of approx. 200–300 bps" in cost of debt and equity over years 4–11) [V presentation]. The **developer captures this re-rating spread when it sells** to an InvIT at 12–12.5% IRR [V].
- **Over the asset life:** traffic growth + WPI-linked rate revision + deleveraging. IRB claims value "keeps on improving till the 18th or 19th year" of a 25-year concession [V presentation].
- **In O&M:** long fixed-price O&M contracts with the sponsor. IRB's O&M book of **₹42,400 Cr** is a ~20-year annuity [V].
- **Who loses value:** EPC contractors in a weak award cycle (median EPC bid discounts **~30% FY25, ~35% FY26** below NHAI estimates [V-snip ICRA]); BOT bidders whose traffic forecasts miss; concessionaires hit by policy changes (WPI factor, annual pass, alternate free roads, MAV bans).

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets (what drives traffic)

- **Traffic composition:** commercial vehicles (LCV/bus/2-axle/3-axle/MAV) typically pay **2–6× the car tariff** and can be **~60–75% of toll revenue** on freight corridors [K/E]. MAV share drives sensitivity to freight and diesel. On IRB's assets, Yedeshi–Aurangabad and Solapur–Yedeshi revenue fell in FY24–FY25 because MAVs were **diverted by court order** (Aurangabad High Court restriction since Aug-2023) [V CRISIL; V presentation].
- **Freight:** roads carry **~70% of domestic freight** (2022) [V PRS]; trucks expected to grow from **4 mn (2022) to ~17 mn (2050)** [V PRS/NITI]. E-way bills are the best high-frequency proxy. FY26 e-way bill growth ran **~mid-to-high teens** after GST 2.0 [K; FLAG — not fetched].
- **Passenger cars:** car traffic is the growth leader on expressways (MPEW, AV). Car volumes respond to income, fuel prices and **the annual pass**, which shifts frequent car users onto a fixed fee (~33% of car ETC transactions by Jun-2026 [V-snip]).
- **Network effects:** new feeder links and expressways divert or induce traffic. **Positive:** Palsit–Dankuni toll revision +~47% after its completion certificate (14-Jul-2025) [V CRISIL]; Q1 FY27 Palsit toll +47.7% [V presentation]. **Negative:** NHIT notes traffic diversion "due to the alternate road being made free by MoRTH" at two sites [V NHIT].
- **Shocks:**
  - **Covid (FY21):** toll +1.5% nationally [V PRS]; IRB revenue fell to ₹5,299 Cr [V screener]; the concession framework gave **100% concession extension + 50% O&M reimbursement** for force-majeure periods [V presentation].
  - **West Asia (2026):** NHIT saw MAV traffic disruption on one corridor [V]; IRB InvIT said geopolitics "weighed on the broader economy to some extent" [V]. IRB's Q1 call said "not seeing any meaningful impact… growth driven primarily by domestic consumption" [V].
  - **Diesel price hikes (May-2026, four rounds)** raise truck operating costs. Toll is ~10–15% of a truck trip's cost [E], so they slow marginal freight but do not stop it.
- **Observed traffic growth (FY26–Q1 FY27):**
  - ICRA national traffic ~6% (FY26) [V].
  - Cube traffic **+9.3%** (Q1 FY27) [V].
  - IRB InvIT toll +8% LFL (Q1 FY27) [V].
  - IRB WOS toll: MPEW **+9.2%**, AV **+21.2%** (Q1 FY27) [V].
  - **IRB Sep-2026:** WOS **+17% LFL**; group +24% incl. new assets [V].

### 4.2 "Where ₹100 of toll goes" — two archetypes [E composites, ILLUSTRATIVE]

**(a) A stabilised BOT/TOT toll SPV (year 8 of 20; ~55% debt to EV):**
- Toll ₹100.
- O&M + tolling ₹7.
- Major-maintenance provision ₹6.
- Insurance/admin ₹2.
- **EBITDA ₹85** (IRB's BOT segment margin is 89–91%; InvIT segment 94–95% [V]).
- Interest ₹33.
- Amortisation of intangible ₹30.
- Tax ₹5 (MAT/80-IA history; varies).
- **PAT ~₹17**.
- But **cash available for equity/unitholders after principal ~₹30–35**, because amortisation is non-cash. That is why InvIT DPU far exceeds EPS.

**(b) An EPC/HAM developer (consolidated, construction-heavy year):**
- Revenue ₹100.
- Materials + subcontract ₹65–70.
- Staff ₹5.
- Other ₹5–8.
- **EBITDA ₹18–22** (reference group FY26: **21.3%** [V screener sums]).
- Interest ₹4–9 (rises when HAM equity is funded with debt).
- Depreciation ₹2–4.
- Tax ₹3–4.
- **PAT ₹6–10 before asset-sale gains.**

### 4.3 Capacity: who can build and who can own

- **Construction capacity** is not the constraint. With awards at ~7,000 km against ~10,000 km/year of execution capacity, the order books of mid-size EPC firms are depleting (NHAI order book "declining" — ICRA [V]). Competition is fierce (bid discounts ~35% FY26 [V-snip]).
- **Ownership capacity** sets the limit for monetisation. It depends on InvIT investor appetite (sovereign/pension capital) and bank/bond market depth. Rising G-sec yields (7.2–7.4%) and the repo hike tighten it at the margin.

## 6. Business-model archetypes and their economics

| Archetype | Examples | Revenue model | EBITDA margin | Capital intensity | Return driver | Key risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Sponsor + O&M platform (toll owner/recycler)** | **IRB** (since FY24–25) | Toll on WOS + InvIT distributions + PM fees + O&M/EPC to own trusts | 52–57% consolidated; 89–91% BOT segment, 94–95% InvIT segment, 15% construction [V] | High, but recycled (equity reused) | Bid IRR (15–16%) vs transfer IRR (12–12.5%) spread; leverage; traffic | Traffic, rates, policy, concession end (MPEW 2030), complexity |
| **B. EPC contractor + HAM developer (asset-light-ish)** | PNC, KNR, HG, GR, DBL, Ashoka | EPC contract revenue; HAM annuities in SPVs; periodic HAM SPV sales to InvITs | 19–26% (screener FY26) [V] | Moderate: 10–15% equity per HAM SPV [K] | Order book × execution × margin; sale gains | Award cycle (FY24–26 trough), bid discounts ~35%, working capital, land delays |
| **C. Road InvIT (yield vehicle)** | NHIT, Cube, IRB InvIT, IRBIT, Capital, Vertis, Interise | Toll and/or annuity from SPVs, distributed ≥90% of NDCF | 70–85% (Cube 73.5%, IRB InvIT 79% FY26 [V screener]); NHIT 80% Q1 FY27 [V] | Very high; leverage 42–55% of EV | Traffic + WPI escalation + accretive acquisitions; cost of debt | Rates (yield spread), acquisition pricing, concession run-off (finite life → return of capital) |
| **D. Government monetiser** | NHAI (TOT, NHIT sponsor, SPV securitisation) | Upfront fees; ~10% sponsor stake | n/a | n/a | Debt reduction (₹3.48 → ₹2.40 lakh Cr) | Political optics of tolls; under-pricing vs over-pricing bundles |
| **E. Global integrated concession majors** | Ferrovial, Vinci, Transurban, Atlas Arteria | Toll concessions (often 30–99 years), construction, airports | Toll divisions 65–75% [K] | Very high | Duration (99-yr 407 ETR), dynamic pricing, CPI-linked tolls | Regulation, rates, traffic |

**How the economics differ:**
- **Duration:** Indian NH concessions run 15–30 years (TOT ~15–20; BOT ~20–30; HAM 15 years of annuity after a ~2–2.5 year build). Global toll majors own 30–99-year assets (407 ETR to 2098 [K]). Value per unit of EBITDA is therefore structurally lower in India: **an Indian InvIT trades ~12–13× EV/EBITDA, against ~20×+ for long-dated global toll roads** [E; K for global].
- **Indexation:** India uses a WPI formula (3% + 40% of WPI) for post-2008 concessions, so pricing is **less than full inflation pass-through** when WPI > 5% and **more than full** when WPI < 5% (the fixed 3% floor). Transurban's tolls rise at CPI or a minimum of 4% [K]; French autoroutes at 70% of CPI plus works-linked add-ons [K].
- **Traffic risk allocation:** HAM (none) < TOT (proven traffic) < greenfield BOT (full).
- **Exit:** HAM SPVs sell to InvITs at ~1.0–1.4× book [E/K]; IRB's Pvt→Pub sales imply ~12–12.5% IRR to buyers [V].

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Institutions
- **MoRTH** (policy, NH Fee Rules, budget); **NHAI** (~60% of MoRTH spend; concession authority; AAA quasi-sovereign); **NHIDCL** (NE and border roads); **IHMCL** (tolling technology, FASTag ecosystem); **NPCI** (NETC switch); **SEBI** (InvIT Regulations 2014, as amended); **RBI** (bank exposure to roads, ~24% of infra credit) [V PRS; K].
- Model Concession Agreements: BOT (2009 MCA, amended), HAM MCA (2016), TOT MCA (2018) [K].

### 7.2 Toll-rate indexation (the WPI formula)
- **National Highways Fee (Determination of Rates and Collection) Rules, 2008:** base rates (per km, by vehicle class, 2007-08 base) revised **every 1 April** by a **fixed 3% a year (compounding) plus 40% of the change in WPI**. Results are rounded to the nearest ₹5 [K; V IRB presentation "3% + 40% of WPI"; V CRISIL "fixed 3% plus 40% of WPI change"].
- **Base WPI month:** ICRA says **newer projects are linked to December WPI and older projects to March WPI** [V ICRA via autocarpro]. Pre-2008 concessions run under the **1997 rules / project-specific formulas**, some with fuller WPI pass-through [K; FLAG].
- **Rounding bites when inflation is low:** IRB said the **1-Apr-2026 revision was "roughly 2.5%, largely due to rounding to the nearest ₹5"**, after ~3–3.5% in FY26 [V call 30-Jul-2026].
- **Arithmetic for 1-Apr-2027** [E]: WPI (Dec-2026) at ICRA's **8–8.5%** → revision ≈ 3% + 0.4 × 8.25% ≈ **6.3%**. ICRA: **6.2–6.4% (newer) and 4.5–5.5% (older; March-2027 WPI at 4.5–5.5%)** [V].
- **The WPI "linking factor" episode:**
  - **13-Sep-2025:** an NHAI circular changed the factor used to convert WPI 2004-05 to the 2011-12 series from **1.641 to 1.561**. That cut base toll rates by **~3–5%**.
  - **13-Oct-2025:** CRISIL estimated a **~100 bp median cut in equity IRR** across 64 toll roads with **~₹95,000 Cr** invested [V-snip CRISIL press release].
  - The Highway Operators' Association of India and Roadstar Infra InvIT challenged it.
  - **8-Jul-2026: NHAI reversed it and restored 1.641** [V ION Analytics, 8-Jul-2026].
  - **The primer presents the cut as current policy risk and never mentions the reversal (see §3.4).**
- **WPI deflation risk:** with the 3% fixed component, a WPI decline of up to −7.5% still yields a non-negative revision under the formula [E: 3% + 0.4 × (−7.5%) = 0]. Real toll yields fall in deflation, but nominal rates rarely fall. **HAM annuities** are indexed via a price-index multiple (WPI/CPI blend) on the bid project cost during construction [K].

### 7.3 The FASTag Annual Pass (from 15-Aug-2025)
- **₹3,000 for one year or 200 crossings**, private non-commercial cars/jeeps/vans, at NH/NE fee plazas. Notified by amending the NH Fee Rules (gazette **17-Jun-2025**). **₹3,075 for FY27** from 1-Apr-2026 [V-snip NBM&CW / FPJ / angelone].
- **Uptake:** **>77 lakh passes and >63 crore transactions** by Jun-2026; pass users ≈ **33% of car/jeep/van ETC transactions** [V NBM&CW, Gadkari RS reply]. **FY26 pass receipts ₹1,795.18 Cr** (7.5 months) [V-snip RS reply].
- **Economics:** ₹3,000 ÷ 200 = **₹15 per crossing**, against a typical car single-trip fee of ~₹60–₹350 [K/E], so frequent car users get a ~75–95% discount.
- **Concessionaires are compensated:**
  - The pass is treated as a **Change in Law** for BOT/TOT/InvIT projects whose bid due date was before the 17-Jun-2025 notification.
  - NHAI's **1-Sep-2025 circular** sets a **daily compensation formula** (annual-pass transactions × single-trip car fare × a plaza constant); an SOP of **17-Oct-2025** set up a portal linked with NPCI [V-snip JSA/Drishti/BS].
  - For new bids, the pass is disclosed in the bid documents and **not compensated** [V-snip]. **This shifts car-traffic risk onto new TOT/BOT bidders.**
- **Evidence:** NHIT says "Revenue for FY27 includes compensation for annual pass" [V]. Cube's Q1 FY27 revenue growth (+19.3%) ran ahead of toll growth (+11.3%), partly because of annuity receipts and an annual-pass compensation recognition [V PR; V-snip scanx gives ~₹108 Cr — its "₹1,081 crore" appears to be ₹ mn mislabelled; FLAG].
- IRB calls the pass "reinforcing effectiveness in promoting seamless and economical travel" in its concession-terms slide [V]. There is no IRB disclosure of the compensation amount [gap].

### 7.4 Concession protections (NHAI MCA terms, as IRB summarises them) [V presentation]
- **Termination payments:** 90% of debt covered even on concessionaire default; up to **150% of equity + 100% of debt** for NHAI default or political force majeure; aggregate NHAI liability up to **115% of TPC**.
- **Floor and cap:** concession **extended up to 20%** if traffic falls short of target on target dates; **reduced up to 10%** for surplus.
- **Buy-back:** NHAI may buy back if traffic consistently exceeds design capacity for 2 years.
- **NHAI support:** up to **40% of TPC** as equity support (grant) or construction support.
- **Force majeure:** 100% extension of concession for the affected period + 50% O&M reimbursement (as applied for Covid).
- **Change of ownership** allowed 1 year after COD and punch-list completion. This enables sales to InvITs.
- **TOT-specific:** "revenue-linked concession period" of 20 years (TOT-17, TOT-18) [V LoA filings]. The period adjusts if realised toll deviates from a base case [K; FLAG mechanism].

### 7.5 SEBI InvIT framework
- Leverage cap **49% of the value of InvIT assets; up to 70% if AAA-rated and with ≥6 years of continuous distributions** [K: SEBI; FLAG exact conditions]. IRBIT reports **net debt/EV 45%** (SEBI basis) [V CRISIL]; Cube 45.17% [V]; NHIT 0.42× [V].
- Distribute **≥90% of net distributable cash flows**, at least half-yearly (quarterly in practice) [K].
- **Privately placed vs public:** IRBIT and NHIT are privately placed but listed (high minimum lot sizes); IRB InvIT, Cube (since 2026) and Capital Infra are public [V/K].
- Sponsor holding: a minimum of 15% for 3 years post-listing (lowered for later years) [K; FLAG]. IRB keeps ~16% of IRB InvIT, topped up via the ₹351 Cr preferential issue [V].

### 7.6 Other policy items
- **MLFF / ANPR barrier-free tolling:** **17 plazas awarded, 5 operational; bids invited for 104** (Jul-2026) [V NBM&CW]. Live sites include **Choryasi (Gujarat)**, **Mundka–Bakkarwala (Delhi UER-II)** and **Daulatpura (Delhi–Jaipur NH-48, Jun-2026)** [V-snip]. IRB says "certain clarifications [on MLFF] are still required" for TOT-20/21/22 bidding [V call].
- **GNSS tolling:** rules notified **Sep-2024** for satellite-based ETC [V-snip BS]. The plan is a **hybrid within FASTag** with dedicated GNSS lanes; IHMCL has issued an EOI for "toll charger" software. A Bengaluru–Mysuru pilot is reported (single source) [V-snip]. GNSS-equipped private vehicles get **20 km/day free** under the 2024 rules [K; FLAG].
- **Plaza spacing rule:** NH Fee Rules require **60 km between plazas** on the same section in the same direction, with exceptions; local-resident concessions within 20 km [K]. The minister repeatedly promised to remove plazas within 60 km (2022) [K].
- **Committee on Estimates (2025):** recommended revising the toll framework (base rate and inflation indexing), a refund mechanism for incomplete highways, and better FASTag scanning [V PRS]. **This is a latent political risk for toll levels.**
- **Overloading enforcement:** Cube's CK asset saw a revenue jump from "start of overloading [penalty collection] at Aroli Plaza since Feb'26" [V NHIT — this note is in the NHIT deck about its own CK asset]. Overloading fees are a material swing item for MAV-heavy roads.
- **BOT eligibility:** net-worth criterion **25% (50% for PE)** of project cost [V call].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **1995–2004: Pioneering BOT.**
   - NHAI Act 1988 (operational 1995).
   - The Mhaiskar family's Ideal Road Builders builds the **Thane–Bhiwandi bypass, among India's first BOT toll roads** [primer; K].
   - The **Golden Quadrilateral (NHDP-I)**, launched 1998–2001, is mostly built on EPC/annuity [K].
   - **Mumbai–Pune Expressway (opened 2002)** was built by MSRDC; **IRB won its toll rights in 2004 (₹918 Cr upfront, 15 years to 2019)** [K].
2. **2005–2012: The BOT boom.**
   - The 2009 MCA and VGF; aggressive premium bids (GMR, GVK, Reliance Infra, IRB, IL&FS, Ashoka, Sadbhav, Essel). IRB lists in **Feb-2008** (IPO ~₹940 Cr — presentation says "INR 9.24 bn") [V].
   - **2011–12 peak awards on BOT-premium terms** followed by a **2012–14 bust:** land delays, GDP slowdown, premium deferment and many terminated projects. **IL&FS** collapsed in Sep-2018, partly on roads [K].
3. **2015–2017: The government takes the risk back.**
   - **HAM introduced (2016)** and EPC scaled up. Private share of financing collapses from 51% (FY15) [V PRS].
   - **May-2017: IRB InvIT Fund IPO, India's first InvIT** (₹5,035 Cr; ₹102/unit) [K; V "India's First Public InvIT launched 2017"].
   - **Bharatmala approved Oct-2017** [V PRS].
4. **2018–2021: Monetisation begins.**
   - **TOT-1 (2018)** was won by Macquarie for **₹9,681 Cr, ~1.5× NHAI's estimate** [K].
   - NHAI borrows heavily (debt peaks at **₹3.48 lakh Cr, FY22** [V]).
   - **FASTag mandatory from 15/16-Feb-2021** [K]. IRB wins **MPEW TOT-2 ("Mumbai Pune 2.0") from MSRDC in 2020 for ₹8,262 Cr for 10 years** [K; V "Awarded Mumbai Pune 2.0 (TOT)" 2020].
   - **Nov-2021: NHIT (NHAI InvIT) Round 1** [V]. **Dec-2021: Cintra (Ferrovial) and GIC close the ~₹5,300 Cr preferential investment in IRB**; the Private InvIT is floated with GIC at 49% (~₹4,000 Cr) [V].
5. **2022–2024: Private road InvITs proliferate; NHAI stops borrowing.**
   - NHAI market borrowing is halted from FY23 [V PRS]. Cube, Vertis, Interise, Bharat Highways (2024 IPO), Capital Infra (Jan-2025 IPO) [K].
   - **IRB wins TOT-12 (Lalitpur–Lakhnadon) and TOT-13 (Jhansi–Gwalior–Kota) and the Hyderabad ORR TOT (2023, ₹7,380 Cr, 30 years from HMDA)** [V presentation; K for the HORR fee].
   - Toll pool +41.6% in FY23 [V].
   - **Road awards slump from FY24** after the 2024 elections and approval delays [V-snip].
6. **2025–2026: Recycling and policy turbulence.**
   - **NHIT R4 (₹18,380 Cr, Mar-2025)** [V-snip].
   - **Annual pass (15-Aug-2025)**; WPI linking-factor cut (13-Sep-2025) and its reversal (8-Jul-2026); GST 2.0 (22-Sep-2025).
   - **IRB InvIT buys 3 assets from IRBIT (Nov-2025)** and VM7 HAM from IRB (Dec-2025). **IRBIT wins TOT-17 (₹9,270 Cr) and TOT-18 (₹3,087 Cr)**; Ganga Expressway Group-1 tolls from 17-May-2026 [V].
   - West Asia shock (Feb–Apr 2026); repo hike to 5.50% (7-Oct-2026). **SYTL+CGTL transferred to IRB InvIT (30-Sep-2026).** Cube goes public (2026).

**The cycle pattern:** private BOT boom (2005–12) → bust and government EPC/HAM (2013–23) → private *ownership* boom through monetisation (2018–) while private *construction risk* stays low. The current debate is whether the **BOT-toll revival** (NHAI's FY27 BOT pipeline of 1,388–1,735 km, the toll-cum-annuity hybrid, relaxed net-worth rules) can succeed when the 2025 BOT packages drew no bids.

### 9.4 TOT pricing arithmetic: what IRB paid for TOT-17 [E, ILLUSTRATIVE]
- Upfront ₹9,270 Cr for 366 km over 20 years. Q1 FY27 toll ₹174.7 Cr → **~₹700 Cr annual run-rate**; EBITDA at ~88% ≈ ₹616 Cr → **entry EV/EBITDA ~15×**.
- **Unlevered project IRR** (20 years, 25%-of-toll overlay every 6th year, no terminal value): **~8% at 6% toll growth, ~10% at 8%, ~12% at 10%** [E, computed].
- IRB's stated 15–16% bid equity IRR therefore needs **~9–10% toll growth**, back-ended debt (the TOT-17 loan of ₹6,930 Cr runs to Mar-2043 [V CRISIL]) and refinancing gains. **TOT is a bet on India's nominal traffic × WPI growth staying ≥9% for two decades.**

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **FASTag saturation:** **>98% of NH toll** is via FASTag (Jul-2026) [V-snip]; ~97% at IRB [V]. Cash leakage is largely gone; the remaining leakage is **exemptions, mis-classification of vehicle class and blacklisted tags**. Cash/non-FASTag users pay double [K].
2. **MLFF (barrier-free, ANPR + RFID gantries):** 5 operational plazas, 17 awarded, 104 in bidding [V]. **Risks for concessionaires:**
   - enforcement/recovery from violators (no barrier), which depends on Vahan-linked e-challans [K];
   - who bears revenue loss in the transition (the IRB call flags "clarifications" pending) [V];
   - the MLFF cost burden in TOT bids (TOT-20/21/22 added initial capex) [V].
3. **GNSS (satellite) distance-based tolling:** a hybrid rollout. The concession question is whether **distance-based charging (pay per km actually driven)** replaces plaza-based "section" tolling. Plaza tolling charges the full section even for partial trips (in effect a subsidy to the concessionaire); distance tolling could **cut revenue on sections with high local/short-trip traffic** and raise it where plazas are sparse [E]. The 20 km/day free allowance [K] is a direct revenue haircut on peri-urban sections.
4. **Annual pass and future passes:** ~33% of car ETC transactions are already pass-based [V-snip]. A truck pass or a broader "toll-free local" scheme would be a material change in law. Compensation exists for old bids only.
5. **EVs:** no toll exemption at the national level (some states exempt EVs on state roads/expressways) [K; FLAG]. **The issue is fuel-cess funding (CRIF ₹46,930 Cr FY27e [V])**: EVs erode cess, which raises the government's reliance on tolls and monetisation [E].
6. **Freight modal shift:** Dedicated Freight Corridors (Eastern and Western DFC, commissioned 2023–25 [K]) and Gati Shakti could take long-haul bulk from parallel NHs. The Western DFC parallels several IRB corridors (Gujarat, Rajasthan, Maharashtra) [E].
7. **Parallel free roads / expressways:** new greenfield expressways (Delhi–Mumbai, Bharatmala) **divert traffic from older tolled NHs** (NHIT AP/AS sites; MoRTH making an alternate road free) [V]. The Mumbai–Pune "Missing Link" (Khopoli–Kusgaon tunnels, an MSRDC project) changes MPEW route length and tolling [K; FLAG].
8. **Construction technology:** precast, cement-concrete pavements and drones cut EPC margins for laggards [K].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **The toll pool compounds at nominal GDP-plus, and the WPI formula is about to turn from headwind to tailwind.**

   - FY27 growth slows to 7–9% (2.5–4% rate hikes, rounding), but **FY28 rate revisions jump to ~6.3%** as WPI runs at 8%+ [V ICRA].
   - Traffic: 5–9% (Cube +9.3%, NH ~6%). Every 1 pp of WPI adds ~0.4 pp to post-2008 tolls.
2. **The award cycle is broken for builders but irrelevant for owners.**
   - Awards ~7,000 km (FY26), NHAI 3,124 km; EPC bid discounts ~35%; the reference EPC group's revenue −15.7% and ROCE collapsing; stocks at 0.4–0.9× book [V].

3. **Capital recycling through InvITs is the business model.**
   - IRB: bid 15–16% → transfer 12–12.5%; ₹8,400 Cr transferred; ₹4,900 Cr equity released; ₹14,000 Cr redeployed [V].

4. **Rates are the valuation lever.**
   - Long-duration, leveraged (InvITs at 42–45% net debt/EV), mostly floating-rate debt [V CRISIL].

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Toll asset (BOT/TOT):**
`Toll revenue = Σ_vehicle-classes (Traffic_PCU or vehicles × Tariff per class) × Days`, where `Tariff_t = Tariff_t−1 × [1 + 3% + 0.4 × ΔWPI]` rounded to ₹5, adjusted for exemptions + annual-pass compensation + overloading fees.
`EBITDA = Toll − O&M − major-maintenance provision` (≈ 85–91% of toll).
`Equity cash flow = EBITDA − interest − principal − tax − capex`.
`Value = PV(equity cash flows to concession end)`; no terminal value.

**HAM asset:** `Annuity income = (60% × Bid project cost × price-index multiple) ÷ 30 half-years + interest (bank rate + 3%) on the reducing balance + O&M payments`.

**InvIT:** `DPU = (Σ SPV CFADS − trust interest/principal − reserves) × ≥90% ÷ units`; `Yield = DPU ÷ price`.

**IRB (sponsor platform):** `PAT ≈ WOS toll EBITDA + InvIT distributions/fees + O&M/EPC margin − interest − D&A − tax + recycling gains`.

### C.1 Worked example 1 — the 1-Apr-2027 toll revision on a post-2008 NH concession [E, ILLUSTRATIVE]
- Car single-trip tariff today ₹165. WPI (Dec-2026 vs Dec-2025) +8.25% (ICRA mid-point).
- Revision = 3% + 0.4 × 8.25% = **6.3%** → ₹175.4 → rounds to **₹175** (+6.1%).
- With WPI at +1% (as before 1-Apr-2026): 3.4% → ₹170.6 → **₹170** (+3.0%). At a ₹65 base, 3.4% gives ₹67.2 → **₹65 (0%)**. **This is why low-tariff vehicle classes saw ~2.5% average increases in 2026** (IRB [V]).

### C.2 Worked example 2 — Mumbai–Pune Expressway, Q1 FY27 [V + E]
- Q1 FY27 gross toll ₹499.8 Cr (+9.2%) = **₹5.49 Cr/day**.
- Rate effect ~+2.5% (Apr-2026) → **traffic/mix ≈ +6.5%** [E].

### C.4 Worked example 4 — HAM project, ₹1,000 Cr bid project cost [E, ILLUSTRATIVE]
- NHAI pays ₹400 Cr during construction.
- Developer funds ₹600 Cr (₹450 Cr debt + ₹150 Cr equity).

### C.5 Worked example 5 — an InvIT unit (IRB InvIT Fund) [V + E]
- Q1 FY27 DPU ₹1.625 = interest ₹1.00 + return of capital ₹0.625. Annualised ₹6.50 ÷ ₹61 price = **10.7% cash yield**.
- Against the 10-year G-sec at ~7.3% → **~340 bp spread**.
- Of that yield, ~38% is return of capital (finite concession life). The "income" yield is ~6.6%, below the G-sec. **A road InvIT is an amortising bond with traffic and WPI upside, not a perpetuity.**
