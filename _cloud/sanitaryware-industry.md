**Units, conventions and FX**
- ₹ crore (Cr) = 10 million. ₹ lakh crore = ₹1 trillion. 1 lakh = 100,000; 10 lakh = 1 million (mn).
- **Pieces ("pcs")** are the unit for sanitaryware and faucet volumes — but a "piece" can be a ₹600 squatting pan or a ₹60,000 one-piece rimless WC, and a faucet "piece" can be a ₹300 PTMT bib-cock or a ₹40,000 thermostatic mixer. Cera states its sanitaryware capacity in **metric tonnes (MTPA)**, not pieces (trap 6).
- **Gas:** ₹/SCM (standard cubic metre) ≈ ₹/m³ as Cera quotes it; LNG benchmarks in $/mmbtu. Rule of thumb ~**30 SCM per mmbtu**, i.e. ₹1/SCM ≈ $0.35/mmbtu at ₹95–96/$ [E, from the GEL Morbi formula, V-sib]. Thangadh/Morbi propane users quote **₹/kg**.
- **Brass:** ₹/kg, as Cera quotes its procurement price (₹665/kg Dec-2025 → ~₹900/kg Jul-2026 [V]). Brass for faucets is ~57–63% copper + ~35–40% zinc + lead/other (CW617N / "60/40" and "63/37" grades) [K].
- FY26 = Apr-2025 to Mar-2026. Q1 FY27 = Apr–Jun 2026. **Q2 FY27 = Jul–Sep 2026.** All Indian companies here have a March year-end. Roca, Geberit, Masco, TOTO (Mar YE — TOTO and LIXIL use March) report as noted.
- **FX: ₹94.9 = US$1** (19-Oct-2026, as given in the brief; not re-verified — a sibling dossier used ₹96 from GEL's Sep-2026 formula). Euro €1 ≈ ₹108–110, CHF 1 ≈ ₹117–119, ¥100 ≈ ₹62–64 [E; cross-rates not verified — use ranges].
- **Macro context (from the brief and sibling dossiers [V-sib]):** **West Asia war from 28-Feb-2026** (US/Israeli strikes on Iran) → Hormuz disruption, LNG/LPG shock; ceasefire reported 7-Apr-2026 but supply stayed tight and prices re-intensified by late October (Kajaria's chairman cited Brent ~$116 on 26-Oct). India's emergency allocation capped CGD industrial users at 80% of six-month average use (Mar-2026). **Gujarat Gas, GSPC and GSPL merged into Gujarat Energy Ltd (GEL) from May-2026** — Morbi/Thangadh's piped-gas supplier is now "GEL"; older sources say "Gujarat Gas". **US tariffs:** IEEPA tariffs struck down by the US Supreme Court **20-Feb-2026**; Section 122 10% surcharge to **24-Jul-2026**; thereafter a **10% Section 301** duty on top of MFN for India. Section 232 50% on copper semi-finished products and derivatives [K, Jul/Aug-2025 proclamation — scope over brass faucets not verified; FLAG].
- "Organised" = branded, multi-location, listed or rated companies (Cera, Hindware, Parryware/Roca, Jaquar, Kohler, Grohe/American Standard, Duravit, Hansgrohe, TOTO, Kerovit, Somany, Astral, Johnson Bathrooms (Prism), Asian Granito, Simpolo, Essess, Plumber, etc.). "Unorganised" = the hundreds of single-plant ceramic potteries in **Thangadh** (Surendranagar district) and **Morbi**, plus thousands of small brass/PTMT fittings workshops (Jamnagar, Rajkot, Delhi-NCR/Jagadhri, Ludhiana) and traders of Chinese imports.

## READ THIS FIRST: twelve definitional traps

1. **Cera: standalone vs consolidated on screener — use standalone.**
   - Screener's **consolidated** Cera page stops at **FY25** (annual) and **Jun-2025** (quarters) because Cera stopped consolidating after divesting its two 51% LLPs on 29-Sep-2025 [V screener; V CARE]. Its "P/E 23.7, ROCE 22.4%, ROE 18.2%, BV ₹1,049" are **stale FY25-based ratios** — do not use.
   - The **standalone** page has FY26 and Q1 FY27: **P/E 28.3, BV ₹1,142, ROCE 19.2%, ROE 14.8%** [V screener 3-Nov-26]. Consolidated and standalone differed by <1% of revenue anyway (FY25: ₹1,926 Cr vs ₹1,915 Cr).
2. **Cera changed its revenue definition in Q1 FY27 — revenue fell ~2.5–3% with no change in profit.**
   - From Q1 FY27, **turnover discounts** (dealer incentive schemes) are **netted from revenue** instead of booked as an expense; "reported revenue for the current quarter has been reduced by 2.5%, and…the corresponding quarter of the previous year…by 3%"; the previous quarter (Q4 FY26) was also restated [V Q1 call].
   - Hence Q1 FY26 revenue is **₹407 Cr** (restated) vs ~₹420 Cr originally reported; Q4 FY26 is **₹624 Cr** on screener (restated) vs **₹644 Cr** in the Q4 call (original) [V]. **Screener's quarterly row is therefore a mix of bases**: Jun-25 and Mar-26 restated, Sep-25 (₹488 Cr) and Dec-25 (₹499 Cr) original. Their sum (₹2,018 Cr) does **not** equal FY26 annual **₹2,050 Cr** (original basis).
   - **FY26 on the new basis ≈ ₹1,990–2,000 Cr** [E: ₹2,050 Cr × ~0.97]. CareEdge's FY26 "total operating income" is **₹2,036 Cr** (FY25 ₹1,880 Cr) [V CARE] — a third basis (probably net of some discounts/other operating income) — FLAG.
   - EBITDA margin rises mechanically by ~0.4 pp: "14% on ₹100 → 14% divided by ₹97 comes to something like 14.4%" (management) [V]. **Always state the basis when comparing FY27 margins with FY26.**
3. **Cera's net cash is ~₹800–900 Cr, NOT ₹1,470 Cr (our primer is wrong).**
   - Company: **cash and cash equivalents ₹853 Cr at 31-Mar-2026** [V Q4 call]; **₹943 Cr at 30-Jun-2026** (CareEdge: "unencumbered cash and bank balances/liquid investments of around ₹943 crore") [V Q1 call; V CARE]. Debt ₹47 Cr (Mar-26) [V screener] → **net cash ≈ ₹806 Cr (Mar-26)**, **≈ ₹890–900 Cr (Jun-26)** [E].
   - Screener: borrowings ₹47 Cr; investments ₹815 Cr; cash ~₹48 Cr inside "other assets" [V screener; cash line V-sib] → **~₹816 Cr net cash** — consistent with the company.
   - The primer's "~₹1,470 Cr net cash" and "~₹1,517 Cr cash & investments" are **≈ Cera's net worth (₹1,472 Cr)** [V CARE/CRISIL/PPT] — the primer confused net worth with cash. Every primer statement built on it ("a fifth of the company is money in the bank") is overstated by ~75%. At ₹5,853 Cr m-cap, net cash is **~14%** of market value, not ~20%.
4. **"Sanitaryware" vs "faucetware/bath fittings" vs "bathware" vs "bathroom solutions".**
   - **Sanitaryware** = vitreous-china ceramic fixtures (HS 6910; IS 2556 series): WCs (EWC, one-piece, wall-hung, squatting pans), wash basins, urinals, cisterns (IS 774), bidets.
   - **Faucetware / bath fittings / CP fittings** = brass (or PTMT/ABS) taps, mixers, showers, health faucets, angle valves, concealed diverters (HS 8481.80; IS 8931). "CP" = chrome-plated.
   - **Bathware** (Hindware, Kajaria/Kerovit, Somany) = sanitaryware + faucets (+ wellness, accessories). **Hindware's "bathware" segment (₹1,520 Cr FY26)** is comparable to Cera's sanitaryware + faucet + wellness (~₹1,830 Cr) — **not** to Cera's total revenue (which includes tiles). Jaquar's "group" revenue (₹8,703 Cr FY26) includes **lighting (~8–10%)**, sanitaryware, wellness, water heaters and exports.
   - **Wellness** = bathtubs, whirlpools, shower enclosures, steam, (at Cera) kitchen sinks and mirrors — 1–2% of Cera's revenue.
5. **Pieces vs value.** Faucet volumes are many times sanitaryware volumes (Jaquar produces **52.9 mn bath fittings vs 4.8 mn sanitaryware pieces** a year [V BT]). Never add faucet and sanitaryware pieces. Cera's revenue split is ~47:40 sanitaryware:faucet by value but perhaps 1:2–1:3 by pieces [E].
6. **Cera capacity: tonnes, not pieces; faucets per month, not per year.**
   - **Sanitaryware: 33,750 MTPA installed (31-Mar-2026)** [V CARE]. The primer's "~36 lakh pieces" is not in any company document this session — at an average fired weight of ~10–12 kg per piece it would be **~2.8–3.4 mn pieces** [E; FLAG].
   - **Faucets: 48 lakh pieces p.a. = 4 lakh/month** (raised from 3 lakh/month by a brownfield expansion); **4.3 lakh made in Mar-2026**; being raised to **5 lakh/month (60 lakh p.a.) from Q4 FY27** for ~₹4–6 Cr [V CARE; V Q4 call; V CRISIL]. The primer's "~18.5 L faucet pieces" is ~8–10 years stale.
7. **Utilisation is quoted on in-house capacity only, while 50–60% of what Cera sells is bought in.**
   - Sanitaryware utilisation **70% (Q4 FY26), 61% (Q1 FY27; one of two kilns shut Mar–May 2026), ~80% from June** [V]. Faucets **106% (Q4 FY26), 96% (Q1 FY27)** [V].
   - **Outsourced share: sanitaryware 60%, faucets 46% (Q4 FY26)**; "around 59% of revenue is derived from outsourced manufacturing facilities in FY26, with tiles and wellness products being entirely outsourced" [V Q4 call; V CARE]. Sanitaryware outsourcing is "mostly from Morbi, the multiple suppliers" [V Q4 call]. Our primer says sanitaryware is "mostly in-house" — **wrong**.
   - Some items are **always** bought out: cisterns' plastic parts, seat covers, flushing mechanisms (sanitaryware); polymer/ABS chrome-plated SKUs (faucets) [V Q1 call].
8. **"Traded" vs "manufactured" and the tiles line.** Cera's tiles (~10–11% of revenue) are **100% outsourced** (Morbi and other third parties) [V CARE]. Keep consistent with the ceramic-tiles dossier: tiles were **11% of Q1 FY27 revenue, +22% YoY**; **9% of Q4 FY26, −8.3%** (Morbi shutdown) [V]. Management bundles **tiles + construction chemicals (CC)** at "~₹250 Cr" for FY27 (+20%) [V Q4 call].
9. **EBITDA: three bases.**
   - Screener OP (ex-other income): FY26 **₹269 Cr (13.1%)** [V].
   - Company "EBITDA excluding other income": Q4 FY26 ₹98 Cr (15.2%), Q1 FY27 ₹49.2 Cr (10.1%) — matches screener [V].
   - Company investor-deck EBITDA **including other income**: FY23 ₹323 Cr, FY24 ₹355 Cr, FY25 ₹353 Cr, **FY26 ₹322 Cr** [V PPT; "Operating EBITDA & EBITDA Margins calculated including other income"].
   - CareEdge PBILDT: FY25 ₹258 Cr (13.70%), **FY26 ₹255 Cr (12.55%)**, Q1 FY27 ₹49 Cr (10.13%) [V CARE].
   - Use screener in tables; say "ex-other income".
10. **Gross margin.** Management: Q1 FY27 gross margin **46%** vs a "typical" **~51%**, expected back to ~51% from Q3 if brass and gas hold [V Q1 call]. Screener's 100 − material-cost % is a proxy (Cera's "material cost" includes purchases of traded goods). Use the company figure.
11. **"Project" vs "retail" vs "institutional".** Cera's project (B2B: builders, institutions) share rose ~30% → 35% → **39% (FY26)** → **43% (Q1 FY27)** [V Q4 call; V CARE]. Project orders are **pre-priced for 6–12 months**, so price hikes reach projects 1–2 quarters later than retail [V]. Hindware's "institutional" is **~25%** [V]. Project business carries **lower margins** [V CARE].
12. **"Dealer network" numbers mix tiers.** Cera: **7,000+ dealers and 29,000+ retailers** [V CARE]; the primer's "~20,000 touch-points" is not a company number. Cera retail formats (May-2026): **13 Experience Centres** (company-owned, ~7,000 sq ft avg), **272 CERA Style Galleries** (dealer-owned, >1,000 sq ft), **292 CERA Style Hubs** (dealer-owned, tier B/C), **1,613 CERA Style Centres** (retailer-owned), with **1,400+ more CSCs planned over 3–4 years** [V PPT]. Senator flagship stores: **40 (FY26 call) → target 60 (FY27)**; but the Q1 FY27 call says **"from 35 to something like 50 stores"** by FY27-end [V both] — internal inconsistency, FLAG. Hindware: **500+ brand stores, 500+ distributors, 35,000+ retailers** [V].

# PART 1 — INDUSTRY DOSSIER

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **A ~₹40,000–55,000 Cr industry (sanitaryware + bath fittings, domestic, FY26) in which the organised top ten hold roughly half and one private company (Jaquar) is bigger than the next three combined** [E; see §2 for the sizing build].
  - Organised company revenues FY26 (Indian bathware only, approx.): **Jaquar group ₹8,703 Cr** (incl. lighting/exports; bath fittings + sanitaryware + wellness ≈ ₹7,500–7,900 Cr [E]) [V BT]; **Cera ~₹1,830 Cr** ex-tiles [E from V]; **Hindware bathware ₹1,520 Cr** [V]; **Kohler India ~₹1,900–2,300 Cr** [E from V-snip "$230 mn FY24", 17% CAGR]; **Parryware/Roca India ~₹2,000 Cr** [V-snip undated, FLAG]; **Kerovit ₹414 Cr** [V-sib]; Somany bathware ~₹280 Cr [E from V-sib quarterly]; Grohe/American Standard, Duravit, Hansgrohe, TOTO, Astral, Johnson Bathrooms, AGL, Simpolo etc. collectively ~₹2,500–4,000 Cr [E].
  - **Cera is #3–#5 by value, not "#1 in sanitaryware" on revenue** — but it is among the top three in *ceramic sanitaryware* with Hindware and Parryware [K; CRISIL/CARE describe it as "among the largest"]. Hindware claims **#1 Top-of-Mind Awareness (21%)** in sanitaryware [V Hindware deck]. Jaquar claims **"nearly 60%" of India's (organised) bath-fittings market** [V BT — company claim, not audited].
- **2026 is a cost-shock year that the organised players are passing on.**
  - **Brass** (60% of faucet COGS at Cera [V]) rose from **₹665/kg (Dec-2025) → ₹747 (Jan) → ₹758 (Feb/Mar) → ₹800 (Apr) → ₹860 (May) → ₹880 (Jun) → ~₹900/kg (Jul-2026)** (+35%) [V Cera Q1 call]; CareEdge: "increased by roughly 35% from December 2025 to June 2026" [V]; Hindware: brass **+~40% YoY** in Q1 FY27 [V]. LME copper set records (~$14,533/t Jan-2026; **$14,850/t on 17-Aug-2026**; ~$14,675/t on 28-Oct-2026) and zinc ~$3,960/t [V-snip].
  - **Gas:** Cera's weighted gas cost **₹48.43/m³ in Q1 FY27 vs ₹33.17 a year earlier (+46%)** and ₹35.55 in Q4 FY26 [V]; still roughly **half of Morbi's ₹82–90/SCM** (GEL's Sep-2026 price; ₹82 for sanitaryware/long-term users) [V-sib]. Gas = **3.3% of Cera's revenue (Q1 FY27) vs 2.3% (Q4 FY26)** [V].
  - **Price hikes:** Cera cumulative **~12% sanitaryware, ~16% faucets** (Mar–May 2026: 4%/11% in March; 10%/5% surcharge in April; 8%/5% in May) [V]; "most players" took **16–18% in faucets and 10–12% in sanitaryware** [V Cera Q4 call]; Hindware faucet prices **+~20% over 12 months** [V].
- **Supply side: Thangadh and Morbi broke; branded players with contracted gas did not.**
  - Thangadh (~250 sanitaryware units) was **~10% operational in March-2026** with propane at ~₹70/kg vs ~₹41 earlier and ~40,000 workers idle [V-snip ET]; Morbi's piped-gas sanitaryware units "continued to run low key" while propane tile units shut [V-snip]. Morbi PNG offtake fell to **0.36 mmscmd (83 units) on 31-Mar-2026**, recovering to **2.70 mmscmd (290 units) by 22-Apr** [V-snip Gujarat Gas data].
  - Cera kept firing on **GAIL gas at contracted/subsidised rates (~64–70% of its gas)**; Sabarmati Gas (the other ~30%) cut supply by half, since fully restored [V CRISIL]; Cera shut **one of two kilns March–May** as a precaution, lost 30–35% of production in Q1 [V Q1 call], and is **internalising Morbi-outsourced SKUs** [V].
- **Demand: retail recovered from H2 FY26 after a two-year slump; projects grew throughout.**
  - Cera: "sluggish demand from Q3 of FY24"; retail improved from Q3 FY26; Q4 FY26 volume +12%; Q1 FY27 revenue **+19.5%** (sanitaryware +14%: volume +10, price +2, mix +2; faucets +25%: volume +18, price +4, mix +3) [V].
  - Hindware bathware Q1 FY27 **+14%**; Kerovit Q2 FY27 **+21%**; Somany bathware Q1 FY27 **+22%** [V; V-sib].
  - Real estate: ANAROCK top-7-city **completions 5.19 lakh units in 2025; 5.40 lakh scheduled for 2026 (a decade high)** — the fittings-demand driver — even as **sales fell 14% in 2025 to ~3.96 lakh** and unsold inventory rose to ~5.77 lakh [V-snip ANAROCK].
- **Margins are the debate.** Cera's EBITDA margin (ex-OI) went **16% (FY23–FY24) → 15% (FY25) → 13.1% (FY26) → 10.1% (Q1 FY27)** [V screener]. ### 1.2 Value chain (inputs → customer)

1. **Ceramic sanitaryware (vitreous china).**
   - Body: **ball clay + china clay (kaolin) + feldspar + quartz** (roughly 25–30% ball clay, 20–25% kaolin, 25–30% feldspar, 20–25% quartz [K, standard vitreous-china recipe]); Gujarat/Rajasthan/Kerala clays; feldspar/quartz from Rajasthan [K].
   - Process: slip preparation → **casting** (gypsum moulds, or high-pressure casting with resin moulds — faster, less labour, fewer defects) → demoulding, fettling → drying → **glazing** (spray; robotic glazing at Cera [V PPT]) → **firing in tunnel/shuttle kilns ~1,200–1,250°C, 12–20 hours** on natural gas/propane [K] → inspection, refiring of defects → packing.
   - **Yields** (first-quality output ÷ cast pieces) are the key manufacturing KPI: ~80–90% for good organised plants, much lower for complex one-piece/rimless SKUs and for small potteries [K/E; not disclosed by Cera].
   - **Energy:** Cera's gas intensity **314.7 SCM per tonne of finished product (FY26)**; 309.0 (FY25), 279.0 (FY24), 304.0 (FY23), 360.0 (FY22); power **536.5 kWh/t (FY26)** [V PPT]. At Q1 FY27's ₹48.43/m³, gas ≈ **₹15,200 per tonne of finished product** (~₹150–180 per average WC) [E].
2. **Faucets (brass route).** Brass ingot/rod (from Jamnagar/Rajkot recyclers or primary-copper rod makers) → **hot forging or gravity/low-pressure die casting** → CNC machining → **polishing/robotic grinding** → **nickel-chrome electroplating or PVD** (Cera claims "India's first" multi-colour PVD and robotic surface grinding [V PPT]) → assembly with **ceramic disc cartridges** (largely imported — Spain/Hungary/China [K]) and aerators → pressure testing. Brass ≈ **60% of faucet cost** at Cera [V Q4 call]. Gas use in faucets is "negligible" [V CRISIL].
3. **Bought-out and outsourced supply.** Morbi sanitaryware units supply simpler WCs/basins/pans to branded players on contract (Cera: "mostly from Morbi") [V]; seat covers, flush mechanisms, concealed cisterns, PTMT/ABS fittings are bought out by everyone [V Cera]; Chinese OEMs supply premium smart toilets, sensor faucets, shower panels [K].
4. **Distribution.** Company → (C&F/depots; Cera's centralised Kadi warehouse since FY25 [V CARE]) → **distributors/dealers** (Cera 7,000+) → **retailers/sub-dealers** (Cera 29,000+; Hindware 35,000+) → plumber/contractor/architect-influenced end customer. Parallel **project/B2B channel**: direct to developers, hotels, hospitals, government (Cera ~40%; Hindware ~25%). **Loyalty apps** for plumbers/retailers (Cera: three programmes with 29k+, 73k+ and 11k+ members, ₹1,680+ Cr of secondary sales tracked) [V PPT]. **After-sales service** is a differentiator (Cera: 453 technicians, 24-hour complaint TAT claim) [V PPT].
5. **Influencers.** Plumbers decide most mass-market faucet purchases; architects/interior designers decide premium; developers' procurement teams decide projects (price-driven, pre-priced for 6–12 months) [K; V Cera].

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **Brand + distribution, not the kiln.** Cera earns ~13–16% EBITDA while outsourcing ~59% of revenue [V CARE]; Hindware, which insources more (sanitaryware ~70%, faucets ~50%), earns ~11% [V]. Jaquar, vertically integrated on faucets (52.9 mn pieces), dominates faucets by scale [V BT]. Value sits in **(i)** the brand's ability to hold a 20–40% retail price premium over unbranded, **(ii)** dealer reach and credit discipline, **(iii)** SKU breadth/design (a modern catalogue has thousands of SKUs — inventory days ~100–105 at Cera [V CARE]), and **(iv)** cost position on the in-house half (gas, yields, brass sourcing).
- **The unorganised potteries capture little value** — they sell on price to traders and to branded players' outsourcing desks; their margins are hostage to propane/PNG prices [K; V-snip].
- **Faucets are the more attractive product pool** (higher value per bathroom, faster growth, less capital per rupee of revenue, no gas), but brass-price risk is direct — the 2026 brass spike hit faucet gross margins "severely" [V Cera Q1 call].

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **New residential construction (≈ 40–50% of demand [E]; lag: fittings go in at finishing, 24–36 months after launch).**
   - ANAROCK top-7 cities: **completions 5.19 lakh units (2025); 5.40 lakh scheduled for 2026, highest in a decade** (MMR + Pune ~57%; with Bengaluru ~70%); 30.5 lakh delivered 2017–2025 [V-snip]. A typical Indian apartment has **2–3 bathrooms** → each completion consumes ~2–3 WCs, ~2–4 basins and ~6–12 faucet/shower pieces [E]. 5.4 lakh completions in seven cities alone ≈ **1.1–1.6 mn WCs and 4–6 mn faucet pieces** [E] — a meaningful slice of organised volume.
   - Sales fell **14% to ~3.96 lakh units in 2025** with value +6% (premium mix), and unsold inventory rose **4% to ~5.77 lakh** [V-snip ANAROCK] — supportive for completions (and therefore fittings) in 2026–27 but negative for launches/fittings in 2028+.
   - The West Asia shock put 2026 deliveries "at risk" (labour, cement/steel/energy costs) [V-snip BT 11-Jun-2026].
   - **Project share is rising at the branded players** (Cera 30% → 39–43%) because big developers consolidated and prefer branded specs [V]; this channel is lower-margin, pre-priced and receivable-heavy (Cera's receivable days rose from 35 (FY23) to 48 (FY26) [V CARE]).
2. **Replacement and renovation (≈ 35–45% [E]; "a majority" per our primer, unverified).**
   - Bathrooms are renovated every ~10–15 years [K]; India's post-2000 urban stock is now in its first major renovation cycle. Renovation is retail-led and premium-skewed (the renovator chooses the better WC). Cera's retail share fell to ~61% (FY26) from ~65% (FY25) while retail was "sluggish" from Q3 FY24 to Q2 FY26, and recovered from H2 FY26 [V CARE; V Q4 call].
3. **Rural / tier-2/3 and government sanitation (≈ 10–20% [E]).**
   - **Swachh Bharat Mission (Gramin)**: launched 2-Oct-2014; **>10 crore household toilets** built; rural India declared ODF on 2-Oct-2019; SBM-G Phase II (ODF-Plus) to 2025-26 [K]. These toilets used **squatting pans and basic EWCs, mostly unorganised** — the government push expanded the category more than it helped branded players directly.
   - **PMAY-Urban 2.0** (1 crore urban homes approved Sep-2024) and **PMAY-Gramin** (2 crore more rural homes) [K] — affordable housing uses entry-level fittings (Polipluz's target; Hindware value lines).
   - **Tier mix at Cera: Tier-1 38%, Tier-2 24%, Tier-3 38% (Q1 FY27)**; Q4 FY26 Tier-3 40% [V] — Cera is more tier-3-exposed than its "premium" image suggests. Hindware: **Tier 2/3 ~70% of sales** [V].
   - Cera "focus markets": NE, West Bengal, Uttarakhand, Bihar, Tamil Nadu, MP, Jharkhand, Goa, Chhattisgarh, Odisha [V Q4 call].
4. **Commercial and institutional (≈ 10–15% [E]).** Hotels, hospitals, offices, airports, malls, metro stations — urinals, sensor taps, concealed cisterns, wall-hung WCs. Cera cites tie-ups with McDonald's, Tea Post, Connplex, JSW One Homes, Shalby Hospitals, Federal Bank [V Q4 call].
5. **Premiumisation and the "bathroom as a design space".** Rising share of premium SKUs (Cera premium 41% → 44% in one quarter [V]; Hindware premium ~40% [V]); one-piece, wall-hung, rimless, sensor, smart toilets (Kohler/TOTO "$18,500 smart toilets" for wealthy Indians [V-snip Bloomberg/Malay Mail, Jan-2025]); larger bathrooms in premium housing (Kohler says 30–40% of new construction is premium/entry-luxury — a Jaquar statement [V BT]). India is now a **top-3 market for Kohler** per its CEO [V-snip].
6. **Water efficiency.** Dual-flush (3/6 L or 2.6/4 L) and low-flow aerators; green-building codes (IGBC/GRIHA) and municipal norms in water-stressed cities favour branded, tested products [K].

**Demand indicators to track:** ANAROCK/PropEquity completions; cement volume (finishing-stage proxy); Cera/Hindware retail vs project mix; dealer sentiment in Q2/Q3 calls; PMAY sanctions; branded price hikes sticking.

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Indirect tax (GST)
- **Sanitaryware (HSN 6910): 18%** — before and after GST 2.0 (22-Sep-2025). HSN listings show "New GST rate 18%, Old GST rate 18%" and a 2026 rate guide says sanitaryware "continues at 18% (standard rate)" under the two-slab structure [V-snip busy.in, disytax; FLAG — the CBIC notification itself was not fetched]. History: **28% at GST launch (1-Jul-2017) → 18% from 15-Nov-2017** (23rd GST Council, Guwahati, 10-Nov-2017) [K]. Industry kept lobbying for 5–12% (Budget 2021 and Budget 2025 representations) [V-snip BS headlines] without success.
- **Taps/faucets/valves (HSN 8481): 18% since 1-Jul-2017; unchanged by GST 2.0** [V-snip cleartax/busy.in listings; FLAG].
- **What GST 2.0 did change in the bathroom's neighbourhood:** **cement 28% → 18%** (56th Council, 3-Sep-2025, effective 22-Sep-2025) [V-snip constructionworld/odishatv]; marble/granite blocks 12% → 5% [V-sib]. Tiles (6907) stayed 18% [V-sib]. **Net effect for sanitaryware: none directly; mildly positive via cheaper construction.** Traders in Indore sought a cut to 5% in Aug-2025 [V-snip FPJ] — not granted.
- **Trap:** a 2025 blog claim that tiles/sanitaryware moved "28% → 18%" in Sep-2025 conflates the 2017 cut [V-sib].

### 7.2 BIS standards and Quality Control Orders (QCOs)
- **Draft "Ceramic Tiles, Sanitary Wares and Laboratory Wares (Quality Control) Order, 2023"** (DPIIT, under ss.16–17 and 25(3) of the BIS Act 2016) makes the **ISI mark compulsory (Scheme-I licence)** for: **IS 2556 Parts 2 (washdown WCs), 3 (squatting pans), 4 (wash basins), 5 (lab sinks), 6 (urinals/partition plates, 2021), 7 (accessories), 8 (close-coupled and one-piece pedestal WCs, 2021), 9 (pedestal bidets), 14 (integrated squatting pans), 15 (universal WCs), 16 (wall-mounted WCs), 17 (wall-mounted bidets); IS 774:2021 (vitreous china flushing cisterns); IS 15622:2017 (pressed ceramic tiles); IS 4457:2007 (acid-resisting tiles)**; exports exempt [V draft text, alephindia copy — the S.O. number and commencement date are **blank** in the draft].
- A **BIS circular of 2-Apr-2026** on implementing Amendment No. 2 to IS 15622 (last date 28-Jun-2026) [V-sib] implies the tiles part is in force; **whether and from when the sanitaryware part is notified/enforced was NOT verified** — FLAG. The prompt's premise of "QCOs on sanitaryware/faucets" should be stated as: **sanitaryware QCO drafted (2023) and listed by compliance consultants; enforcement date unverified; no QCO found for faucets (IS 8931:2021 remains voluntary)** [V-snip BIS pages show an empty "QCO file" for IS 8931].
- **Why it matters:** a QCO is a non-tariff barrier against sub-standard Chinese imports and a compliance cost for small Thangadh/Morbi potteries (each needs a BIS licence and in-house testing); it favours organised players. Cera holds **BIS licence 2556 for Parts 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 15** and EN 997/13407/14688 certifications [V PPT].

### 7.3 Trade remedies and tariffs
- **Inbound (protecting India):** no Indian ADD on sanitaryware/faucets found [V-snip negative]. Customs duty ~20% BCD on 6910 [K; FLAG].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; timeline in §G)

1. **1960s–1980s: the Somany era begins.** Hindustan Sanitaryware & Industries (HSIL, "Hindware") set up in 1960 with Twyfords (UK) by the Somany family [K; primer]. **Parryware** started under EID Parry (Murugappa) in 1952/60s [K]. **Cera** founded by **Vikram Somany** (from the same Somany ceramic family) — the company says "Founded the business in 1980" [V PPT]; the primer's "1978 MoU" for Kadi as India's first natural-gas-fired sanitaryware plant [primer]. Jaquar founded in Delhi ~1960 [K].
2. **1990s–2000s: oligopoly of three Indian brands + entry of MNCs.** Roca–Parryware JV (2006), Roca buys out (2008) [K]; Kohler, Grohe, American Standard, Duravit, TOTO enter in the 2000s [K]. **Cera's corporate shell:** incorporated 1998 as Madhusudan Oils & Fats; acquired by Madhusudan Industries in a 2001 scheme and renamed Cera Sanitaryware [V CARE].
3. **2008–2014: Cera diversifies.** Faucetware plant at Kadi (~2010 [K]), tiles (sourced/JV, ~2012 [K]), wellness; Cera's revenue grows ~25% CAGR to ~₹800 Cr by FY15 [V screener FY15 ₹822 Cr].
4. **2014–2019: Swachh Bharat and the first premiumisation wave.** >10 crore toilets; tile makers enter bathware (Kajaria Kerovit 2014 [V-sib], Somany); GST launches at **28% for sanitaryware (Jul-2017)**, cut to **18% (Nov-2017)** [K]; real-estate slump after demonetisation/RERA (2016–2019) keeps growth single-digit (Cera FY19 ₹1,344 Cr, FY20 ₹1,209 Cr — down) [V screener].
5. **2018–2023: the Hindware restructuring.** HSIL demerges consumer products/retail into **Somany Home Innovation Ltd** (effective 1-Apr-2018; listed 2019; renamed **Hindware Home Innovation**) [V screener "About"; K]; HSIL sells its **building-products (bathware) division to SHIL's subsidiary Brilloca Ltd** (slump sale, 2022, ~₹630 Cr [K, FLAG]) and renames itself **AGI Greenpac** (glass packaging) [K]; **Brilloca renamed Hindware Ltd** [K]. Hence "HSIL" today is a glass company — the bathware lives in Hindware Ltd.
6. **FY21–FY23: the post-COVID boom.** Housing upcycle + renovation; Cera revenue **₹1,202 Cr (FY21) → ₹1,803 Cr (FY23)**, OPM 16%, PAT ₹101 → ₹210 Cr [V screener]; Cera shareholder count jumps (34,804 → 1.79 lakh by Sep-2024 after index inclusions) [V screener].
7. **FY24–H1 FY26: the retail slump.** "Sluggish demand from Q3 of FY24"; projects strong, retail weak; heavy dealer discounts; Cera revenue +3.8%/+2.4% (FY24/FY25), margin 16% → 15% → 13% [V]. Hindware's revenue falls (FY23 ₹2,872 → FY26 ₹2,510 Cr) and turns loss-making (pipes, appliances) [V].
8. **FY26: brass shock + brand investment.** Brass +29–35%; Cera launches Senator (luxury) and Polipluz (value); FY26 revenue +7%, PAT −17% [V]. Cera divests its two 51% LLPs (29-Sep-2025) [V].
9. **Mar-2026: the gas shock.** West Asia war (28-Feb); Morbi/Thangadh shut (Mar–Apr); Cera shuts one kiln Mar–May, internalises SKUs; industry-wide price hikes of 10–12% (SW) and 16–18% (faucets) [V].
10. **2026 restructuring wave:** Hindware's three-way scheme (NCLT 14-Oct-2026); Kajaria buys out WestBridge in Kerovit; Jaquar plans ₹800–1,000 Cr capex and ₹12,000 Cr revenue; Kohler ₹800 Cr India investment [V; V-sib; V-snip].

**Cycles:** sanitaryware is a **late-cycle housing derivative with a renovation floor** — Cera's revenue fell only twice in 12 years (FY20 −10%, FY21 −0.6%) [V screener]; margins swing with **brass and discounting**, not with gas (gas is 2–3% of Cera's revenue).

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Pressure casting and robotics** cut labour and defects and enable complex one-piece/rimless SKUs — the organised players' moat against potteries (Cera: robotic glazing, CNC master moulds, 3D printers for design; faucet robotic grinding, PVD multi-colour, CNC [V PPT]).
2. **Smart/sensor bathrooms** (washlets, sensor faucets, thermostatic showers) — electronics-heavy and often Chinese/Japanese-sourced; risk that value shifts to whoever controls the electronics [K].
3. **PTMT/plastic and stainless fittings** cannibalise low-end brass taps (pipe companies Astral, Hindware Truflo, Supreme, Prince push PTMT through plumbers) [K; V Hindware deck "PTMT faucets & accessories"].
4. **Concealed systems and wall-hung WCs** (Geberit model) shift value to in-wall frames/cisterns — Indian brands buy these mechanisms [K].
5. **E-commerce and D2C** (Amazon/Flipkart/Pepperfry, brand webstores) — price transparency in faucets [K].
6. **Kiln decarbonisation** (electric/hydrogen kilns trialled in Europe) [K]; Cera's 55–60% renewable power and low gas intensity are ahead of Indian peers [V].
7. **Chinese OEM brands** entering via e-commerce with premium-looking product at mid prices [K].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Formalisation is the secular engine; housing completions set the year.** The organised half of the market grows ~1.5–2× the overall market as buyers move from unbranded to branded (warranty, design, service). Completions (5.4 lakh in top-7 cities in 2026, a decade high [V-snip]) and the renovation cycle decide the year's volume; government sanitation programmes broadened the base but at the unorganised end.
2. **Faucets are the profit pool, and brass is their swing factor.** Faucets are ~40% of Cera's revenue and growing ~2× sanitaryware (Q1 FY27 +25% vs +14% [V]); Jaquar's dominance (claims ~60%) is built on faucets [V]. Brass is ~60% of faucet cost and rose ~35% in six months (₹665 → ~₹900/kg) [V]; the industry passed through 16–18% in faucet prices [V]. Gross margin (51% → 46% at Cera) is the tell [V].
3. **Gas decides who can make sanitaryware in a shock — and Cera's GAIL allocation is a real moat.** Cera paid ₹48/m³ in Q1 FY27 while Morbi paid ₹82–90/SCM and Thangadh's propane units mostly shut [V; V-sib; V-snip]. But the edge covers only the ~40% of sanitaryware Cera makes in-house; its outsourced 60% comes "mostly from Morbi" [V].
4. **Make-vs-buy is the strategic variable.** Cera (59% outsourced) vs Hindware (SW ~70% in-house) vs Jaquar (integrated) — the asset-light model wins on ROCE in calm years and loses on supply security and cost control in shocks. 2026 forced insourcing [V].
5. **Channel economics: projects up, retail back, discounts the hidden cost.** Project share up to ~40%+ (pre-priced, lower margin, longer receivables); retail recovered from H2 FY26; FY26's margin fall came largely from "higher trade discounts" [V]. A&P is rising (₹49 → ₹85 Cr at Cera) to build premium (Senator) and value (Polipluz) brands [V].
6. **MNC and private-giant capital is coming.** Jaquar (₹800–1,000 Cr capex; ₹12,000 Cr target), Kohler (₹800 Cr), Roca, Grohe — the listed mid-cap (Cera) faces better-funded rivals in faucets and premium [V; V-snip].
7. **Tax and quality rules are stable; trade rules hurt exporters, not Cera.** GST 18% unchanged by GST 2.0; QCO drafted; no Indian ADD; GCC ADD and US tariffs hit Thangadh/Morbi exporters [V-snip].

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Revenue = Σ segments [ in-house pieces × in-house realisation + outsourced pieces × outsourced realisation ] − turnover discounts**, where
- in-house pieces = capacity × utilisation × yield;
- realisation = list price × (1 − dealer/trade discount) × mix (premium/mid/entry; retail vs project);
**EBITDA = Revenue × gross margin − (employees + A&P + freight + other overheads)**; gross margin = f(brass ₹/kg, gas ₹/m³, Morbi purchase prices, price-hike timing, mix).

### C.1 Worked example 1 — Cera Q1 FY27 (company data) [V inputs; E arithmetic]
- Revenue ₹486 Cr = SW ₹228 (47%) + FW ₹194 (40%) + tiles ₹53 (11%) + wellness ₹10 (2%) [E from V mix].
- Gross margin 46% → gross profit ~₹224 Cr [E from V].
- Less employees ~₹78 Cr (₹72 Cr run-rate + ₹6.3 Cr LTS) [V], A&P ~₹20 Cr (₹85 Cr/yr budget) [E], other overheads ~₹77 Cr [E balancing] → **EBITDA ₹49.2 Cr (10.1%)** [V].
- Management's bridge to "underlying" ~14.5%: + LTS 1.3 pp + one-kiln under-absorption 0.75 pp + old-project foreclosure 0.8 pp + delayed price pass-through 1.5 pp = +4.35 pp [V].

### C.2 Worked example 2 — one in-house WC at Kadi vs a Thangadh pottery WC (Q1 FY27) [E composite; FLAG]
| ₹ per piece (two-piece EWC, ~18 kg fired) | Cera Kadi (in-house) | Thangadh unit (propane) |

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed, and new primer issues

### 4.5.1 Insights (expert-level additions)

1. **Half of Cera's "margin collapse" is an accounting tailwind fading, not the business.**
   - FY25 revenue and EBITDA included **₹34.15 Cr of "credit balances written back"** (dealer credit notes and payables reversed into other operating revenue); FY26 only ₹13.51 Cr.
   - Strip them out and the core margin fell **13.7% → 12.55% (−1.15 pp)**, not 15.3% → 13.1%. CareEdge already strips them out.
   - The writer should present FY25 as a flattered base and FY27 guidance (13.5–14%) as a genuine step-up over core FY26.
2. **Cera made *fewer* toilets in FY26 than in FY25 (−11%) while selling more.**
   - In-house SW output fell to 18.73 lakh pieces (utilisation 86% → 81%) as cheap Morbi supply substituted for Kadi output.
   - Then the Mar-2026 gas shock flipped the economics, and Cera is now internalising.
   - Implication: Cera's make-vs-buy is **opportunistic on relative cost**, so its in-house utilisation (and the greenfield decision) will track the Morbi–Kadi gas spread, not just demand.
3. **Faucets are now the larger manufacturing business at Cera.**
   - FY26 in-house faucet revenue ₹438 Cr exceeded in-house sanitaryware ₹398 Cr for the first time (52:48), with 271 new faucet products vs 35 in SW.
   - Faucets run at 97% and get the capex; sanitaryware has spare capacity. The "sanitaryware company" label is increasingly a misnomer, and brass, not gas, is the main manufacturing cost risk.
4. **Discounts are the real price variable in Indian bathware.**
   - Hindware's AR shows **₹1,902 Cr of discounts and rebates on ₹4,348 Cr of contract value (43.7%)**.
   - Cera's FY26 margin decline was attributed by management mainly to "higher trade discounts".
   - So a "12% list-price hike" can be worth much less, or more, depending on discount discipline. Q2/Q3 FY27 commentary on discounts matters as much as on brass.
5. **Imports matter more than the first pass said, and exports are an outlet for the unorganised sector.**
   - HS 6910 imports ₹715 Cr (+16%, 74% China) ≈ 9–10% of the sanitaryware market, while exports of ₹1,571 Cr make India a net exporter.
   - The US (−23%) and Saudi (−15%) channels shrank in FY26. Lost export volume from Morbi/Thangadh is a **domestic price-competition risk at the entry end** (Polipluz's target segment) once gas normalises.
6. **The market is smaller and more concentrated than Parts 1–3 implied.**
   - At ₹20,000–25,000 Cr with 5–6 national players, Jaquar alone is ~a third of all bathware value and Cera ~8–9%.
   - Hindware says it gained share growing 10% in FY26. Cera's FY26 SW growth of 5.4% was likely **below market**; its FW growth of 13.7% roughly at market.
   - Cera's Q1 FY27 acceleration (+14% SW, +25% FW) should be read against Hindware's 15–20% guidance and Kerovit's +22%: **the whole organised pack is growing high-teens in a price-inflated year.**
7. **Treasury income is a material and volatile part of Cera's earnings.**
   - MTM gains on ₹800 Cr of Level-1 mutual funds were ₹40 Cr in FY26 (₹52 Cr in FY25), ~15–20% of PBT.
   - A bond-market drawdown would show up in "other income" exactly as the Dec-25 quarter's provision did on screener. Quarterly PAT comparisons need an ex-OI lens.
8. **KMP pay is high for the size.** ₹20.45 Cr of KMP remuneration is ~10% of FY26 PAT (CMD ₹8.4 Cr), and the founder-linked Indian Council of Sanitaryware Manufacturers is a related party. Worth one governance line, not a red flag (no royalty; rent to the promoter entity only ₹1.3 Cr).
