## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **"Tata Motors" means two different companies, and one of them is not a passenger-vehicle maker.**
   - **TMPV** (BSE 500570, the old listing) = JLR + Tata India PV + Tata.ev (TPEM). ~83% of FY26 revenue is JLR [E: £22.9 bn × ~₹119 avg FY26 ≈ ₹2.73 lakh Cr of ₹3.36 lakh Cr ≈ 81–85%; primer says ~85%].
   - **TMCV** (BSE 544569) = trucks, buses, pickups, SCVs (plus Iveco from Q3 FY27). It is legally called "Tata Motors Limited".
   - **Why TMCV is in this sub-sector — a classification inheritance.** Screener (NSE industry taxonomy) now classifies TMCV as **Industrials › Capital Goods › Agricultural, Commercial & Construction Vehicles › Commercial Vehicles**, and TMPV as **Consumer Discretionary › Automobile & Auto Components › Automobiles › Passenger Cars & Utility Vehicles** [V screener 24-Oct-26]. Our own NSE-750 snapshot (data/constituents.csv, July-2026) already gives TMCV the macro-sector "Capital Goods" while TMPV is "Automobile and Auto Components" [V repo]. But the library's sub-sector map (subsectors/_template/members.json and data/subsectors.json) places TMCV under "Automobile & Ancillaries || Passenger Cars & Utility Vehicles" — evidently inherited from the pre-demerger Tata Motors' industry tag. commercial-vehicles.html, published 21-Oct-2026, treats TMCV as a "non-member reference" and says "no primer" (there is one: tata-motors-cv.html). **Writer: keep TMCV as a member (the map says so) but state plainly that it is a CV maker, put its numbers in a separate column/subgroup, never add its truck volumes to PV volumes, and link commercial-vehicles.html.**
2. **TMPV's FY26 reported PAT (₹82,645 Cr) is not earnings.** It contains the ₹82,616 Cr non-cash demerger gain [primer]. Ex-gain PAT ≈ **₹29 Cr** [E: 82,645 − 82,616]; company PBT before exceptional items **₹2,519 Cr** (vs ₹28,650 Cr in FY25) [primer, from company results]. Screener's "P/E 97.8" is on a TTM that still includes the Q2 FY26 gain in some line — **meaningless; do not print** [V screener]. Q1 FY27: screener PAT ₹859 Cr (incl. minorities) vs company "PAT ₹775 Cr" (attributable) [V screener; V Autocar Pro].
3. **TMPV bases do not line up year on year.** Screener FY25 revenue ₹3,66,094 Cr is restated (PV continuing ops), but FY25 PAT ₹28,149 Cr and Q1 FY26 PAT ₹4,003 Cr are the old whole-company numbers including CV. So "TMPV PAT fell 79% in Q1 FY27" (₹4,003 → ₹859 Cr) mixes bases. Use **operating** comparisons (revenue, EBITDA, EBIT, JLR £ figures, Tata PV segment) and say "PAT not comparable" for FY25/Q1 FY26.
4. **TMCV has three revenue numbers.** FY26 **standalone (incl. Tata Cummins joint operation) ₹77,399 Cr** (+11% vs restated FY25), **consolidated ₹83,855 Cr** (screener), and screener's FY25 **10-month ₹58,217 Cr** (which makes FY26 "growth" look like +44% — wrong) [V screener; primer]. Company "EBITDA" also differs from screener OP: FY26 standalone EBITDA ₹10.2K Cr (13.2%) vs consolidated screener OP ₹7,617 Cr (9.1%); Q2 FY27 standalone EBITDA ₹2,806 Cr (11.7%) vs consolidated ₹2,770 Cr (10.9%) vs screener OP ₹2,648 Cr (10.5%) [V PPT; V screener]. TMCV's consolidated PBT swings with the **mark-to-market of its Tata Capital stake** (Q2 FY27 −₹708 Cr; Q1 FY27 +₹1,135 Cr; Q2 FY26 −₹2,027 Cr) [V PPT].
5. **Maruti: standalone ≈ consolidated now, but "EBITDA" has two definitions.** Company "Op. EBITDA" Q1 FY27 = ₹4,311 Cr = **8.6% of net sales** (₹49,959 Cr); screener OP ₹4,313 Cr = 8.2% of "sales" ₹52,470 Cr (net sales + other operating income) [V presentation; V screener]. Company margins are on **net sales**, screener's on revenue incl. other operating income — a ~0.4–0.5 pt gap. FY26: company ~12.3% [primer] vs screener 11.7%.
6. **Wholesale ≠ retail, and the GST cut distorted both bases.** Buyers deferred purchases in Aug–Sep 2025 ahead of the 22-Sep-2025 GST cut, then rushed in Oct–Nov 2025. So Sep-2026 YoY (+21.8% wholesale, +32.1% retail) is flattered; FADA calls Sep-2026 "the most base-distorted print of the year" and puts underlying growth at **~17% in the first five months of FY27** [V-snip FADA via Autocar Pro]. Dealer PV inventory fell from ~52 days (Mar-25) to ~28 days (Mar-26) [V Autocar Pro], then **rebuilt to ~43–45 days by end-Sep-2026** [V-snip FADA]. H2 FY27 YoY comparisons will be against the post-GST surge — SIAM itself expects growth to "moderate" [V].
7. **"Market share" has four bases.** (a) SIAM wholesale domestic (Maruti ~39% FY26); (b) FADA/Vahan retail (Maruti 39.71% FY26); (c) company "Vahan share" (Tata PV 13.6% FY26, 14.3% Q1 FY27); (d) company-defined segments (Hyundai's "mid-SUV share"). Tata Motors **did not report monthly figures to SIAM** for much of 2025–26 — SIAM's Sep-2026 release gives "total incl. Tata 4,54,574" and "excl. Tata 3,85,764" [V Autocar Pro]. Always name the basis.
8. **"Domestic sales" at Maruti includes non-cars.** Maruti's domestic 5,57,988 in Q1 FY27 includes LCVs (9,579) and **sales to other OEMs** (23,181 — mainly Toyota-badged Maruti models) [V presentation]. Domestic PV (cars + UV + vans) = 5,25,228. Toyota's rebadged Glanza/Hyryder/Rumion/Taisor volumes are counted again in Toyota's own share — industry double-count risk if you sum OEM-reported numbers.
9. **Hyundai: India's largest *cumulative* exporter, not the largest *annual* PV exporter.** HMIL says it is "cumulatively the largest exporter of passenger vehicles" and the first to reach **4 million exports** (Oct-2026) [V BSE]. In FY26 **Maruti exported 4.48 lakh units vs Hyundai's ~1.90 lakh** — Maruti is #1 annually (~49% of India's PV exports) [primer]. Our Hyundai primer calls HMIL "India's largest passenger-vehicle exporter" / "India's #1 PV exporter" — wrong as an annual statement (see §3.4).
10. **JLR's numbers come in four flavours.** £ (JLR's own reporting) vs ₹ in TMPV consolidated; wholesale **excluding** the China JV (CJLR) vs retail **including** CJLR; "adjusted EBIT" (JLR) vs EBIT in TMPV's segment table; "free cash flow" (after investment) vs "operating cash flow" in guidance (FY26 FCF −£2.2 bn [V-snip] vs "operating cash outflow £2.3 bn" [V Autocar Pro]). And **CJLR stopped local production of Jaguar/Land Rover-badged cars in June-2026**, then began building the first **CJLR Freelander** (a Chery-partnered brand) on 30-Jul-2026 [V JLR media; V Autocar Pro] — China volumes before/after are not comparable.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Four members, three businesses, one stock-market label.**
  - **India mass-market PV (Maruti, Hyundai, Tata India PV inside TMPV):** FY26 revenue ≈ ₹3.13 lakh Cr [E: Maruti ₹1,83,316 + Hyundai ₹70,763 (screener) + Tata PV segment ₹58,465 Cr (company)]; together ~63% of India's PV wholesales (Maruti ~39%, Tata ~13.6%, Hyundai ~12.6%) [E from §5].
  - **Global luxury (JLR inside TMPV):** £22.9 bn revenue FY26 (−20.9%), adjusted EBIT 0.7%, PBT bei £14 m [primer, company]. JLR is the largest single revenue pool among the four and the smallest profit pool in FY26.
  - **Trucks and buses (TMCV):** FY26 consolidated ₹83,855 Cr; Q2 FY27 wholesales 135.1K (+42%) [V]. Not a PV business.
  - **All four (screener consolidated, FY26): revenue ₹6,73,516 Cr, operating profit ₹56,427 Cr (8.4%), reported PAT ₹1,05,787 Cr — ₹23,171 Cr ex TMPV's demerger gain; m-cap ₹7,46,684 Cr on 23-Oct-2026** → aggregate m-cap ÷ ex-gain PAT ≈ **32×** [E: sums of screener 24-Oct-26].
- **Demand: a two-year slump, a tax-cut boom, then a fuel and rate shock.**
  - Domestic PV wholesales grew only **+2.0% in FY25 (43.02 lakh)** after FY24's 42.18 lakh [K SIAM] — the small-car segment was shrinking, entry buyers were priced out by BS-VI/safety cost inflation and dealers were carrying ~50+ days of stock.
  - **GST 2.0 (effective 22-Sep-2025)** replaced the 28% + cess structure (29–31% on small cars, 43–50% on larger cars/SUVs) with **18% for small cars (petrol ≤1,200 cc / diesel ≤1,500 cc and ≤4 m) and a flat 40% (no cess) on everything else**; EVs stay at 5% [V-snip tax-guide consensus; K]. Small-car on-road prices fell ~8–9%, bigger cars ~3–6% [E].
  - **FY26 PV wholesales 46.43 lakh (+7.9%), a record**; PV exports ~9.1 lakh (+17.5%, record) [V Outlook Business; V-snip]. **H1 FY27 26.37 lakh (+28.3%)**; Q2 FY27 13.61 lakh (+30.7%), UVs 68% of it [V Autocar Pro]. Maruti's **mini cars +124% in Q1 FY27** — the GST cut revived the entry segment the industry had written off [V].
  - Then: the West Asia war (from 28-Feb-2026) → Brent >$110, four pump-price hikes in May (+₹7.4/l petrol), CNG +₹6/kg, rupee ~₹95/$, and an **RBI rate hike to 5.50% on 7-Oct-2026**. Demand held through September (record retail 4.27 lakh), but **dealer inventory is back to 43–45 days** and SIAM flags "inflation-driven rate increases" as a risk [V].
- **Margins fell even as volumes boomed — commodities and FX, not demand.**
  - Maruti Q1 FY27 Op. EBITDA **8.6% vs 12.6%** a year earlier, material cost **80.5% of net sales (+600 bp)** — "adverse commodity prices in the context of West Asia conflict" and "adverse foreign exchange movement" [V presentation].
  - Hyundai Q1 FY27 EBITDA **9.3% vs 13.3%**; PAT −35%; a Mobis India supplier fire (late May) cost ~13,900 units [V press release; V-snip].
  - Tata India PV Q1 FY27 EBITDA 4.3%, EBIT −0.5%; commodity headwind **4.5 pts** in Q1, ~3 pts expected in Q2 [V Autocar Pro].
  - TMCV Q2 FY27 standalone EBITDA 11.7% (−90 bp YoY) with a −5.9 pt variable-cost hit offset by volume and price [V PPT].
  - Four-member aggregate Q1 FY27: revenue **+15.8%**, operating profit **−17.1%**, OPM **7.6% vs 10.7%** [E, screener].
- **The SUV/share war has re-ordered the league table.**
  - FY26 wholesale: **Maruti ~39%, M&M 6.60 lakh (+20%, now #2), Tata 6.31 lakh (+14%, #3), Hyundai 5.85 lakh (−2.3%, #4)** [V Autocar Pro]. Hyundai's FADA retail share fell from 13.48% to 12.29% [V].
  - Q2 FY27: Tata PV domestic dispatches **1,96,674 (+40.3%)** on Nexon/Punch (+37%) and Curvv/Sierra (5,274 → 25,365) [V SIAM filing]; Maruti Q1 domestic +29.5%, Hyundai Q1 domestic only +5.4% [V].
  - UVs are **~68% of PV sales** (Q2 FY27) vs ~66% in Q1 FY26 [V].
- **EVs: a Tata-Mahindra duopoly in a market tripling from a low base.** Sep-2026 e-PV registrations **35,948 (+94%), penetration 8.3%**; H1 FY27 1,91,751 (+89%); **Tata 39.9%, M&M 22.8%, JSW MG 17.4%, VinFast 5.6%, Maruti ~4.8%** of H1 [V Autocar Pro/Nuvama]. Fuel-price hikes pushed Tata's EV bookings to ~3.5× the pre-war average [V].

### 1.2 Value chain (inputs → customer) for a mass-market Indian car

1. **Technology & platform** — parent licence (Suzuki → Maruti; Hyundai Motor Co → HMIL; JLR's own and Tata's own engineering for Tata) → **royalty** (Maruti ~3.5% of net sales historically, ₹5,038 Cr in FY25 = 36.1% of net profit [V-sib tractors]; Hyundai ~2–3.5% of revenue with a framework that allows up to 5% without minority approval [primer; FLAG — check FY26 annual report]).
2. **Raw materials & tier-1/2 components** — ~75–80% of net sales at mass OEMs (Maruti material cost 74.5% Q1 FY26 → **80.5% Q1 FY27**) [V]. Steel (~45–55% of material weight), aluminium, copper, plastics/polymers (crude-linked), rubber, glass, precious metals (Pd/Rh/Pt in catalytic converters), electronics/semiconductors, and for EVs the **battery pack (35–45% of an EV's cost)** and **rare-earth (NdFeB) magnets** in traction motors and EPS [K/E]. The auto-components sub-sector (auto-components.html) is the other side of this ledger — content per vehicle grew ~4 pts faster than production in FY26 [V-sib].
3. **Assembly** — press, weld, paint, assembly, powertrain shops; highly automated; labour ~4–6% of sales (Maruti employee cost 4.9% of net sales Q1 FY27) [V].
4. **Logistics** — rail (Maruti moves ~25% of dispatches by rail [K]) and car-carrier trucks; exports via Mundra, Pipavav, Chennai/Ennore, Kamarajar, Mumbai.
5. **Dealer** — OEM sells **wholesale to franchised dealers**; dealer margin ~3–5% on cars plus incentives; dealer carries inventory financed by banks/OEM-captive finance (trade advances). Discounts at the dealer level are partly OEM-funded ("consumer offers"), visible in OEM realisations.
6. **Retail customer / fleet** — ~75–80% of new cars are financed [K, industry est.]; insurance, extended warranty, accessories, service and spares (high-margin, annuity-like) and **used-car** businesses (Maruti True Value, Hyundai H-Promise) [primer].
7. **Exports** — CBU (fully built) to ~100 countries (Maruti) / 60–85 (Hyundai; Venue to 35+ from Pune) via the parent's distribution network; parent-directed allocation (Suzuki uses India as its global small-car and e-Vitara hub; Hyundai uses India for emerging markets) [primer; V BSE].

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **OEM EBITDA per vehicle (FY26):** Maruti ~₹89,000 (₹21,530 Cr screener OP / 24.2 lakh units) → **~₹63,000 in Q1 FY27**; Hyundai ~₹1.10 lakh (₹8,561 Cr / 7.75 lakh) → **~₹85,000 in Q1 FY27**; Tata India PV ~₹63,000 (6.9% × ₹58,465 Cr / ~6.4 lakh) → **~₹42,000 in Q1 FY27**; JLR **~£4,980** (6.7% × £22.9 bn / 307,900) ≈ ₹6.3 lakh; TMCV ~₹2.4 lakh per truck/bus (₹10.2K Cr standalone EBITDA / 4.28 lakh) [E, arithmetic on V/primer inputs].
- **Royalty to the parent** captures 2–4% of revenue *before* the listed OEM's margin — i.e. ~25–35% of the listed company's net profit at Maruti (FY25) [V-sib].
- **Dealers** capture ~3–5% gross; in FY25 dealer profitability fell with 50+ days of stock; GST 2.0 transition also stranded dealers with **compensation-cess credits** on pre-22-Sep inventory (industry estimate ~₹2,500 Cr, partly resolved) [K, FLAG].
- **Financiers** (banks ~60–70%, NBFCs incl. Mahindra Finance, captive Hyundai/Toyota/Maruti-tie-up) capture a spread on the ~80% financed.
- **After-sales** (spares and service) is the highest-margin, least cyclical slice of the OEM (not separately disclosed by Maruti/Hyundai; TMCV calls Parts & Services a growth engine [V PPT]).
- **Component suppliers** capture the content-per-vehicle growth (premiumisation adds ADAS, screens, sunroofs, LEDs, more airbags — 6 airbags mandatory debate).

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Affordability = price ÷ income, financed.** The FY23–FY25 problem was that entry-car prices rose ~35–45% from FY19 (BS-VI, 6 airbags push, ABS/ESC, commodity inflation) while lower-middle-class incomes did not [K/E]. GST 2.0 cut ~8–9% from small-car on-road prices; Maruti's mini segment **+124% in Q1 FY27** and compact+mid-size +26.2% [V]. The 7-Oct-2026 repo hike to 5.50% and pump-price hikes push the other way.
2. **Premiumisation and the SUV shift.** UV share of domestic PV: ~28% (FY19) → ~50% (FY23) → ~60%+ (FY24–25) → **~66% (Q1 FY26) → ~68% (Q2 FY27)** [V-snip mobilityoutlook; V Autocar Pro; K for earlier]. Buyers trade up rather than in; ~half of first-time buyers now buy a compact SUV [K, industry commentary].
3. **Fuel economics and powertrain choice.** Petrol at ₹102/l (Delhi, from late May-2026) vs CNG ₹83/kg vs home-charged electricity ~₹8/kWh. Running cost per km (illustrative, compact car): petrol ~₹6.0–6.5 (17 km/l), CNG ~₹3.0–3.5 (25 km/kg), EV ~₹1.1–1.3 (~140 Wh/km) [E]. CNG share of PV **21.98% in FY26** [V Autocar Pro]; Maruti ~40% CNG [V-snip], Tata 27%, Hyundai 18% [V]. EV bookings at Tata ran **~3.5× the pre-West-Asia average** after May [V].
4. **Credit.** ~75–80% of cars financed; auto-loan rates fell ~50–75 bp in the 2025 cutting cycle [K/E]; the Oct-2026 hike reverses part of that. A 50 bp rise on a ₹8 lakh, 5-year loan adds ~₹190/month to the EMI (~1.1%) [E].
5. **Rural and replacement.** Hyundai rural penetration **26%** (all-time high, Q1 FY27) [V]; Maruti rural ~45% of domestic [K]. Good 2025 and 2026 monsoons supported rural demand [V-sib tractors/two-wheelers].
6. **Pay Commission / income tax.** The FY26 Union Budget's income-tax cut (₹12 lakh zero-tax threshold, from Apr-2025) and the 8th Pay Commission (constituted 2025) are cited by OEMs as middle-class demand supports [K].
7. **Exports.** Destination mix: Latin America (Mexico, Chile), Middle East, Africa (South Africa), ASEAN, Japan (Fronx, Jimny — Maruti exports to Japan), now Europe (e-Vitara). **Mexico + Middle East ≈ 20–22% of India's PV exports** (SIAM) — and both are now problems: Mexico raised import duties on non-FTA cars (up to 50% from 2026 [K]) and the West Asia war disrupted Gulf shipments [V Outlook Business]. Q2 FY27 PV exports **−5.2%** [V].
8. **Supply-side constraints.** Rare-earth magnets (China export licensing from 4-Apr-2025 [K]), the Mobis India fire (Hyundai, May-2026), a component-supplier fire at JLR (Q1 FY27), and Sanand production loss (Tata, 5 days in July) [V] — supply, not demand, set several quarters' volumes.

### 7.2 Emissions and fuel economy — CAFE
- **CAFE II** (FY23–FY27): fleet-average 113 g CO2/km (≈4.78 l/100 km) at reference weight 1,082 kg, slope 0.002 [K; V Autocar Pro for slope/weight].
- **CAFE III (FY28–FY32, 1-Apr-2027 to 31-Mar-2032)** — public draft 16-Jul-2026 (comments to 6-Aug), **notified ~30-Sep/1-Oct-2026** [V-snip Business Today/Manorama — gazette text not fetched; FLAG final parameters]. Draft: **94.76 g/km (3.996 l/100 km) in FY28 tightening to ~78.9 g/km (ICRA) / 76.77 g/km before neutrality factors (TERI) by FY32** — ~16–21% tighter than CAFE II in year one, 30–34% by FY32; formula a × (W − b) + c with **slope 0.00158 → 0.00131** and **reference weight 1,229 kg**; compliance in a 3-year then a 2-year block; credits banked/traded (pooling), deficit bought from BEE at **₹2,500 per g/km per vehicle in FY28 rising to ₹4,500 in FY32**; super-credit multipliers **BEV/REEV 3.0, PHEV & flex-fuel strong hybrid 2.5, strong hybrid 1.6, FFV 1.1**; carbon-neutrality factors **E20+ 8%, flex-fuel 22.3%, CNG 5% (or CBG blend)**; tech credits 1 g each, capped at 9 g [V Autocar Pro explainer 18-Jul-2026].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

- **1983–2002: the Maruti monopoly.** The Maruti 800 (Dec-1983) created the Indian car market; Hyundai (1998 Santro) and Tata (1998 Indica) were the first serious challengers [primer].
- **2002–2008: Suzuki takes control; Tata goes global.** Government exits Maruti (2002–07); Tata buys JLR from Ford (Jun-2008, $2.3 bn) [primer].
- **2008–2018: the hatchback decade.** Small cars ~70%+ of the market; diesel boom then bust; Maruti share ~50%+ in FY19 [K].
- **FY19–FY22: the triple shock.** NBFC crisis (IL&FS, Sep-2018), BS-VI leap (Apr-2020), COVID, then the semiconductor shortage (FY22): domestic PV fell from 33.8 lakh (FY19) to 27.1 lakh (FY21) [K]. JLR lost money in FY19–FY21 (China, Brexit, diesel collapse; £3.1 bn impairment FY19) [K].
- **FY23–FY24: the SUV boom.** Pent-up demand + new SUVs (Creta, Nexon, Punch, XUV700, Scorpio-N, Brezza, Grand Vitara) → 38.9 → 42.2 lakh; UVs pass 50% then 60% [K]. JLR's "Refocus" and Range Rover/Defender order books lift EBIT to 8.5% (FY25) and net cash [primer].
- **FY25: the stall.** +2.0%; small cars shrink; dealer inventory 50+ days; heavy discounting [K; V Autocar Pro for 52 days Mar-25]. Hyundai's ₹27,870 Cr all-OFS IPO (Oct-2024) [primer].
- **FY26: shocks and a tax cut.** US tariffs (Apr-2025) pause JLR US shipments; China rare-earth curbs; **JLR cyberattack (from 31-Aug/1-Sep-2025, ~5 weeks' shutdown)**; **GST 2.0 (22-Sep-2025)**; the Tata Motors demerger (1-Oct-2025); record 46.4 lakh PV wholesales; M&M and Tata overtake Hyundai [primer; V].
- **FY27: boom meets war.** H1 PV +28%; e-PV penetration 8%; but West Asia war commodity inflation crushes margins (Maruti 8.6%, Hyundai 9.3%); four fuel-price hikes (May-2026); RBI hike (Oct-2026); CAFE III notified; Iveco closes (Oct-2026); HMG's $15.5 bn plan [V].
- **Listing/M&A waves:** Maruti IPO (2003), Hyundai IPO (Oct-2024), TMCV listing (Nov-2025); JLR (2008), Iveco (2026), SMG merger (2025), Talegaon purchase (₹787 Cr, 2024) [primer; V].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Tax and fuel set the affordability line.** GST 2.0 cut small-car prices ~8.5% and revived the entry segment Maruti owns (mini +124% Q1 FY27) — the first policy-driven demand reset since the 2008–09 excise cuts. The 2026 fuel shock (petrol ₹102/l, CNG ₹83/kg) and the Oct-2026 rate hike push the line back the other way. Volume in FY27–FY28 is a race between these two.
2. **Commodity and FX inflation is now the margin variable, not volume.** Volumes rose 15–46% across members in H1 FY27 while EBITDA margins fell 3–4 pts (Maruti 12.6 → 8.6; Hyundai 13.3 → 9.3; Tata PV 4.3 with a 4.5-pt commodity hit). The four-member aggregate OP fell 17% on revenue +16% in Q1 FY27 [E]. Price hikes (Maruti Jun/Aug, Tata +1%, Hyundai) lag by a quarter.
3. **The SUV-led share war has created four near-equal challengers behind Maruti.** M&M (#2), Tata (#3) and Hyundai (#4) sit within ~1.5 pts of each other at 12.6–14.2% (FY26); Hyundai lost 1.2–1.3 pts in a year. Maruti held ~39–41% only because the GST cut revived small cars — its UV share is below its overall share [primer].
4. **JLR is the profit pool's swing factor and it is structurally weaker than in FY25.** US tariffs (10% even after the deal), China (CJLR production ended), VME at 7.1%, a costly EV transition (£18 bn/5 yrs) and the Jaguar air-pocket. FY27 guide EBIT ~4% vs 8.5% in FY25.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Revenue = Σ (domestic units × domestic ASP) + (export units × export ASP) + spares/services + other operating income (scrap, incentives, PLI)**
**EBITDA = Revenue − materials (≈ units × BOM per car, commodity- and FX-linked) − royalty − employee − other (freight, ad-pro, warranty, dealer support/discounts) ; EBITDA/vehicle ≈ (ASP − BOM − variable selling cost) − fixed cost per unit**

### C.1 Worked example 1 — one Maruti car, Q1 FY26 vs Q1 FY27 (₹ per vehicle; company basis) [E from V presentation]
| | Q1 FY26 | Q1 FY27 |
|---|---|---|
| Units | 5,27,861 | 6,82,724 |
| Net sales/vehicle | ₹6,93,800 (₹36,621 Cr ÷ units) | ₹7,31,800 (₹49,959 Cr ÷ units) |
| Material (74.5% → 80.5%) | ₹5,16,900 | ₹5,89,100 |
| Employee + other − other op. income (12.9% → 10.9% net) | ₹89,500 | ₹79,800 |
| **Op. EBITDA/vehicle** | **₹87,500 (12.6%)** | **₹63,100 (8.6%)** |
| Depreciation | ₹29,000 | ₹26,300 |
| **Op. EBIT/vehicle** | **₹58,100** | **₹37,100** |
- Read: ASP +5.5%, BOM per car +14% → EBITDA/vehicle −28%. Volume +29% offset part of it (EBITDA −6.7%).

### C.2 Worked example 2 — one Range Rover-brand vehicle at JLR (FY26) [E composite]
- Revenue/vehicle ~£74,400 (£22.9 bn ÷ 307,900) ≈ ₹93 lakh.
- Variable cost ~62% (~£46,100) incl. ~£2,700 of US tariff on a US car at 10% of a ~£55k CIF [E] → contribution ~£28,300.
- Fixed cost per car at 308k ≈ £21,300 (≈ £6.6 bn ÷ 308k) → **EBITDA/vehicle ~£5,000 (6.7%)**; D&A ~£4,500 → **EBIT ~£500 (0.7%)**.
- At FY25's ~401k wholesales the same fixed cost spread to ~£16,500/car → EBIT ~8.5% [E] — the operating-leverage mechanism.

### C.3 Worked example 3 — industry revenue pool FY26 [E]
- Domestic: 46.43 lakh × ~₹9.0 lakh ex-GST ≈ ₹4.18 lakh Cr; Exports: ~9.1 lakh × ~₹6.8 lakh ≈ ₹0.62 lakh Cr; **Total ≈ ₹4.8 lakh Cr ($51 bn)**. GST collected (blended ~25% pre-reform, ~30% post on mix) ≈ ₹1.1–1.3 lakh Cr [E].

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed

1. **The parent takes about a third of earnings at both MNC subsidiaries, and Suzuki takes more as the business grows.** Royalty alone is **37% of Maruti's PAT and 36% of Hyundai's**. Add dividends (₹2,474 Cr to Suzuki) and fees, and Suzuki received about **₹8,900 Cr from Maruti in FY26**. Maruti's royalty *rate* fell (3.47% → 3.09% of net sales) as exports via Suzuki and lower-royalty models grew. But **sales to Suzuki rose 74% to ₹25,467 Cr**, so Suzuki is now Maruti's licensor, export distributor *and* largest customer. Any minority analysis should treat export margins as transfer-priced.
2. **"Underlying" TMPV is loss-making, not break-even.** Continuing-ops PAT was **−₹1,377 Cr** after ₹4,142 Cr of exceptional costs, ₹2,786 Cr of them cyber/supplier. The ₹82,616 Cr gain has no net-worth effect. On this basis the four-member m-cap ÷ ex-gain PAT is ~34×, not 32×. The whole ₹42.2K Cr of net debt is JLR's; India PV is net cash ₹8K Cr.
3. **JLR's real FY26 hole was North America**, where wholesale fell 32.7% (tariff pause plus cyber) against retail −16.9%: JLR destocked the US channel. North America is the profit pool that management now wants to grow 10% a year. Q1 FY27 NA wholesale was flat at 27.2k. The UK–US 10% quota is administered **quarterly**, so a strong US quarter can hit the cap.
4. **Maruti has quietly cut its long-range capacity ambition but sped up the near term.** The plan is 2.9 m by FY27 (two 250k lines started May–July 2026) and 3.65 m by FY31, versus the older "4 m by FY31" talk. Meanwhile it lifted its industry forecast to **6.1–6.3 m by FY31** because of GST 2.0. That implies Maruti expects to hold ~45–50% of domestic + export volume with less capacity than once planned — i.e. utilisation, not share, drives the next leg [E].
5. **GST 2.0's real winner by tax bucket is Maruti.** It sold ~49% of the industry's 18%-slab cars in Q1 FY27 but only ~34% of 40%-slab cars (PL call notes). The policy reset sits where Maruti dominates and leaves the SUV fight, where M&M, Tata and Hyundai compete, on a flat 40%. **M&M's m-cap has overtaken Maruti's** (₹3.56 vs ₹3.53 lakh Cr), suggesting the market values the SUV/EV franchise above the small-car revival.
6. **Hyundai's operating trend is the weakest of the four, and its guidance is now a test.** OPM has fallen for five quarters (13.9% → 9.3%) on flat revenue. The fire, the Middle East and Pune start-up costs explain part of it, but the 11–14% FY27 EBITDA guide needs H2 margins of ~12–15% against an industry Hyundai itself expects to grow only "low single digits" in H2. Mitigants: discounts below industry (2.8%), two launches, the Pune third shift, and ₹10.6K Cr of cash.
7. **The EV race is a three-way fight that excludes the two MNC subsidiaries.** FY26 e-PV retail: Tata 39%, JSW MG 27%, M&M 21%; Hyundai 2.9%, Maruti 0.7%. CAFE III dropped the small-car concession Maruti had lobbied for but kept CNG/ethanol neutrality factors and hybrid multipliers. Hyundai already says it is CAFE-II compliant, and both MNCs plan a CNG + hybrid route (Maruti CNG 7.46 lakh FY26 → 9 lakh target). **PM E-DRIVE gives private e-cars nothing**, so EV economics rest on 5% GST, PLI (Tata ₹1,044 Cr FY26) and fuel prices.
8. **India's listed subsidiaries are now worth a large share of their parents.** Suzuki's 58.7% of Maruti is ~86% of Suzuki's own m-cap; HMC's 82.5% of HMIL is ~22% of HMC's. Toyota trades at 9.75× TTM, Suzuki 8.4× and HMC 8.3×, against Maruti 24.6× and HMIL 30.4×. The India premium is a 3–4× multiple gap, a recurring argument for further parent stake creep or secondary sales.
