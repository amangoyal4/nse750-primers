## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Hindalco has three EBITDA numbers for FY26. Quote the basis every time.**
   - **Company consolidated EBITDA ₹38,097 Cr** (record; includes other income; before the Oswego fire exceptional items) [V hindalco.com Q4 FY26 PR].
   - **Screener "operating profit" ₹34,880 Cr (12.7%)** [V screener]. Screener books the fire losses partly in "other income" (FY26 other income **−₹4,074 Cr**; Dec-25 qtr −₹2,061 Cr; Mar-26 qtr −₹3,146 Cr; Jun-26 qtr −₹1,236 Cr) and excludes other income from OP.
   - **Novelis Adjusted EBITDA $1,645m (US GAAP)** — a different measure from Novelis' Ind AS contribution. Novelis' *unadjusted* FY26 EBITDA was only **$877m** [V sec.gov 8-K] because the fire losses are added back in "adjusted".
   - **Use:** company EBITDA ₹38,097 Cr for net debt/EBITDA and segment mix; screener OP for the cross-company peer table (Part 3 aggregates use screener on one basis).
2. **Hindalco reported PAT vs "PAT before exceptional items".** FY26 reported **₹13,391 Cr** vs pre-exceptional **₹18,733 Cr**; Q4 FY26 ₹2,597 vs ₹5,796 Cr [V]. **Q1 FY27 also had an exceptional item of −₹2,299 Cr** (fire-related, net) and still printed record PAT ₹7,013 Cr [V]. So the "clean" Q1 FY27 PAT is ~₹8,700–9,000 Cr [E: 7,013 + ~2,299 × (1 − ~25% tax)]. Screener's P/E (9.55×) does not reconcile to m-cap ÷ TTM reported PAT (₹1,98,812 ÷ ₹16,400 = **12.1×**); on FY26 pre-exceptional PAT it is 10.6× [E]. Use "10–12× earnings" in prose and state the basis.
3. **Novelis EBITDA/t: $462 or $500 for FY26?** Reported adjusted EBITDA/t **$462** (FY25 $480); Hindalco also quotes **$500/t "excluding exceptional items"** (i.e., also stripping the Oswego operating effects that remain *inside* adjusted EBITDA, ~−$104m) [V both]. The primer uses $462. Show both; the trend story is: **$510 (FY24) → $480 (FY25) → $462 reported / $500 underlying (FY26) → $563 (Q1 FY27)**. Management's long-term target is **>$600/t**, and it says tariff costs should *not* be added back to judge it [V-snip call].
4. **"Oswego fire losses" are three different numbers.** (a) **$925m pre-tax losses net of insurance** booked in FY26 special items; (b) **–$104m** estimated hit inside FY26 *adjusted* EBITDA plus **145 kt** lost shipments; (c) **cash**: cumulative **$1.4bn net of $300m insurance received** by Q1 FY27, with an expected **ultimate net cash cost of ~$600m** after all recoveries (70–80% expected to be recovered) [V sec.gov; V-snip call/broker]. Two fires: **16-Sep-2025 and 20-Nov-2025**, both in the hot-mill area; hot mill **restarted June 2026** (earlier than the "end of June" target) [V].
5. **India Aluminium "Upstream" EBITDA/t is not a pure smelting margin.** Hindalco's upstream segment = bauxite + alumina (including **external alumina sales**, ~190 kt guided for Q2 FY27; net long ~800 kt alumina) + captive coal/power + primary metal incl. wire rod/billets [V-snip call; K]. Revenue per tonne shipped in Q1 FY27 was **~$4,230/t** (₹13,403 Cr ÷ 335 kt at ₹94.5) vs LME **$3,576** [E] — the gap is premia, value-added primary products and alumina sales. **Do not subtract "cost of production" from LME and expect to get EBITDA/t.**
6. **"Cost of production" is quoted on different bases.** Hindalco: **$1,926/t in Q4 FY26** (hot-metal cost, $; +1% YoY) [primer]. NALCO: **~₹1.70 lakh/t in Q1 FY27 vs ~₹1.56–1.57 lakh/t FY26 average** (≈ **$1,800/t** and ≈ $1,780/t at ₹94.5 and ₹88) [V-snip call; E conversion]. VAML: **hot-metal cost ~$1,698/t in Q1 FY27**, FY27 guidance $1,650–1,700 (+$50–100 for Middle-East disruption) [V-snip]. They differ in what is included (alumina at cost vs market, royalty, overhead). Rank them with caution; all three sit in the **first/second quartile of the global cost curve** [K CRU/industry].
7. **NALCO standalone vs consolidated.** NALCO's headline FY26 PAT **₹5,816 Cr** is **standalone** (FY25 ₹5,325 Cr, +9.2%) [V-snip company PR]; **screener consolidated FY26 PAT is ₹5,797 Cr** (FY25 ₹5,268 Cr) — the difference is JV/associate losses (e.g., Angul Aluminium Park, KABIL, NPCL [K]). Primer EBITDA **₹8,613 Cr (~48%)** vs screener OP **₹7,928 Cr (44.4%)** — primer figure likely includes other income (₹666 Cr) [E: 7,928 + 666 = 8,594]. Use screener in tables. NALCO Q1 FY27: PAT ₹2,002.38 Cr consolidated vs ₹2,003.14 Cr standalone [V-snip].
8. **Alumina is two things: NALCO's product and everyone else's input.** NALCO exports ~**1.3 Mt/yr** of alumina (FY26 total alumina sales **14.46 lakh t, of which domestic 1.38 lakh t** → exports ~**13.1 lakh t** [V-snip; E]). So high alumina prices help NALCO and (net long ~800 kt) Hindalco, but hurt non-integrated smelters (BALCO historically bought alumina; Gulf smelters import). The 2024–25 alumina spike was an *input-cost* shock for the world and a *profit* windfall for NALCO.
9. **"Capacity" vs "production" vs "sales" vs "cast metal".** NALCO's smelter nameplate is **4.60 lakh tpa** but FY26 **cast metal was 4.72 lakh t** (103% utilisation) and **metal sales 4.74 lakh t** [V-snip]. NALCO's refinery nameplate is **21 lakh tpa (normative; primer says "2.1 MT")** but FY26 **calcined alumina output was 22.75 lakh t and hydrate 23.00 lakh t** [V-snip] — the primer's "2.1 MT" is the nameplate, not output. After the 5th stream (+1 Mtpa) nameplate → **~3.1 Mtpa** (company language "3.2 Mt alumina capacity by FY28") [V-snip].
10. **Hindalco's segment revenues do not add up to consolidated revenue.** FY26: Novelis ~$18.4bn (~₹1.62 lakh Cr at ₹88) + copper ₹69,838 + upstream ₹41,447 + downstream ₹15,938 ≈ **₹2.89 lakh Cr vs consolidated ₹2,74,944 Cr** → ~₹14,000 Cr of inter-segment eliminations (upstream metal sold to downstream) [E]. Q1 FY27: ₹90,334 Cr segment sum vs ₹84,825 Cr consolidated [E]. Do not add segment revenues.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Two listed members, three business models, one metal.**
  - **Integrated primary producers (India):** NALCO (pure-play: bauxite → alumina → metal, plus merchant alumina) and Hindalco's India Aluminium Upstream. Both are LME price-takers with captive bauxite, coal and power.
  - **Converter/recycler (global):** Novelis — world's largest flat-rolled aluminium maker and recycler; earns a conversion spread per tonne, not the metal price.
  - **Custom copper smelter (India):** Hindalco's Birla Copper, Dahej — a TC/RC + by-product business now squeezed by a **$0 2026 TC/RC benchmark** [V-sib copper].
  - **Combined FY26 (screener basis): revenue ₹2,92,787 Cr (+14.7%), operating profit ₹42,808 Cr (14.6%), PAT ₹19,188 Cr (−9.8%, Oswego exceptionals); m-cap ₹2,56,583 Cr on 30-Oct-2026 close** [E: sums of screener 1-Nov-26]. Hindalco is **93.9% of revenue but 77.5% of m-cap**; NALCO is **6.1% of revenue, 18.5% of operating profit, 30.2% of PAT and 22.5% of m-cap**.
- **FY26 → Q1 FY27 was the best stretch for Indian primary aluminium in history.**

### 1.2 Value chain (inputs → customer), with physical ratios

1. **Bauxite → alumina (Bayer refinery).** ~**2.6–3.0 t bauxite per t alumina** in India (NALCO FY26: 77.0 lakh t bauxite excavated for 22.75 lakh t calcined alumina = **3.4 t/t**, partly because of stock build and moisture) [V-snip; E]. Inputs: caustic soda (~**100–130 kg/t** alumina [K]), steam/coal/HFO (NALCO flagged HFO and caustic as FY26 cost drivers [primer]), lime. Waste: **red mud / bauxite residue** (~1–1.5 t per t alumina [K]) — Hindalco runs India's first bauxite-residue quarry backfilling at Dalla (UP) [V].
2. **Alumina → aluminium (smelter).** **~1.93 t alumina per t aluminium** [K]; **~0.40–0.45 t carbon anodes** (calcined petroleum coke + coal-tar pitch; "CP coke" was a NALCO Q1 FY27 cost driver [V-snip]); **aluminium fluoride ~15–20 kg/t** [K]; **power 13,000–15,000 kWh/t (DC)** [primer]. NALCO: 6,953 MU net generation for 4.72 lakh t cast metal ≈ **14,700 kWh/t all-in** [E].
3. **Power.** Indian smelters are coal-captive: thermal coal **~0.7–0.8 t per MWh** for Indian grades [K] → **~10–11 t of coal per t of aluminium** [E]. NALCO FY26 captive coal 40 lakh t ≈ "~57% of fuel needs" [primer] → total coal need ~7 Mt [E].
4. **Casting / value-add.** Ingots → wire rod (conductors, cables), billets (extrusions), slabs (rolling). Domestic premia (NALCO: **~$60/t → ~$110/t** in its latest tender) [V-snip call].
5. **Downstream conversion (Novelis model).** Buys primary + scrap (Novelis recycled content **63%**; can sheet 80%+) → rolls into sheet → sells at **metal pass-through + conversion premium**; earns **$450–600/t** [V].
6. **Recycling (secondary aluminium).** Scrap remelting uses **~5% of primary energy** (~95% saving) [K]. India's secondary industry is fragmented (hundreds of remelters around Jamnagar, Chennai, Delhi NCR, Pune) and **imports ~2 Mt of scrap** [V-sib].
7. **End users.** Electrical (cables, conductors, transformers), transport (auto, rail, aerospace), packaging (foil, cans), building & construction, consumer durables, machinery, solar frames.

### 1.3 Where value is captured [E unless tagged]
- **The integrated smelter with captive power captures the "metal rent"** (LME + premium − cash cost). In Q1 FY27 that was **$2,331/t at Hindalco upstream** and roughly **$1,800–1,900/t at NALCO's metal** [V; E] — 45–55% EBITDA margins.
- **The merchant alumina refiner** captures the alumina spread: normally ~$80–150/t, but **$400+/t in late 2024** (NALCO's alumina cash cost is ~$250–300/t [K/E; flag]).
- **The converter** (Novelis, India FRP/extrusion) earns a **steady $250–600/t** conversion margin (Hindalco India downstream **$226–303/t**; Novelis **$462–563/t**) [V].
- **The custom copper smelter** now earns almost nothing from TC/RC (benchmark **$0**, spot **−$235/dmt**; Hindalco cited **−26 to −30 c/lb** RC) and lives on **by-products** (sulphuric acid spiked with the West Asia sulphur shock) [V-sib; V-snip].
- **The non-integrated smelter buying grid power and market alumina** is the marginal, loss-making producer in down-cycles.

## 6. Business-model archetypes and their economics

| Archetype | Example | Revenue driver | Margin driver | Typical EBITDA margin | Through-cycle ROCE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Fully-integrated primary + merchant alumina** | NALCO | LME × metal t + alumina price × alumina t | Captive power/coal cost, alumina price | 16% (FY23 trough) → 44–51% (FY25–Q1 FY27) [V screener] | 14–44% (FY23–FY26) [V] |
| **2. Integrated primary with captive downstream** | Hindalco India upstream + downstream | LME + premium; VAP conversion | Captive coal, power efficiency; mix | 25–55% upstream [V]; 5–6% downstream (EBITDA/t $226–303) [V/E] | n/a by segment |
| **3. Global rolling & recycling converter** | Novelis | Tonnes × conversion premium; metal pass-through | Scrap spread (recycled content 63%), utilisation, product mix (auto/aero) | ~9–11% of revenue; $460–560/t [V] | ~8–12% [E] |
| **4. Custom copper smelter** | Hindalco Dahej | TC/RC + by-products (acid, DAP, gold/silver) + rod premium | TC/RC terms; acid prices; recoveries | 4.0% FY26; 5.3% Q1 FY27 [V-sib] | low single digit to ~15% [E] |

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Indian trade policy
- **Basic customs duty (BCD):** primary aluminium **7.5%** [K]; aluminium scrap **2.5%** (the last major non-ferrous scrap with a duty; copper scrap nil since 1-Feb-2025) [V-sib]. Industry asks: **AAI** — raise primary and downstream to **10%** and scrap to **7.5%**; **FIMI** — **15%** on primary and downstream [V-snip]. **MRAI** (recyclers) asked the PMO in Jul-2026 to *remove* the 2.5% scrap duty [V-sib]. The **Union Budget 1-Feb-2027** is the decision point [V-sib].
- **BIS Quality Control Order:** *Aluminium and Aluminium Alloy Products (Quality Control) Order, 2026*, issued ~**13-Mar-2026**, superseding the 2025 order; some products effective immediately, others from **1-Dec-2026** for larger enterprises (dates differ across sources — FLAG, check the gazette) [V-snip alcircle/mondaq]. Effect: non-BIS-certified imports (often Chinese semis) blocked.
- **Anti-dumping:** ADD on **aluminium foil from China, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand extended to 15-Dec-2026** pending a sunset review (applicants: Hindalco, SRF Altech) [V-snip angelone/alcircle]; **provisional ADD of ~$619–873/t on Chinese foil up to 80 microns** (six months; 2025 [K]); a fresh probe on Chinese foil reported Jun-2026 [V-snip].
- **Scrap:** HS 7602; FY26 imports 2,028 kt (+12%), $4.55bn [V-sib DGCI&S]. Non-ferrous scrap EPR (Hazardous & Other Wastes) live from **1-Apr-2026**, recycled-content mandates from FY28–FY29 [V-sib].
- **Mining:** MMDR Act auctions; royalty on bauxite linked to LME-based price [K]; captive-mine end-use rules.

### 7.2 Carbon
- **EU CBAM definitive phase from 1-Jan-2026** for aluminium and steel [V-sib]. **Default values** (Implementing Reg. (EU) 2025/2621): India unwrought aluminium (CN 7601) **1.87 tCO₂e/t, 2.06 with the 2026 mark-up**; bars/rods (7604) 3.41; plates/sheets (7606) 4.13; **mark-ups 10% (2026), 20% (2027), 30% (2028+)** [V-snip greensutra/eurometal]. Certificate price ~**€75/t (Q1 2026)** [V-snip; unconfirmed].
  - **Trap:** for aluminium, CBAM currently charges **direct (scope 1) emissions** (anode consumption, PFCs) — **not the indirect emissions of coal power**, which are 80%+ of an Indian smelter's 16–20 tCO₂e/t footprint [K; FLAG — check the regulation for self-generated electricity]. So the CBAM bill per tonne of Indian primary is small *today* (~2 t × €75 × (1 − free allocation phase-out ~2.5% in 2026) ≈ **€150/t gross, much less net** [E]) but would jump ~8–10× if indirect emissions are added in a future review.
  - India's exports of aluminium to the EU are modest (VAML is the largest exporter) [K]; the bigger CBAM impact is on downstream customers' "green aluminium" procurement.
- **India CCTS** (Carbon Credit Trading Scheme, under the Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act 2022): nine sectors including aluminium [V-snip]. **GHG Emission Intensity Target Rules** (draft Apr-2025, notified Oct-2025 [K; FLAG]) set unit-level intensity targets for FY2025-26 and FY2026-27 for aluminium smelters/refineries (13 aluminium units incl. Hindalco, NALCO, VAML/BALCO [K; FLAG]). One source says targets for 208 units incl. secondary aluminium were introduced Jan-2026 — uncorroborated [V-snip]. EU draft rules (May-2026) list UK, China and California schemes, not India's CCTS, for CBAM carbon-price deductions [V-snip].
- **Green aluminium premia:** low-carbon (<4 tCO₂e/t) metal attracts ~$20–50/t premia in Europe [K; FLAG]; Hindalco's 65 MW RTC RE plant and VAML's RE contracts are the Indian responses [V].

### 7.3 US policy (Novelis)
- **Section 232 tariff on aluminium: 10% (2018) → 25% (12-Mar-2025) → 50% (4-Jun-2025)**, covering primary, semis and a long list of derivatives [K; V-sib "50%"]. Impact on Novelis: **−$143m adj. EBITDA in FY26**, **$70m in Q1 FY27** (vs $24m in Q4 FY26), expected to fall to "nearly nil" by end-FY27 as Oswego/Bay Minette reduce import dependence (Novelis imports hot-band/coil from its Canadian and other plants) [V; V-snip].
- **US Midwest premium**: first above **90 c/lb in Dec-2025** and above **$1/lb on 23-Jan-2026 ($1.0095/lb)**; Fastmarkets closing high ~**$2,004/t (8-Jan-2026)**; one report cites **$2,182/t (28-Jan)** and SMM a record **$1.1325/lb (~$2,497/t)** later in 2026 (date unclear) [V-snip — conflicting bases; FLAG]. Because Novelis buys metal at LME + Midwest and passes it through with a lag, rising premia produce **favourable metal price lag** (+$191m special item in Q4 FY26) but squeeze customers [V].
- **Section 301 10% on Indian goods (24-Jul-2026)** — affects Indian aluminium exports to the US on top of 232 [V-sib].

### 7.4 PSU-specific (NALCO)
- DIPAM dividend guidelines: CPSEs should pay **at least 30% of PAT or 4% of net worth, whichever is higher** [K]. NALCO FY26 payout 36% (₹11.50/share; ₹2,112 Cr) [V-snip; V screener 36%]. GoI's 51.28% stake → ~₹1,083 Cr of FY26 dividends to the exchequer [E].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)
- **1958–1990s: state-led build-out.** Hindalco (1958, Renukoot, with Renusagar captive power) [primer]; NALCO incorporated **7-Jan-1981**, Panchpatmali mine from 1985 with French (Pechiney) technology [primer]; BALCO (1965, PSU).
- **2001–2007: privatisation and globalisation.** Sterlite (Vedanta) bought 51% of BALCO in 2001 [V-sib]; Hindalco bought **Novelis for ~$6bn in 2007** — then among the largest overseas acquisitions by an Indian company, near the top of the cycle [primer].
- **2008–09 crash:** LME fell from ~$3,300 (Jul-2008) to ~$1,300 (Feb-2009) [K]; Novelis lost heavily; Hindalco raised equity.
- **2010–2016: India's smelter wave and the long bust.** Mahan, Aditya (Hindalco), Jharsuguda (Vedanta) and Utkal alumina were built just as Chinese overcapacity sent LME to **~$1,450–1,500 in 2015** [K]. ## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **The LME price and the regional premium — the price you cannot control.** LME cash averaged $2,202 (FY24) → $2,526 (FY25) → $2,772 (FY26) → $3,576 (Q1 FY27) → $3,227 (Q2 FY27) → $3,061 (Oct-26) [V]. Integrated Indian producers convert ~80–90% of every LME dollar above their ~$1,700–1,900 cost into EBITDA, so earnings swing 2–3× the price. Premia now matter as much as LME moves: the US Midwest premium above $1/lb (Section 232 at 50%) and an elevated MJP (Gulf curtailment) add $400–2,000/t in some markets [V-snip].
2. **Alumina — the second commodity.** The 2024 spike (~$780–800/t FOB WA [K]) and normalisation (~$307 Q1 CY26) [V-snip] explain NALCO's profit path more than LME does. NALCO exports ~1.3 Mt/yr; after the 5th stream, ~2.3 Mt — making alumina NALCO's biggest single sensitivity [E]. Hindalco is net long ~800 kt [V-snip]; VAML has shifted from alumina-buyer to self-sufficient (Lanjigarh) [V-snip].
3. **Coal and power — the cost you can control.** Power is ~35–40% of smelting cost [primer]. Indian smelters' advantage is captive coal + captive power; their vulnerability is coal price/linkage and the monsoon (Hindalco Q2 cost +5–6%; NALCO refinery coal stock only 2–3 days vs 10–15 preferred) [V-snip]. Captive mine ramp-ups (NALCO Utkal +42%; Hindalco Chakla/Bandha 1.5 Mt by FY28; Meenakshi 12 Mtpa rated) are the cost-curve defence [V; V-snip].
4. **Carbon — the coming tax on coal-based metal.** CBAM definitive since 1-Jan-2026 (default India unwrought 1.87 → 2.06 tCO₂e/t with mark-up, rising to 30% mark-up by 2028) [V-snip]; India's CCTS targets; green premia. Today's CBAM cost is small because indirect power emissions are excluded [K; FLAG]; the risk is a widening to indirect emissions — Indian primary is 16–20 tCO₂e/t all-in vs ~4 for hydro-based metal [K].
5. **China's 45 Mt cap and the Gulf shock — the supply side has stopped growing.** China at ~44.2 Mt (98% of cap) [V-snip]; world growth 1.1% in 2025 [V-snip]; the Gulf (9% of world capacity) partially offline since Mar-2026 [V-snip]; LME stocks at ~237 kt [V]. ## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Primary producer:** EBITDA = Metal tonnes × (LME + regional premium + VAP premium − cash cost/t) + Alumina sold × (alumina price − alumina cash cost/t) + other.
**Converter (Novelis):** EBITDA = Shipments (kt) × conversion EBITDA/t, where conversion = product price − metal cost (LME + premium, passed through, hedged) − conversion cost; ± scrap spread (primary vs scrap price × recycled content); ± metal price lag.
**Custom copper smelter:** EBITDA = concentrate tonnes × TC + payable Cu lbs × RC + free metal + by-product credits (acid, DAP, Au/Ag) + rod premium − conversion cost.

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed, and new primer issues

**Insights (expert-level, each sourced):**
1. **The Gulf shock was bigger than Alba and Qatalum: EGA's Al Taweelah was physically hit (28-Mar-2026)** and is only partly back, with full output in Q1 2027. Gulf supply to Asia therefore stays impaired through FY27.
   - That explains three things: why Hindalco expects the MJP to stay high "until next year"; why NALCO lost its 40–52% Gulf alumina export market (lower spot alumina, $305–336); and why Hindalco targets $3,200 for FY28 hedges.
   - It also matters for NALCO's smelter: EGA is its technology licensor, and EGA's own engineering capacity is now stretched by reconstruction [E].
   - Sources [V-snip; V transcripts].
2. **India's real import dependence is ~55–58%, and India is simultaneously exporting ~40% of its primary metal.**
   - Hindalco's supply table shows only ~631 kt a quarter of domestic primary sales out of ~1,070 kt produced, against 860 kt of imported scrap and primary.
   - Consumption is ~6.1 Mt, not 4.5–5 Mt, and has grown ~12% a year since FY22, roughly three times primary output growth (~1.6% a year).
   - The policy fight (duty on scrap versus primary) is about who captures that gap. Budget 2026-27 left duties unchanged, so the BIS QCO (Dec-2026) is the operative barrier [V; E].
3. **NALCO's alumina engine is profitable at prices where Western refiners make nothing.**
   - Alcoa's alumina segment broke even at $353/t realised in Q3-2026, and Hydro's Bauxite & Alumina EBITDA fell ~80%.
   - NALCO's ₹20–22k/t cost (~$215–235) still leaves ~$100–140/t at $330–350.
   - The FY26 segment note shows chemicals ROCE of **59%** even in a year when alumina prices fell $219/t.
   - With 1 Mt of new capacity in FY28 and Gulf customers impaired, the risk is volume placement, not margin. NALCO is pushing domestic alumina sales to 2.5–3 lakh t [V; E].
4. **NALCO's metal segment earns 158% ROCE because alumina is transferred at cost.** Inter-segment alumina was ₹1,809 Cr for ~9.2 lakh t, ≈ ₹19,700/t, about the segment's all-in cost. A "metal vs alumina" margin split therefore understates the alumina contribution to integrated metal. Any sum-of-parts valuation should use the integrated chain, not the segment split [E from V].
5. **Hindalco's Q1 FY27 PAT had three non-operating tailwinds the market may annualise wrongly:**
   - +₹2,286 Cr of unallocable/GAAP adjustments within EBITDA.
   - The new tax regime, cutting the effective rate to ~26% from 30–34%; this one is structural.
   - Rupee depreciation lifting Novelis' ₹ EBITDA 37% against 24% in $.
   - Against these, an inventory-profit elimination of −₹778 Cr that should reverse as LME falls in Q2.
   - The P/E "9.55×" on screener strips exceptionals, while reported TTM is 12.1× [V; E].
6. **Novelis leverage was already at its 4.5× peak in June, not September.**
   - Deleveraging depends on three things management cannot fully control: insurance timing (some recoveries slip into FY28), $300–400m of working-capital release, and tariffs fading.
   - A new US risk is also on the books: **US Customs' $114m anti-dumping/tariff deposit on Novelis Brazil shipments** [V AR; 8-K].
7. **Governance flags at NALCO and Hindalco not in Parts 1–3:**
   - NALCO operated **without any independent director from 1-Apr-2026**, so no Audit Committee existed when Q1 FY27 results were approved. One independent director was appointed 14-Aug; Audit and NRC were still not reconstituted at 16-Sep.
   - CAG qualified NALCO's contingent-liability note (ORISED mineral-land tax unquantified after the Supreme Court's 2024 ruling).
   - At Hindalco: a **new brand royalty (0.25% of revenue, ₹225 Cr cap each at Hindalco and Novelis)** and a CBI coal-allocation case at the charge-framing stage [V].
8. **China's 45 Mt cap is leaking, not binding.** NBS output was 45.02 Mt in 2025 and is annualising ~46.7 Mt in 2026, with a record August.
   - Combined with Indonesian ramp-ups and Gulf restarts in 2027, this supports NALCO's and Hindalco's own view of a 2027 move back to balance.
   - Both managements guide LME at ~$3,000–3,300, below the Q1 FY27 average of $3,577. The record margins of Q1 FY27 should be presented as a peak, not a run-rate [V-snip; V].
