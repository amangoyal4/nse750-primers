## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **EBITDAR, not EBITDA — and screener's "operating profit" is neither.**
   - Under **Ind AS 116** (from FY20) almost every aircraft lease is capitalised: the "rent" disappears from the P&L and reappears as **depreciation of a right-of-use (ROU) asset + interest on a lease liability**. IndiGo therefore reports **EBITDAR** = PBT + finance cost − finance income + depreciation + aircraft & engine rentals (short-term/variable/damp leases still expensed) + exceptional items [V IndiGo definitions slide].
   - Screener's "Operating Profit" (FY26 ₹12,037 Cr, 14% OPM) is revenue from operations minus all cash-type expenses **including the forex loss and the remaining aircraft rentals**, so it sits between EBITDA and EBITDAR. IndiGo's own FY26 EBITDAR is **₹15,089 Cr (17.8% of revenue from operations)**; ex-forex **₹23,189 Cr (27.3%)** [V]. **Use the company's EBITDAR in prose; never compare screener OPM with a global airline's EBITDA margin.**
   - Screener's "Interest" (FY26 ₹5,944 Cr) is mostly **lease interest**, and "Borrowings" (₹77,749 Cr Mar-26; **₹87,303 Cr Sep-26**) is mostly **capitalised lease liability** (₹55,159 Cr at Sep-26) [V].
2. **RASK and CASK are net of finance income.** IndiGo deducts finance income (interest on its ~₹52,700 Cr cash pile: ₹3,530 Cr in FY26; ₹822 Cr in Q2 FY27) from both revenue and cost before dividing by ASK [V]. Its "CASK" is therefore not comparable with a carrier that reports gross. CASK ex-fuel **ex-forex** is the management KPI (FY26 ₹3.00; Q2 FY27 ₹3.45).
3. **Reported PAT vs PAT ex-forex vs PAT ex-forex ex-exceptional — three different FY26 numbers.**
   - Reported FY26 PAT **−₹2,394 Cr** [V].
   - **PAT ex-forex ₹5,706 Cr** (adds back the ₹8,100 Cr forex loss net of ₹876 Cr hedge gain) [V Q4 FY26 presentation].
   - **PAT ex-forex ex-exceptional ₹7,503 Cr** (also adds back the **₹1,219 Cr new-labour-code provision** — Q3 FY26 ₹969 Cr + Q4 true-up ₹250 Cr — and **₹577 Cr December-disruption costs**: regulatory compensation, travel vouchers, the DGCA penalty and associated costs) [V AR FY26 MD&A; V Q3 FY26 call 22-Jan-2026: labour-code provision "around 9.7 billion rupees"].
   - **Our primer calls ₹7,503 Cr "ex-forex"** — that is wrong; ₹7,503 Cr is ex-forex **and** ex-exceptional. See §3.4.
   - The forex loss is overwhelmingly **non-cash mark-to-market on dollar lease liabilities "payable over 8–10 years"** [V AR FY26]; IndiGo's CFO put the realised cash forex loss in Q1 FY27 at only ~₹82 Cr [V transcript].
4. **"Load factor" means two things.** IndiGo reports PLF = RPK ÷ ASK (distance-weighted; FY26 84.4%). DGCA's monthly table reports a **seat factor** = passengers ÷ seats per airline (e.g. IndiGo 81.1% in Sep-2026). They are close but not the same and not always the same direction. Never mix them in one chart.
5. **DGCA calendar-year vs fiscal-year totals.** DGCA's monthly press note gives Jan-to-month cumulative numbers (CY2025: **1,669.46 lakh** domestic passengers, +3.48%) [V-snip DGCA via The Week/Outlook Business]. ICRA and IndiGo's AR use **fiscal years** (FY26 domestic **1,674.2 lakh, +1.2%** per ICRA; "+1.1%" per IndiGo AR) [V-snip; V AR]. Label every total CY or FY.
6. **Domestic market share has three bases.**
   - **DGCA monthly share** = an airline's domestic passengers ÷ all scheduled domestic passengers in that month (IndiGo **65.4% in Sep-2026**; 59.6% in Dec-2025 during the meltdown) [V-press; V-snip].
   - **Fiscal-year share on IndiGo's own passenger count**: FY26 IndiGo domestic passengers 107.0 mn ÷ industry 167.4 mn = **63.9%** [E from V AR + V-snip ICRA]. The primer's "~63%" is consistent; screener's company blurb says "~64%".
   - **Seat/capacity share** (OAG/CAPA) differs again; "~63% of seats" in the primer is a capacity number. Name the basis.
7. **International share: Indian carriers vs foreign carriers vs IndiGo.** IndiGo's "~21% international passenger share" (screener blurb) is IndiGo's share of **all India-international passengers including foreign airlines** [V-snip screener]. Indian carriers together carry roughly **45–48%** of India's international traffic; foreign carriers (Gulf carriers above all) carry the rest [K, DGCA quarterly international data; not re-fetched — §H]. IndiGo's own split: international = **31.6% of FY26 ASK** (54.5 bn of 172.4 bn) and "close to 33%" in Q1 FY27; target **~40% by 2030 (FY30)** [V AR; V transcript].
8. **Fleet counts include damp-leased and freighter aircraft.** IndiGo's "442 aircraft" (30-Sep-2026) includes **5 damp-leased Boeing 787s (from Norse Atlantic) and 3 A321 freighters**; the passenger own-operated fleet is **434**. At 31-Mar-2026 the 441 included **20 damp-leased** aircraft (6 B787, 5 B737, 4 A321, 5 A320) [V]. Grounded (AOG) aircraft due to Pratt & Whitney GTF engines are **inside** the fleet count; IndiGo stopped disclosing a precise AOG number in recent decks (it was ~60–70 at the 2024 peak [K]) — §H.
9. **Yield vs fare vs RASK.** IndiGo "yield" = **passenger ticket revenue ÷ RPK** (₹6.30 in Q2 FY27, +34.3%) — it excludes ancillaries. RASK = total revenue less finance income ÷ ASK (₹5.76). "Fares +30%" headlines in 2026 are yield, not RASK. CY/FY and domestic/international mix shifts also move yield.
10. **Global peers report on different years and currencies.** Ryanair FY = Apr–Mar (FY26 ended 31-Mar-2026), € and IFRS 16; Southwest = calendar year (FY2025), US$ and US GAAP (operating leases stay off the P&L as rent — so Southwest "EBITDA" is not EBITDAR); Emirates FY = Apr–Mar, AED. Convert at period-average rates and state the year.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **The Indian airline industry in late 2026 is a near-duopoly in a cost shock.**
  - **IndiGo 65.4% + Air India group 26.8% = 92.2% of domestic passengers in Sep-2026**; Akasa 5.6%; SpiceJet **0.6%** (from ~4% a year earlier); Star Air, Alliance Air, Fly91 and others share the rest [V Business Today, 27-Oct-2026, quoting DGCA].
  - Domestic traffic is **shrinking**: Sep-2026 **118.23 lakh** passengers, **−6.5% YoY** vs Sep-2025's 126.43 lakh (ICRA: −6.5% YoY); Aug-2026 **121.26 lakh, −6.3% YoY** [V-snip Angel One]; **Jan–Sep 2026 cumulative 1,223.53 lakh vs 1,233.70 lakh (−0.82%)** — the first negative year-to-date print outside COVID [V Business Today 27-Oct-2026; V ICRA special comment 28-Oct-2026]. NB: Business Today labels 126.43 lakh as "August" and calls −6.49% a month-on-month fall; arithmetic and ICRA show it is the **Sep-2025** base (YoY) — see §H.
  - ICRA (half-yearly update, 27-Aug-2026): FY27 domestic traffic growth cut to **3–6%**, international traffic of Indian carriers **−3% to −6%**, **industry net loss ₹36,000–38,000 Cr in FY27** after ₹28,000–30,000 Cr in FY26; outlook **Negative** since Mar-2026 [V ICRA; V-snip Upstox for the international figure].
- **Price is up, volume is down, cost is up more.** IndiGo Q2 FY27: capacity −1.0%, passengers −4.7%, **yield +34.3% to ₹6.30**, revenue from operations **+25.9%** — but fuel cost **+67.2%** (₹9,967 Cr) and the rupee averaged ₹95.09 vs ₹87.06, so the quarter still lost **₹1,879 Cr** (ex-forex −₹724 Cr) [V].
  - Fuel was **38% of IndiGo's Q2 FY27 total expenses** (₹9,967 Cr of ₹26,188 Cr) and **40% of expenses ex-forex** (₹9,967 Cr of ₹24,855 Cr), vs 28% of FY26 total expenses [E from V].
  - The government softened the shock for domestic flying: for **April, May and 1–8 June 2026** public-sector OMCs capped the domestic-airline ATF increase at **~25% over the March reference** (Delhi ₹96,638/kl → **₹1,04,927/kl** on 1-Apr) while international flights paid the market price (Delhi international reference **₹2,07,341/kl, +114.5%**) [V Angel One 1-Apr-2026; V-snip FPJ; V IndiGo CFO]. From 9-Jun pricing reverted to the MOPAG-linked formula [V transcript]. A further ₹5/litre cut for domestic airlines from 1-Jul-2026 left Delhi ~₹1.10 lakh/kl [V-snip; §H].
- **IndiGo is gaining share while the industry bleeds.** Sep-2026 share 65.4% is near its record (a 22-Jul-2026 AviationA2Z headline: "IndiGo hits record market share as Air India loses ground") [V-snip]. IndiGo has the only balance sheet in the industry that can carry the loss: **total cash ₹52,685 Cr (free ₹39,929 Cr) at 30-Sep-2026** against total debt incl. leases ₹87,303 Cr [V].
  - **Air India group** (Tata 73.82%, Singapore Airlines 25.1%) lost **₹22,238 Cr in FY26** (FY25 ₹10,859 Cr) on revenue ₹71,870 Cr (−~9%): Air India ₹15,368 Cr loss on ₹51,452 Cr revenue; Air India Express ₹6,767 Cr on ₹19,088 Cr [V National Herald, 28-Jul-2026, from the airlines' annual reports]. Its year included the **AI171 Ahmedabad crash (12-Jun-2025, Boeing 787-8, 241 of 242 on board and ~19 on the ground killed)** [K], the **Pakistan airspace ban on Indian carriers (since 24-Apr-2025)** [K] and the 2026 West Asia closures.
  - **Akasa Air** FY26 loss **~₹2,716 Cr** (+~37%) [V-snip NewsBytes; fetch blocked → unverified].
  - **SpiceJet** is a ₹1,143 Cr market-cap shell of its former self: 0.6% share, no FY26 accounts on screener, promoter holding down to 24.19% (Mar-2026), negative book value [V screener; V-press].
- **The cost structure is dollar-denominated and the rupee is at record lows.** IndiGo's FY26 forex loss of **₹8,976 Cr** (₹8,100 Cr net of hedges) turned a ₹5,706 Cr ex-forex profit into a ₹2,394 Cr reported loss; the closing rupee moved from 85.50 to 94.56 [V]. Every ₹1 move in closing USD/INR has cost IndiGo roughly **₹900–1,000 Cr of forex loss** in recent quarters (Q4 FY26: rupee ~89.9 → 94.56, forex in EBITDAR ₹4,208 Cr; Q2 FY27: 94.66 → 95.85, ₹1,155 Cr) [E from V]. That implies a net dollar liability of roughly **$9–10 bn** — the ~$5.75 bn capitalised operating lease liability (₹55,159 Cr ÷ 95.85) plus finance leases, maintenance/supplementary-rental provisions and other $ payables, net of $ deposits [E; IndiGo does not publish the net figure — §H].
- **Operational reliability became a regulatory issue.** IndiGo's **3–5 December 2025 meltdown** (2,507 flights cancelled, 1,852 delayed, >3 lakh passengers hit) followed the DGCA's revised **Flight Duty Time Limitations (FDTL Phase II)** for pilots; DGCA's inquiry blamed "over-optimisation of operations, inadequate regulatory preparedness, deficiencies in system software support" and IndiGo's failure to maintain buffers; penalty **₹22.20 Cr** (₹1.80 Cr + ₹30 lakh/day × 68 days) + **₹50 Cr bank guarantee**, caution to the CEO, warning to the accountable manager (17-Jan-2026) [V-snip IANS/Business Today/India TV]. IndiGo booked **₹577 Cr** of exceptional disruption costs in FY26 (compensation, vouchers, the ₹22.2 Cr penalty — which it said it was "evaluating" but provisioned) [V AR; V Q3 call]. DGCA also ordered a **~10% cut in IndiGo's winter-2025 domestic schedule** [V Q3 FY26 call, analyst question acknowledged by the CEO]. The CEO left in March.
- **Valuation still prices dominance, not this year's P&L.** IndiGo m-cap **₹1,88,600 Cr** at ₹4,878 (52-week range ₹3,895–5,970); P/E not meaningful (TTM PAT −₹4,105 Cr); book value ₹129/share → **P/B ~37.8×**; ROE −27.1%, ROCE 5.2% [V screener 1-Nov-26]. FY26 EBITDAR-based EV ≈ m-cap + total debt − total cash = 1,88,600 + 87,303 − 52,685 ≈ **₹2.23 lakh Cr ≈ 9.6× FY26 ex-forex EBITDAR / 14.8× reported EBITDAR** [E].

### 1.2 The value chain (inputs → customer)

1. **Aircraft OEMs** — Airbus (A320neo family, A350) and Boeing (737 MAX, 787) are a **duopoly**; ATR for turboprops. India has **no OEM**. Delivery slots are sold out to ~2032–35 [K]. Airbus delivery delays and Boeing's production caps (FAA cap on 737 MAX output since Jan-2024 [K]) constrain growth.
2. **Engine OEMs** — CFM International (LEAP-1A/-1B; GE–Safran), Pratt & Whitney (PW1100G GTF, RTX), Rolls-Royce (Trent XWB on A350, Trent 1000 on 787), GE (GEnx on 787). IndiGo signed an **MoU with CFM for >1,000 LEAP-1A engines** for future deliveries (announced on the Q1 FY27 call, 23-Jul-2026) — effectively moving future A320neo-family deliveries away from P&W after the GTF groundings [V transcript].
3. **Lessors** — AerCap, SMBC, Avolon, BOC Aviation, Air Lease, CDB etc. Indian airlines lease ~80–90% of their fleets. IndiGo at 30-Sep-2026: **330 operating leases, 66 finance leases, 41 owned, 5 damp leases** [V]. India's Cape Town Convention law (aircraft repossession) was strengthened by the **Protection of Interests in Aircraft Objects Act, 2025** after Go First's 2023 insolvency froze lessors' aircraft [K — verify the Act's date]. GIFT City (IFSC) leasing: IndiGo inducted 13 of 13 aircraft in Q1 FY27 and allocated **US$820 mn** to aircraft purchases through its GIFT City entity in FY26; first Indian carrier to use a **JOLCO** (Japanese operating lease with call option) [V AR; V transcript].
4. **Fuel** — ATF from public-sector OMCs (IOCL, BPCL, HPCL) and private refiners (Reliance, Nayara). Price = international benchmark (Singapore jet/MOPAG) + freight + OMC margin, then **central excise (11% [K]) + state VAT (up to 25–29% in some states; 1–5% in many after the 2022–25 rationalisation [K])**. ATF is **outside GST**, so airlines cannot claim input tax credit on it — a structural ~10–15% cost penalty vs a GST-credit regime [K/E].
5. **Airports** — AAI (government; ~125 operational airports) and private PPP operators: **Adani Airport Holdings** (Mumbai CSMIA, **Navi Mumbai NMIA — first commercial flights 25-Dec-2025, IndiGo first airline, ~15 daily flights at launch** [V IndiGo Q3 FY26 call]), Ahmedabad, Lucknow, Jaipur, Mangaluru, Guwahati, Thiruvananthapuram; **GMR** (Delhi IGI, Hyderabad, Goa-Mopa); **Fairfax/BIAL** (Bengaluru); **Zurich Airport** (**Noida International Airport, Jewar — IndiGo "the first airline to commence operations" there in Q1 FY27** [V transcript]). Airports earn aeronautical charges (landing, parking, UDF/user development fee charged to passengers) regulated by **AERA** for major airports. IndiGo's "airport fees and charges" were **₹6,548 Cr in FY26 (7.7% of revenue from ops)** [V]. Airports went from **74 operational (2014) to 160+ (2025)** [V IndiGo AR citing MoCA]. *(No airports report exists in our library — do not plan a link.)*
6. **MRO, ground handling, catering, distribution** — engine shops abroad (P&W, Lufthansa Technik, ST Engineering), Air Works, AIESL (Air India's), IndiGo's planned in-house MRO at Bengaluru; Safran's LEAP MRO at Hyderabad [V AR]. Ground handling: IndiGo's subsidiary **Agile Airport Services (26,271 staff)** [V AR]; Celebi, Bird, AISATS. Distribution: own website/app (IndiGo's dominant channel), GDS/OTAs (MakeMyTrip, EaseMyTrip, Yatra, ixigo).
7. **Air navigation** — AAI is also the ANSP; it will invest **₹15,000 Cr by 2028** in ATC automation/navigation [V AR].
8. **Regulators** — MoCA (policy, bilaterals), **DGCA** (safety, licensing, FDTL, fares monitoring, market data), **BCAS** (security), AERA (airport tariffs), Ministry of Petroleum (ATF pricing interventions), state governments (VAT on ATF).

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **Value is captured upstream and at the airports, not by airlines.** OEMs (Airbus operating margin ~8–10%, engine makers' aftermarket 20%+), lessors (AerCap ROE ~12–15%), monopoly airports (regulated returns ~14–16% on equity under AERA [K]) and the fuel supply chain (OMC margins + state VAT) all earn steadier returns than airlines.
- **Indian airlines in aggregate have lost money in most years since 2008.** Kingfisher (2012), Jet Airways (Apr-2019) and Go First (May-2023) failed; Air India needed a ₹2.2 lakh Cr state write-off before the Tata sale (Jan-2022) [K]. ICRA projects an industry net loss of **₹28,000–30,000 Cr in FY26 and ₹36,000–38,000 Cr in FY27** [V ICRA].
- **IndiGo is the exception** — the one Indian carrier that has earned through-cycle profits (cumulative FY17–FY25 PAT **₹6,980 Cr**, after COVID-era losses of **₹12,274 Cr in FY21–FY23** [E from V screener]). It captures value by (a) the lowest CASK in India, (b) scale-based bargaining power with Airbus/lessors (sale-and-leaseback gains), (c) network density (~2,000–2,150 daily flights), (d) ~41,900 employees with high productivity, and (e) a ₹40,000 Cr free-cash buffer that lets it survive shocks that kill rivals.

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Income and the middle class.** Air travel elasticity to GDP in India is ~1.5–2× [K, industry rule of thumb]. The structural gap (0.14 domestic seats per capita) is the bull case every airline cites [V AR].
2. **Price elasticity is high — and 2026 proved it.** IndiGo's yield rose **+21.3% in Q1 FY27 and +34.3% in Q2 FY27**; its passengers fell **−4.7% in Q2**; industry domestic traffic fell **−6.3%/−6.5% in Aug/Sep 2026** [V]. A rough arc elasticity of −0.2 to −0.3 on yield [E] — demand is less elastic than LCC theory assumes because supply was also cut, but the volume loss is real. IndiGo's CFO (Jul-2026): post-COVID yields rose **40–45%** vs pre-COVID on revenge travel and constrained capacity [V transcript].
3. **Rail competition.** Indian Railways AC classes and Vande Bharat (incl. sleeper variants) compete on <800 km routes [K]. Higher air fares push marginal travellers back to rail.
4. **Diaspora & Gulf labour corridors.** ~9 mn Indians live in the GCC [K]; Kerala, Tamil Nadu, UP, Bihar, Telangana to UAE/Saudi/Qatar/Kuwait/Oman is a huge VFR/labour market. **This is exactly the market hit by the 2026 West Asia war**: IndiGo's Middle East daily departures fell from **~150 pre-war to ~20**, then recovered to **90–95%** of pre-war by end-June 2026 [V CFO, Q1 FY27 call].
5. **Outbound tourism and students** — SE Asia (Thailand, Vietnam, Bali, Malaysia), Central Asia (Almaty, Tashkent, Baku, Tbilisi), Türkiye, Greece; students to UK/Europe/Australia. IndiGo's 46 international destinations + **94–98 partner destinations via codeshares** [V AR; V Q2 FY27 PPT].
6. **Business travel** — corporate demand concentrated on metro trunk routes (Delhi–Mumbai, Delhi–Bengaluru, Mumbai–Bengaluru); premium products (Air India business, IndiGoStretch) target it.
7. **Airport capacity unlocks** — Navi Mumbai (Dec-2025) and Noida/Jewar (2026) relieve the two most slot-constrained metros (Mumbai CSMIA ran at ~55 mn passengers on one runway system [K]); new airports in tier-2/3 cities (Hindon, Purnia, Rewa, Adampur, Bhavnagar were new IndiGo stations in FY26) [V Q4 FY26 PPT].
8. **Festive/seasonal pattern** — Q3 (Oct–Dec: Diwali, weddings, winter holidays) and Q4/Q1 (summer) are peaks; **Q2 (Jul–Sep, monsoon) is the seasonal trough** — IndiGo has lost money in almost every recent September quarter — losses in Q2 FY20–FY23 [K] and Q2 FY25–FY27, a profit only in Q2 FY24 (Q2 PAT: FY24 +₹189 Cr; FY25 −₹987 Cr; FY26 −₹2,582 Cr; FY27 −₹1,879 Cr [V screener]).

## 7. Regulation and policy

1. **DGCA safety & FDTL.** DGCA's revised **Civil Aviation Requirement on Flight Duty Time Limitations** for pilots (issued Jan-2024 after a Delhi High Court case by pilot unions; weekly rest raised from 36 to **48 hours**, night duty redefined (00:00–06:00), **night landings capped at 2 per duty (from 6)**; implemented in phases — Phase I 1-Jul-2025, **Phase II 1-Nov-2025**) [K — verify clause details]. IndiGo's Dec-2025 collapse was blamed on inadequate preparation for Phase II [V AR: "revised crew rostering rules (Flight Duty Time Limitation Phase II) … presented execution challenges during a peak travel period"]. DGCA granted IndiGo temporary relaxations (to **10-Feb-2026**) and fined it for non-compliance over 68 days [V-snip]. IndiGo "transition to the revised FDTL norms in February" 2026 [V Q3 call]. **Effect: structurally more pilots per aircraft** — IndiGo had **5,786 pilots** at Mar-2026 [V AR].
2. **DGCA market interventions.** Ordered a ~10% cut in IndiGo's winter-2025 schedule (redistributing slots) [V Q3 call]; monitors fares (has no power to cap fares except in emergencies; it did cap fares on some routes post-disruption in Dec-2025 [K — verify]).
3. **Passenger rights (CAR Section 3 Series M Part IV)** — compensation for denied boarding/cancellations; IndiGo's FY26 exceptional ₹577 Cr included regulatory compensation [V].
4. **ATF taxation** — outside GST; state VAT; central excise; periodic calls for GST inclusion [K].
5. **Airport economic regulation** — AERA tariff orders for major airports; UDF increases at privatised airports (Delhi, Mumbai, Navi Mumbai) feed ticket prices [K].
6. **Bilaterals & airspace** — MoCA negotiates seat entitlements (Gulf caps); **Pakistan airspace closed to Indian carriers since 24-Apr-2025**; West Asia airspace closures since 28-Feb-2026 [V-snip; V IndiGo AR].
7. **Foreign ownership** — 49% FDI cap for foreign airlines in Indian carriers (100% for NRIs) [K]; SIA's 25.1% of Air India is under this rule.
8. **Leasing & Cape Town** — GIFT City IFSC leasing regime (tax holiday) [K]; **Protection of Interests in Aircraft Objects Act** to give lessors Cape Town-compliant repossession [K, verify year].
9. **Labour codes** — four labour codes notified in **Nov-2025**; IndiGo provisioned **₹1,219 Cr** for higher gratuity/leave liabilities [V].
10. **RCS-UDAN** — VGF-subsidised regional connectivity; extended (UDAN 2.0) in 2025 Budget for 10 more years and 120 new destinations [K].
11. **SAF blending** — indicative 1% SAF mandate for international flights from **2027**, 2% from 2028 [K]; IOCL building SAF plants "to comply with SAF blending mandates starting from year 2027" [V IOCL website].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

- **1953–1994: state monopoly** (Indian Airlines domestic, Air India international). **1994 Open Skies** → Jet Airways, Sahara, ModiLuft, East-West, Damania; most died by 1997 [K].
- **2003–2008: the LCC boom** — Air Deccan (2003), SpiceJet (2005), IndiGo and GoAir (2005–06), Kingfisher (2005). Fare wars + ATF at $140/bbl (2008) → industry losses; Kingfisher bought Deccan (2008) and collapsed (2012) [K].
- **2007: Air India–Indian Airlines merger** — a decade of state bailouts (₹30,000 Cr 2012 turnaround plan) [K].
- **2012–2019: IndiGo's ascent** — profitable every year FY10–FY19 except none; IPO Nov-2015; **Jet Airways collapse (Apr-2019)** handed slots to IndiGo/SpiceJet [K].
- **2020–2022: COVID** — FY21 IndiGo loss ₹5,806 Cr, FY22 −₹6,162 Cr [V screener]; fare bands imposed by MoCA (May-2020 to Aug-2022) [K].
- **Jan-2022: Tata takes Air India** (₹18,000 Cr deal), then merges AirAsia India and Vistara into a two-brand group (AIX: Oct-2024; Vistara: 12-Nov-2024) [K].
- **2023: Go First collapses (May)** on P&W engine failures; IndiGo orders **500 A320neo-family aircraft (Jun-2023, record)**; Air India orders 470 (Feb-2023) [K; primer]. P&W discloses the GTF powder-metal defect (Jul–Sep 2023) → IndiGo AOGs peak at ~70 in 2024 [K].
- **FY24–FY25: record profits** — IndiGo PAT ₹8,172 Cr (FY24) and ₹7,258 Cr (FY25) [V screener].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Duopoly structure — IndiGo ~65%, Air India group ~27%, everyone else ~8%.** The domestic market is now a two-player game (Sep-2026: 92.2% combined) [V]. Concentration supports fare discipline (IndiGo yield +34% in Q2 FY27 with capacity −1%), but the No. 2 is a ₹22,000 Cr-a-year loss-maker owned by a conglomerate that will keep funding it [V]. Expert framing: *the domestic market is an IndiGo-priced market; Air India competes on premium and long-haul; the third player (Akasa) is sub-scale.*
2. **Fuel (ATF) and its tax wedge.** Fuel went from 28% to 38–42% of IndiGo's cost in two quarters; Singapore jet +~120% YoY in Q1 FY27 [V]. ATF is outside GST and carries state VAT and central excise; the 2026 government cap on domestic ATF lasted only ~10 weeks [V]. Every Indian airline P&L is a derivative of Brent × crack × rupee.
3. **The rupee and the dollar lease book (Ind AS 116).** ~60–65% of costs are dollar-linked; the lease liability (₹55,159 Cr at IndiGo alone) is marked to market every quarter. The rupee's fall from ₹85.5 (Mar-25) to ₹95.85 (Sep-26) produced ₹8,976 Cr of FY26 forex loss and ₹1,333 Cr in Q2 FY27 at IndiGo [V]. Reported PAT is now mostly a currency print — analysts must read EBITDAR ex-forex and PAT ex-forex.
4. **Supply constraints — engines, OEM delays, pilots.** P&W GTF groundings (~40 IndiGo aircraft still AOG in Jan-2026), Airbus/Boeing delivery delays, and DGCA's FDTL Phase II (more pilots per aircraft) cap capacity growth. Paradoxically this is what keeps fares up — and it forced expensive damp leases (20 at IndiGo in Mar-2026) [V].
5. **Geopolitics and airspace.** Pakistan's airspace ban (since Apr-2025) lengthens every Indian carrier's westbound flights; the West Asia war (from 28-Feb-2026) shut Gulf corridors (IndiGo's Middle East departures ~150/day → ~20 → 90–95% by end-June) and hands share to Gulf carriers that can overfly Pakistan (Emirates India pax +7% vs Indian carriers −26% in Apr–Jun 2026) [V].
6. **Internationalisation of IndiGo.** International = 31.6% of FY26 ASK, ~33% in Q1 FY27, target ~40% by FY30; levers: A321XLR (4 in fleet), damp-leased 787s from Norse (5 at Sep-2026, down from 6), 60 A350-900s from 2027, IndiGoStretch, codeshares (Garuda, British Airways, AEGEAN added FY26; 94–98 partner destinations), BluChip loyalty [V]. Whether a domestic LCC can become a profitable long-haul network airline is *the* long-term question.
7. **Reliability as a regulatory and franchise risk.** The Dec-2025 meltdown (2,507 cancellations in three days, cancellation rate 9.65% in Dec) cost IndiGo share (59.6% in Dec), a DGCA penalty, a forced 10% schedule cut, ₹577 Cr of exceptional costs and its CEO [V]. Share recovered to 65.4% by Sep-2026 — the franchise proved resilient, but the regulator is now more interventionist.
8. **Balance-sheet asymmetry.** IndiGo's ₹52,685 Cr cash (₹39,929 Cr free) vs Air India's dependence on Tata/SIA capital, Akasa's on its backers, and SpiceJet's on emergency funding [V]. In a down-cycle the industry consolidates toward the cash-rich player.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Airline profit = ASK × (RASK − CASK)**, where
- RASK ≈ PLF × Yield × (1 + ancillary/ticket ratio) + cargo & other per ASK;
- CASK = fuel CASK (fuel burn per ASK × ATF price incl. taxes) + ownership CASK (D&A + lease interest + rentals) + maintenance + staff + airport + other + forex.

Equivalent per-flight view: **Seats × LF × (average fare + ancillary) − (block hours × hourly cost + fuel + airport charges + ownership)**.

### C.1 Worked example — one A321neo domestic flight, Delhi–Mumbai (~1,150 km), FY26 economics [E composite]
- 232 seats [K, IndiGo A321neo config] × 1,150 km = **2.67 lakh ASK**.
- At FY26 LF 84.4% → 195 passengers; RPK 2.25 lakh.
- Ticket revenue = 2.25 lakh RPK × yield ₹5.06 = **₹11.4 lakh** (≈ ₹5,840 per passenger).
- Ancillary at 12.3% of ticket (FY26: ₹9,092 ÷ ₹73,648 Cr) = ₹1.4 lakh. Total ≈ **₹12.8 lakh** ≈ RASK ₹4.79 on this flight (system RASK ₹4.99 includes cargo/other).
- Cost at FY26 CASK ₹5.00 = **₹13.4 lakh**, of which fuel ₹1.47/ASK = ₹3.9 lakh; ex-fuel ex-forex ₹3.00 = ₹8.0 lakh; forex ₹0.52 = ₹1.4 lakh.
- **Result: ~−₹0.6 lakh per flight reported; ~+₹0.8 lakh ex-forex.** Multiply by ~2,150 flights/day.

### C.2 Same flight in Q2 FY27 [E]
- LF 80.3% → 186 pax; RPK 2.14 lakh × yield ₹6.30 = ₹13.5 lakh ticket (+18%); ancillary ~₹1.6 lakh → ~₹15.1 lakh.
- CASK ₹6.22 → cost ₹16.6 lakh (fuel ₹2.44/ASK = ₹6.5 lakh, +66%).
- **Result ≈ −₹1.5 lakh per flight** (≈ −9% margin) — consistent with reported Q2 PBT margin −8.0% [V].

### C.3 Company-level reconciliation (IndiGo FY26, ₹ Cr) [V]
- Revenue from ops 84,962 + other income 4,552 = total income 89,513.
- − Expenses ex-fuel ex-forex 55,313 − fuel 25,389 − forex 8,976 = PBT before exceptional −165.
- − Exceptional 1,796 (labour codes 1,219 + disruption 577) = PBT −1,961; tax ~433 → PAT **−2,394**.
- EBITDAR 15,089 = PBT −1,961 + exceptional 1,796 + finance cost 5,891 − finance income 3,530 + D&A 10,808 + rentals 2,085. Ex-forex EBITDAR 23,189 adds forex net of hedges 8,100.
