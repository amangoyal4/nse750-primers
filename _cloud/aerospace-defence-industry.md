## 0. Executive frame (one paragraph)

Indian A&D is a **monopsony-driven, budget-funded, order-book business**. The Ministry of Defence (MoD) is ~90%+ of demand for the PSU primes. Every rupee of revenue traces back to a chain:
1. A DAC Acceptance of Necessity (AoN).
2. A contract, which typically carries a 10–15% advance.
3. Multi-year milestone execution.

Three structural shifts since 2020 re-rated the sector:
- **Reserved domestic budget.** 75% of the capital acquisition budget is ring-fenced for domestic industry (it was 58% in FY22).
- **Import embargoes.** Five, now six, Positive Indigenisation Lists (PILs) cover more than 5,400 items.
- **A war-tested demand shock.** Operation Sindoor (7–10 May 2025) was followed by record FY26 AoNs (₹6.81 lakh Cr) and contracts (₹2.28 lakh Cr).

Domestic production nearly doubled, from ₹84,643 Cr in FY21 to ₹1.78 lakh Cr in FY26. Exports rose 25x, from ₹1,522 Cr in FY17 to ₹38,424 Cr in FY26.

Listed equity is dominated by three DPSUs: HAL, BEL and BDL, together about 88% of the 12-member group's FY26 revenue. They have large order books and are profitable, at 28–30% EBITDA for HAL and BEL. A handful of smaller private sub-system makers trade at 85–200x earnings on the expectation that they capture a rising share of a ₹2+ lakh Cr annual procurement pool.

## 1. What the sub-sector does: the value chain and where value is captured

### 1.1 Value chain (tiers)

| Tier | Activity | Indian examples (listed **bold**) | Typical economics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T0: Customer** | MoD (Army, Navy, IAF, Coast Guard), MHA paramilitary, DRDO (as developer), ISRO/IN-SPACe (space), export governments; civil OEMs (Boeing, Airbus, Safran, GE, Rolls-Royce) for aerospace | — | Monopsony; DAP 2020 rules; L1 or nomination pricing |
| **T1: Platform prime / system integrator** | Fighters, helicopters, trainers, missiles, radars, warships, tanks | **HAL** (aircraft and helicopters), **BEL** (radars, EW, C4I, air-defence systems), **BDL** (missiles: Akash, Astra, Konkurs, Invar, MRSAM parts), shipyards (MDL, GRSE, CSL: non-members), L&T, TASL, BrahMos Aerospace | Very large tickets (₹5,000–1,00,000 Cr). High bought-out content (50–70%) and many imported sub-systems. Margin comes from integration, design authority and long MRO tails |
| **T2: Sub-system / LRU makers** | Avionics, radar sub-assemblies, RF/microwave, EW, fire control, mission computers, simulators, optics | **Data Patterns**, **Astra Microwave**, **Apollo Micro**, **Paras** (optronics, space optics, anti-drone), **Zen** (simulators, counter-drone), Centum, Alpha Design, Sika (non-member) | High gross margins (55–75%), long receivables, IP-driven. Sell to T1 primes and directly to MoD |
| **T3: Precision components, aerostructures, engine parts** | Machined and fabricated parts, assemblies, hydraulics, aerostructures, engine parts | **MTAR** (also nuclear and clean energy: Bloom Energy fuel-cell hot boxes), **Aequs** (aero components, Airbus/Boeing/Safran tier-2), **Dynamatic** (Airbus A320 flap-track beams, A330 parts, Boeing CH-47 ramps, hydraulics), Azad Engineering, PTC Industries (non-members) | Build-to-print with long qualification cycles (3–5 years per part). | **T4: Materials** | Superalloys, titanium alloys, special steels, armour, castings and forgings | **MIDHANI** (superalloys, titanium, armour, molybdenum), Bharat Forge, PTC (titanium) | Long inventory cycles (900–1,400 days at MIDHANI), cost-plus to DRDO/ISRO, low ROCE |
| **Services** | MRO/ROH (repair and overhaul), upgrades, training, spares, performance-based logistics | HAL (about a third of revenue is ROH), BEL (upgrades), Zen (AMC), Dynamatic | Highest-margin, annuity-like revenue. Fleet age drives it |

### 1.2 Where value is captured
1. **Design authority and IP.** The owner of the design gets the long tail of spares, upgrades and MRO for 30–40 years. DRDO/ADA hold design for LCA, Akash, Astra and QRSAM; the production agency (HAL, BDL, BEL) holds Transfer of Technology (ToT) rights. The draft DAP 2026's shift from "Make in India" to "Owned by India" (IP ownership by Indian firms) is aimed directly at this choke-point. Sources: alphadefense.in 10-Feb-2026; JSA Prism March 2026 (search-level).
2. **System integration.** A prime earns a margin on the integrated price, including bought-outs. BEL's material cost is 50–59% of revenue (normally ~55%), while its EBITDA is ~28–29%. That spread is the integration premium. Source: BEL Q1FY27 concall via PL Capital note, 28-Jul-2026 [V] plindia.com/ResReport/BHE-28-7-26-PL.pdf.
3. **Annuity MRO.** HAL's ROH (repair and overhaul) carries higher margins than new-build manufacturing. Fleet growth (Su-30MKI ~260, ALH ~350+, LCA rising to 220+) underwrites it. [K: HAL annual reports]
4. **Sub-system IP held by private firms** (Data Patterns' radar/EW electronics, Astra Microwave's TR modules and radar sub-systems). These carry the highest gross margins in the chain but are paid last, which shows in receivable days of 250–300.
5. **Value leakage abroad.** Engines (GE F404/F414, Safran M88, Saturn AL-31FP), seekers, radars, semiconductors, superalloy ingots and certain titanium grades are still imported. BDL's FY26–Q1FY27 execution slipped on imported radars and seekers. Source: niftytrader.in Q1FY27 BDL note [V].

### 3.3 Other demand drivers
- **Fleet replacement and squadron gap.** The IAF has **31 active fighter squadrons against 42 authorised**, and the Standing Committee (2023) said at least 180 fighters were needed (PRS [V]). MiG-21s retired in September 2025 [K]. Responses so far: LCA Mk1A 83 + 97 = 180, MRFA 114 Rafale AoN, AMCA.
- **Naval build.** 65 ships and submarines were under construction (PRS [V]). This drives BEL naval radars and sonars, and BDL torpedoes and missiles (Varunastra, MRSAM).
- **Air defence after Sindoor.** QRSAM: BEL guides ~₹30,000 Cr in FY27, pending CCS clearance. Other programmes are Akash-NG, MRSAM, Project Kusha (long-range SAM; BEL sub-system potential >₹40,000 Cr), counter-drone systems and directed-energy weapons (DEW). Source: PL BEL note [V].
- **Ammunition and fuzes.** BEL's electronic-fuze contract is its largest programme, with ~8 years of visibility (PL [V]). Solar Industries and the ex-OFB DPSUs (Munitions India) are context.
- **Exports.** DPSU and private exports are about 55:45 (FY26). Key items are BrahMos (Philippines, US$375 mn, 2022), Akash (Armenia), Pinaka (Armenia), radars, and Boeing/Airbus aerostructures (counted as defence exports where under DGFT/DDP authorisation). Sources: [K] MoD; ETV [V].

## 4. Supply side, cost structure, capacity

### 4.1 Capacity and production base
- **DPSUs.** There are 16: HAL, BEL, BDL, BEML, MIDHANI, MDL, GRSE, GSL, HSL, and the seven ex-OFB companies created on 1-Oct-2021 (MIL, AVNL, AWEIL, TCL, YIL, IOL, GIL). PSUs make ~76% of production (MoD FY26 [V]).
- **Ex-OFB funding.** MoD gave the ex-OFB DPSUs ₹5,757 Cr for modernisation plus a ₹5,000 Cr emergency authorisation fund over FY22–FY25. Support runs to FY27 (PRS [V]).
- **Private base.** The private sector is ~24% of production, ~₹42,000 Cr in FY26 (MoD [V]). Large private primes:
  - L&T (K9 Vajra, naval, AMCA bidder).
  - Tata Advanced Systems (C-295 final assembly line at Vadodara, opened Oct-2024; WhAP; AMCA bidder).
  - Bharat Forge/KSSL (ATAGS, artillery shells).
  - Adani Defence, Solar (ammunition, loitering munitions), Mahindra Defence.
  - The MSME base: >16,000 MSMEs, by MoD's frequent claim [K].
- **HAL scale.** 20 production divisions and 11 R&D centres [K: HAL AR].
  - LCA line capacity: 16/yr at Bengaluru, a third line at Nashik giving 24/yr (HAL statements [K]). Engines are the binding constraint, not airframe capacity.
  - HAL has completed 9+ Mk1A airframes awaiting engines (ADU/ThePrint search-level).
  - GE has delivered only 10 F404-IN20 engines against the 99 contracted in 2021, with 12 more promised by December 2026 (ThePrint, 18-Sep-2026 [V]).
- **Industrial corridors (UP, TN).** Combined commitments ~₹70,000 Cr, ~₹10,000 Cr grounded (Defence Secretary, as reported by Indian Masterminds; search-level). UPDIC (Uttar Pradesh): ~₹39,571 Cr of proposals and ~₹13,486 Cr grounded as of July 2026 (The Hindu via study site; search-level). Treat as indicative.

- Aerostructure players are still sub-scale and loss-making (Aequs) or low-ROCE (Dynamatic ~10%).

### 4.3 Imports and China
- India still spent 28% of modernisation expenditure on foreign sources in FY24 (PRS [V]); 27% per IDSA [V].
- **Supplier mix of imports, 2021–25** (SIPRI fact sheet, March 2026 [V] sipri.org fs_2603_at_2025.pdf):
  - Russia: **40%**, down from 70% in 2011–15 and 51% in 2016–20.
  - France: **29%**.
  - Israel: **15%**.
  - These shares are from Manorama citing SIPRI; Russia's 40% is confirmed in the SIPRI text [V].
- India took **48% of Russian arms exports** in 2021–25 (SIPRI [V]). That leaves a large fleet tail (Su-30MKI, T-90, T-72, MiG-29, Kilo, S-400) whose spares and engines (AL-31FP licence-built by HAL Koraput) are under sanctions risk. This is a structural MRO opportunity for HAL, BEL and private firms.
- **China** supplied 80% of Pakistan's arms imports in 2021–25 (SIPRI via Manorama [V]). Pakistan's imports rose 66%. This is the threat driver.
- **Component dependence.** BEL plans to eliminate imports of all modules and sub-systems except semiconductors within five years. Its current indigenous content is 78–80% (PL [V]). Semiconductors, FPGAs, high-end RF and seekers remain import-dependent; for drones, Chinese-origin motors, batteries and cameras are a recurring problem [K: 2024–25 Army drone order cancellations over Chinese parts, press].

### 6.1 PSU versus private: nomination, cost-plus, advances, working capital
- **Nomination vs competition.** DPSUs win most platform orders on nomination (strategic-partner logic, DRDO production-agency designation). Private players compete L1 under Buy (Indian-IDDM) or get developed through Make-I/II and iDEX.
  - Draft DAP 2026 (Feb-2026) reviews DRDO Development-cum-Production Partner selection "to ensure a level playing field". It adds spiral development and **five years of assured orders** for Make/iDEX projects and lifts IDDM indigenous content from **50% to 60%** (newsonair/MoD, 13-Feb-2026 [V]).
  - The AMCA prototype competition **excluded HAL**, shortlisting TASL, the L&T–BEL–Dynamatic consortium and Bharat Forge–BEML–Data Patterns. RFPs were issued in late May 2026 for five prototypes (~₹15,000 Cr budget). Award is expected Jan–Mar 2027 (idrw/livefist, search-level).
- **Advances.** Typical DAP payment terms [K: DAP 2020 Ch. II]:
  - 10–15% advance against a bank guarantee at contract signing.
  - Stage payments against milestones.
  - Balance of ~10–20% on delivery and acceptance.
  - DPSUs often receive larger advances on nominated contracts. When orders slow, advances fall and NWC balloons: BEL's NWC went 18 → 85 → 135 days FY24–FY26 [V]; ICRA notes BEL's "working capital intensity rose in FY2025 because customer advances fell with lower order inflows" (search-level).
- **Cost-plus.** Older cost-plus nomination contracts give a fixed profit margin (~10–12.5% on cost [K]). Newer fixed-price contracts let DPSUs keep the gains from localisation. That is why HAL's OPM rose from 22% (FY22) to 30–32% (FY24–FY26) and BEL's from 22% to 29% [V].
- **Private working capital.** Data Patterns' NWC/OI rose to 127% by Sep-2024 (ICRA, search-level). Screener shows DP NWC at 357 days in FY26 [V]. Private firms fund this with equity raises: DP's QIP 2024, Apollo's warrants, Paras' IPO 2021.

**Cyclicality.** The sector is **budget-cyclical**, not GDP-cyclical. Order inflow tracks the DAC/CCS calendar and the fiscal year-end rush: Q4 typically carries 35–45% of annual revenue at HAL and BEL. HAL's Q4FY26 revenue was ₹13,942 Cr out of ₹33,089 Cr (42%) (screener consol [V]). Stock cycles are driven by the AoN news-flow and by P/E, while the earnings path is smooth.

## 11. Global lens
- **Spending.** World military spending was US$2,887 bn in 2025 (+2.9% real; 2.5% of world GDP). The top five (US $954 bn, China $336 bn, Russia $190 bn, Germany $114 bn, India $92.1 bn) are 58% of the total. India is **5th**, overtaking the UK ($89.0 bn), with **3.2%** of world spend (SIPRI April 2026 [V]). India's spend is ~2.0% of GDP versus 3.4% for the US and 7.5% for Russia (SIPRI [V]; PRS [V]).
- **Arms trade.** Global major-arms transfers rose 9.2% between 2016–20 and 2021–25.
  - The US is **42%** of global exports (up from 36%); France 9.8%; Germany 5.7%.
  - European imports rose 210%, to 33% of the global total.
  - Source: SIPRI March 2026, via Manorama [V].
  - India's export share is ~0.2% (2016–23), so it is a price-taker niche exporter (BrahMos, Akash, Pinaka, radars, artillery shells, Dornier, ALH and aerostructures). The MoD says it exports to ~80–100 countries [K].
- **European rearmament (2024–26)** creates a competing pull for Western supply chains (forgings, energetics, electronics). It is also an export opportunity for Indian ammunition and parts makers. Shells and propellant from Indian private firms reached European buyers in 2024–25 [K: press].
- **Commercial aerospace.**
  - Airbus and Boeing together carry a backlog of ~15,000 aircraft (~8,700 Airbus + ~6,000 Boeing at end-2025 [K]), and both are capacity-constrained.
  - Indian carriers hold ~1,700+ aircraft on order (Air India 470 + 100; IndiGo ~900+ incl. A350s; Akasa ~200) [K].
  - **Boeing** sources **>US$1.25 bn/yr** from India through **>375 suppliers** (ADU, 15-Aug-2026 [V]). **Airbus** sources ~US$1.4 bn today and targets US$2 bn by 2030 (Goyal via PTI/Business Standard, Feb-2026; search-level; BS page returned 403).
  - Safran: LEAP rotating parts and HAL forgings (HAL–Safran LEAP turbine-ring forgings agreement, Jul-2026: Sahi [V]); MRO for LEAP and M88 in Hyderabad [K].
  - Indian Tier-2/3 players (Aequs, Dynamatic, MTAR, Azad, PTC, Bharat Forge, TASL) ride this "China+1 / cost-competitive hub" outsourcing. The US–India interim trade pact (Feb-2026) removed US tariffs on certain Indian aircraft parts (search-level).
- **Global valuation context.** The Indian defence index at ~56x P/E (Jul/Aug-2026; search-level) compares with Western primes at ~20–30x and the European rearmament winners (Rheinmetall, Hensoldt, Saab) at ~35–60x forward [K]. India carries a scarcity premium: few listed vehicles, PSU free floats of 25–49%.

## 12. Technology and disruption
- **Drones and counter-drone.** Sindoor's main tactical lessons were mass drones/loitering munitions (Harop, SkyStriker, Nagastra) and layered air defence (Akash, Akashteer C2 by BEL, S-400, L-70/Shilka upgrades).
  - EP-6 targets loitering munitions, kamikaze drones, counter-UAS and long-range missiles (Swarajya [V]).
  - BEL has supplied ~80% of its 2-kW laser DEW orders and is developing high-power microwave systems (PL [V]).
  - Zen (anti-drone), Paras (anti-drone optics and jammers) and Apollo (weapon electronics) are the listed private plays.
- **AESA radars and EW.** Domestic AESA (Uttam for LCA, Virupaksha for Su-30) shifts value to Indian TR-module makers (Astra Microwave, BEL, DP).
- **Engines** are the strategic gap: there is no Indian-designed fighter engine in service (the Kaveri was shelved for LCA). HAL's whole LCA cash cycle hostage to GE delivery is the vivid proof. The levers are F414 ToT (~80%), the Safran 120 kN co-development and the Kaveri derivative for UCAVs (Ghatak).
- **Space.** Commercial launch (Skyroot, Agnikul), small-satellite constellations and space-based ISR. The Space-Based Surveillance Ph-3 (52 satellites, ~₹26,968 Cr, CCS Oct-2024 [K]) is a pipeline for DP, Paras, Ananth and Alpha.
- **Semiconductors.** An FPGA/RF import dependence remains. ISM-linked fabs and OSATs (2024–26) are years away from defence-grade parts.
- **AI-enabled C4ISR, software-defined radios** (BEL SDR is an export driver: PL [V]), and simulation/digital twins (Zen).

5. **MRFA (114 Rafale)** contract structure and Indian partner workshare (Dassault–TASL fuselage line, Safran M88 MRO).
6. **FY27 capital-budget absorption** (₹2.19 lakh Cr) and RE versus BE in Feb-2027.
7. **BDL execution recovery** (Akash, Astra) versus imported seeker/radar bottlenecks.
8. **Export trajectory.** Whether FY26's ₹38,424 Cr spike, which included one-offs, sustains toward ₹50,000 Cr by FY29–30.
9. **Working-capital trend** at HAL (NWC 301 days) and BEL (135 days) as advances unwind.
10. **Valuation.** Index ~56x P/E with consensus EPS growth of ~15–20%. Any AoN-to-contract slippage or margin mean-reversion is the main de-rating risk.
11. **Russia-sanctions spares** (Su-30 and S-400 deliveries; the 27-Mar-2026 AoN for additional S-400 missiles), and the US CAATSA posture.
12. **Boeing/Airbus build rates.** The 737 MAX rate increase to 42+/month and the A320 rate-75 target drive Aequs, Dynamatic and MTAR volumes.

## A. The 3–5 defining forces

1. **Sovereign, ring-fenced domestic demand.** The capital acquisition budget is ₹1.85 lakh Cr in FY27, with 75% (₹1.39 lakh Cr) reserved for domestic industry. FY26 AoNs of ₹6.81 lakh Cr (3.9x YoY) were driven by post-Sindoor threat perception. The budget compounds at ~10–15%/yr nominal, and the domestic earmark has nearly doubled in share since FY21 (40% → 75%). So the **addressable** domestic pool grew ~25% CAGR FY21–FY27 [E]: 0.40×~₹90,000 Cr (FY21) ≈ ₹36,000 Cr → ₹1,39,100 Cr in FY27.
   - **Reasoning:** This is policy-locked demand, not cyclical, which is why order books are 3–10x revenue.
2. **Execution and supply-chain bottlenecks, not demand, set revenue.** HAL's LCA is >30 months late on GE engines. BDL's FY26 revenue fell 27% on imported seekers and radars. HAL's NWC rose from 37 to 301 days.
   - **Reasoning:** With order books this large, revenue = OB × execution rate, and the execution rate (HAL ~17%, BDL ~10%) is limited by imported long-lead items and acceptance testing. Whoever localises the bottleneck (engines, seekers, TR modules, superalloys) captures the value.
3. **DPSU incumbency vs private entry.** DPSUs hold ~76% of production and 80–90% nomination share (BEL), with strong ROCE (BEL 37%, HAL 32%). Policy is tilting toward private firms:
   - The AMCA competition excluded HAL.
   - Draft DAP 2026 calls for a level playing field for DRDO production partners, 5-year assured orders for Make/iDEX and 60% IDDM.
   - The private share of production rose from 22% to 24% in FY26, and private firms are 45% of exports.
   - **Reasoning:** Margin pools may shift from integrators to sub-system IP owners, and DPSU nomination rents face erosion over 5–10 years.
4. **Exports and global supply-chain integration.** Exports went ₹1,522 Cr (FY17) → ₹38,424 Cr (FY26). Boeing sources >US$1.25 bn/yr and Airbus ~US$1.4 bn/yr from India. European rearmament adds demand for ammunition and parts.
   - **Reasoning:** This diversifies away from the single-customer monopsony. Exports carry better payment terms (LCs, advances) and dollar revenue, and they make Tier-2/3 aero firms (Aequs, Dynamatic, MTAR) a different, global-cycle business.
5. **Valuation re-rating that already prices in the pipeline.** HAL went from 13x to ~35x P/E (Mar-2022 → Oct-2026), BEL ~20x → ~45x, and BDL ~21x → ~79x (peak 134x). The index is at ~56x.
   - **Reasoning:** Equity returns from here depend on EPS delivery (execution) more than on further multiple expansion. Sector stocks are highly sensitive to AoN and contract news-flow.

## B. Deep-dive themes

### B1. Operation Sindoor and the post-conflict procurement super-cycle
- **The operation.** Op Sindoor (7–10 May 2025) involved:
  - IAF strikes with BrahMos, SCALP, Crystal Maze-2, Rampage and Spice-2000.
  - Army use of SkyStriker loitering munitions and Excalibur rounds.
  - Air defence by Barak-8 and Akash (Swarajya, 18-May-2025 [V]).
- **Immediate procurement response.**
  - EP-6 of up to ~₹40,000 Cr (DAC, ~16-May-2025), with contracts capped at ₹300 Cr each, signed within 40 days and delivered within 1 year.
  - 13 contracts for ₹1,981.9 Cr in June 2025: IDD&IS counter-drone systems, low-level light radars, VSHORADS, loitering munitions, protective gear.
- **Structural response, FY26:**
  - AoN of ₹6.81 lakh Cr across 109 proposals, against ₹1.76 lakh Cr across 56 in FY25.
  - Contracts of ₹2.28 lakh Cr across 503 proposals.
  - Capital budget fully used (₹1.86 lakh Cr RE).
  - FY27 BE capital acquisition up 24.6%.
- **Calendar-2026 AoNs:** Rafale-MRFA (12-Feb, ₹3.60 lakh Cr package), S-400 missiles and MTA (27-Mar, ₹2.38 lakh Cr), air defence and anti-drone (3-Jul, ₹52,000 Cr), ALH and radars (7-Sep, ₹1.10 lakh Cr).
- **Listed beneficiaries.** BEL (QRSAM, Akashteer, EW, DEW), BDL (Akash, Astra, VSHORADS missiles), Zen and Paras (counter-drone), Data Patterns and Astra (radar/EW sub-systems), HAL (LCH, Su-30 sustainment, LCA).
- **Caveats.**
  1. A large part of the AoN value (MRFA, S-400, P-8I) is **import-heavy or foreign-OEM-led**. SIPRI explicitly flags India's "continued and probably increasing reliance on foreign suppliers" (up to 140 French combat aircraft, 6 German submarines) [V].
  2. Emergency procurement historically suffers delays: the CAG found 72% of Army EP contracts examined delivered late (The Wire, search-level).
  3. Equity prices front-ran the cycle: the index rose ~31% in May 2025.

### B2. The engine and imported long-lead bottleneck (why OB ≠ revenue)
- **HAL's case.** HAL's OB rose from ~₹1.89 lakh Cr to ₹2.55 lakh Cr in FY26, yet revenue grew only 7%. FY27 guidance is 10–12% (Sahi [V]).
  - GE contracted 99 F404 engines in 2021 (~US$716 mn [K]) but had delivered only 10 by Sep-2026 (ThePrint [V]).
  - HAL invoked liquidated damages on GE (search-level). It has also had to absorb IAF acceptance exemptions.
  - Inventory days at HAL rose from 527 (FY23) to 859 (FY26) and NWC days from 37 to 301 [V]. Cash is tied up in finished-but-undelivered airframes while customer advances are consumed.
- **BDL's case.** The order book (₹26,176 Cr) is 10.7x revenue, but FY26 revenue was ₹2,442 Cr (−27%) and Q1FY27 just ₹572 Cr. The bottleneck is imported radars and seekers (NiftyTrader [V]). Inventory days were 1,400 [V].
- **Policy fixes:**
  - The F414 deal with ~80% ToT, the Safran 120 kN engine, MIDHANI superalloys and titanium, and the 6th PIL (LRUs and raw materials for Su-30, LCA, T-72/T-90, MRSAM).
  - BEL's 5-year aim to stop importing modules and sub-systems except semiconductors (PL [V]).
- **Investor implication.** Model revenue from **capacity and inputs** (engines received, seekers available), not from order books. Watch inventory and advances as leading indicators.

### B3. PSU vs private economics: margins, working capital and the capital-light illusion
- **Margins are similar; returns are not.**
  - DPSUs report EBITDA comparable to private electronics firms (HAL 30%, BEL 29% vs DP 40%, Astra 28%, Apollo 29%, Paras 27%).
  - DPSU ROCE is far higher (BEL 37%, HAL 32% vs DP 22%, Astra 19%, Paras 17%, Apollo 16%).
  - The reason is **customer advances**. Nominated DPSU contracts come with large advances, which fund inventory and earn treasury income: HAL's other income is ~₹3,900 Cr, ~32% of PBT.
- **Private firms' working capital.** Private Tier-2 firms wait 215–290 days for receivables (often from DPSUs, not MoD directly). They carry 270–560 days of inventory and fund it with equity: NWC 266–357 days for DP, Astra, Apollo and Paras [V]. Their reported PAT margins (15–29%) overstate free cash flow. DP's NWC/OI reached 127% (ICRA, search-level).
- **Where the cycle is shifting.** As advances on legacy orders unwind and fresh orders slow, DPSU NWC days climb (BEL 18 → 135, HAL 37 → 301), compressing their cash-flow advantage.
- **Aerostructures.** Aequs and Dynamatic run export build-to-print, with low NWC (consol NWC 42–49 days) but low margins (7–11% consol EBITDA) and high capex/D&A. MTAR sits in between (EBITDA 20%, NWC 71 days in FY26), with Bloom Energy fuel-cell concentration.
- **Rule of thumb [E]:** Each ₹100 Cr of incremental revenue needs:

| Archetype | Incremental capital per ₹100 Cr revenue |
|---|---|
| DPSU primes | ~₹30–80 Cr of net working capital, after advances |
| Private electronics | ~₹70–100 Cr |
| Aero precision | ~₹10–15 Cr of NWC plus ~₹60–100 Cr of fixed capex |

## C. The revenue and profit equation (worked ₹ example)

**Core identities:**
- Revenue_t = Opening order book_t × Execution rate_t.
- Closing OB = Opening OB + Order inflow − Revenue.
- EBITDA = Revenue × EBITDA margin.
- PAT = (EBITDA − D&A + Other income) × (1 − tax).
- Other income ≈ Cash (funded by customer advances) × yield.

**Worked example: BEL FY27E [E].** Built from PL Capital's Q1FY27 disclosures [V] and screener FY26 [V].

| Step | Calculation | ₹ Cr |
|---|---|---|
| Opening OB, 1-Apr-2026 | Closing Q1 OB 72,260 + Q1 revenue 5,533 − Q1 inflow ~3,750 | ≈ **74,000** |
| FY27 revenue (guide +15%) | 27,480 × 1.15 | ≈ **31,600** |
| Implied execution rate | 31,600 ÷ 74,000 | ≈ **43%** |
| Order inflow (guide) | 25,000 base + 30,000 QRSAM | **55,000** |
| Closing OB, Mar-2027 | 74,000 + 55,000 − 31,600 | ≈ **97,400** (book-to-bill 1.74x; OB cover 3.1x) |
| EBITDA @28% (guide) | 31,600 × 28% | ≈ **8,850** |
| D&A | ~2% of revenue | ≈ −650 |
| Other income | ~₹13,000 Cr cash × ~7.5% + misc | ≈ +1,100 |
| PBT | | ≈ **9,300** |
| Tax @25.3% | | ≈ −2,350 |
| **PAT** | | ≈ **6,950** (+15% vs FY26 standalone 6,048) |

**Sensitivities:**
- If QRSAM slips to FY28, closing OB ≈ ₹67,400 Cr (cover ~2.1x), and FY28 revenue growth is at risk.
- Each 100 bp of EBITDA margin ≈ ₹316 Cr of EBITDA ≈ ₹236 Cr of PAT (~3.4% of PAT).
- The Q1FY27 gross margin fell 775 bp YoY to 45.5% on product mix (PL [V]). Mix is the main margin swing factor.

**Contrast, HAL FY27E [E]:**
- Opening OB ₹2,54,538 Cr × 14–15% execution ≈ ₹36,400 Cr revenue (+10%, matching the 10–12% guide).
- EBITDA at 30% ≈ ₹10,900 Cr. Other income ~₹3,500–3,900 Cr.
- PAT ≈ ₹10,000 Cr [E].
- HAL's execution rate is engine-gated. Every 10 LCA delivered ≈ ₹4,000–5,500 Cr of revenue at ~₹400–550 Cr per aircraft [E from ₹48,000 Cr / 83 and ₹62,370 Cr / 97].

## F. KPI glossary with ranges
See the §10 table for definitions, formulas and good/average/weak ranges. Additional terms:
- **AoN (Acceptance of Necessity):** DAC or competent-authority approval that starts a capital acquisition. Not a contract. AoN validity is typically 1–2 years, then it is re-accorded.
- **CCS:** Cabinet Committee on Security. Required for large contracts (>₹3,000 Cr [K]), so it is the last gate before signing (e.g. QRSAM).
- **CNC:** Contract Negotiation Committee. Sets price after L1 or nomination.
- **IDDM:** Indigenously Designed, Developed and Manufactured. ≥50% IC under DAP 2020; ≥60% proposed in DAP 2026.
- **IC (Indigenous Content):** Per DAP, base contract price less imported content, freight and foreign services, divided by base price.
- **ROH / MRO:** Repair and overhaul / maintenance, repair and overhaul. HAL's annuity segment.
- **LD (Liquidated Damages):** Penalty for late delivery, typically 0.5% per week up to a 10% cap [K: DAP]. Booked as a revenue deduction or provision.
- **Customer advance:** Typically 10–15% at signing against a bank guarantee. Funds DPSU inventory and generates other income.
- **Execution rate:** Revenue ÷ opening OB. Good: electronics 30–45%, platforms 12–20%, missiles 15–30%.
- **Book-to-bill:** Order inflow ÷ revenue. >1 means the backlog grows.
- **Other-income dependency:** Other income ÷ PBT. A >30% flag means earnings are inflated by advance-funded treasury income.
- **Export authorisation:** DDP-issued permission for export of munitions-list items. A leading indicator of export revenue.
