## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **CAGL's GNPA is recognised at 60+ dpd for group loans (90+ dpd for retail finance); most peers use 90+ dpd.** Q2 FY27 GNPA **1.79%** "(GL: 60+ dpd, RF: 90+ dpd)"; PAR 90+ (the like-for-like number) **1.11%** [V PPT]. Its Stage 2 is 16–60 dpd for GL (31–90 for RF) and Stage 1 is 0–15 dpd for GL [V PPT p.11]. Compare CAGL with peers on **PAR 90+**, never on GNPA. Screener's CAGL GNPA row (e.g. 2.18% Jun-26) is the company's 60+ dpd number.
2. **"Portfolio" in MFIN data is a bureau-based universe that shrank partly by RECLASSIFICATION, not only by run-off.** MFIN Q3 FY26: "the fall is accentuated by an approximate 5% shift (as a share of September 30, 2025 POS) from MFI to retail portfolios … majority … driven by banks" [V]. Q1 FY27: banks' portfolio −28.5% YoY, "most of which due to reclassification of microfinance portfolio to retail" [V]. MFIN has taken this up with the RBI, arguing all unsecured loans to households with income ≤₹3 lakh are microfinance by regulation [V]. So: **industry GLP −24% from peak overstates the true contraction in lending to low-income households; banks' "−30%" is part real, part relabelling.** Write it that way.
3. **Lender-type shares in MFIN releases are not internally consistent.** Banks' share: 33.2% (Mar-24), 33% (Jun-25), 31.4% (Sep-25), **26.7% (Dec-25), 32.7% (Mar-26), 25.3% (Jun-26)** [V, each release]. The Mar-26 figure (32.7%) looks like a different basis or a typo — Jun-26 says banks' 25.3% is "slightly lower than its share in the previous quarter". Use Jun-26 shares for the "today" pie and avoid charting the Mar-26 bank share without a footnote. Flagged in §H.
4. **Two different "₹3 lakh" and "50%" rules:** RBI's 2022 framework defines a microfinance loan as a collateral-free loan to a household with annual income ≤ ₹3 lakh, with total loan repayments capped at 50% of monthly household income (FOIR) [K: RBI Master Direction, 14-Mar-2022, effective 1-Apr-2022]. MFIN's guardrails are a **separate, SRO-level** overlay: lenders per borrower and a ₹2 lakh indebtedness cap **per borrower (client), counting microfinance plus unsecured retail loans** [V MFIN 25-Nov-2024]. Our primer calls it a "household indebtedness ceiling" — wrong unit (see §3.4).
5. **MFIN guardrail versions:** (a) **8-Jul-2024** directive: max **4** microfinance lenders per client; total microfinance loans per client capped at **₹2 lakh** [V MFIN Nov-24 release]; (b) **7-Oct-2024** underwriting directive on missed EMIs in bureau reports [V]; (c) **25-Nov-2024** tightening ("Guardrails 2.0"): lenders cut to **3** from 4; ₹2 lakh now **includes unsecured retail loans**; no lending to a client >60 dpd with >₹3,000 outstanding (was >90 dpd); only processing fee and credit-life insurance may be deducted from the sanctioned amount; boards to pass on efficiency gains in pricing [V]. **Effective dates** in market reporting: 4-lender cap including NBFC-MFI from 1-Jan-2025 and 3-lender cap from 1-Apr-2025 [K, not in the fetched release — verify before printing exact dates].
6. **"AUM growth 19.5%" at CAGL hides a shrinking group-loan book.** Group-loan (GL) AUM was ₹23,746 Cr in Sep-26 vs ₹24,227 Cr in Mar-26 (−2% YTD) while retail finance (RF) rose to ₹7,200 Cr (23.3% of AUM) [V PPT]. Management: GL fell "on account of write-offs and graduation" [V transcript]. Industry-level "microfinance growth" will increasingly understate household credit growth because borrowers graduate to individual loans (CAGL MD: "you may also have to take slightly different interpretation of microfinance growth going forward") [V].
7. **CAGL "credit cost" has three bases.** (i) Quarterly ₹ Cr (Q2 FY27 ₹299.2 Cr) = ECL provisions + write-off impact net of reversals; (ii) **non-annualised % of AUM** (Q2 0.99%; Q1 0.72%) [V PPT p.11]; (iii) annualised (H1 FY27 **3.43%** = 2.98% "normative" + 0.45% macro-prudential buffer) [V transcript]. FY guidance (3–4%) is annualised on average AUM. Do not annualise a quarter by ×4 without saying so.
8. **NIM and ROA denominators differ.** CAGL NIM 15.1% (Q2 FY27) is NII ÷ average **AUM/loan book**-type base [V PPT, basis not defined in deck]; ROA 5.8% is PAT ÷ average **total assets** [V]. Screener's "ROE 10.5%" is FY26 (not TTM). H1 FY27 company ROE is 23.8% [V]. Quote the period every time.
9. **Banks and SFBs are not microfinance pure-plays.** Bandhan's secured share is ~57% of loans (Jun-26, primer); Equitas' microfinance is ~1/8 of loans; Jana 73% secured [primer files]. Their P&L and P/B mix microfinance with housing, MSME, vehicles, gold. Use them for **market-share and funding** arguments, not as like-for-like valuation comps for CAGL. In Part 3, banks are a separate subgroup and never summed with NBFC-MFIs.
10. **"NBFC-MFI" is a regulatory category, not a description of who does microfinance.** NBFC-MFIs must hold ≥ **60% of total assets (net of intangibles)** in microfinance "qualifying assets" since **RBI circular 6-Jun-2025** (was 75% of total assets from Mar-2022; 85% of net assets from 2011) [V MFIN 9-Jun-2025]. Non-MFI NBFCs (e.g. IIFL Samasta was an NBFC-MFI; others like Svatantra are NBFC-MFIs; some large NBFCs lend microfinance) are capped at 25% of assets in microfinance [K]. CAGL's retail-finance push (23.3% of AUM) is only possible because of the 60% rule.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Indian microfinance just went through its worst credit cycle since Andhra Pradesh 2010 — and by mid-2026 asset quality is back to pre-crisis levels while the book is still ~24% below its peak.**
  - Industry portfolio (MFIN): **₹4,33,697 Cr (Mar-24 peak)** → ₹4,08,049 Cr (Sep-24) → ₹3,75,030 Cr (Mar-25, −13.5% YoY) → ₹3,53,233 Cr (Jun-25) → ₹3,39,510 Cr (Sep-25, −16.8% YoY) → **₹3,14,728 Cr (Dec-25 trough)** → ₹3,25,174 Cr (Mar-26, first QoQ rise in eight quarters, +3%) → **₹3,28,708 Cr (Jun-26, +1.1% QoQ, −6.9% YoY)** [V MFIN Micrometer releases]. Peak-to-trough −27.4%; Jun-26 is −24.2% below peak [E].
  - **PAR 31–180: 2.2% (Mar-24) → 6.3% (Mar-25) → 5.6% (Jun-25) → 2.0% (Mar-26) → 1.6% (Jun-26)** [V MFIN]. PAR 31–90 0.8% and 91–180 1.2% in Mar-26 [V].
  - **~50 lakh clients "have lost access to formal finance"** (MFIN, Sep-25 and Dec-25 releases) [V]; unique clients ~7.8 crore (Mar-24, Mar-25) → ~7.5 crore (Jun-25) [V].
- **The cycle had three overlapping causes (all verified in MFIN text): over-indebtedness, a funding freeze, and state politics.**
  - MFIN's Jun-2025 diagnosis of FY25: "heatwave, external incitement, concerns on overleveraging, Karnataka issue etc. affecting credit quality. Consequent to that funding to the sector also shrinked" [V].
  - The **funding winter** outlasted the credit problem: by Sep-25 PAR 31–90 was 1.09% and "98% of clients are within the MFIN Guardrails", yet the portfolio fell for a sixth straight quarter "due to continued funding squeeze" [V]. In Q3 FY26, medium MFIs disbursed only ₹995 Cr and small MFIs ₹503 Cr [V].
  - Funding to member NBFC-MFIs jumped **+91.4% YoY in Q1 FY27** — the thaw [V].
- **Market share is shifting from banks to NBFC-MFIs.**
  - NBFC-MFIs: 39.4% (Mar-24) → 39% (Jun-25) → 39.2% (Sep-25) → 42.1% (Dec-25) → 44.2% (Mar-26) → **44.3% (Jun-26)** [V].
  - Banks: 33.2% (Mar-24) → **25.3% (Jun-26)**; portfolio −30% YoY at Mar-26 and −28.5% at Jun-26 [V] (part reclassification — trap 2).
  - CAGL MD (Oct-26): "banks have significantly pulled back and MFIs have garnered better market share … NBFCs have also garnered better market share. SFBs have contracted a little" [V].
- **CreditAccess Grameen is the cleanest large survivor — and just posted its best quarter ever.**
  - Q2 FY27: AUM **₹30,946 Cr (+19.5% YoY)**; PAT **₹501.6 Cr (+298.7%)**, "highest quarterly PAT"; NIM **15.1%**; ROA **5.8%**, ROE **23.2%**; GNPA 1.79% (60+ dpd), PAR 90+ 1.11%, CRAR 26.9% [V press release/PPT].
  - **H1 FY27 PAT ₹995 Cr already exceeds full-year FY26 (₹778 Cr)** [V].
  - FY27 guidance **raised** on 22-Oct-2026: ROA 5.2–5.8% (was 4.0–4.8%), ROE 21–24% (was 16–20%), NIM 14.4–14.7% (was 12.8–13.2%), cost/income 29–30% (was 33–35%); AUM growth 20–25% and credit cost 3.0–4.0% retained [V PPT p.14].
  - CAGL ≈ **9.4% of the MFIN industry portfolio** (₹30,319 Cr AUM vs ₹3,28,708 Cr, Jun-26) and ~21% of NBFC-MFI portfolio [E: 30,319 ÷ (44.3% × 3,28,708 = ~1,45,600)]. (Caveat: CAGL AUM includes ~23% retail finance that may sit outside the microfinance bureau; on GL only, ₹24,061 Cr Jun-26 → ~7.3% of industry [E].)
- **Yet the stock is near its 52-week low.** ₹1,050 on 3-Nov-2026 (52-wk range ₹994–1,634); m-cap **₹16,851 Cr**; **P/E 10.6× TTM** (TTM PAT ₹1,587 Cr); **P/B ~1.9×** (Sep-26 equity ₹8,919 Cr) — vs ~3.1× and ₹24,700 Cr in our Jul-26 primer [V screener; primer]. The de-rating coincided with the promoter block sale (6-Aug), the drought/monsoon deficit, the repo hike (7-Oct) and a proposed cap on insurance commissions (CAGL: 25–30 bp ROA risk) [V transcript]; **no single cause is verified** — flag for the writer.

## 1. What the sub-sector does: products, value chain, where value is captured

### 1.1 Products

- **Core product: the JLG income-generation loan (IGL).** Collateral-free loan to a woman borrower who belongs to a joint-liability group (5–10 women) within a centre (kendra) that meets weekly/fortnightly; repayment in small equated instalments over 1–3 years [K; CAGL product table V].
  - CAGL IGL: **₹5,000–2,00,000, 18.00%–23.75% interest, 1–3 years**; other life-cycle loans (home improvement, water & sanitation, education, medical, festival, emergencies) ₹1,000–50,000 over 11–104 weeks [V PPT p.29].
  - Industry average loan disbursed per account (NBFC-MFIs) **₹45,024 in FY24** (+9.9% YoY) [V MFIN Q4 FY24]; CAGL average outstanding per GL borrower **₹62,300** (Sep-26) vs ₹55,500 a year earlier [V PPT p.43]. MFIN Q1 FY27: growth "driven primarily by NBFC-MFIs and higher loan sizes, reflecting a degree of consolidation around existing borrowers" [V].
- **SHG-bank linkage (NABARD model)** — the other half of Indian "microfinance": banks lend to self-help groups (10–20 members) under NABARD's SHG-BLP and DAY-NRLM; interest subvention for women SHGs [K]. Scale [K, approx, NABARD Status of Microfinance in India 2024-25 — verify]: ~1.4 crore SHGs savings-linked; bank loans outstanding to SHGs roughly ₹2.5–3 lakh crore. **SHG loans are NOT in MFIN's ₹3.29 lakh crore**; they are a parallel, state-subsidised channel. Treat as [K] and say so.
- **Graduation products (where the money is moving):** individual business loans (IBL), secured business loans (SBL, loan against property), affordable housing (AHL), two-wheeler loans, digital personal loans. CAGL ranges: IBL ₹1,000–3,00,000 at 18.00–24.00%; SBL ₹3–20 lakh at 16.00–23.50% over 2–15 years; AHL ₹2–24 lakh at 13.00–19.50% over 2–20 years; two-wheeler ₹40,000–1,20,000 at 22.50%; digital ₹10,000–1,00,000 at 23.75% [V PPT p.29].
- **Fee/insurance income:** credit-life and other insurance distribution, processing fees; MFIN guardrails allow only processing fee and credit-life insurance to be deducted from the sanctioned amount [V]. A proposed regulatory cap on insurance commissions would cost CAGL **~25–30 bp of ROA** (management, Oct-26) [V transcript].

### 1.2 Value chain (inputs → borrower)

```
Equity (PE/DFIs/IPO) + Debt (bank term loans, NCDs, ECBs, securitisation/DA, CGSMFI-backed bank loans)
        │  cost of funds ~9–12% for NBFC-MFIs (CAGL 9.1%); deposits ~7–8% for SFBs/banks [E]
        ▼
Lender: NBFC-MFI / SFB / bank (direct or via BC partners) / NBFC
        │  branch (6–7 loan officers per GL branch at CAGL) → centre meeting → group
        ▼
Credit bureau check (CRIF High Mark, Equifax, Experian, CIBIL) + MFIN guardrails (≤3 lenders, ≤₹2 lakh, no 60+ dpd >₹3,000)
        ▼
Borrower household (income ≤ ₹3 lakh; FOIR ≤ 50%) → livestock, petty trade, tailoring, agriculture-allied, consumption smoothing
        ▼
Weekly/fortnightly cash or digital repayment at the centre (CAGL: >99% of regular collections at centre meetings; digital 26.3%) [V]
```

- **Bank's role is dual:** the biggest *lender to* NBFC-MFIs (CAGL: bank term loans 61.5% of borrowings) and a direct microfinance *competitor* (25–33% of industry portfolio) [V PPT; V MFIN]. **Banks' pull-back is therefore felt twice by NBFC-MFIs** — as lost wholesale funding (FY25–FY26) and as less competition for borrowers (FY26–27).
- **Business correspondents (BCs):** IndusInd (Bharat Financial), IDFC First and others source through BC subsidiaries/partners; much of the "bank" microfinance book is BC-originated [K].
- **Credit bureaus:** the microfinance bureau is separate from the consumer bureau. MFIN's guardrails are enforced on bureau data; MFIN monitoring of Aug–Sep 2024 "shows good adherence" [V]; ~98% of clients within guardrails by Sep-25 [V].

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **At the lender with the lowest through-cycle credit cost and lowest cost-to-serve,** not the one with the highest yield. Yields are similar (CAGL GL 18–23.75%; industry roughly 22–26% for NBFC-MFIs [K/E]); dispersion is in credit cost (CAGL FY25 7.7% vs peers where FY25 losses exceeded 20% of equity) and opex (CAGL opex/AUM ~4.5–5.1% for a decade) [V PPT 10-yr table].
- **Funding cost is the second lever:** SFBs/banks fund at deposit rates (lower), but carry CRR/SLR and higher regulatory overheads; NBFC-MFIs pay 9–12% but can price freely post-2022 [K/E]. CAGL's 9.1% CoF is at the bottom of the NBFC-MFI range — its rating (AA-/Stable from CRISIL, ICRA, India Ratings) and 76–80+ lenders are part of the moat [V PPT; transcript "more than 80 lending institutions"].
- **Distribution is the asset:** 2,326 branches, >15,000 loan officers covering >4 lakh villages weekly [V PPT]. It is reused for graduation products (RF), which is why the retail push has a cost advantage.

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

- **Rural livelihoods and incomes.** Loans fund livestock, petty trade, tailoring and agriculture-allied activity; repayment depends on wage income and remittances, so monsoons, heatwaves and floods drive PAR. CAGL Q2 FY27: "temporary PAR increase in Aug-26/Sep-26 due to excess rainfall/heavy flooding across certain regions in Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, and Madhya Pradesh" and "minor increase in monthly PAR accretion rate due to lower wage income and customer migration on account of deficient monsoons" [V PPT p.9]. October 2026: borrowers in pockets of central Maharashtra "requesting for additional time for repayment, including restructuring options" [V transcript].
- **Monsoon 2026 is the near-term demand and credit risk.** All-India ~87% of LPA [V-snip]; MFIN in Sep-26 still called it "better-than-anticipated … other than Rajasthan, none of the top 10 states has seen a deterioration in the monsoon outlook since June" [V] — that was before the late-season deficit. North-East monsoon (Oct–Dec) expected normal, helping Tamil Nadu/south peninsula [V transcript].
- **Female workforce participation:** CAGL cites FWPR up from >24% (FY18) to >46% (FY24) [V PPT p.30] — more working women with independent income streams supports both demand and repayment capacity.
- **Formalisation and credit-history graduation:** CAGL adds ~35% new-to-credit customers each quarter (2.3 lakh new borrowers in Q2 FY27, 4.9 lakh in H1) [V]; ~6–8% of customers a year graduate to individual products [V transcript].
- **Pent-up demand after the credit crunch:** MFIN (Mar-26): "the good monsoon [2025] is creating pent up demand and Microfinance is well positioned to cater to this demand provided sufficient liquidity is available" [V]. Sep-26: "at present, a slowdown in demand does not appear to be a concern" [V].
- **Competition from digital/unsecured retail:** gold loans, personal loans and consumer durables reached the same households; MFIN brought unsecured retail inside the ₹2 lakh cap for that reason [V].

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 The regulators and the rulebook

- **RBI** regulates all lenders; **NBFC-MFI** is a licence category since Dec-2011 (Malegam Committee, Jan-2011) [K].
- **RBI Master Direction – Regulatory Framework for Microfinance Loans, 14-Mar-2022, effective 1-Apr-2022** [K; MFIN Nov-24 release refers to "RBIs (Regulatory Framework for Microfinance Loans) Directions, of March 2022" V]:
  - Microfinance loan = collateral-free loan to a household with annual income ≤ **₹3 lakh** (rural and urban alike; previously ₹1.25 lakh rural / ₹2 lakh urban) [K].
  - Loan repayment obligations (all loans of the household) ≤ **50% of monthly household income** [K].
  - **Pricing deregulated:** removal of the NBFC-MFI margin cap (10–12%) and interest-rate ceiling; board-approved pricing policy; no prepayment penalty; standard factsheet [K].
  - Harmonised across REs (banks, SFBs, NBFCs, NBFC-MFIs) [K].
  - NBFC-MFI qualifying assets ≥ 75% of total assets (changed in 2025) [V MFIN].
  - MFIN notes the regime moved "from being prescriptive to principles based and has placed greater responsibility on MFIN as SRO" [V].
  - MFIN (Sep-2026) argues the ₹3 lakh threshold, unchanged since 2022, "merit[s] review" as incomes and prices have risen [V].
- **MFIN (and Sa-Dhan) as SROs:** MFIN is "India's first RBI-recognized self-regulatory organization" [V]; guardrails are SRO directives binding on members (trap 5).

### 7.3 MFIN guardrails — text (25-Nov-2024 release) [V]

- "Number of microfinance lenders to a microfinance client reduced to 3 from 4."
- "The total indebtedness or outstanding loan amount of a microfinance client to be Rs. 2 lakh. However, it will include both microfinance loans and unsecured retail loans."
- "The existing Industry norm of not lending to a NPA client [overdue for >90 days] with an outstanding amount of >Rs.3,000 has been tightened to 'no lending to a delinquent client [overdue for >60 days] with an outstanding amount of >Rs.3,000'."
- "Interest rates to be closely reviewed by the Board of member REs to ensure that efficiency gains are passed on to clients. Further, other than processing fee and credit life insurance, no other charges can be deducted from the sanctioned loan amount."
- **Live debate (Oct-26):** CAGL analysts asked about customers "facing guardrails" who cannot be renewed because of lender-count/limit breaches [V transcript]; MFIN argues the ₹3 lakh income ceiling and reporting rules need review [V]. No 2026 revision of guardrails was found [gap].

### 7.4 State action

- **Karnataka (Feb-2025)** [K, verify]: ordinance targeting coercive recovery by microfinance lenders and moneylenders after reports of harassment and suicides; provisions included mandatory registration of unregistered lenders with district authorities, a bar on coercive recovery, and penalties up to 10 years' imprisonment and ₹5 lakh fine; loans by unregistered lenders made unenforceable. RBI-regulated entities were largely outside the registration requirement but the **on-ground effect** was a collections disruption across all lenders. MFIN cited "Karnataka issue" among FY25 stress causes [V]. CAGL's Karnataka GL AUM fell ~9.5% YoY to Sep-26 [V].

- **Bihar (Feb-2026)** [V]: bill passed; not applicable to RBI-regulated MFIs (s.2(2)); borrower-protection and fair-recovery provisions apply to all lenders; MFIN warned borrowers "not to fall prey to rumours".
- **Template:** Andhra Pradesh ordinance (Oct-2010) froze collections and destroyed the AP book [K]; Assam Microfinance Act (2020) and Assam relief scheme (2021) [K].

[V-snip]; validity to 31-Aug-2026 or ₹20,000 Cr. ~36 lakh borrowers expected to benefit [V-snip IANS headline]. MFIN called it "a significant policy development" [V].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **1970s–1990s — origins.** Grameen Bank (Bangladesh, 1976 project, 1983 bank) [K]; NABARD SHG-bank linkage pilot 1992 [K]; SEWA Bank (1974) [K].
2. **1999–2010 — the NBFC-MFI boom.** Grameen Koota (CAGL's ancestor, 1999, Vinatha Reddy) [primer]; SKS Microfinance IPO (Aug-2010) [K]; PE money; AP concentration.
3. **Oct-2010 — Andhra Pradesh crisis.** AP ordinance after borrower suicides; collections collapse; SKS lost most of its AP book; Malegam Committee (Jan-2011) → NBFC-MFI category (Dec-2011) with margin and interest-rate caps, household-income limits and a 2-lender cap [K].
4. **2014–2016 — licences.** Bandhan universal bank licence (Apr-2014; launched Aug-2015); 10 SFB in-principle licences (Sep-2015) incl. Ujjivan, Equitas, Janalakshmi, ESAF, Utkarsh, Suryoday, Disha/Fincare [K]. The industry's largest players left the NBFC-MFI category.
5. **Nov-2016 — demonetisation.** Cash shock; Karnataka, Maharashtra, UP stress; Janalakshmi nearly failed; CAGL FY17 credit cost 3.7% [V 10-yr table] and its Dutch parent infused ₹550 Cr in FY17 [V PPT p.24].
6. **2018–2019 — listings and M&A.** CAGL IPO (Aug-2018) [primer]; Spandana IPO (Aug-2019) [K]; Bharat Financial merged into IndusInd (Jul-2019) [K]; CAGL acquired Madura Micro Finance (2019–20) [primer].
7. **2020–2022 — COVID.** Moratoriums, restructuring; Assam stress; CAGL FY21 credit cost 6.7%, ROA 1.1%, GNPA 4.43% [V].
8. **Mar-2022 — harmonised framework** (₹3 lakh, 50% FOIR, pricing freedom) [K]. Growth resumed at 20–30%/yr; yields rose (CAGL yield 18.3% FY22 → 20.9% FY24) [V].
9. **FY23–FY24 — the boom.** Industry GLP ₹3.48 → ₹4.34 lakh crore (+24.5%) [V]; Fusion (Nov-2022) and Muthoot Microfin (Dec-2023) IPOs [K]; multi-lending deepened; South's share up from 28% to 31% [V].
10. **FY25–FY26 — the bust.** Heatwave (summer 2024), elections, over-indebtedness, "external incitement", Karnataka ordinance; guardrails; funding freeze; GLP −27% peak to trough; PAR 31–180 to 6.3%; ~50 lakh borrowers out [V]. Listed NBFC-MFIs lost ₹2,297 Cr in FY25 ex-CAGL (Fusion −1,225, Spandana −1,035, Muthoot −223, Satin +186) [E sum of V screener].
11. **FY26 H2–FY27 — recovery.** PAR 31–180 1.6% (Jun-26); QoQ growth from Mar-26; funding +91% YoY (Q1 FY27); CAGL record PAT; guarantees; but a weak 2026 monsoon and a rate hike [V].

**The cycle in one rhythm (CAGL's own decade, [V]):** credit cost 3.7% (FY17, demonetisation) → −0.3% (FY18) → 1.3% → 3.0% (FY20) → 6.7% (FY21, COVID) → 4.6% → 2.4% → 2.1% (FY24) → **7.7% (FY25)** → 6.7% (FY26) → ~3.4% annualised (H1 FY27). **Three crises in ten years; the "normal" year is the exception.** Ten-year average credit cost ≈ **3.7%** [E: simple mean of FY17–FY26 = (3.7−0.3+1.3+3.0+6.7+4.6+2.4+2.1+7.7+6.7)/10 = 3.79%]. Ten-year average ROA 3.4%, ROE 13.9% (company "cross-cycle performance") [V PPT p.18].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert leads with)

1. **The credit cycle is the business (2024–26 crisis and its normalisation).** Growth and losses are 12–18 months out of phase. Industry GLP +24.5% in FY24 → PAR 31–180 from 2.2% to 6.3% in a year → GLP −27% peak to trough → PAR 1.6% by Jun-26 [V MFIN]. CAGL credit cost 2.1% → 7.7% → 6.7% → ~3.4% (H1 FY27 ann.) [V]. The writer should frame every number by "where in the cycle".
2. **Over-indebtedness and the guardrails (MFIN Jul-2024 / Nov-2024).** The cause of the bust was the number of lenders per borrower and the stacking of unsecured retail loans on top of microfinance; the cure was the SRO's 3-lender / ₹2 lakh / 60-dpd rules [V]. They cap growth per borrower (hence "higher loan sizes … consolidation around existing borrowers" [V]) and favour sole/primary lenders — CAGL is the sole MFI lender to 45% of its customers [V].
3. **Funding liquidity is the transmission mechanism.** Banks fund NBFC-MFIs (CAGL 61.5% of borrowings) and compete with them. When banks stopped lending in FY25–26 the portfolio kept shrinking even after PAR normalised ("the sector continues to face liquidity issues" — MFIN, Mar-26) [V]. The thaw (+91.4% funding YoY in Q1 FY27), CGSMFI 2.0 and the Feb-2025 risk-weight rollback are what restarted growth [V/K]. Scale players with AA- ratings and ECB access (CAGL) were funded throughout; small MFIs were not.
4. **State politics and repayment culture.** Karnataka (Feb-2025), Tamil Nadu (2025), Bihar (Feb-2026) — coercion-prevention laws arrive whenever stress meets an election. The risk is behavioural (willingness to pay), correlated across lenders and uninsurable. CAGL's Karnataka GL book −9.5% YoY and TN −5.6% YoY (Sep-26) show the cost even for the best operator [V].
5. **Regulatory loosening of the business model (QA 60%, pricing freedom, risk weights).** RBI's 2022 framework freed pricing; the Jun-2025 QA cut to 60% lets NBFC-MFIs hold up to 40% non-microfinance assets [V]. This is the licence for CAGL's retail push (RF 5.9% → 23.3% of AUM in 18 months) and Project "Shakti" [V/primer].
6. **Rural income shocks: the 2026 drought.** ~87% of LPA, East & NE driest since 1901 [V-snip]. Microfinance PAR has historically been resilient to drought (CAGL: "historically, we've not seen this kind of drought … our data at least indicates that we've not had any significant impact because of drought") [V transcript], but migration and lower wage income are already visible in PAR accretion [V].
7. **Interest rates and NIMs.** RBI cut to 5.50% (Jun-2025) and hiked back to 5.50% (Oct-2026) after a further cut [K/V-sib]. CAGL's CoF fell from 9.8% (FY25) to 9.1% (Q2 FY27) while yields rose to 22.3% → record 15.1% NIM [V]; CAGL is now giving back 50 bp of pricing (Oct-26) and maybe 50 bp more [V]. Risk-adjusted NIM, not headline NIM, is what to track.
8. **Structural shift from banks to NBFC-MFIs, and from JLG to individual/secured.** Banks 33.2% → 25.3% share; NBFC-MFIs 39.4% → 44.3% [V]; SFBs pivot to secured. Simultaneously the borrower graduates: microfinance growth "range bound" while "retail growth" rises (CAGL MD) [V]. The industry is becoming "rural retail finance".

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Lender equation:** PAT ≈ [AUM × (Yield − CoF × Borrowings/AUM) + Fee income − Opex − Credit cost × AUM] × (1 − tax).
Equivalent ROA tree: ROA = NIM + other income/AUM − opex/AUM − credit cost/AUM, then × (AUM/assets) × (1 − t); ROE = ROA × assets/equity.
Volume driver: AUM = active borrowers × average outstanding per borrower. CAGL: 44.98 lakh × ₹68,800 = ₹30,946 Cr [V PPT p.43].

### C.1 Worked example — CAGL Q2 FY27, annualised, per ₹100 of average AUM [E from V P&L; avg AUM (30,319 + 30,946)/2 = ₹30,633 Cr]
- Interest on loans: 1,777.1 × 4 / 30,633 = **23.2**
- Other interest/DA income: (23.6 + 30.2) × 4 / 30,633 = **0.7**
- Finance cost: 553.0 × 4 / 30,633 = **−7.2**
- **NII = 16.7** (company NIM 15.1% is on a larger base — trap 8)
- Non-interest income: 74.0 × 4 / 30,633 = **+1.0**
- Opex (254.5 + 110.4 + 16.7) × 4 / 30,633 = **−5.0**
- **PPOP = 12.7**
- Credit cost 299.2 × 4 / 30,633 = **−3.9**
- PBT = **8.8**; tax at 25% = −2.2; **PAT = 6.6 per ₹100 of AUM**
- Assets ≈ 1.11× AUM → ROA ≈ **5.9%** (company 5.8%); equity ≈ 0.29× AUM → ROE ≈ **23%** (company 23.2%).

### C.2 Worked example — one ₹50,000 JLG loan, 2-year tenure, weekly repayment [E composite]
- Average outstanding over life ~₹27,000 (amortising).
- Interest at 22% on average outstanding ≈ ₹5,900/yr; processing fee ~1% ≈ ₹500 once.
- Funding at 9.2% on ~78% debt-funded ≈ −₹1,950/yr.
- Opex at 5% of average AUM ≈ −₹1,350/yr (≈ 1 LO handles ~300 borrowers [E: 44.98 lakh ÷ 15,089 LOs = ~298 V-derived]).
- Credit cost: normal 2% ≈ −₹540/yr; stress 8% ≈ −₹2,160/yr.
- **Pre-tax profit per loan: ~₹2,050/yr normal; ~₹440/yr in a stress year; negative if credit cost >~15% of AUM** [E].

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed

1. **The borrower base shrank by ~1.7 crore, not "~50 lakh".** MFIN's own count is 6.06 crore unique borrowers (Aug-26) vs 7.8 crore (Mar-24), and loan accounts fell from 14.9 crore to 9.27 crore. Some of this is reclassification to retail bureaus and the end of multi-lending (accounts per borrower ~1.9 → ~1.5), but the "financial inclusion reversal" is roughly 3× larger than the oft-quoted 50 lakh. This is the most striking new number for the report's human-cost section.
2. **Even CAGL breached lender covenants in the bust.** At Mar-25 it missed PAR, ROE, collection-efficiency, write-off-ratio and self-sufficiency covenants on seven facilities (BoB, HSBC, Citi, DFC, StanChart, an ECB fund, Maanaveeya) and needed waivers again in FY26. Funding stress at weaker MFIs was therefore not just "banks being cautious" — covenants mechanically froze lines. The covenant table is a primary-source exhibit for the funding-transmission theme.
3. **The insurance cap is a related-party story too.** CAGL's distribution income from **CreditAccess Life Insurance — a fellow subsidiary under the same Dutch parent — nearly doubled to ₹51.6 Cr in FY26**; total fee & commission income is ₹136 Cr. IRDAI's proposed 2%/2.5% credit-life commission cap (23-Sep-2026) therefore hits both CAGL's ROA (25–30 bp, management) and the group's insurance economics. The share-price leg of 17–29 Sep lines up with the draft.
4. **Drought risk is concentrated in CAGL's states, not the industry's.** IMD shows Central India normal (97%) but the **South Peninsula at 76% of LPA (4th lowest since 1901)**; Karnataka + Tamil Nadu are 47% of CAGL's AUM but a much smaller share of the industry (East & NE 36.6%). The first stress has appeared in central Maharashtra (6 districts; restructuring requests; early-bucket PAR rising from 0.7% to 1.0%), and MFIN is already rebutting farmer-death attributions in Nanded and Latur. Writers should frame "drought" as a **CAGL-specific geographic risk** rather than an industry-wide one.
5. **CAGL's dominance is local, not national.** It holds ~21–22% of Karnataka and Maharashtra microfinance but only 7.3% of India; Bandhan's JLG book (₹35,000 Cr) is still larger than CAGL's GL book (₹23,746 Cr). Market-share gains from bank retreat are therefore capped by state politics in its two home states. The top-8 lenders hold only ~36% of the industry [E].
6. **AUM growth is now ticket-size growth.** CAGL: AUM +19.5% vs borrowers +1.3%; average GL outstanding ₹55.5k → ₹62.3k. Fusion: AUM +14% vs MFI borrowers −25%; Muthoot: AUM +22% vs clients −5%. With the ₹2 lakh per-borrower cap binding ("Many have hit the limit" — CAGL CEO), the industry's next growth constraint is the guardrail itself and the ₹3 lakh household-income ceiling MFIN wants revised.
7. **Pricing power has turned into a policy choice.** CAGL is cutting rates (50 bp, maybe 50 bp more) from a record 15.1% NIM, while Spandana prices at 24.9% yield on 12.3% CoF. The cross-sectional spread of funding costs (Satin 8.3% adjusted, CAGL 9.1%, Fusion 9.35% marginal, Muthoot 9.93%, Spandana 12.3%) is now the main differentiator — and both CAGL and Muthoot Microfin are AA-/Stable (CRISIL), narrowing CAGL's funding moat.
8. **The "1.34% peak delinquency" in our primer is a mislabel.** 1.34% is CAGL's Q3 FY26 quarterly (non-annualised) credit cost; the monthly PAR 15+ accretion peaked at ~0.95% (Dec-24), troughed at 0.07% (Mar-26) and has since **tripled to 0.26% (Sep-26)** — still low, but the direction has turned.
