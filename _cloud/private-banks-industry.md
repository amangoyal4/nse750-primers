## 1. What the sub-sector does, and where value is captured

A private sector bank is a licensed deposit-taker owned by private shareholders (not the Government of India), regulated by the RBI under the Banking Regulation Act 1949. It borrows short-dated, mostly retail money (current accounts, savings accounts, term deposits), lends it out for longer at higher rates, and earns fees on payments, cards, distribution of third-party products (insurance, mutual funds), forex and transaction banking.

**The value chain of a rupee of deposit (concept, [E]):**
1. **Liability franchise (gathering money):** branches (HDFC ~9,500+, Axis 6,295 incl. extension counters [V Axis IP], IndusInd 3,137 [V]), digital apps, payroll/corporate salary accounts, government business, NRI remittance corridors (Federal, SIB, CSB in Kerala). Value captured here = the gap between what the bank pays depositors and what the next-cheapest funding would cost. CASA (current + savings) is the prize: current accounts pay ~0%, savings 2.5–3% (after 2025 cuts) vs term deposits 6–7%.
2. **Balance-sheet transformation:** maturity and liquidity transformation, governed by CRR (3% of NDTL since 29 Nov 2025 [V]), SLR (18%) and LCR (≥100%).
3. **Asset origination:** retail (mortgages, auto, personal loans, cards, gold, microfinance), MSME/business banking, corporate. Value captured = yield minus funding cost minus expected loss.
4. **Underwriting and collections:** the cost line that separates winners from losers over a cycle (credit cost ranges from ~0.3% of loans at KVB/Federal to >2% at IDFC First/Bandhan in stress years).
5. **Fee/franchise layer:** cards and payments (Axis ~38% of UPI payer volume, 13.4% of cards in force [V]), wealth (Axis Burgundy AUM ₹7.54 lakh Cr [V]), bancassurance, subsidiaries (HDFC Life/ERGO/AMC/HDB/Securities; ICICI Pru Life/Lombard/AMC/Securities; Kotak Securities/AMC/Life/Prime [V Kotak PR]).

**Where the profit pool sits:** of the 19 banks' combined FY26 standalone pre-provision profit, roughly two-thirds comes from net interest income and one-third from fees/other income [E from screener: combined NII ₹4.20 lakh Cr vs other income ₹1.81 lakh Cr, FY26]. Concentration: HDFC Bank + ICICI Bank earned ₹1.25 lakh Cr of the group's ₹1.97 lakh Cr standalone PAT (63%) [V screener].

## 7. Regulation and policy (the RBI shapes the P&L)
- **Repo cycle:** cuts Feb-25 (6.25%), Apr-25 (6.00%), Jun-25 (5.50%, −50 bp, stance to neutral, CRR cut announced), Dec-25 (5.25%) — 125 bp in total; holds through Aug-26; **7 Oct 2026: hike of 25 bp to 5.50%**, stance "calibrated tightening"; SDF 5.25%, MSF/Bank Rate 5.75%; FY27 CPI 5.2% (Q3 6.0%, Q4 5.7%), GDP 7.1%; CPI Aug-26 4.8%; next MPC 2–4 Dec 2026 (SCC Online 9 Oct 2026; Business Standard 7 Oct 2026) [V].
- **CRR:** 4% → 3% in four 25 bp steps effective 6 Sep, 4 Oct, 1 Nov, 29 Nov 2025; ~₹2.5 lakh Cr released (announced 6 Jun 2025) [V-secondary]. Each 100 bp of CRR on ₹100 of deposits frees ₹1 to lend at ~8–9% ≈ +5–7 bp NIM [E].
- **External benchmark (EBLR) pricing:** since Oct 2019 retail and MSME floating loans must be linked to an external benchmark (mostly repo). ICICI: 57% of domestic loans repo/EBLR-linked, 30% fixed, 13% MCLR [V ICICI IP]. Hence rate cuts hit loan yields within a quarter while deposit costs fall over 4–6 quarters (TDs reprice on maturity) — the mechanism behind FY26 NIM compression, and the reverse in a hiking cycle.
- **FCNR(B) swap window:** RBI circular dated 8 Jun 2026 (FMOD.MAOG.No.S-56/01.06.016/2026-27): fresh FCNR(B) deposits of 3–5 years swappable with RBI at concessional cost; window closed early — deposits mobilised up to 31 Aug 2026 (press release 14 Aug 2026), swaps till 11 Sep [V Yes Bank/HDFC filings + BS]. Trigger: rupee record low of 96.96/$ on 20 May 2026 amid West Asia war, oil spike, FPI selling [V-secondary BS]. 10 Oct 2026: RBI special dollar window for oil marketing companies and a new CRR-like requirement on some FX transactions (Bloomberg) [V-secondary].
- **Unsecured lending:** Nov 2023 risk-weight increase of 25 pp on consumer credit (PL, cards) and bank loans to NBFCs [K RBI circular 16 Nov 2023]; NBFC-loan risk weights restored and MFI loans eased in Feb–Apr 2025 [K].
- **LCR:** revised framework (Apr 2025) adds 2.5 pp run-off on internet/mobile-banking-enabled retail deposits; effective 1 Apr 2026 [K, RBI circular 21 Apr 2025].
- **ECL:** final directions issued 27 Apr 2026, effective 1 Apr 2027; Stage 1/2/3 forward-looking provisioning; comparatives from period ending 31 Mar 2028; one-time capital impact phase-in reportedly to 31 Mar 2031 (single source) (KPMG May 2026; ICRA; Taxguru) [V-secondary]. Banks are building buffers now (Axis one-time ₹2,001 Cr "West Asia" standard-asset provision in Q4 FY26 [V]; ICICI contingency provisions ₹13,100 Cr [V]).
- **Ownership rules:** promoter caps 26% after 15 years (2016 licensing guidelines), any acquisition ≥5% needs RBI approval; foreign investment cap 74% (49% automatic) [K]. RBL/ENBD crossed into "foreign-owned private bank" territory with RBI approval.
- **Governance directions:** RBI (Commercial Banks – Governance) Directions, 2025 [V cited in HDFC filing]; CEO tenure cap 15 years for promoter-CEOs, 70-year age cap [K].
- **Deposit insurance:** DICGC ₹5 lakh per depositor [K]; premium 12 paise per ₹100 [K]; risk-based premium proposals 2025-26 [K, unverified status].
- **PSL:** 40% of ANBC to priority sectors; shortfalls go to RIDF at low yields — a drag on new and corporate-heavy banks (HDFC post-merger) [K].

## 10. Global lens [K unless marked]
- Indian large private banks earn RoA 1.8–2.5% vs ~1.0–1.3% for large US banks (JPMorgan ~1.3–1.5%), ~0.5–0.8% for European banks and ~0.3–0.6% for Japanese megabanks — because Indian NIMs (3–4.5%) are 1.5–2× global and credit costs are now low [K, company annual reports 2024-25].
- Price-to-book: HDFC 1.90×, ICICI 2.78×, Kotak 3.23× [V screener] vs JPMorgan ~2.4–2.6×, European banks ~0.8–1.2×, Japanese megabanks ~1.0–1.3× [K, approximate 2025-26].
- Size: HDFC Bank (~US$455 bn of standalone assets [E: screener ₹43.65 lakh Cr at Mar-26 ÷ ₹96/$]) ranks outside the global top 30 by assets but in the top 10–15 by market value [K].
- Foreign strategic buyers (SMBC, ENBD, Fairfax, Blackstone) are paying 1.3–2.0× book for Indian banks they view as structurally higher-RoA franchises [E: RBL deal at ₹280 vs BV ~₹225 pre-deal ≈ 1.2×; IDBI ₹81 vs BV ~₹63 ≈ 1.3×; Federal ₹227 vs BV ~₹150 at announcement ≈ 1.5×].

## 11. Technology and disruption
- UPI dominates retail payments; banks earn little from UPI directly (zero MDR) but it drives CASA engagement; Axis ~38% of UPI payer PSP volume [V].
- Digital deposits are "flightier" — hence the LCR run-off add-on from 1 Apr 2026 [K].
- Account Aggregator, GST and UPI data enable MSME cash-flow lending (business banking growth 18–28% at the leaders) [V].
- AI in collections/underwriting: IndusInd "50+ ML models evaluate ~0.5 mn loans monthly" [V IndusInd IP].
- Fintech-originated small-ticket PLs: 90–179 dpd ~4% (FSR Jun 2026, secondary) — the risk channel through bank–fintech partnerships [V-secondary].
- Wealth/fee migration: households moving savings to MFs (SA share 28.7%) — banks respond by owning AMCs (ICICI Pru AMC listed Dec 2025 [K primer]; Kotak AMC; HDFC AMC) and distributing MFs.

## A. The defining forces (3–5), with reasoning

**Force 1 — The deposit war: cheap money is the scarce input, and it is getting scarcer.**
Evidence: SA share of system deposits fell from 34.6% (Mar-22) to 28.7% (Mar-26); term deposits rose from 55.2% to 61.6% of the total; 46.3% of term deposits are ≥₹1 Cr (bulk, rate-sensitive) (RBI BSR-2 Mar-26) [V]. HDFC Bank's CASA ratio slid from ~38% (Sep-23) to 34% (Mar-26) to 32.3% (Jun-26) and 31.6% (Sep-26) [V HDFC Q2 update; secondary for Sep-23]. IndusInd 31.3% → 29.4%; Yes 35.1% → 32.7% → 30.0% (Sep-26, 31.7% ex-FCNR); RBL avg CASA 25.2%; CSB 23% → 19% [V]. The exception: IDFC First (50.8%) and Federal (+18% CASA growth, 32.2%) — both paying up on savings rates or NRI flows. The FCNR(B) window (US$11.5 bn at HDFC alone) is a policy-made deposit supply — it temporarily flatters Q2 deposit growth (HDFC +18.8%, Yes +19.5%, IDFC +25.9% incl. FCNR) [V]. Reasoning: in a bank, the loan side is easy to grow and hard to price; the deposit side decides both the speed and the margin. Post-merger HDFC (LDR ~110% in 2023 → ~95–97% now) [K/V] is the purest case.

**Force 2 — The rate cycle whiplash: 125 bp of cuts compressed margins; the first hike (Oct 2026) starts the reversal.**
Evidence: Axis NIM 3.80% (Q1 FY26) → 3.62% (Q4) → 3.46% (Q1 FY27), "cycle bottom" per management [V]; HDFC 3.26% on total assets, its lowest on record [V-secondary]; Kotak 4.65% → 4.53% [V]. But banks with costlier deposits that reprice gained as TD costs fell: Federal NIM +39 bp to 3.33%, KVB +48 bp to 4.34%, Karnataka +38 bp to 3.20%, IDFC +25 bp to 5.96%, Yes +20 bp to 2.7%, IndusInd 3.46 → 3.57% [V]. Mechanism: ~57% of ICICI's domestic loans reprice with repo within ~a quarter (EBLR), 30% are fixed, 13% MCLR [V]; deposits reprice only as TDs mature (69.8% of system TDs are 1–3 years) [V]. Hence cuts hurt EBLR-heavy, CASA-rich banks first; high-TD, fixed-rate-asset banks (gold, MFI, vehicle) benefit when TD costs fall. A hike reverses the order. The 7 Oct 2026 hike to 5.50% and "calibrated tightening" stance [V] plus RBI CPI path of ~6% in Q3 FY27 make H2 FY27 a margin-expansion window for EBLR-heavy banks — offset by the FCNR swap cost and a renewed TD price war.

**Force 3 — The unsecured and microfinance credit cycle is ending; the ECL regime is coming.**
Evidence: MFIN PAR 31–180 2.0% (Mar-26) vs 6.3% (Mar-25), industry MFI book −12% YoY (to Jun-26) [V-secondary]. Reasoning: FY26 earnings at the mid-size banks were crushed by credit costs (Bandhan PAT −55%, IndusInd −65%); FY27 profits jump as provisions fall (IDFC Q1 PAT +132%, RBL Q2 +170%) — the "recovery trade".

**Force 4 — Ownership and leadership reset: foreign strategic capital and new CEOs at half the sector.**

## C. The revenue/profit equation (worked ₹ example, ILLUSTRATIVE)
`PAT = [Avg interest-earning assets × NIM + Fee & other income − Operating expenses − Provisions] × (1 − tax rate)`
`RoA = NII/assets + Other income/assets − Opex/assets − Provisions/assets − Tax/assets`; `RoE = RoA × (Assets ÷ Equity)`.
Worked example — a ₹1,00,000 Cr-asset mid-size private bank:
- Interest-earning assets 92% = ₹92,000 Cr; NIM 3.8% → NII ₹3,496 Cr (3.50% of assets).
- Fees and other income ₹1,500 Cr (1.50%).
- Net total income ₹4,996 Cr; opex ₹2,500 Cr (2.50% of assets; cost-to-income 50%).
- PPOP ₹2,496 Cr (2.50%).
- Loans ₹70,000 Cr × credit cost 0.6% = ₹420 Cr (0.42% of assets).
- PBT ₹2,076 Cr; tax 25.2% = ₹523 Cr; **PAT ₹1,553 Cr → RoA 1.55%**.
- Equity 11% of assets (₹11,000 Cr; leverage 9.1×) → **RoE ~14.1%**.
Sensitivities: NIM ±10 bp → PAT ±₹69 Cr (±4.4%); credit cost +50 bp → PAT −₹262 Cr (−17%); CASA +5 pp (₹4,250 Cr of deposits moved from TD at 6.5% to SA at 2.75%) → interest saved ₹159 Cr pre-tax (+~₹119 Cr PAT, +7.7%) [E arithmetic].

## DEEP-DIVE WORKING NOTES (analysis for the report)

### DD1. HDFC Bank's LDR arithmetic after the merger [E unless marked]
- At merger (1 Jul 2023) HDFC Ltd brought ~₹6+ lakh Cr of loans funded mostly by bonds and borrowings; the combined LDR jumped to ~110% [K]. Borrowings on the standalone balance sheet: ₹6.62 lakh Cr (Mar-24) → ₹5.48 lakh Cr (Mar-25) → ₹4.89 lakh Cr (Mar-26) [V screener; consolidated ₹7.31 → ₹6.35 → ₹5.88 lakh Cr]; deposits ₹23.80 → ₹27.15 → ₹31.05 lakh Cr [V].
- Each ₹1 lakh Cr of bonds retired and replaced by deposits: bond cost ~7.5–8% vs incremental deposit cost ~6.5–7% (TD) → saves ~₹1,000 Cr/yr pre-tax, but only if funded with TDs; funded with CASA (~3%) it saves ~₹4,500 Cr. The problem: incremental deposits have come mostly as TDs (Sep-26: TD +22.8% vs CASA +10.8%) [V].
- CASA ratio path: ~38% (Sep-23, secondary) → 34.2% (Mar-26: 10,603/31,053) → 32.3% (Jun-26) → 31.6% (Sep-26) [V]. A 6.5 pp fall on ₹33 lakh Cr deposits = ~₹2.1 lakh Cr shifted from CASA to TD; at a ~3.5–4 pp rate gap that costs ~₹7,500–8,500 Cr a year pre-tax ≈ 17–19 bp of NIM on ~₹45 lakh Cr assets [E].
- PSL burden: the merged mortgage book enlarged ANBC; PSL shortfall → RIDF deposits at low yield [K].
- Result: NIM on total assets 3.26% Q1 FY27 vs ~4.1% pre-merger standalone (FY23) [K]; RoA ~1.8% (vs ~2.0% pre-merger) [E/K].
- FCNR effect: US$11.5 bn (₹1.10 lakh Cr) of FCNR(B) ≈ 3.3% of deposits; ~half on-lent abroad → inflates both sides; swap cost borne largely by RBI concession [V/E].
- Market verdict: P/B de-rated from ~2.7× (2023, [K]) to 1.90× [V]; FII ownership 48.8% → 39.4% in five quarters [V]; DIIs 35.8% → 43.4% [V].

### DD2. NIM through the rate cycle — the EBLR asymmetry [E, ILLUSTRATIVE]
Model bank A (CASA-rich, EBLR-heavy, like ICICI/HDFC/Axis/Kotak): ₹100 of loans, 57% EBLR (reprices within ~3 months), 30% fixed, 13% MCLR (reprices over ~12 months); deposits 40% CASA (SA rate cut ~25–50 bp with a lag; CA 0%), 60% TD (reprice as they mature — ~35% per year).
- After 125 bp of repo cuts: loan yield falls 0.57×125 + 0.13×(~60) ≈ 79 bp within a year; deposit cost falls: SA −50 bp × 25% weight = −12.5 bp; TD −100 bp × 60% × 35% repriced in year 1 ≈ −21 bp → ~−34 bp in year 1, ~−55 bp by year 2 → **NIM compresses ~40–45 bp in year 1, recovers ~20 bp in year 2** (Axis −34 bp YoY observed; HDFC 3.38→3.26) [V observations].
Model bank B (TD-heavy, fixed-rate assets — gold, MFI, vehicle; like KVB, Federal, IDFC, Bandhan): 25% EBLR loans → yield falls ~35–40 bp; deposits 70% TD reprice → CoD falls 50–60 bp by year 2 → **NIM expands** (Federal +39 bp, KVB +48 bp, Karnataka +38 bp, IDFC +25 bp YoY) [V].
- CRR cut (4%→3%): on ₹100 deposits frees ₹1 → earns ~8–9% on loans/~6.5% on G-secs → +5–8 bp NIM, partially offsetting [E].
- Hike of 25 bp (7 Oct 2026): bank A loan yield +14 bp within a quarter; deposit cost +0 initially → +10–14 bp NIM over two quarters before TD repricing catches up [E]. RBI's CPI path (Q3 FY27 6.0%) implies further hikes are possible (statement: rate cuts "off the table") [V].
