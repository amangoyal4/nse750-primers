## 1. What the sub-sector does — product, process, value chain

**Product.** Cement is a hydraulic binder: limestone (CaCO₃) + clay/laterite/bauxite/iron-ore correctives are crushed, blended and burnt at ~1,450 °C in a rotary kiln to make **clinker**; clinker is ground with ~4–5% gypsum to make **Ordinary Portland Cement (OPC)**, or with fly ash (**Portland Pozzolana Cement, PPC**, up to 35% fly ash) or granulated blast-furnace slag (**Portland Slag Cement, PSC**, up to 70% slag) to make blended cements. India is a blended-cement market: Dalmia reports 82% blended share in Q1 FY27 and a clinker-conversion (CC) ratio of ~1.7x [V, Dalmia Q1FY27 presentation]; UltraTech’s CC ratio is 1.50x (Q1FY27) vs 1.48x a year earlier [V, UltraTech Q1FY27 presentation]. Higher CC ratio = less clinker (the energy- and CO₂-intensive part) per tonne of cement = lower cost and emissions.

**Process economics in one line.** ~1.5–1.6 t limestone → 1 t clinker; ~0.6–0.7 t clinker → 1 t blended cement; thermal energy ~700–750 kcal/kg clinker; electricity ~65–75 kWh/t cement (UltraTech: 68.17 kWh/t in Q1FY27, down from 69.29 [V]).

**Plant archetypes.**
- *Integrated unit (IU)*: captive limestone mine + kiln + grinding at the mine head. 3–4 year gestation; greenfield IU cost $110–120/t of capacity (Ind-Ra, 2024, via Indian Infrastructure) [V].
- *Split grinding unit (GU)*: clinker railed/trucked from the IU to a GU near the market or near fly-ash/slag sources (thermal power plants, steel plants). 1–2 year gestation (CRISIL, Nov 2025) [V]; roughly a third of IU cost per tonne [K, industry rule of thumb — e.g. JSW Cement DRHP and analyst models, ~₹3,000–4,500/t for GUs vs ₹8,000–10,000/t for IUs].
- *Bulk terminal / packing plant*: coastal or rail-fed (e.g. Dalmia’s 3 MTPA Chennai bulk terminal planned for Q3 FY28 [V]).
- CRISIL expects ~two-thirds of FY26–28 incremental capacity to be split GUs and ~65% brownfield (CRISIL Ratings, Nov 2025, via NBM&CW) [V].

**Value chain and where value is captured.**
1. *Mining (limestone)* — captive leases; since the MMDR Amendment 2015 new limestone blocks are auctioned with a revenue-share premium on top of royalty, DMF (District Mineral Foundation, 30% of royalty for pre-2015 leases / 10% for auctioned) and NMET (2%) [K, MMDR Act 2015]. Lease access is the single biggest moat: it decides where an IU can exist and its 30–100-year life (Dalmia discloses limestone reserves of 26+ to 100+ years by region, 3.3+ bn t total [V]).
2. *Clinkerisation* — energy (petcoke/coal/alternative fuels) and kiln efficiency. Scale players capture value through cheaper fuel sourcing, WHRS power and higher alternative-fuel (AFR) mix.
3. *Grinding & blending* — fly ash/slag availability; proximity to power/steel plants.
4. *Logistics* — the biggest single cost (UltraTech: logistics = 31% of total cost, ₹1,149/t in Q1FY27 [V]). Lead distance (UltraTech 360 km; Dalmia 277 km [V]) and rail/road/sea mix decide regional reach. Cement is a low value-to-weight product (~₹5,000/t ex-GST), so economic radius by road is typically 250–400 km; this is why markets are regional.
5. *Distribution & brand* — trade (dealer/retailer, individual home builders, “IHB”) vs non-trade (institutional/infra/RMC/builders). Trade carries a ₹10–30/bag premium [K, industry practice; Dalmia’s 66% trade share and 25% premium share of trade in Q1FY27 V]. UltraTech: 66.3% trade, 79.7% bagged, 23% rail, 2% sea, 75% road [V].
6. *Downstream* — ready-mix concrete (RMC), construction chemicals, building products (UltraTech RMC: 477 plants, 4.62 mn m³, ₹2,235 cr revenue, 35% RoCE in Q1FY27 [V]); adjacent pivots into wires & cables (UltraTech, launch Q3FY27, ₹1,800 cr planned investment [V]) and paints (Grasim’s Birla Opus — group adjacency).

**Value capture summary.** Profit pools sit with (a) owners of low-cost limestone + fuel-efficient kilns in surplus-limestone states (Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, AP/Telangana, Karnataka, Gujarat), (b) players with dense GU networks near consuming markets (low lead distance), (c) brands that command trade premiums. Pure grinders without clinker security and sub-scale single-region players capture least (see business-model archetypes, §6).

## 2. Size and growth

**Volume.**
- India production FY2026 (Apr-25–Mar-26): **491.4 million t, +8.6% YoY** (ICRA, May 2026) [V]; the OEA/DPIIT core-index cement series also shows +8.6% for April–March 2025-26 (IPR March 2026, released 20 Apr 2026) [V]. Two independent primary sources agree.
- FY2025 implied: ~452.5 mt (491.4/1.086) [E, derived]; FY2024 ~425.7 mt [E]. The DPIIT annual series (index 2011-12=100) is 107.5 (FY13), 118.1 (FY15), 145.7 (FY20), 130.0 (FY21), 156.9 (FY22), 170.6 (FY23), 185.7 (FY24), 197.4 (FY25) [V, IPR 2026_03]; FY26 ≈ 214.4 [E = 197.4 × 1.086].
- FY27 YTD: Q1 FY27 production 130.2 mt, +8.8% YoY (ICRA, via search; Q1 figure consistent with Apr 42.8 mt [V] + Jul data); April 2026 42.8 mt, +9.4% [V, ICRA]; July 2026 42.3 mt, +13.1%; 4M FY27 172.6 mt, +9.9% (ICRA, 27 Aug 2026) [V]. August 2026 cement output +12.5% YoY and April–August +10.3% (OEA core-sector release, 21 Sep 2026, via Construction World / Policy Edge) [K – press reports of official release, not fetched].
- **5-year CAGR FY21–FY26**: 297.8 → 491.4 mt ≈ **10.5%** (covid-depressed base) [E]; **10-year CAGR FY16–FY26**: 283 → 491 mt ≈ **5.7%** [E]; CRISIL quotes a 9.5% volume CAGR over the three fiscals to FY25 (Nov 2025) [V].

**Value (₹ and USD).**
- FY26 industry revenue ≈ 491 mt × ~₹5,200/t net realisation ≈ **₹2.55 lakh crore** (~**US$29–30 bn** at ₹86–87/$) [E: volume × realisation; realisation basis = UltraTech domestic grey ₹5,034–5,163/t and BOBCAPS universe ₹5,182/t for Q1FY27 V]. Gross of GST (18% since 22 Sep 2025; 28% before) and dealer margins, consumer spend is ~₹3.1–3.2 lakh crore [E].
- Industry average retail price ~**₹345/bag (50 kg) in FY26** (+2% YoY) and ~₹350/bag in 4M FY27 (−2% YoY) (ICRA) [V]; i.e. ~₹6,900/t gross (incl. GST, dealer margin, freight to retail).
- Listed set: UltraTech’s slide says listed companies with >5 MTPA capacity total **607 MTPA**; UltraTech had **35% of their grey volume, 37% revenue, 39% EBITDA and 42% operating cash flow in FY26** [V, UltraTech Q1FY27 p2].

**Domestic vs export.** India is overwhelmingly domestic: exports (cement + clinker) are low single-digit % of output, mainly to Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Maldives and some Middle East/Africa [K; I could not fetch an official FY26 export tonnage — see H]. Imports are negligible (a small coastal flow of clinker/cement; no China dependence in finished cement). Unlike steel, Chinese overcapacity does not reach Indian cement directly because of freight-to-value; the transmission channel from China is via **fuel** (petcoke/coal) prices.

**Global share.** USGS MCS 2026 (cement production, thousand t, estimates): World 3,900,000 (2024) / 3,800,000 (2025e); **China 1,800,000 / 1,700,000; India 440,000 / 470,000**; Vietnam 91,000 / 100,000; Turkey 85,000 / 89,000; US 85,000 / 84,000; Iran 71,000 / 68,000; Indonesia 68,000 / 64,000; Brazil 65,000 / 67,000; Egypt 53,000 / 64,000; Saudi Arabia 51,000 / 54,000; Russia 67,000 / 59,000. Clinker capacity: China 1.8 bn t, **India 400 mt (2025e)**, world 3.8 bn t [V, USGS MCS 2026]. India ≈ **12.4% of world output in 2025** (470/3,800) [E], second only to China (~45%). Note USGS India (calendar-year, 470 mt) is slightly below ICRA FY26 491 mt — different period and coverage; ICRA/DPIIT is the more credible India number.

**Per capita.** ~**290 kg/person** (Economic Survey 2025-26, as reported by CemNet; CemNet page returned 403 on fetch) [K]; other sources cite ~257–260 kg vs global average ~**540 kg** (Infomerics, via search) [K]; China ~1,300–1,700 kg (varies by year) [K]. Arithmetic check: 491 mt / ~1.46 bn people ≈ **336 kg** on production basis for FY26 [E] — the Economic Survey number is lower because it uses an earlier year.

## 3. Demand drivers (with numbers)

**Demand mix (end-use).** CRISIL (Jul 2025): **rural housing ~one-third** of domestic demand, **infrastructure ~30%** (second-largest), the rest urban housing (~25–30%) and commercial/industrial (~10–15%) [V for rural & infra shares, CRISIL press release 7 Jul 2025; remainder E/K]. Broadly: **housing (rural + urban) ~60–65%, infrastructure ~25–30%, commercial & industrial ~10–12%** [E composite of CRISIL V and industry presentations K].

**Drivers.**
1. *Government capex*. Union capex (Dalmia slide citing Budget documents): ₹5.9 lakh cr (FY22), 7.3 (FY23), 9.5 (FY24), 10.5 (FY25), **11.0 (FY26RE), 12.2 (FY27B)**; state capex (ex-UTs) 3.9 (FY22) → 5.9/7.8 (FY26/FY27B) [V, Dalmia Q1FY27 p6 — chart values read from extracted text; treat as indicative]. Roads (Bharatmala successor programmes, expressways), railways (DFCs, station redevelopment), metros, irrigation (Ken–Betwa), airports (Jewar) are named in UltraTech’s regional demand commentary [V].
2. *Housing*. PMAY-Gramin and PMAY-Urban 2.0 (Union Cabinet, 2024: 2 crore additional rural houses and 1 crore urban houses) [K, PIB 2024]. Dalmia’s slide shows PMAY(G) current run-rate vs required ~3.0 vs 6.8 mn units/yr and PMAY(U) 0.9 vs 4.3 [V, chart text] — i.e. the programmes are behind schedule, so there is catch-up potential. Real-estate unsold inventory fell from 44 months (CY21) to ~21–24 months (CY23–CY25) [V, Dalmia p6].
3. *Urbanisation*: 34.4% (CY21) → ~36.0% (CY26e) [V, Dalmia p6].
4. *Commercial*: data centres / GCCs cited in the South (UltraTech Q1FY27) [V].
5. *GST cut (22 Sep 2025)* from 28% to 18% (GST Council, 3 Sep 2025) lowered retail price by ~₹25–30/bag (JM Financial estimate) — Elara measured all-India retail (gross of GST) falling ₹29 MoM to ₹340/bag in Sep 2025 while GST-adjusted prices were stable at ₹369 [K — Business Standard / Elara via search, not fetched]. ~8% cheaper cement for IHB customers supports affordability; most analysts expected only marginal elasticity.

**Demand elasticity rule of thumb.** Cement demand has run at ~1.0–1.3x real GDP growth over FY15–FY26 (5.7% 10-yr cement CAGR vs ~5.5–6% real GDP CAGR) [E]. FY27: ICRA expects 6–7% (Jul/Aug 2026, cut from 7–8% in May 2026) [V]; HDFC Securities ~8.5% volume CAGR FY26–FY28E [V, via Construction World]; CRISIL sees incremental demand of 30–40 mt per year FY26–28 [V].

**Regional demand (Q1 FY27, UltraTech commentary) [V]:** North — infra steady (Delhi–Amritsar, Mumbai–Delhi expressways, Jewar), housing growing but LPG prices hit labour in Delhi/HP; Central — strong (Lucknow–Kanpur, Varanasi–Kolkata expressways, Ken–Betwa); East — **subdued** (fund-flow delays, sand/labour/diesel shortages in WB & Jharkhand; Vikramshila bridge collapse in Bihar; state elections); West — firm (Mumbai Metro, Vadodara–Mumbai Expressway, solar parks in Kutch); South — infra **muted** (fund-flow delays in TN, Kerala, AP), commercial strong (data centres).

## 4. Supply side — capacity, utilisation, and cost structure

**Installed capacity.**
- **668 MTPA at 31 Mar 2025** (CRISIL Ratings, Nov 2025) [V]; ~**700 MTPA** per CMA (cited by ICRA/PTI, Dec 2025) [V via Outlook Business]. Implied end-FY26 ≈ 668 + 50–55 (Equirus FY26 additions, via NBM&CW) [V] or + 43–45 (ICRA Dec 2025 estimate) ≈ **~715–720 MTPA** [E]. CRISIL had expected 70–75 mt of FY26 commissioning (Nov 2025) [V] — the realised number appears closer to 50–55 mt. I could not fetch an authoritative end-FY26 total (see H).
- Clinker capacity ~400 mt (USGS 2025e) [V].
- **Additions:** CRISIL: 160–170 mt grinding capacity over FY26–FY28 vs 95 mt in FY23–FY25, ₹1.2 lakh crore capex (≈50% higher than prior three years), 10–15% of capex on green energy/efficiency (Nov 2025) [V]. ICRA: 43–45 MTPA FY26, **42–44 MTPA FY27** (Dec 2025; reiterated Jul 2026) [V].
- **Implied capex per tonne**: ₹1.2 lakh cr / 165 mt ≈ **₹7,300/t (~US$85/t)** blended IU+GU [E]. UltraTech’s recent-guidance: ~US$70–80/t brownfield, greenfield ~US$100+/t [K, UltraTech concalls 2024-26].

**Utilisation.** ~**70% in FY25** vs decadal average 65% (CRISIL) [V]; ICRA projects **70–71% in FY27**, similar to FY26, on a larger base [V]. Ind-Ra’s FY26 outlook had “utilisations to remain below 70%” [K, via NBM&CW search]. Regional: North & Central above average (>75–80%), South lowest (~55–60%) due to an overhang, East mid-60s and adding the most capacity (ICRA, Equirus) [V qualitative; numbers K/E]. Leaders run hotter: UltraTech domestic grey **81% in Q1FY27** on 200.1 MTPA [K via search, company concall]; Ambuja targets 70–75% consolidated utilisation (ICICI Direct, via search) [K].

**Why utilisation never reaches 85%+ nationally.** (1) Seasonality: Q1 and Q4 strong, Q2 (monsoon) weak — Q2 volumes typically 8–12% below Q1 (HDFC Sec: Q2FY27 ~10% QoQ seasonal decline) [V]; (2) regional mismatch — capacity is built where limestone is (South/Central/Rajasthan), demand is where people are (UP, Bihar, Maharashtra, WB); (3) capacity is reported on a grinding basis and clinker is the true constraint; (4) a long tail of sub-scale and stressed plants.

## 6. Business-model archetypes and how their economics differ

| Archetype | Examples | Economics | Typical EBITDA/t (FY26) | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pan-India scale leader** (5 regions, IU+GU hub-and-spoke, RMC, brand) | UltraTech; Adani (Ambuja+ACC) | Lowest logistics via network optimisation (lead distance 360 km), fuel arbitrage, WHRS + RE >40% of power; pricing leadership | ₹1,100–1,250 (UltraTech FY26 ₹1,103; Q1FY27 ₹1,214 [V]) ; Adani ₹900–1,000 | Execution of 30+ MTPA/yr adds; acquired-asset integration (ICL at ~₹600/t EBITDA, targeted ~₹1,000 by FY28 per Arihant [K]) |

## 7. Regulation and policy

1. **Pricing & distribution decontrol** — partial decontrol 1982, full decontrol 1 Mar 1989; delicensed 1991 [K]. Price is market-set but highly sensitive to regional supply discipline; this has repeatedly attracted antitrust action.
2. **Competition (CCI).** CCI fined 11 cement companies (incl. UltraTech, ACC, Ambuja, Shree, India Cements, Ramco, Binani, Century, Jaypee, JK Cement, Lafarge/Nuvoco) **₹6,307 cr** in June 2012 for cartelisation/capacity withholding (complaint by Builders’ Association of India); COMPAT remanded (Dec 2015); CCI re-imposed **~₹6,300–6,700 cr** (Aug 2016); NCLAT upheld (25 Jul 2018); **Supreme Court admitted appeals (Oct 2018) with 10% deposit and is yet to decide** — no 2026 judgment found (Bar & Bench, 2018; search) [K]. Contingent liability remains for listed incumbents. Separately, CCI clears all large M&A (e.g. UltraTech–India Cements; UltraTech–Kesoram; Ambuja–Penna/Orient) [K].
3. **GST.** Cement was in the 28% slab from GST launch (1 Jul 2017); the GST Council (3–4 Sep 2025) cut it to **18% effective 22 Sep 2025**; coal GST raised from 5% to 18% (with full input credit), and the ₹400/t compensation (“clean energy”) cess on coal was removed — JM Financial said this eases costs especially for East/Central producers (Outlook Business, 4 Sep 2025) [V]. Retail prices fell ~₹25–30/bag (7.5–8%) on pass-through [V].
4. **Mining & royalty.** Limestone is a major mineral under the MMDR Act 1957; post-2015 auctions; royalty on limestone is ~15% of average sale price (IBM ASP) for most grades [K]; DMF and NMET add-ons. **Supreme Court, *Mineral Area Development Authority v. SAIL*, 25 Jul 2024**: 8:1 nine-judge bench held royalty is not a tax and states have legislative competence (List II Entry 49/50) to tax mineral-bearing land, overruling *India Cement v. State of TN* (1989); a follow-up order (14 Aug 2024) allowed states to recover arrears for transactions after 1 Apr 2005, staggered over 12 years from 1 Apr 2026, without interest/penalty (SC Observer / Moneylife) [V-headline/K]. States moved: **Tamil Nadu** Minerals Bearing Land Tax — **₹160/t on limestone** from 20 Feb 2025 (Ramco paid ₹171.8 cr in FY26 and ₹79.1 cr in FY27 till Aug; ~₹84–86/t of cement for Ramco) [V via Construction World 28 Aug 2026]; **Karnataka** passed a Mineral Rights and Mineral Bearing Land Tax law (Dec 2024) with ~₹25/t on limestone, pending assent then [K via search]; Odisha/Jharkhand/others considered levies on coal/iron-ore lands [K].
   **Then the Centre over-rode it:** the **MMDR Amendment Act, 2026** (Parliament passed ~14 Aug 2026; PIB explainer 18 Aug 2026) inserts **Section 9D**: “No tax, cess or other levy, by whatever name called, shall be imposed by a State Government on mineral rights or mineral-bearing lands” except per conditions prescribed by the Centre; **unpaid/uncollected past levies are invalid, but amounts already paid are not refundable**; PIB notes states currently impose ~14 taxes/charges on mining and some mineral-land taxes at “rates as high as 20 percent” [V, PIB]. Ramco disclosed the TN MBLT ceases from **22 Aug 2026** [V via Construction World]. This removes a ₹10–90/t cost overhang for South-heavy producers and the 2005-onwards arrears risk (for unpaid amounts) [E]. Watch for litigation by states on federal-competence grounds.
5. **Environment & carbon.** Cement is a “designated consumer” under PAT (Perform-Achieve-Trade) since 2012 [K]; now covered under India’s **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS)** — GHG Emission Intensity Target Rules: draft 16 Apr 2025 for cement, aluminium, chlor-alkali, pulp & paper with FY2023-24 baseline and targets for FY2025-26 and FY2026-27; final targets reported notified **8 Oct 2025**, with cement reductions of ~**7–7.6%** vs baseline over two years (Saraf & Partners, via search) [K]. Non-compliance penalty = environmental compensation at 2× average credit price (JSA) [K]. EU CBAM (definitive phase from 1 Jan 2026) matters little because India exports little cement to the EU [K].
6. **Fuel import policy.** Petcoke import for fuel is banned generally, but **allowed for cement (and lime kiln, calcium carbide, gasification) as feedstock on actual-user basis**, no quantity cap for cement (DGFT Notification 10/2023; Bigmint) [K via search]. ~three-fourths of India’s petcoke use is cement [K].
7. **Standards.** BIS Quality Control Orders make IS-marked cement mandatory; BIS IS 18189:2023 allowed **Composite Cement / LC3 (limestone calcined clay cement)** — enabling lower clinker factors [K].
8. **Freight.** Railways’ Gati Shakti cargo terminals, bulk-cement wagon (BCFC) policy and a 2023-24 freight rationalisation help; rail share ~23% for UltraTech [V]. Diesel price is the main road-freight driver.
9. **Incentives.** State industrial-policy incentives (SGST refunds, interest subsidies) are material for new plants in North-East, Rajasthan, MP, Odisha, Bihar — e.g., Dalmia booked ₹45–75 cr per quarter of incentives [V, Dalmia p23]; these flatter reported realisation/EBITDA for newer plants.

## 10. Global lens

- **Size**: India is the **#2 producer (~470 mt 2025e, ~12% of world)** behind China (1.7 bn t, ~45%) and far ahead of #3 Vietnam (100 mt) [V, USGS 2026]. India has ~400 mt clinker capacity vs China 1.8 bn t [V].
- **Direction**: China’s output fell from ~2.4 bn t (2020–21) to 1.7 bn t (2025e) on the property bust [K/V]; Global Cement (Feb 2026) reports world demand returning to growth only in 2027 (CemNet headline) [K]. India is the only large market with sustained 6–9% growth; India is the swing growth market for equipment and fuel suppliers.
- **Consumption gap**: India ~290–340 kg/capita vs world ~540 kg and China >1,200 kg [K/E] — runway of 2x before India reaches world-average intensity.
- **Valuation**: Indian cement trades at EV/tonne of US$150–250 for leaders (UltraTech ~US$200+/t) vs global majors (Holcim, Heidelberg, Cemex) at ~US$100–150/t [K/E, brokerage comps]; replacement cost US$110–120/t (Ind-Ra) [V] — the premium reflects growth and consolidation optionality.
- **Margins**: Global majors earn EBITDA ~US$30–45/t (Holcim/Heidelberg cement segments) [K]; Indian EBITDA/t ₹900–1,100 ≈ **US$11–13/t** [E] — Indian cement is a high-volume, low-price (~US$60/t ex-GST realisation) market; returns are driven by low capex/t and asset turns.
- **Decarbonisation benchmarks**: GCCA 2050 net-zero roadmap; EU ETS/CBAM; India’s CCTS intensity targets (2025-27) are the first binding regime [K].

## 11. Technology and disruption

1. **Green power**: WHRS (waste-heat recovery, ~₹0.7/kWh vs grid ₹7.9) and captive solar/wind (₹4.5/kWh) [V, UltraTech]. Industry leaders target 60–85% green power by FY28–FY30 [V UltraTech 85% by FY30]. CRISIL: 10–15% of FY26–28 capex goes into green energy/efficiency [V]. Every 10 pp shift from grid to RE saves ~₹20–25/t [E: 68 kWh/t × 10% × ₹3.3/kWh saving].
2. **Alternative fuels & raw materials (AFR)**: TSR (thermal substitution rate) is low in India (~5–7%; UltraTech AF mix 6.9% Q1FY27 [V]) vs 40–60% in Europe [K]; RDF/MSW co-processing, biomass. ARM used by UltraTech: 49.8 mt in FY26 [V].
3. **Lower clinker factor**: LC3/composite cement (BIS 2023), more PSC (slag) — Dalmia blended 82%, PSC-heavy in East [V].
4. **Logistics**: EV trucks (UltraTech deployed 45 electric 55-tonne trucks, fleet of 750+ green vehicles [V]); rail sidings; coastal bulk shipping; digitised dispatch to cut lead distance.
5. **Carbon capture** pilots (Dalmia, UltraTech MoUs) — pre-commercial [K].
6. **Disruption risks**: (a) none to the product itself in the medium term (no substitute at scale); (b) **pre-cast and RMC** shift volume from trade to non-trade (lower price, but better efficiency); (c) steel/aluminium formwork and Mivan systems lower wastage; (d) mass timber / alternative binders negligible in India.
7. **Digital**: dealer apps, AI kiln optimisation (process control), predictive maintenance — 1–2% fuel savings typical [K].

## A. The 3–5 defining forces (with reasoning)

1. **Consolidation into a duopoly-plus (UltraTech + Adani ≈ 44% of capacity, top-5 ≈ 65%).** *Why it defines the sector:* in a regional, undifferentiated commodity with high fixed costs, pricing power comes from share within each region. The 2022 Holcim exit set off a land-grab: UltraTech absorbed Kesoram (10.75 MTPA) and India Cements (14.45 MTPA); Adani absorbed Sanghi (6.1), Penna (14), Orient (8.5) and is now folding ACC into Ambuja; Dalmia took Jaypee’s Central assets (5.2). The South went from fragmented (<50% top-5) to >62% top-5 [K]. Remaining targets are few (Heidelberg India, JAL remnants, small southern players) — the next phase is organic. Implication: fewer price wars once integration completes, but leaders now compete with each other on share.
2. **The capacity race vs demand — utilisation stuck at ~70%.** CRISIL: 160–170 mt added FY26–28 vs incremental demand of only 30–40 mt per year (~100–120 mt over three years) [V]. Capacity is being added faster than demand in every year since FY24, which caps price increases (ICRA: FY27 realisations +3–5%, BOBCAPS: Q1FY27 flat YoY [V]) and keeps EBITDA/t below the ~₹965 decadal average (CRISIL) [V]. The leaders build because scale lowers cost (UltraTech’s ₹178/t EBITDA advantage [V]); the laggards suffer.
3. **Energy & freight cost volatility (≈ 45–55% of cost).** P&F + logistics = 43.5% of UltraTech sales and ~53% of its cost [V]. Fuel is imported/linked to global petcoke/coal; the 2022 and 2026 West Asia shocks each took ₹150–300/t out of EBITDA within two quarters [V BOBCAPS]. Green power (WHRS + RE) and lead-distance cuts are the structural offsets, which is why capex now includes 10–15% green spend [V].
4. **Government-led demand (housing + infra ≈ 85–90% of demand).** Union capex ₹12.2 lakh cr (FY27B) [V-chart], PMAY 2.0, and rural housing (~⅓ of demand) [V] make cement a direct proxy for public capex and rural incomes. Demand ran at 8.6% in FY26 and ~10% YTD FY27 [V] despite softer GDP, but fund-flow delays (East, South) show the dependence [V UltraTech].
5. **Policy & state fiscal levers.** GST cut (28→18%, Sep 2025) [V]; the SC mineral-tax ruling (Jul 2024) and its reversal by the MMDR Amendment 2026 (Aug 2026) [V]; CCTS carbon intensity targets [K]; and state incentives. The net direction since 2025 has been **favourable** (lower GST, capped mineral levies), partially offset by CCTS compliance costs.

North and Central typically run ₹10–30/bag above pan-India, East ₹20–40 below [K/E]. **Why:** East has the most new capacity (Dalmia, UltraTech Patratu, Shree, JSW, Nuvoco, Adani GUs) and a large non-trade/infra share; South has chronic ~55–60% utilisation; North/Central have high utilisation and fewer, larger players — but UltraTech adds +20.7 MTPA in the North by FY28 [V], so the North premium is at risk.

### B3. Cost curve under fuel shock — the per-tonne bridge
UltraTech Q1FY27 grey cement per-tonne cost stack (ex-ICL) [V]: logistics ₹1,149 → fuel ₹915 → other ₹738 → raw material ₹682 → power ₹322 (+ employee ~₹250 [E]) ≈ **₹4,050/t**, against realisation ₹5,218 → EBITDA ~₹1,170–1,210 (reported consolidated ₹1,214). YoY: fuel +5%, power −10% (green mix), raw material +9% (diesel in mining, limestone), logistics −0.4% (lead distance −8 km) [V]. Dalmia shows the same: fuel +₹91/t QoQ despite inventory cushion of >₹150/t [V]. **Lesson:** leaders offset a ~₹45/t fuel hit with ~₹35/t power saving and logistics efficiencies; smaller players without WHRS/RE and with longer leads (ACC ₹434/t, Sagar ₹451/t) [K] take the full shock.

### B4. The mining-levy saga — from SC judgment to Section 9D
Jul 2024 SC → states could tax mineral land with arrears from 2005 → TN levied ₹160/t limestone (Feb 2025), Karnataka ~₹25/t, others mulled → Ramco paid ₹171.8 cr in FY26 (~₹84–86/t of its cement) [V] → Aug 2026 MMDR Amendment voids unpaid levies, no refund of paid amounts [V]. Net: removes an estimated **₹10–20/t industry-average** cost overhang [E: TN+Karnataka limestone ≈ 15% of national production × ₹100–160/t × 1.5 t limestone/t clinker × 0.65 clinker/t cement] and a potential multi-thousand-crore arrears claim. Residual risk: constitutional challenge by states.

## C. Revenue/profit equation — worked ₹ example
**Equation:** Revenue = Capacity × Utilisation × Net realisation/t; EBITDA = Volume × (Realisation/t − Cost/t); ROCE ≈ EBITDA/t × Utilisation × (1 − tax) − D&A/t … ÷ Capital employed/t.

**Worked example — a 10 MTPA integrated player (FY27E, industry-average assumptions) [E]:**
| Line | Assumption | ₹ |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | 10 MTPA × 72% utilisation | 7.2 mt |
| Realisation (net of GST, discounts) | ₹5,150/t — bridge: retail ₹350/bag ÷ 1.18 GST = ₹297/bag ex-GST; less ~₹15–20/bag dealer/retailer margin and trade discounts ≈ ₹257–282/bag → ₹5,150–5,640/t; reported NSRs (UltraTech ₹5,218, Dalmia ₹5,098 [V]) sit at the lower end because of non-trade sales | ₹3,708 cr revenue |
| Raw material | ₹750/t | ₹540 cr |
| Power & fuel | ₹1,250/t | ₹900 cr |
| Freight | ₹1,150/t | ₹828 cr |
| Employee | ₹280/t | ₹202 cr |
| Other (packing, stores, S&A) | ₹770/t | ₹554 cr |
| **Total cost** | **₹4,200/t** | ₹3,024 cr |
| **EBITDA** | **₹950/t** (18.4% margin) | **₹684 cr** |
| D&A | ₹330/t | ₹238 cr |
| Interest | ₹120/t | ₹86 cr |
| PBT | ₹500/t | ₹360 cr |
| Tax @25% | | ₹90 cr |
| **PAT** | ₹375/t | **₹270 cr** |
| Capital employed | ₹7,000/t of capacity × 10 MTPA | ₹7,000 cr |
| **Pre-tax ROCE** | (684 − 238)/7,000 | **~6.4%** |
**Sensitivities:** +₹10/bag price = +₹200/t = +₹144 cr EBITDA (+21%); +US$10/t petcoke ≈ −₹60/t = −₹43 cr (−6%); +5 pp utilisation = +0.5 mt × ₹950 + fixed-cost absorption ~₹50/t ≈ +₹80 cr (+12%). Reaching a 15% ROCE needs ~₹1,500/t EBITDA at 80% utilisation — which is why only the low-cost leaders earn their cost of capital (UltraTech RMC alone 35% ROCE [V]; cement ROCE 10–14% for leaders [K]). New greenfield capacity at ~₹9,000–10,000/t with EBITDA ₹1,000/t earns ~7–8% pre-tax ROCE at maturity [E] — the strategic logic is share, not stand-alone returns.

### Reconciliation notes (cross-batch checks)
1. **UltraTech capacity.** Batch 1 uses 200.1 MTPA domestic / 205.5 global (April 2026, company release). The industry file and the batch 2/3 primer-issue lists that say "UltraTech ~189" are stale. Use 200.1/205.5 everywhere.
2. **UltraTech EBITDA/t.** FY26 computed ~₹1,102 (screener EBITDA ÷ 154.3 MT consolidated); ICICI gives ₹1,103. The industry file cites ₹1,103. These are consistent.
3. **Adani perimeter.** Ambuja's consolidated revenue of ₹40,656 Cr includes ACC (₹25,962 Cr) and Orient. ACC's 43.9 MT includes cement bought from Ambuja under master supply agreements (MSA), so ACC's EBITDA/t of ₹672 is depressed by trading margins. Never add ACC or Orient to Ambuja.
4. **ACC "net cash".** The orchestrator re-fetched ACC's screener balance sheet. Mar-26 Investments are ₹55 Cr and "Other Assets" are ₹13,987 Cr, so the primer's ~₹13,500 Cr "net cash" is the Other Assets line. The company-disclosed cash (₹918 Cr at Mar-26) is the right basis.
5. **India Cements.** UltraTech's stake: 55.49% (Dec 2024) → 81.49% after the open offer → 75.00% after the MPS sell-down (Dec 2025). No merger scheme has been announced.
6. **Ramco PAT.** FY26 reported ₹699 Cr includes land-sale gains (~₹574 Cr, unverified snippet). Use ~₹250 Cr adjusted for P/E context.
7. **JSW Cement PAT.** −₹799 Cr reflects the CCPS fair-value/conversion charge (~₹1,466 Cr, unverified) around the IPO. The P/E shown is on TTM.
8. **Star capacity.** 9.7 MTPA (batch 3) supersedes the 7.7 MTPA in the primer.
9. **Birla Corp.** FY26 volume and EBITDA/t come from a ScanX summary of the filing. The company presentation could not be fetched, so these are marked unverified.
10. **Orient** FY26 volume and EBITDA/t are estimates; Orient no longer publishes a full KPI deck.
11. **Shree net cash.** ₹8,404 Cr is on the screener basis (investments − borrowings). The company-reported treasury differs slightly; state the basis.
