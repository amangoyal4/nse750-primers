### 0.2 Why these five (justification)

1. **Economic signature, not product label, defines the grouping.** The members differ more by *where value is added* than by what metal they cut. A useful test is "what share of ₹100 of revenue is bought-in material?" — EMS ~72–80; T&D towers ~65–72; process equipment ~50–60; precision components ~25–45; automation/consumables ~35–50 [E, built from company disclosures; see §4]. That single ratio drives margin, ROCE and WC behaviour, so it is the right primary axis.
2. **End-market and cycle differ by archetype.** A1 rides electronics policy (PLI/ECMS, China+1) and is only loosely tied to the Indian capex cycle. A2 and A4 (HONAUT) follow process-industry capex (chemicals, pharma, refining, LNG, sugar-ethanol). A3 follows *global* aerospace/energy-turbine cycles (Azad) and *domestic* MSME/auto capex (Jyoti). A5 follows *regulated public capex* (transmission). Tega follows global mining throughput, which is a *volume* (ore milled) rather than a capex variable.
3. **Revenue-recognition and order-book meaning differ.** EMS "order books" are rolling schedules of 6–18 months, not firm EPC contracts; process-equipment books are firm, milestone-based, 12–30 months; Skipper's are EPC/supply contracts of 12–36 months; Azad's are long-term multi-year supply agreements (LTAs) with OEMs; Tega's is mostly replacement-driven run-rate.
4. **Five rather than three or four:** merging A4 into A3 would mix a 15–20% EBITDA, asset-light MNC services model (HONAUT) and a consumables-annuity model (TEGA) with capex-heavy build-to-print shops; merging A5 into A2 would hide that towers are commodity-steel conversion with public-sector counterparties.

### 0.3 Edge cases

- **KRN Heat Exchanger** is classified A2 (heat-transfer) but behaves partly like a *component supplier* to HVAC&R OEMs (Daikin, Blue Star, Schneider, etc.) — shorter cycle, copper/aluminium pass-through, consumer-durable (room AC) seasonality. It is the A2 member most exposed to the copper price.
- **Praj Industries** sits in A2 but is part tech-licensor, part EPC, part equipment fabricator (Kandla/Mangalore "HiPurity" & engineering). Its cycle is policy-driven (ethanol blending, SAF, CBG), not chemical capex.
- **Elecon** is A3 because gears are precision-machined heat-treated products with OEM-like qualification; its material-handling (MHE) segment behaves like A5 project business.
- **Lloyds Engineering** (formerly Lloyds Steels Industries) is A5; its order book is concentrated with group/related steel-plant projects plus defence/nuclear diversification — governance/related-party dimension to watch.
- **Honeywell Automation India** is the only MNC subsidiary (parent Honeywell Inc. ~75% holding [K, BSE shareholding pattern]); it is net-cash and pays royalty/technical fees to the parent — a structural margin leak absent in the Indian-promoter names.

## 1. MACRO SETTING — the Indian capex cycle in numbers

### 1.1 Capacity utilisation (RBI OBICUS)

RBI's 73rd OBICUS round (released 5 Aug 2026, covering Q4 FY26, 1,127 manufacturers surveyed, 1,041 responding) [V: RBI, https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=24075]:

| Quarter | CU (%) | CU seasonally adj. (%) |
|---|---|---|
| Q4 FY25 | 77.7 | 75.5 |
| Q1 FY26 | 74.1 | 75.8 |
| Q2 FY26 | 74.3 | 74.9 |
| Q3 FY26 | 75.6 | 75.5 |
| Q4 FY26 | 77.4 | 75.2 |

- New orders (common sample) grew only **4.0% YoY / 2.9% QoQ** in Q4 FY26 vs 10.3% YoY in Q3 FY26 [V, same]. Order-book backlog +5.8% YoY.
- Inventory/sales ratio: 62.1% total, 21.0% finished goods, 23.9% raw materials in Q4 FY26 [V].
- **Interpretation.** The RBI and most economists treat sustained CU above ~75% (seasonally adjusted) as the threshold at which brownfield capacity additions become necessary. CU-SA has hovered at **74.9–75.8% for five straight quarters** — i.e., *at* the threshold, not decisively above it. This is the single best explanation of why Indian private capex has been "broadening but not booming": capacity is tight enough to justify debottlenecking and selective greenfield (electronics, chemicals, metals, power equipment) but not a 2004–08-style generalised boom. Historically CU peaked at ~83% in 2007-08 and troughed ~63% in Q1 FY21 (COVID) [K: RBI OBICUS archive].

## 2. ARCHETYPE A1 — ELECTRONICS MANUFACTURING SERVICES & ESDM (Kaynes, Syrma SGS, Avalon)

### 2.1 What it does and the value chain

ESDM (Electronic System Design & Manufacturing) spans: (1) design/ODM → (2) components (passives, PCBs, connectors, cells, displays, camera modules, semiconductors) → (3) PCB assembly (SMT/THT) → (4) box-build / system integration, cable harnesses, enclosures → (5) test, after-market and repair. India's listed "EMS" companies sit at (3)–(4), with Kaynes and Syrma moving upstream into (2) (bare multilayer PCBs, OSAT, component JVs) and (1) (ODM/IP — Syrma's ODM is ~17% of revenue, target ~25% [V: BOBCAPS Syrma Q1FY27 note, 31 Jul 2026, https://www.barodaetrade.com/Reports/SyrmaSGS-Q1FY27ResultReview31Jul26-Research.pdf]).

**Where value is captured.** Semiconductors and high-value components capture most of the bill-of-materials (BOM) value and margin globally; the assembler earns a "conversion" margin on a BOM it largely passes through. India's large mobile-phone ecosystem (Foxconn, Pegatron/Tata, Dixon, etc.) is mostly final assembly with domestic value addition historically ~15–20% [K: MeitY/ICEA statements 2024–25]. The listed A1 members deliberately avoid mobile phones and consumer high-volume, and focus on **low-volume / high-mix (LVHM)** industrial, auto, railways, medical, aerospace-defence, smart-meter and IoT work, where gross margins are 2–3× those of mobile EMS (Kaynes reported 34–41% gross margin; Avalon guides 33–35%; Dixon-type mobile EMS sits at ~8–12% gross margin [K: Dixon annual report FY25]). "Gross margin" for EMS here = (revenue – material cost) / revenue, i.e., value-add share.

### 2.3 Demand drivers (with numbers)

1. **Policy stack.**
   - PLI for large-scale electronics manufacturing (mobile phones; 2020, outlay ~₹38,601 crore incl. extensions) and PLI 2.0 for IT hardware (2023, ₹17,000 crore) [K: MeitY].
   - **ECMS — Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme**: Cabinet approval 28 Mar 2025, notified 8 Apr 2025, outlay ₹22,919 crore (raised to ₹40,000 crore in Budget 2026-27 per press [K: Outlook Business/ThePrint snippet]); by **17 Aug 2026: 106 projects approved, ₹69,548 crore investment (vs original target ₹59,350 crore), projected production ₹5,34,101 crore, 74,628 direct jobs, across 15 states; production begun at 38 projects** [V: ThePrint, 17 Aug 2026]. Tranches: 7 projects (Oct 2025) → 24 (₹12,704 crore) → 46 (₹54,567 crore, Feb 2026) → 75 (end-Jul 2026) → 106 [V/K]. Government-claimed self-sufficiency: PCBs 45% of demand, laminates 80%, connectors 75%, Li-ion digital cells 60% [V: same; treat as ministerial claims]. Kaynes (PCB + capacitors/laminates) and Syrma (multilayer PCB facility) are among ECMS applicants/approvees [K: company disclosures; exact approvals to be confirmed in company packs].
   - **India Semiconductor Mission (ISM)**: ₹76,000 crore (Dec 2021) supporting fabs and ATMP/OSAT; ~50% fiscal support from Centre on project cost plus state top-ups (Gujarat ~20%) [K: MeitY]. **Kaynes Semicon, Sanand: ₹3,300 crore OSAT, inaugurated 31 Mar 2026, India's second operational semiconductor unit after Micron; first product Intelligent Power Modules (IPMs) for Alpha & Omega Semiconductor; capacity ~6.3 million units/day at full scale** [V: Business Today, 31 Mar 2026, https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/story/pm-modi-inaugurates-rs-3300-crore-kaynes-semiconductor-plant-in-gujarat-523321-2026-03-31].    - Smart-meter programme under RDSS (Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme; 22.23 crore prepaid smart meters sanctioned [K: Ministry of Power]) — a large but **receivable-heavy** EMS demand pool (see §2.7).
2. **China+1 / export pull.** OEMs in Europe/US dual-sourcing industrial, auto, medical and A&D electronics. Syrma exports ~24% of Q1 FY27 revenue, Europe ~40% of exports, North America ~22% (~37% incl. Mexico/Canada); FY27 export growth guided 30–40% from ~₹1,200 crore in FY26 [V: BOBCAPS]. Avalon is US-centric (~55–60% of revenue export/US historically [K]); US manufacturing losses narrowed to ~₹3.8 crore in Q1 FY27 [V: PL Avalon note, 5 Aug 2026, https://plindia.com/ResReport/AVALON-5-8-26-PL.pdf].
3. **Electronics content per unit rising**: EVs (BMS, motor controllers, chargers), railways (Kavach signalling — Kaynes railways +81% YoY in Q1 FY27 [V: ICICI Direct]), defence electronics, medical devices, data-centre hardware.
4. **Domestic consumption** (consumer electronics, appliances, wearables) — Syrma's consumer segment ~34% of Q1 FY27 revenue [E from BOBCAPS segment data].

**Key point:** a 1-percentage-point change in component pricing that is not passed through moves EBITDA by ~7–10% — which is exactly what happened in Q1 FY27 when **component prices rose 30–35%, PCB prices 10–12%, and lead-times stretched to 6–8 months**, compressing Kaynes' gross margin 685 bps YoY to 34.4% [V: ICICI Direct Kaynes note]. Pass-through clauses exist but operate with a 1–2 quarter lag.

### 2.7 Working capital and cash conversion — the A1 Achilles heel

- Syrma FY26: receivable days 139, inventory 80, payable 148 ⇒ NWC ~71 days (Q1 FY27) vs 63 days a quarter earlier, deliberate inventory build for supply-chain disruption; CFO ₹290 crore vs EBITDA ₹545 crore in FY26 ⇒ **CFO/EBITDA ~53%** [V: BOBCAPS].
- Kaynes: working-capital days ~163 in Q1 FY27 vs ~122 a year earlier; Q1 operating cash flow **–₹259 crore**; management targets cash-positive by FY27-end and is evaluating restructuring/divestment of the metering *service* portion [V: ICICI Direct].
- **Read-across.** EMS companies grow faster than their cash. Growth at 30–50% with 120–160 NWC days absorbs ₹33–44 of working capital per ₹100 of incremental revenue — usually funded by QIPs (Kaynes, Syrma both raised equity 2023–26). In late 2025 and 2026 the market began pricing this: Kaynes' share price fell from a 52-week high of ₹7,705 to ~₹3,735 by Aug 2026, with FII holding sliding from 10.7% (Sep-25) to 5.8% (Jun-26) and DII from 23.6% to 11.7% [V: ICICI Direct shareholding table]. The A1 investment debate has shifted from "growth" to "growth × cash conversion".

## 3. ARCHETYPE A2 — PROCESS, CRYOGENIC & HEAT-TRANSFER EQUIPMENT (INOX India, Anup, GMM Pfaudler, Praj, KRN)

### 3.1 What it does and the value chain

Engineered-to-order (ETO) static equipment and process systems: pressure vessels, shell-and-tube and air-cooled heat exchangers, reactors, columns (Anup); glass-lined and alloy reactors, agitators, filters/dryers, mixing, heavy engineering (GMM Pfaudler, with ~60%+ of revenue from overseas subsidiaries — Pfaudler Germany/US/Italy, Mixion, Hydro Air, Edlon); vacuum-insulated cryogenic tanks, LNG fuel tanks/semi-trailers/terminals, beverage kegs, scientific cryostats (INOX India, a.k.a. INOXCVA); process technology + EPC + equipment for 1G/2G ethanol, CBG, SAF, bio-polymers and high-purity water/ZLD (Praj); fin-and-tube heat exchangers, condenser/evaporator coils and copper-tube assemblies for HVAC&R OEMs (KRN).

Value chain: licensor / process technology (where the IP margin sits — e.g., Praj's ethanol technology, Pfaudler's glass formulations, INOX's vacuum-insulation know-how) → detailed engineering → procurement of plate, forgings, tube, alloys (SS 304/316, duplex, Inconel, Ti, Zr, aluminium 5083/9% Ni for cryogenic) → fabrication (rolling, welding, PWHT, NDT, ASME U/U2/R stamps) → testing/inspection → installation/commissioning → spares & service (GMM and INOX build after-market annuity). **Value is captured where there is (a) proprietary process IP or (b) certification + metallurgy + welding skill that few shops have (exotic alloys, cryogenic vacuum, glass lining).** Plain carbon-steel fabrication is a low-margin commodity.
