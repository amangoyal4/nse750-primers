### 0.2 Why these five (justification)

1. **Economic signature, not product label, defines the grouping.** The members differ more by *where value is added* than by what metal they cut. A useful test is "what share of ₹100 of revenue is bought-in material?" — EMS ~72–80; T&D towers ~65–72; process equipment ~50–60; precision components ~25–45; automation/consumables ~35–50 [E, built from company disclosures; see §4]. That single ratio drives margin, ROCE and WC behaviour, so it is the right primary axis.
2. **End-market and cycle differ by archetype.** A1 rides electronics policy (PLI/ECMS, China+1) and is only loosely tied to the Indian capex cycle. A2 and A4 (HONAUT) follow process-industry capex (chemicals, pharma, refining, LNG, sugar-ethanol). A3 follows *global* aerospace/energy-turbine cycles (Azad) and *domestic* MSME/auto capex (Jyoti). A5 follows *regulated public capex* (transmission). Tega follows global mining throughput, which is a *volume* (ore milled) rather than a capex variable.
3. **Revenue-recognition and order-book meaning differ.** EMS "order books" are rolling schedules of 6–18 months, not firm EPC contracts; process-equipment books are firm, milestone-based, 12–30 months; Skipper's are EPC/supply contracts of 12–36 months; Azad's are long-term multi-year supply agreements (LTAs) with OEMs; Tega's is mostly replacement-driven run-rate.
4. **Five rather than three or four:** merging A4 into A3 would mix a 15–20% EBITDA, asset-light MNC services model (HONAUT) and a consumables-annuity model (TEGA) with capex-heavy build-to-print shops; merging A5 into A2 would hide that towers are commodity-steel conversion with public-sector counterparties.

### 0.3 Edge cases

- **KRN Heat Exchanger** is classified A2 (heat-transfer) but behaves partly like a *component supplier* to HVAC&R OEMs (Daikin, Blue Star, Schneider, etc.) — shorter cycle, copper/aluminium pass-through, consumer-durable (room AC) seasonality. It is the A2 member most exposed to the copper price.
- **Praj Industries** sits in A2 but is part tech-licensor, part EPC, part equipment fabricator (Kandla/Mangalore "HiPurity" & engineering). Its cycle is policy-driven (ethanol blending, SAF, CBG), not chemical capex.
- **Elecon** is A3 because gears are precision-machined heat-treated products with OEM-like qualification; its material-handling (MHE) segment behaves like A5 project business.
- **Lloyds Engineering** (formerly Lloyds Steels Industries) is A5; its order book is concentrated with group/related steel-plant projects plus defence/nuclear diversification — governance/related-party dimension to watch.
- **Honeywell Automation India** is the only MNC subsidiary (parent Honeywell Inc. ~75% holding [K, BSE shareholding pattern]); it is net-cash and pays royalty/technical fees to the parent — a structural margin leak absent in the Indian-promoter names.

## 1. MACRO SETTING — the Indian capex cycle in numbers

### 1.1 Capacity utilisation (RBI OBICUS)

RBI's 73rd OBICUS round (released 5 Aug 2026, covering Q4 FY26, 1,127 manufacturers surveyed, 1,041 responding) [V: RBI, https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=24075]:

| Quarter | CU (%) | CU seasonally adj. (%) |
|---|---|---|
| Q4 FY25 | 77.7 | 75.5 |
| Q1 FY26 | 74.1 | 75.8 |
| Q2 FY26 | 74.3 | 74.9 |
| Q3 FY26 | 75.6 | 75.5 |
| Q4 FY26 | 77.4 | 75.2 |

- New orders (common sample) grew only **4.0% YoY / 2.9% QoQ** in Q4 FY26 vs 10.3% YoY in Q3 FY26 [V, same]. Order-book backlog +5.8% YoY.
- Inventory/sales ratio: 62.1% total, 21.0% finished goods, 23.9% raw materials in Q4 FY26 [V].
- **Interpretation.** The RBI and most economists treat sustained CU above ~75% (seasonally adjusted) as the threshold at which brownfield capacity additions become necessary. CU-SA has hovered at **74.9–75.8% for five straight quarters** — i.e., *at* the threshold, not decisively above it. This is the single best explanation of why Indian private capex has been "broadening but not booming": capacity is tight enough to justify debottlenecking and selective greenfield (electronics, chemicals, metals, power equipment) but not a 2004–08-style generalised boom. Historically CU peaked at ~83% in 2007-08 and troughed ~63% in Q1 FY21 (COVID) [K: RBI OBICUS archive].

## 2. ARCHETYPE A1 — ELECTRONICS MANUFACTURING SERVICES & ESDM (Kaynes, Syrma SGS, Avalon)

### 2.1 What it does and the value chain

ESDM (Electronic System Design & Manufacturing) spans: (1) design/ODM → (2) components (passives, PCBs, connectors, cells, displays, camera modules, semiconductors) → (3) PCB assembly (SMT/THT) → (4) box-build / system integration, cable harnesses, enclosures → (5) test, after-market and repair. India's listed "EMS" companies sit at (3)–(4), with Kaynes and Syrma moving upstream into (2) (bare multilayer PCBs, OSAT, component JVs) and (1) (ODM/IP — Syrma's ODM is ~17% of revenue, target ~25% [V: BOBCAPS Syrma Q1FY27 note, 31 Jul 2026, https://www.barodaetrade.com/Reports/SyrmaSGS-Q1FY27ResultReview31Jul26-Research.pdf]).

**Where value is captured.** Semiconductors and high-value components capture most of the bill-of-materials (BOM) value and margin globally; the assembler earns a "conversion" margin on a BOM it largely passes through. India's large mobile-phone ecosystem (Foxconn, Pegatron/Tata, Dixon, etc.) is mostly final assembly with domestic value addition historically ~15–20% [K: MeitY/ICEA statements 2024–25]. The listed A1 members deliberately avoid mobile phones and consumer high-volume, and focus on **low-volume / high-mix (LVHM)** industrial, auto, railways, medical, aerospace-defence, smart-meter and IoT work, where gross margins are 2–3× those of mobile EMS (Kaynes reported 34–41% gross margin; Avalon guides 33–35%; Dixon-type mobile EMS sits at ~8–12% gross margin [K: Dixon annual report FY25]). "Gross margin" for EMS here = (revenue – material cost) / revenue, i.e., value-add share.

### 2.3 Demand drivers (with numbers)

1. **Policy stack.**
   - PLI for large-scale electronics manufacturing (mobile phones; 2020, outlay ~₹38,601 crore incl. extensions) and PLI 2.0 for IT hardware (2023, ₹17,000 crore) [K: MeitY].
   - **ECMS — Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme**: Cabinet approval 28 Mar 2025, notified 8 Apr 2025, outlay ₹22,919 crore (raised to ₹40,000 crore in Budget 2026-27 per press [K: Outlook Business/ThePrint snippet]); by **17 Aug 2026: 106 projects approved, ₹69,548 crore investment (vs original target ₹59,350 crore), projected production ₹5,34,101 crore, 74,628 direct jobs, across 15 states; production begun at 38 projects** [V: ThePrint, 17 Aug 2026]. Tranches: 7 projects (Oct 2025) → 24 (₹12,704 crore) → 46 (₹54,567 crore, Feb 2026) → 75 (end-Jul 2026) → 106 [V/K]. Government-claimed self-sufficiency: PCBs 45% of demand, laminates 80%, connectors 75%, Li-ion digital cells 60% [V: same; treat as ministerial claims]. Kaynes (PCB + capacitors/laminates) and Syrma (multilayer PCB facility) are among ECMS applicants/approvees [K: company disclosures; exact approvals to be confirmed in company packs].
   - **India Semiconductor Mission (ISM)**: ₹76,000 crore (Dec 2021) supporting fabs and ATMP/OSAT; ~50% fiscal support from Centre on project cost plus state top-ups (Gujarat ~20%) [K: MeitY]. **Kaynes Semicon, Sanand: ₹3,300 crore OSAT, inaugurated 31 Mar 2026, India's second operational semiconductor unit after Micron; first product Intelligent Power Modules (IPMs) for Alpha & Omega Semiconductor; capacity ~6.3 million units/day at full scale** [V: Business Today, 31 Mar 2026, https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/story/pm-modi-inaugurates-rs-3300-crore-kaynes-semiconductor-plant-in-gujarat-523321-2026-03-31].    - Smart-meter programme under RDSS (Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme; 22.23 crore prepaid smart meters sanctioned [K: Ministry of Power]) — a large but **receivable-heavy** EMS demand pool (see §2.7).
2. **China+1 / export pull.** OEMs in Europe/US dual-sourcing industrial, auto, medical and A&D electronics. Syrma exports ~24% of Q1 FY27 revenue, Europe ~40% of exports, North America ~22% (~37% incl. Mexico/Canada); FY27 export growth guided 30–40% from ~₹1,200 crore in FY26 [V: BOBCAPS]. 4. **Domestic consumption** (consumer electronics, appliances, wearables) — Syrma's consumer segment ~34% of Q1 FY27 revenue [E from BOBCAPS segment data].

### 2.7 Working capital and cash conversion — the A1 Achilles heel

- Syrma FY26: receivable days 139, inventory 80, payable 148 ⇒ NWC ~71 days (Q1 FY27) vs 63 days a quarter earlier, deliberate inventory build for supply-chain disruption; CFO ₹290 crore vs EBITDA ₹545 crore in FY26 ⇒ **CFO/EBITDA ~53%** [V: BOBCAPS].
- Kaynes: working-capital days ~163 in Q1 FY27 vs ~122 a year earlier; Q1 operating cash flow **–₹259 crore**; management targets cash-positive by FY27-end and is evaluating restructuring/divestment of the metering *service* portion [V: ICICI Direct].
- **Read-across.** EMS companies grow faster than their cash. Growth at 30–50% with 120–160 NWC days absorbs ₹33–44 of working capital per ₹100 of incremental revenue — usually funded by QIPs (Kaynes, Syrma both raised equity 2023–26). In late 2025 and 2026 the market began pricing this: Kaynes' share price fell from a 52-week high of ₹7,705 to ~₹3,735 by Aug 2026, with FII holding sliding from 10.7% (Sep-25) to 5.8% (Jun-26) and DII from 23.6% to 11.7% [V: ICICI Direct shareholding table]. The A1 investment debate has shifted from "growth" to "growth × cash conversion".

## 3. ARCHETYPE A2 — PROCESS, CRYOGENIC & HEAT-TRANSFER EQUIPMENT (INOX India, Anup, GMM Pfaudler, Praj, KRN)

### 3.1 What it does and the value chain

Engineered-to-order (ETO) static equipment and process systems: pressure vessels, shell-and-tube and air-cooled heat exchangers, reactors, columns (Anup); glass-lined and alloy reactors, agitators, filters/dryers, mixing, heavy engineering (GMM Pfaudler, with ~60%+ of revenue from overseas subsidiaries — Pfaudler Germany/US/Italy, Mixion, Hydro Air, Edlon); vacuum-insulated cryogenic tanks, LNG fuel tanks/semi-trailers/terminals, beverage kegs, scientific cryostats (INOX India, a.k.a. ### 7.1 China+1 and exports

- **Who exports what.** Export dependence is the second big axis after value-add: Azad ~90% [V: Sahi], INOX 58% of Q1 FY27 revenue [V], Tega legacy ~85–90% [K], Avalon majority US [K], GMM >60% via overseas subsidiaries [K], Anup 40% of Q1 FY27 revenue [V: ICICI Direct], Elecon ~29% overseas [K], Syrma ~24% [V: BOBCAPS], Skipper ~25–35% [K], Praj 25% of Q1 FY27 revenue / 37% of order book [V: PL], KRN ~21% of Q1 FY27 revenue [E: ₹52/₹252 crore, K source], HONAUT ~25–35% incl. export services [K], Jyoti ~20–30% incl. Huron [K], Kaynes ~10–15% (overseas business ₹102 crore of ₹946 crore in Q1 FY27 [V: ICICI Direct]), Lloyds low.
- **India's engineering exports** were US$122.4 bn in FY26 (+4.9%) per EEPC [K: EEPC via press, not fetched]; electronics exports ~₹4.24 lakh crore (~US$48 bn, +24%) [K]. Engineering is India's largest merchandise export category.
- **Why China+1 works for these archetypes but not for commodity engineering:** Western OEMs re-sourcing *qualified, low-volume, high-complexity* parts (airfoils, cryogenic vessels, ACHEs, LVHM electronics) pay for supply security and compliance (ASME, AS9100, NADCAP, ITAR-adjacent rules, US "foreign entity of concern" restrictions on Chinese content) — Indian vendors win on a total-cost-plus-risk basis even where China is 10–20% cheaper on piece price (CII estimate of India's cost gap [K]). Commodity steel structures face direct Chinese/Turkish/Vietnamese competition and US anti-dumping.

**Effect on the sub-sector [E].**

3. **Policy optionality** (ECMS, ISM, defence, NEP transmission, SAF/CBG) is priced as free call options.
4. **Low capital-intensity perception** — partly wrong: the fastest growers are WC-heavy and equity-funded (§7.5).

**What it implies — worked reverse-DCF [E].** At the sub-sector's ~73× TTM P/E and a 12% cost of equity, if the market expects a "mature" P/E of 25× in ten years, earnings must compound at **~24–25% a year for ten years** to earn just the cost of equity: 73 × 1.12¹⁰ = 73 × 3.106 = 226.7; 226.7 / 25 = 9.07× earnings growth over 10 years ⇒ (9.07)^(1/10) – 1 ≈ 24.7% (ignoring dividends, which are <1% yield). Only a minority of members have a ten-year record anywhere near that. For Azad (139×) the required growth is ~33% a year for a decade; for Skipper (28×) only ~13%. The asymmetry: any slowdown or cash-conversion disappointment in a 70×+ stock produces 40–55% drawdowns (Kaynes 2025–26 is the template).

### 7.9 Technology and disruption

- **EMS:** Shift to advanced packaging (OSAT, SiP), multilayer/HDI PCBs, SiC/GaN power modules for EVs; automation of SMT lines reduces labour-cost advantage; design-led ODM separates winners.
- **Process equipment:** Modular/skid-mounted plants (Praj GenX), hydrogen (liquid H2 at –253 °C needs INOX-type vacuum-jacketed tech), LNG small-scale, CO₂ capture equipment, ZLD; glass-lining alternatives (fluoropolymer-lined, Hastelloy) pressure GMM's core.
- **Precision:** Additive manufacturing (3D-printed fuel nozzles, blades) could disintermediate some machining over a 10–15-year horizon; 5-axis and turn-mill machines and digital twins raise the bar for Jyoti; CNC controls remain Fanuc/Siemens/Heidenhain-dominated — a localisation gap.
- **Automation:** Software-defined control, cloud/edge analytics, cybersecurity; competition from open-process automation standards.
- **Mining:** Hybrid poly-met liners, sensorised liners (wear monitoring) and digital twins of mills (Tega DynaPrime-type products) increase value per tonne.
- **T&D:** HVDC (needs fewer, heavier towers), compact/monopole designs, conductor upgrades (HTLS) partially substitute for new lines.

## A. THE DEFINING FORCES (with reasoning)

1. **Value-add share decides everything.** Bought-in material ranges from ~25% (Azad) to ~78% (Syrma) of revenue. It sets gross margin, the damage from un-passed input inflation, and how much working capital each ₹ of growth needs. Archetype economics therefore differ more than the shared "industrial products" label suggests, and the report should compare companies within an archetype, not across the bucket.
2. **Policy-made demand with a cycle of its own.** ECMS/ISM/PLI (A1), ethanol/SAF/CBG mandates (Praj), NEP-Transmission (A5) and defence indigenisation (Azad, Jyoti, Lloyds, Kaynes) create demand that does not follow the private-capex cycle. Policy cycles overshoot: ethanol capacity reached ~1,970 crore L against ~1,100 crore L of E20 demand [V: CARE], and Praj's ROCE fell from 31% to 6% within three years [V]. The same could happen to smart meters (receivables) or ECMS components if too much capacity is approved.
3. **The global capex super-cycles in energy and aerospace, and India's qualified share of them.** GE Vernova's 116 GW of gas backlog plus slots, GE Aerospace's >US$210 bn backlog [V], ~US$3 trn of global grid investment over CY24–29 [V], and the LNG liquefaction wave [K] are supply-constrained markets that pay a premium for qualified capacity. Azad, INOX, Skipper, Tega and Anup are exporting into them. China+1 decides who gets the incremental qualification.
4. **A domestic private capex cycle that is broadening but not booming.** CU-SA has sat at 74.9–75.8% for five quarters, new orders grew just 4% YoY in Q4 FY26 [V: RBI OBICUS], bank-sanctioned project cost rose 18% to ₹4.35 lakh crore in FY26 (power 40%) [V: RBI], and capital-goods IIP was +8.3% [V]. That is supportive for T&D, automation and machine tools. It is not yet strong enough to revive chemical-process equipment, where CRISIL expects specialty-chem capex to be pared to ₹16,500 crore [V].
5. **The gap between valuation and cash conversion.** The 15 members trade at ~73× aggregate TTM earnings [V/E]. The fastest growers convert little EBITDA to cash and fund growth with equity. Any slip in growth, or any receivable or inventory surprise, shows up as a large de-rating, as the 2025–26 falls in Kaynes, Praj and Anup show [V: screener 52-week ranges].

## B. DEEP-DIVE THEMES

### B1. Can Indian EMS escape the "pass-through trap"? (A1)
Today ~65–78% of EMS revenue is purchased components [V/E]. India's domestic value addition in electronics is ~15–20% [K]. Margins can expand only by moving up the stack: bare PCBs (~90% imported [V: EMS Now]), OSAT (Kaynes Sanand, ~6.3 mn units/day capacity [V]), components (ECMS: 106 projects, ₹69,548 crore [V]), and ODM/IP (Syrma ODM 17% → 25% target [V]). The catch: these upstream assets turn at <1× fixed assets in their early years. They depend on subsidy disbursement (Kaynes had ₹170 crore of OSAT subsidy by Jul 2026 [V]) and on reaching utilisation, so ROCE will dip before it rises (Kaynes ROCE fell from 22% in FY23 to 13% in FY26 [V: screener]). Investors should watch gross margin together with fixed-asset turns, NWC days and CFO/EBITDA, not revenue growth alone. Smart-meter EMS shows the WC danger most clearly: Kaynes' metering receivables of ₹1,311 crore were more than twice its EMS receivables [V].

### B2. India's place in the aero-engine and gas-turbine bottleneck (A3, partly A2)
The global constraint is qualified supply: castings, forgings, machined airfoils. GE raised priority-supplier input by double digits [V]. GE Vernova is lifting gas-turbine output from ~20 GW to 24 GW (2028) and 30 GW (2030) [V], which implies roughly 50% more airfoil and rotor demand per year by 2030 from that OEM alone [E]. Azad (37% OPM, ~90% export [V]) charges for scarcity. Its risks are concentration, the 2–3-year qualification lag that delays revenue behind capex (four new plants ramping in H2 FY27 [V]), the WC needed to stock superalloys (304 WC days [V]), and a P/E of 139× [V]. A 10-year reverse DCF needs ~33% EPS CAGR [E]. The theme also covers Jyoti (5-axis machines for aerospace; A&D 38% of order book [K]) and INOX (AS9100D certification and space cryogenics [V]).

### B3. Policy cycles in process equipment: ethanol as the case study (A2)
Ethanol was the cleanest policy-made capex boom of the decade. Capacity tripled from 680 to ~1,970 crore L (2018-19 → 2025-26) and blending rose from 5% to 20% five years ahead of schedule [V: CARE]. Then OMC allocation absorbed only ~60% of offered volume [V], distillery utilisation is expected at 65–75% for three years [V], and greenfield EPC stopped. Praj's revenue fell 10% from its FY23 peak while EBITDA margin halved [V]. The lesson carries over to green hydrogen (NGHM), CBG (GOBARdhan ₹23,000 crore+ [V]) and SAF. Equipment vendors make money on the build-out and lose operating leverage when it stops. Companies with export pipelines (Praj: 37% of the order book [V]), lifecycle services and multiple technologies survive best.

### B4. Transmission as the steadiest leg (A5)
NEP-Transmission's ₹9.15 lakh crore, about 1.91 lakh ckm and 33 GW of HVDC [V] produce a multi-year, regulated, IEEMA-indexed order stream. Skipper's flat 10% EBITDA margin over four years of steel volatility [V] shows the pass-through working. The risks lie elsewhere: execution (right-of-way, forest clearance), a crowded TBCB field (16 ISTS schemes awarded in FY26 [K]), interest cost (~38% of Skipper's EBITDA [E]), and Section 232 tariffs on full customs value for US exports [V].

## C. THE REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION (with worked ₹ examples)

**Generic.** Revenue = Opening order book × execution rate + book-and-bill flow (for ETO/EPC), or = Volume × realisation (for EMS and consumables). EBITDA = Revenue × value-add % – fixed conversion cost. Cash generated = EBITDA – tax – capex – ΔNWC, where ΔNWC = (NWC days / 365) × ΔRevenue.

**Worked example 1 — LVHM EMS (A1), ₹1,000 crore revenue company growing 30% [E]:**
- Revenue ₹1,000 crore; BOM 66% ⇒ value-add (gross margin) ₹340 crore.
- Conversion costs (employees ₹90 crore + other ₹110 crore) ⇒ EBITDA ₹140 crore (14%).
- D&A ₹25 crore, interest ₹20 crore ⇒ PBT ₹95 crore; tax 25% ⇒ PAT ₹71 crore (7.1%).
- Growth to ₹1,300 crore at 120 NWC days ⇒ ΔNWC = 120/365 × ₹300 crore = ₹99 crore. Maintenance + growth capex ~₹60 crore. FCF ≈ ₹140 – ₹24 tax – ₹60 – ₹99 = **–₹43 crore**: the company needs external capital despite a 14% EBITDA margin.
- Sensitivity: a 2-percentage-point un-passed BOM increase (66% → 68%) cuts EBITDA by ₹20 crore (–14%) and PAT by ~₹15 crore (–21%).

**Worked example 2 — ETO process-equipment shop (A2), capacity worth ₹800 crore/yr [E]:**
- Fixed cost base (people, bays, overheads) ₹160 crore; variable material + sub-contract 56% of revenue.
- At 90% utilisation, revenue ₹720 crore ⇒ contribution ₹317 crore – fixed ₹160 crore ⇒ EBITDA ₹157 crore (**21.8%**).
- At 65% utilisation, revenue ₹520 crore ⇒ contribution ₹229 crore – ₹160 crore ⇒ EBITDA ₹69 crore (**13.3%**).
- That is a 28% revenue drop causing a 56% EBITDA drop, the Anup Q1 FY27 pattern (23.0% → 7.6% margin [V]).

**Worked example 3 — precision parts (A3), new ₹300 crore plant [E]:** at fixed-asset turns of 1.2× at maturity, revenue is ₹360 crore at a 35% EBITDA margin = ₹126 crore EBITDA. In year 1 (qualification) revenue might be ₹60 crore against ₹50 crore of fixed costs ⇒ EBITDA ~₹0–10 crore. Group ROCE dips until maturity in years 3–4.
