## READ THIS FIRST: twelve definitional traps

1. **Explosives tonnes vs explosives value vs AN tonnes.** Industry volumes are in tonnes of finished explosive; AN (ammonium nitrate) is the main *input* (~60–65% of a typical bulk emulsion/ANFO by weight [V Outlook/PTI: "AN about 60% of explosive composition"]; ~65% of Solar's raw-material *cost* [V CRISIL]). A tonne of AN is not a tonne of explosive. Deepak's TAN tonnes (130 kt in Q1 FY27) are input tonnes. Coal India's ~0.9 mt/yr is explosive tonnes [V].
2. **Revenue swings with AN prices — "growth" is often price, not volume.** Solar's FY24 revenue fell 12% (₹6,918 → ₹6,070 Cr) while profit rose because AN fell [V screener; primer]. Management says FY27's ex-defence growth of ~30–33% is **~10–15% volume and ~18–20% price** (crude/gas → ammonia → AN after the West Asia war) [V FY26 call 15-May-26]. Q1 FY27 domestic explosives +52% YoY is largely price [E]. Always split volume and realisation.
3. **Bulk vs packaged (cartridge) vs initiating systems.** Bulk (site-mixed emulsion/ANFO pumped into blast holes at large opencast mines — Coal India's main buy) is low ₹/t, high volume. Packaged/cartridge (small-diameter, underground, quarries, tunnels, infra) is higher ₹/t. Initiating systems (detonators — electronic, NONEL/shock-tube, (formerly) electric; detonating cord; boosters) are sold per unit and carry the highest margins. **Electric detonators were prohibited (manufacture and possession) from July 2025**, forcing a shift to electronic detonation systems (EDS) [V Solar AR FY26 MD&A].
4. **Solar's "segments" are customer baskets, not product segments.** The company reports revenue by **customer**: CIL, Non-CIL & Institutional, Housing & Infra, International, Defence, Others (FY26 PPT) [V]. In Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27 presentations this was condensed to **Domestic Explosives / International Explosives / Defence / Others** [V]. **"International" = exports from India + sales of overseas subsidiaries (plants in ~10 countries)**, not just exports. **"Defence" includes defence exports** (e.g. international ammunition orders) — so defence and international overlap conceptually but **do not double count** in the table (each rupee is in one basket).
5. **Order book ≠ order inflow ≠ revenue.** Solar's order book was ₹21,300 Cr+ (Mar-26) and **₹21,350 Cr (Jun-26)** [V PPTs]; defence ≈ ₹18,000 Cr of it. The **defence order book is ~₹6,500–7,000 Cr Indian and ~₹11,000 Cr international** (MD, Q3 FY26 call, 4-Feb-2026) [V]. Order inflow is not disclosed quarterly. Non-defence order book (~₹3,000 Cr) is mainly CIL/SCCL rate contracts — most explosives revenue is **not** order-booked at all (spot/short contracts).
6. **The "₹10,200 Cr Pinaka contract" is the MoD's total, not Solar's.** On **6-Feb-2025** (not 2026) the MoD signed contracts totalling **₹10,147 Cr** with **Economic Explosives Ltd (EEL, Solar's subsidiary — renamed Solar Defence & Aerospace Ltd, SDAL, on 17-Feb-2025)** for Area Denial Munition (ADM) Type-1 rockets, **Munitions India Ltd (MIL)** for HEPF Mk-1 (Enhanced) rockets, and **BEL** for SHAKTI software upgrades [V-snip PIB copy/Tribune/overtdefense]. Solar's share is a subset (value not separately disclosed in the sources fetched). Our primer misstates both the date and the attribution (see §3.4).
7. **Consolidated vs standalone vs SDAL.** Solar Industries India (standalone) is mainly the Nagpur explosives business; defence sits largely in **SDAL** (FY26 turnover ₹1,577 Cr, PAT ₹349 Cr — 20.1% of group profit) [V AR FY26 AOC-1]; international sits in ~30 overseas subsidiaries. Use **consolidated** for everything. Q1 FY27 PAT: **₹666 Cr consolidated** vs **~₹653 Cr attributable to owners** (minorities in Nigeria 45%, Zambia 35%, etc.) [V PPT; V-snip].
8. **EBITDA basis.** Company EBITDA FY26 **₹2,750 Cr (27.95%)** vs screener operating profit **₹2,717 Cr (28%)** [V both]. Q1 FY27: company **₹1,024 Cr (27.91%)** vs screener ₹1,015 Cr; CRISIL computes **27.7%** on its own basis [V]. Immaterial, but quote the basis.
9. **Net debt has three numbers.** Company: total debt ₹1,468 Cr, **net debt ₹867 Cr** (Mar-26), 0.32× EBITDA [V FY26 PPT]. Screener: borrowings ₹1,524 Cr − investments ₹183 Cr = ₹1,341 Cr (ignores ₹602 Cr cash and bank) [V screener; V CRISIL cash ₹602 Cr]. CRISIL: gearing 0.23×, net worth ₹6,281 Cr [V]. **Use the company ₹867 Cr.** Post-Omnia, management projects consolidated debt of **₹10,000–11,000 Cr by FY28** [V transcript].
10. **Capex has two numbers.** Company FY26 capex **₹1,556 Cr** [V PPT] vs CRISIL **~₹1,788 Cr / "~₹1,800 Cr"** [V] — CRISIL likely includes capital advances/CWIP movements or acquisitions. FY27 guidance **₹2,050 Cr** (both agree) [V].
11. **Defence production/exports are national aggregates in ₹, including DPSUs, OFB successors, private firms and (for exports) components.** India's FY26 defence production ₹1.78 lakh Cr and exports ₹38,424 Cr [V-snip MoD via press] are not "ammunition" numbers. Solar's ₹2,634 Cr FY26 defence revenue is ~1.5% of national production and ~6% of private-sector production [E].
12. **Coal production numbers conflict.** Ministry of Coal annual report: FY25 all-India **1,047.52 MT**, CIL **781.06 MT** [V]. FY26: Solar's AR says **"1,036 MT … muted … lower production in Coal India and Singareni on account of heavy monsoons"** [V AR]; a coal-market report says CIL **~768 MT (−2%)** vs an 875 MT target [V-snip]; one headline claims "record 1.15 bn t" [V-snip title only — treat as wrong/target]. **Captive & commercial: 210.46 MT (+10.2%)** [V PIB 2-Apr-26]. Use **~1.04 bn t all-India FY26 (≈ −1%)**, CIL ~768 MT, captive 210 MT.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **One listed member, three businesses, one chemistry.** Solar is simultaneously (a) India's largest commercial-explosives maker (~24% domestic share [V CRISIL]; 32 Indian plants [V CRISIL]); (b) an emerging-market multinational explosives company (manufacturing in ~11 countries, sales in 90+; FY26 international revenue ₹3,815 Cr, 39%) [V PPT; V transcript]; and (c) India's leading private defence-energetics house (HMX/RDX/TNT, composite propellants for Akash/Pinaka/BrahMos, Pinaka rockets, 30 mm/155 mm ammunition, grenades, Nagastra loitering munitions; FY26 defence revenue ₹2,634 Cr, +94%) [V PPT; V AR]. The common thread is **high-energy materials (HEM)**: the same licences, safety culture, nitration chemistry and plants.
- **FY26: domestic mining was flat; defence and international carried the year.** CIL+SCCL+private coal overburden removal was "on a negative side"; Solar's domestic explosives grew only ~3% in value (₹3,236 → ₹3,343 Cr) [V call; E]. International +32%, defence +94%. Group revenue ₹9,838 Cr (+30%), EBITDA ₹2,750 Cr (27.95%), PAT ₹1,737 Cr (+35%) [V].
- **Q1 FY27 (Jun-26) was the step change:** revenue **₹3,668 Cr (+70%)**, EBITDA ₹1,024 Cr (+82%), PAT ₹666 Cr (+89%); **domestic explosives ₹1,361 Cr (+52%), international ₹1,364 Cr (+65%), defence ₹933 Cr (+123%)** [V PPT]. Most of the explosives growth is **AN-price pass-through after the West Asia war** — AN in India went from **₹50,500/t (1-Mar-26) to ₹72,750/t (1-Apr-26), +44%**, and CIL's average explosive price from **₹39,588/t to ₹49,783/t (+26%)** [V PTI/Outlook 12-Apr-26]. Margins held (27.9%) because contracts carry rise-and-fall clauses (≈60% of a cost increase passes through immediately, more with a lag) and inventory bought pre-spike [V FY26 call].
- **The industry's input is now a policy variable.** PESO **banned all AN exports on 18-Mar-2026** "until further orders", citing the Middle East and domestic coal needs [V teamlease, notification 13015/4/2020-EXPLOSIVE]. PESO's ANRS portal changes (online R-11(a)/R-11(b) transport passes, barcoded AN bags) disrupted Deepak's Q1 FY27 TAN dispatches (−12% YoY volume) [V Deepak PPT; V-snip lexplosion]. Deepak is commissioning **Gopalpur TAN 376 KTPA in Q2 FY27** → ~1.0 MTPA, "3rd-largest pure-play TAN producer globally" [V]. AN supply is consolidating into one domestic producer (Deepak ~39% of mining-chemicals market [V-snip]) plus imports.
- **Defence is the re-rating engine, but the order book is mostly export.** Defence order book ~₹18,000 Cr: **~₹11,000 Cr international, ~₹6,500–7,000 Cr Indian** (Feb-2026) [V]. India's defence production hit **₹1.78 lakh Cr in FY26 (+15.6%)** and exports **₹38,424 Cr (+62.7%)**; private firms were 24% of production and **45.2% of exports** [V-snip MoD]. FY27 defence budget **₹7.85 lakh Cr (+15%)**, capital outlay **₹2.19 lakh Cr (+21.8%)** — the first budget after **Operation Sindoor (7–10 May 2025)** [V-snip]. Solar guides defence **>₹4,500 Cr in FY27** [V].
- **Omnia changes what Solar *is*.** If the US$1.355 bn deal closes (H1 CY2027), FY28 combined revenue would be **~₹31–32k Cr**, EBITDA **₹6,800–7,000 Cr (22–23% margin)**, with Omnia bringing **AN/nitric-acid plants in South Africa**, the BME bulk-explosives/electronic-detonator brand, and a large **agriculture** business (54% of Omnia revenue) [V transcript; V Omnia FY26]. Manufacturing footprint goes from 11 to 25+ countries [V]. Solar's own AN self-sufficiency gap — the one weakness its MD admits ("all these margins were without the backup of … ammonium nitrate") — is the strategic logic [V].
- **Valuation (screener 23-Oct-26):** m-cap **₹1,64,873 Cr** at ₹18,220 (52-wk ₹11,641–22,700); **P/E 82.7× TTM** (95× FY26); P/B 26×; EV/EBITDA **~60× FY26, ~53× TTM, ~42× FY27 guidance** [V; E]. Versus global explosives leaders at **~7–12× EV/EBITDA** (Omnia deal ~7.3× [E]) and defence leaders such as Rheinmetall. The market prices Solar as a defence-growth asset, not an explosives company.

### 1.2 Value chain (natural gas → blasted rock or a fired rocket)

1. **Gas → ammonia → nitric acid → AN.** Ammonia is the cost driver: Middle-East bulk ammonia FOB **$298/t in Jun-25 → $528 Dec-25 → $801 May-26 → $777 Jun-26** [V Deepak Q1 FY27 PPT chart]. AN is made by neutralising ammonia with nitric acid; TAN is prilled to low density for explosives.
2. **AN → explosive.** Emulsion = AN (+ sodium/calcium nitrate) solution as the oxidiser phase, fuel oil + emulsifier as the continuous phase; sensitised with gassing agents or microballoons. Solar makes its own emulsifiers, sodium nitrate, calcium nitrate and detonator components; AN is the one key input it does not make [V CRISIL].
3. **Explosive → blast.** Bulk via SME trucks; cartridge via licensed magazines and road transport (licensed vehicles, GPS/QR traceability) [V AR]. Each step requires a PESO licence (manufacture, storage/magazine, transport, possession/use) and DGMS rules at the mine.
4. **HEM → defence product.** Nitration (RDX/HMX/TNT), propellant casting (AP/HTPB), filling/integration (warheads, rockets), qualification (DGQA/DRDO trials), delivery. Long, licence- and trial-heavy.

### 1.3 Where value is captured [E unless sourced]
- **AN producer** earns the conversion spread over ammonia; very cyclical (Deepak's group EBITDA margin swung 12% → 26% in two quarters on AN/ammonia tightness [V screener]).
- **Explosive maker** earns a manufacturing + logistics + service margin; commodity in bulk (L1 tenders at CIL), better in packaged/infra, best in initiating systems and services. Solar's pre-defence group margin was **~18–20%** (management: "we will keep generating EBITDA margin around 18% to 20% even before defense") [V transcript 15-Sep-26]; global explosives margins "18–19% by and large"; Omnia's BME ~13–14% [V].
- **Defence energetics** earns the highest margin: SDAL FY26 PAT margin **22%** (₹349 Cr on ₹1,577 Cr) [V AOC-1], versus group PAT margin 17.7%. Rheinmetall's Weapon & Ammunition segment earns ~29% operating margin [V-snip].

## 2. Size and growth

### 2.1 India commercial explosives market
- **No official tonnage series is published in a form we could fetch.** PESO publishes licence data but no annual consumption series in the sources found. [H-list]
- **Bottom-up estimate [E]:** CIL subsidiaries consume **~0.9 mt/yr** [V PTI]. CIL is ~74% of India's coal (768/1,036 MT) [E]; SCCL + captive/commercial add ~275 MT (≈26%) and are similarly blasting-intensive (strip ratios differ). Coal is ~65–75% of Indian explosives demand by volume [E/K industry rule of thumb]. → **India total ≈ 1.4–1.8 mt/yr** of explosives [E].
- **Value [E]:** Solar's FY26 domestic explosives revenue = CIL ₹924 + Non-CIL & Institutional ₹1,222 + Housing & Infra ₹1,197 = **₹3,343 Cr** [V]. At CRISIL's **~24% share** [V] the market is **~₹13,900 Cr** (FY26, pre-war AN prices); with the +26% explosive price reset in Apr-2026, FY27 could be **₹16,000–18,000 Cr** before volume growth [E]. Cross-check: 1.4–1.8 mt × ₹45–55k/t ≈ ₹6,300–9,900 Cr for explosives plus ₹2,500–4,000 Cr of initiating systems/accessories ≈ ₹9,000–14,000 Cr [E]. **State the range ₹10,000–14,000 Cr (FY26).** USD: ~$1.1–1.5 bn [E].

## 6. Business-model archetypes and their economics

| Archetype | Example | Revenue model | Margin (EBITDA) | ROCE | Key risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. CIL-tender bulk supplier** | Keltech, IDL, small players | L1 rate contracts with price-variation formulas on AN | 5–12% | 15–25% if asset-light | L1 price wars, PVC lag, AN availability |
| **2. Integrated domestic + international explosives major** | Solar (ex-defence) | Bulk + packaged + IS + services; plants near mines in India/Africa/Turkey | 18–24% | 25–35% | AN spikes, FX/country risk (Zambia, Nigeria, Turkey hyperinflation) |
| **3. AN-integrated global major** | Orica, Dyno Nobel, Enaex, Omnia, AECI, Maxam | Owns AN plants + explosives + services + digital | 13–20% | 8–15% (big asset bases) | Ammonia/gas costs, mining cycle, plant outages |
| **4. Defence energetics specialist** | SDAL (Solar), MIL, Premier (defence part), Rheinmetall W&A | Contracted rockets, ammunition, propellants, HEM; milestone/delivery billing, advances | 20–35% | 30%+ (Solar) / lower for DPSUs | Trials/qualification delays, single buyer, export licensing |

**Economics differ because of three things:** (i) **who bears AN risk** (archetype 1 is hostage to the PVC formula lag; 2 and 3 have broader pass-through and inventory buffers; 5 *benefits* from AN spikes); (ii) **capital intensity** (AN plants cost ₹2,000–2,700 Cr for ~350–380 KTPA [V Deepak Gopalpur ₹2,675 Cr]; an explosives bulk plant costs tens of crores [E]); (iii) **customer power** (CIL L1 vs the MoD's single-buyer, vs dispersed private miners).

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Explosives regime (civil)
- **Explosives Act, 1884** and **Explosives Rules, 2008** — administered by **PESO** (Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation, under DPIIT/Ministry of Commerce & Industry), headed by the Chief Controller of Explosives (Nagpur). Licences for manufacture (quantity-specific), possession, sale, transport, import/export; magazine licences; Form-based daily accounts [K; V CRISIL "industrial licensing … Chief Controller of Explosives and the Directorate General of Mines Safety"].
- **Explosive Substances Act, 1908** (criminal law) and **MHA** oversight of security; police verification for magazine/transport licences [K].
- **DGMS** (Directorate General of Mines Safety) — use of explosives in mines (Coal Mines Regulations 2017, Metalliferous Mines Regulations 1961) [K].
- **Ammonium Nitrate Rules, 2012** (post-Hyderabad 2013 / Beirut 2020 tightening) — AN with >45% AN content treated as an explosive: licences to manufacture, store, transport, sell [V CRISIL]. **ANRS** (Ammonium Nitrate Return System) online portal: R-11(a) advance intimation integrated with R-11(b) transport pass; **barcoding of AN bags** under Rule 9 [V-snip lexplosion]. The 2026 ANRS changes disrupted Deepak's dispatches [V].
- **AN export ban: PESO notification 18-Mar-2026** (complete ban; licences in abeyance; until further orders) [V].
- **Electric detonators prohibited** (manufacture and possession) — transition completed **July 2025** → EDS [V Solar AR].
- **QR-coding of explosives and GPS-tracking of transport vehicles** mandated [V AR].
- **Explosives Bill, 2025** ("Explosives Regulation Bill" in Solar's AR): DPIIT draft to repeal the 1884 Act; comments closed 17-Jul-2025; central government to prescribe the licensing authority; higher penalties (up to 3 years / ₹1 lakh for illegal manufacture/import/export) [V-snip Deccan Herald/lexplosion]. **Enactment status unknown as of Oct-2026** [H-list].
- **Licences are scarce and valuable:** Solar paid ₹115 Cr for Sivasakthi, a licensed company with nil revenue [V] — a market price for a licensed explosives entity.

### 7.2 Defence regime
- **DAP 2020** (Defence Acquisition Procedure) — categories Buy (Indian-IDDM), Make-I/II/III, iDEX; **DAP 2026** revision in progress [K; FLAG]. **Positive Indigenisation Lists** (5 lists for services, 5 for DPSUs; items embargoed for import after set dates) [K].

### 9.2 Pass-through mechanics (the one KPI the market watches)
- **Rise-and-fall clauses** in contracts pass ~**60% of an AN increase** immediately; some contracts reprice after a quarter [V FY26 call: "price goes up by, example, $200, but you will get only on the price escalation based only for, say, 60%"].
- Historic sensitivity: when RM rose **50–70%** (2022), Solar's EBITDA margin fell **2–3 points** [V].
- Inventory: Q4 FY26 inventory built ahead of the war; inventory days 95 → 130; "inventory gain in the coming quarters" [V].
- **AN-price elasticity [E]:** AN ≈ 65% of RM; RM ≈ 50% of sales → AN ≈ 32% of sales. A 44% AN increase with 60% pass-through and no other action = 0.32 × 0.44 × 0.40 ≈ **5.6% of sales of unrecovered cost** on the explosives book — offset by lagged repricing, inventory gains and defence mix. Q1 FY27 margin held at 27.9% → the offsets worked [E].

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Explosives:** Revenue = Σ (tonnes × realisation/t) + (detonators × ₹/unit) + services; EBITDA = Revenue − AN cost (AN tonnes × AN price × (1 − pass-through lag)) − other RM − freight − conversion costs.
**Defence:** Revenue = Σ (order book × execution rate); EBITDA = Revenue × contract margin (20–35%), timing set by trials and inspections.

### C.1 Worked example — one tonne of bulk emulsion to CIL, before and after the AN shock [E composite]
| ₹ per tonne of explosive | Feb-2026 | Apr-2026 (after reset) |
|---|---|---|
| CIL average price | 39,588 [V] | 49,783 [V] |
| AN (0.62 t × AN price) | 0.62 × 50,500 = 31,310 | 0.62 × 72,750 = 45,105 |
| Other RM (sodium/calcium nitrate, fuel, emulsifier, gassing) | 3,000 | 3,600 |
| Freight, SME truck, site service | 2,200 | 2,600 |
| Conversion (power, labour, overhead) | 1,500 | 1,500 |
| **Contribution/EBITDA** | **1,578 (4.0%)** | **−3,022 (−6.1%)** if CIL price is only +26% while AN +44% |
**Read:** at CIL's notified bulk price, a pure bulk supplier with no AN hedge would be underwater after April-2026 — which is why PVC formulas, inventory and product mix matter, and why small L1 suppliers struggle. (The AN fraction and cost lines are composites; CIL's price is an average across products, so real bulk margins differ — FLAG.)
