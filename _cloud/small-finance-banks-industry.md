## READ THIS FIRST: twelve definitional traps

1. **Gross advances vs GLP vs AUM vs on-book advances — every bank uses a different base.**
   - **AU** "Gross loan portfolio (GLP)" ₹1,44,250 Cr (Jun-26) **includes ₹3,789 Cr of assigned (off-book) loans**; it excludes ₹1,010 Cr of "credit substitutes" (NBFC CPs/NCDs held as investments). GNPA is computed on gross advances (on-book) [V].
   - **Equitas** "Gross advances" ₹47,641 Cr **includes IBPC sold, securitisation and direct assignment (DA)**; ex the ₹837 Cr DA *bought* in Q3 FY26, growth is 24% not 27% [V deck]. GNPA is quoted both excluding (2.36%) and including (2.31%) the securitisation book; screener shows 2.42% (a third basis) [V].
   - **Jana** "Advances" ₹37,612 Cr include IBPC & securitisation; balance-sheet advances are ₹34,699 Cr [V]. Our primer calls it "AUM ₹36,289 Cr" (Mar-26).
   - **Ujjivan** "Gross loan book (GLB)" ₹45,699 Cr includes ₹130 Cr securitisation; balance-sheet advances ₹44,753 Cr [V].
   - **Rule for the writer:** use each bank's headline loan figure (GLP/gross advances/GLB/advances) in the peer table and label it "gross loan book (company definition)". Use balance-sheet figures only for leverage/ROA arithmetic.
2. **NIM has (at least) four definitions in this group.**
   - AU: on daily-average interest-earning assets **including off-book** (assigned) loans — 5.9% Q1 FY27 [V].
   - Equitas: **changed methodology in Q1 FY27** from average of opening/closing to daily-average interest-earning assets; prior quarters restated. New basis 7.24% vs old basis 7.07% for the same quarter [V deck]. Our primer's FY25 "6.64%" is old-basis.
   - Ujjivan: 8.4% Q2 FY27 (on average interest-earning assets) [V].
   - Jana: 7.5% Q1 FY27 [V].
   - NIM on total assets (DuPont) is ~0.6–1.0 pp lower: Equitas NII/ATA 6.60% vs NIM 7.24% [V deck].
   - **Do not rank banks on NIM without noting that the MFI-heritage banks earn 7–8.5% because 25–50% of their books yield 20–25%, while AU's 5.9% reflects a 92% secured book.**
3. **Credit cost: on assets vs on advances, quarterly vs annualised, gross vs net of recoveries, with or without CGFMU premium.**
   - AU: credit cost **0.8% of average total assets incl. CGFMU premium** (Q1 FY27, annualised); FY26 0.96% of ATA [V]. On advances FY26 it is ~1.26% [E: ₹1,615 Cr ÷ avg GLP ₹1,28,016 Cr].
   - Ujjivan: **changed its credit-cost methodology from average GLB to average balance sheet (ATA)** for FY27 guidance (0.9–1.0% of ATA) [V deck footnote]. Q2 FY27 0.7% of ATA.
   - Equitas: 1.37% Q1 FY27 on advances (annualised) [V]; DuPont 1.03% of ATA.
   - **Jana's "Net credit cost % of average GLP" (0.45% Q1 FY27) is quarterly, NOT annualised, and net of bad-debt recoveries booked in other income** — annualised it is ~1.8% (₹166 Cr × 4 ÷ avg GLP ~₹37,000 Cr) [E from V deck figures]. Gross provisions were ₹178 Cr.
4. **PCR with vs without technical write-offs (TWO).**
   - AU: 85% *including* TWO; **~64% excluding TWO** (~70% adding guarantee cover and contingency provisions) at Mar-26 [V].
   - Equitas: **71.02% ex-TWO, 86.96% incl. TWO** [V].
   - Ujjivan: 85% (Q2 FY27), which *includes ₹130 Cr of floating provision earmarked for PCR* [V deck].
   - Jana: **62.64%** — appears to be ex-TWO, the lowest in the group [V].
   - Never compare AU's 85% with Jana's 63%; they are different definitions.
5. **GNPA/NNPA on screener vs company decks differ.** Jana Jun-26: deck 2.24%/0.85% (incl. IBPC & securitisation in the denominator) vs screener 2.39%/0.91% [V both]. Equitas: deck 2.36% vs screener 2.42%. AU and Ujjivan match. Use company figures in prose; note the basis in tables.
6. **CGFMU-covered MFI books lower the true loss but not reported GNPA.** AU: 96% of its MFI book is CGFMU-covered; ~7.7% of gross advances under government guarantee schemes [V]. Jana: ~80% of the unsecured book under CGFMU/CGTMSE; expects a ₹65 Cr claim by Q3 FY27 [V]. Ujjivan: ₹970 Cr covered at Sep-26, ₹1,306 Cr after ₹336 Cr fresh coverage [V]. The guarantee premium (~1–1.5% p.a. of covered book [K, approximate]) sits in opex or credit cost depending on the bank — AU shows cost-to-assets **"ex CGFMU premium"** (4.0%) and credit cost **"incl. CGFMU premium"** [V].
7. **Jana's PAT has zero tax and a DTA history.** Jana pays **no tax** (carried-forward losses from the 2017–19 Janalakshmi collapse); FY24 PAT ₹670 Cr included a large **deferred tax asset recognition** — screener shows Q4 FY24 PBT ₹166 Cr → PAT ₹322 Cr (tax −93%), i.e. a ~₹156 Cr DTA credit, and FY24 tax −30% [V screener, E arithmetic]. FY24 PAT ex-DTA was ~₹514 Cr (= PBT). **Compare Jana on PBT, or tax-adjust it at 25%, when ranking RoA/RoE.** At a 25% normal tax, FY26 PAT ₹326 Cr becomes ~₹245 Cr and Q1 FY27 RoA 1.4% becomes ~1.05% [E].
8. **AU's FY24 base is pre-Fincare; FY25 onward includes Fincare.** AU's deck notes "FY24 numbers are on standalone basis" and Mar-24 deposit figures are shown "on a proforma merged basis" [V]. Screener's FY25 jump (revenue ₹10,555 → ₹16,064 Cr; deposits ₹87,182 → ₹1,24,269 Cr) is ~40% merger, not organic. **Never quote AU's FY24→FY25 growth without "incl. Fincare".**
9. **Equitas FY26 PAT (₹103 Cr) hides a Q1 FY26 loss of ₹224 Cr** caused by a one-time ₹185 Cr stress provision on standard MFI assets plus ~₹145 Cr from tightened NPA norms [V deck]. Q2–Q4 FY26 PAT summed ₹327 Cr; Q1 FY27 ₹184 Cr [V screener]. FY26 "RoE 1.7%" is a trough artefact.
10. **Ujjivan's book has three different "microfinance" numbers.** Q2 FY27: **JLG microfinance ₹11,274 Cr (25%)**; **group loans** incl. non-MF group loans ₹15,658 Cr (34%); **"micro banking" (GL + IL)** ₹22,037 Cr (48%) [V deck]. Secured share 52% (₹23,803 Cr). Our primer says "roughly half its loan book is JLG microfinance" — that is the micro-banking number, not JLG.
11. **"Cost of funds" vs "cost of deposits"; "retail deposits" definitions differ.** Jana quotes CoF 7.4% and "cost of deposits" 7.44% [V]. Jana "retail deposits" = CASA + TDs < ₹3 Cr (64.9%) [V]; Equitas "retail" = CASA + retail TD per RBI definition (66%) [V]; AU uses "stable deposits" = CASA + retail TD + non-callable bulk TD (79%) — a broader measure [V]; Ujjivan "Retail TD + CASA" ₹33,807 Cr (67%) [V].
12. **Crisil's "SFB sector" totals usually exclude AU.** Crisil's Oct-2025 call that "SFB advances will cross ₹2 lakh crore in FY26" cannot include AU (AU alone was ₹1.40 lakh Cr at Mar-26; the four members alone were ₹2.63 lakh Cr) [V-snip; E]. ICRA's sector numbers (GNPA 3.6–3.9%) are also a specific rated universe. **State the universe every time a "sector" number is used.**

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Four listed members; two business models; one regulator-shaped licence.**
  - **AU** is a ₹1.44 lakh Cr, ~92% secured retail/commercial lender (vehicles, mortgages/LAP, MSME, gold) that happens to hold an SFB licence and is about to leave it. It is **53% of the group's loan book, 54% of deposits, 70% of FY26 PAT and 75% of market cap** [E: sums below].
  - **Ujjivan, Equitas and Jana** are "MFI-heritage" SFBs — all born from microfinance NBFCs in the 2015 licence round — each 2–3 years into a forced march from unsecured group loans to secured lending: secured share **Ujjivan 52% (Sep-26), Jana 72.8% (Jun-26), Equitas ~85% (MFI 13% + small unsecured)** [V].
  - **All four, FY26 (straight sums, screener standalone):** deposits **₹2,80,646 Cr** (+19.9% YoY); gross loan book (company definitions, Mar-26) **₹2,63,436 Cr**; NII **₹18,968 Cr** (+9.9%); PAT **₹3,763 Cr** (+8.1%; still −12% vs FY24's ₹4,285 Cr); net worth **₹37,384 Cr**; market cap **₹94,463 Cr** on 22-Oct-2026 → **2.5× aggregate book, 25× FY26 PAT, ~16× annualised Q1 FY27 PAT** [E].
- **The 2024–26 microfinance (JLG) stress cycle is over for these four — and it was a P&L event, not a solvency event.**
  - Industry: MFI GLP shrank for **seven consecutive quarters** from a Mar-2024 peak (~₹4.4 lakh Cr [K]) to **₹3,14,728 Cr in Dec-2025** [V-snip MFIN], then grew **+3% QoQ to ₹3,25,174 Cr (Mar-26)** and **+1.1% to ₹3,28,708 Cr (Jun-26)** [V MFIN]. **PAR 31–180 fell from 6.3% (Mar-25) → 5.6% (Jun-25) → 2.0% (Mar-26) → 1.6% (Jun-26)** [V MFIN].
  - Company: Equitas's MFI net-slippage ratio fell from **20.3% (Q1 FY26) to 2.8% (Q1 FY27)**, MFI 1–90 DPD **8.05% → 1.10%** [V]; Ujjivan PAR **4.81% (Jun-25) → 3.57% (Sep-26)**, PCR 73% → 85%, X-bucket collection efficiency 99.7% [V]; Jana slippages ₹590 Cr peak (Q2 FY26) → ₹291 Cr (Q1 FY27) [V]; AU slippages −22% YoY, credit cost 1.4% → 0.8% of ATA [V].
  - Profits: **Q1 FY27 PAT ₹1,452 Cr vs ₹562 Cr in Q1 FY26 (+158%)**; MFI-heritage trio **₹656 Cr vs −₹19 Cr** [E: sums of screener]. Ujjivan's Q2 FY27 PAT ₹365 Cr was its highest ever (RoA 2.4%, RoE 19.7%) [V].
- **Funding is the structural disadvantage; the 2025 rate cuts helped SFBs *more* than banks this time, and the Oct-2026 hike starts to reverse it.**
  - Cost of funds Q1 FY27: **AU 6.48%, Ujjivan 6.9%, Equitas 7.05%, Jana 7.4%** [V] vs ~4.9–5.5% for large private banks [K/E] — a 150–250 bp penalty paid for an unknown brand, small branch network and DICGC-sized retail tickets.
  - CASA: **AU 29%, Equitas 25%, Ujjivan 26%, Jana 19%** [V] vs ~35–40% for large private banks [K]. SFB-sector CASA fell from **35% (Mar-22) to 27% (Sep-25)** (ICRA) [V].
  - SFB liabilities are mostly **fixed-rate term deposits that reprice over 12–24 months**, while their loans are largely **fixed-rate** (MFI, vehicles, gold) — so a falling repo is good for SFB NIMs with a lag (CoF down 50–75 bp YoY at every member) and a rising repo (Oct-2026) is a slow headwind. AU's NIM: "margins recovered after initial rate-cut-led fall" (FY26 5.65% vs FY25 5.94%; Q4 FY26 5.96%) [V]. Equitas's CoF already turned **up 11 bp QoQ** in Q1 FY27 "due to upward revisions in SA and TD rates" and management guides NIM down to ~7.1% [V].
- **Universal-bank optionality is now a two-speed story.** AU has in-principle approval (Aug-2025) and a final-licence application on file (Mar-2026); Ujjivan's and Jana's applications were returned (Jana Oct-2025; both reported 13-Apr-2026) for MFI concentration; Equitas paused [V/V-sec]. The binding criterion turned out to be **qualitative portfolio diversification**, not the published quantitative tests (₹1,000 Cr net worth, GNPA ≤3%, NNPA ≤1%, two years' profit) that Ujjivan and Jana arguably met.
- **Regulation has loosened at the margin:** RBI cut the SFB **PSL target from 75% to 60% of ANBC from FY26** (circular 20-Jun-2025) [V-snip] — all four of our primers still say 75%. Self-regulatory **MFIN Guardrails** (≤3 lenders per borrower, ₹2 lakh total-indebtedness cap from Apr-2025 [K]) and state laws (Karnataka ordinance Feb-2025 [K]; Bihar Bill Feb-2026 [V]) tightened the MFI side.
- **Valuations price AU as a franchise and the rest as cyclicals** (screener 22-Oct-26): AU **3.5× book, 24.8× TTM P/E** at 15–17% RoE; Ujjivan **1.6×**, 10.2× TTM at ~19% current RoE; Equitas **1.24×**, 14.9× TTM; Jana **0.96×**, 11.4× TTM (zero-tax PAT) — Jana trades **below book** with a promoter in NCD default and a strategic investor awaiting RBI approval.

## 1. What the sub-sector does: products, value chain, where value is captured

### 1.1 What an SFB is (licence anatomy)
- A **small finance bank** is a scheduled commercial bank created under RBI's **27-Nov-2014 SFB licensing guidelines**; 10 in-principle licences were granted in **Sep-2015** (AU Financiers, Capital Local Area Bank, Disha Microfin (→ Fincare), Equitas, ESAF, Janalakshmi, RGVN (→ North East SFB), Suryoday, Ujjivan, Utkarsh) [K]. "On-tap" SFB licensing guidelines followed on **5-Dec-2019** [K], under which Shivalik (an urban co-op bank, converted 2021) and Unity (Centrum–BharatPe, 2021, absorbing PMC Bank) came in [K].
- The licence trades **a deposit franchise and full banking powers** for a **down-market mandate**:

| Rule | SFB | Universal bank | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Priority-sector lending (PSL) | **60% of ANBC from FY26** (was 75%; 40% core per sub-targets + 20% to any PSL sub-sector of choice) [V-snip RBI/2025-26/61, 20-Jun-2025] | 40% | Forces lending to agriculture, MSE, weaker sections; generates PSLC fee income (Jana ₹19 Cr in Q1 FY27 [V]) |
| Small-ticket rule | ≥50% of loan portfolio in loans ≤ ₹25 lakh [K] | None | Keeps books granular; caps big-ticket MSME/corporate drift |
| Minimum CRAR | **15%** (Tier-1 ≥ 7.5%) [K] | 11.5% incl. buffers (CET1 8%) [K] | Idle capital lowers RoE by ~1–2 pp at the same RoA |
| Unbanked rural centres | ≥25% of banking outlets [K] | Same norm in branch authorisation (25% URC) but less binding | Higher cost to serve |
| Exposure limits | 10%/15% of capital funds single/group [K] | 20%/25% (LEF) [K] | Limits large-ticket lending |
| Minimum paid-up capital | ₹200 Cr (on-tap, 2019) [K] | ₹1,000 Cr (on-tap universal, 2016) [K] | — |
| Deposits insured | DICGC **₹5 lakh** per depositor per bank [K] | Same | Makes a ₹5 lakh FD at an SFB as safe as at SBI — the core of SFB retail deposit marketing |
| Third-party products / forex | Allowed (AD-II/limited) [K] | Full | Fee income smaller |

### 1.3 The value chain (₹ flows)
1. **Raise:** retail term deposits (12–36 months, 7–8% for SFBs), savings (5–7% teaser tiers at SFBs vs 2.5–3% at big banks [K/E]), current accounts (0%), bulk TDs (from corporates, governments, trusts, other banks — Equitas: banks are 20% of TD by category [V]), certificates of deposit, **refinance from NABARD/SIDBI/NHB/MUDRA** (Equitas ₹5,722 Cr, Jana ₹4,262 Cr — "competitive to deposit rates with no CRR and SLR" [V Jana]), Tier-2 bonds.
2. **Hold:** CRR (3% of NDTL after the 2025 cut [K]) and SLR (18% [K]) — **~20% of deposits earn little or government-bond yields**; LCR buffers (AU 119%, Jana 153%, Ujjivan 131%, Equitas 178%) [V].
3. **Originate:** through branches (AU 2,920 touchpoints; Equitas 1,063 outlets; Ujjivan 880 branches; Jana 826 outlets) [V], business correspondents (Equitas 366 BCs [V]; Jana's BC book), DSAs and digital.
4. **Collect:** weekly/fortnightly centre meetings for JLG (cash-heavy; Ujjivan ~45–50% of micro-banking collections now cashless [V deck chart]); NACH/UPI for retail.
5. **Recover:** SARFAESI on property, repossession of vehicles, CGFMU/CGTMSE guarantee claims on unsecured micro loans, ARC sales (Equitas sold ₹216 Cr + ₹55 Cr NPAs to ARCs in Q2–Q3 FY26 [V]).
6. **Earn fees:** processing fees, insurance and MF distribution (Equitas LI+GI premium ₹25 Cr/quarter, MF AUM ₹518 Cr [V]), PSL certificates, cards.

### 1.4 Where value is captured
- In the **spread between a 13.7–17.4% portfolio yield and a 6.5–7.4% cost of funds** (AU 13.7%/6.48%; Ujjivan 17.4%/6.9%; Equitas 15.74%/7.05%; Jana 16.4%/7.4%) [V], less **opex of 4–6.4% of assets** — the highest in Indian banking because tickets are tiny and collection is physical.
- Over a cycle the MFI book captures the highest *gross* margin but gives back 3–8% of the book in bad cycles (Equitas MFI net slippage 20% annualised in Q1 FY26 [V]); **secured retail (vehicles, LAP, housing, gold) is where through-cycle RoA is earned** — which is exactly why every MFI-heritage SFB is migrating there and why AU is valued at 3.5× book.

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 The licence and its rules (see §1.1 table)
- **SFB licensing guidelines (27-Nov-2014)** and **on-tap SFB guidelines (5-Dec-2019)**: promoters must hold ≥40% for 5 years and dilute to 30% within 10 and 15% within 15 years; listing mandatory within 3 years of reaching ₹500 Cr net worth [K]. Equitas and Ujjivan used **holdco reverse mergers** to satisfy promoter-dilution/listing rules (Equitas Holdings → ESFB 2023; UFSL → Ujjivan SFB 2024) — that is why both show **0% promoter** today [V screener; K].
- **PSL: 75% → 60% of ANBC from FY26** (RBI/2025-26/61, 20-Jun-2025): core 40% sub-sector allocation unchanged; the "additional" 35% cut to 20%, deployable in any PSL sub-sector where the bank has an advantage [V-snip]. CareEdge read it as supporting diversification amid MFI stress; SFB stocks rose up to 4% on 23-Jun-2025 [V-snip].
- **50% of loans ≤ ₹25 lakh** — unchanged [K].
- **CRAR 15%** — unchanged; Tier-1 7.5% [K].

### 7.2 Voluntary transition to universal bank (RBI, 26-Apr-2024)
- **Eligibility** (as published) [K; criteria confirmed V-sec c4scourses]: (i) scheduled status with a satisfactory track record of **≥5 years**; (ii) shares **listed**; (iii) minimum **net worth ₹1,000 Cr** (the universal on-tap level); (iv) meets prescribed CRAR; (v) **net profit in each of the last two financial years**; (vi) **GNPA ≤ 3% and NNPA ≤ 1% in each of the last two financial years**; (vii) **diversified loan portfolio** (the qualitative test that tripped Ujjivan and Jana). Existing promoters may continue; no new lock-in; promoter-dilution/NOFHC rules of the universal on-tap regime apply case by case.
- **Applications and outcomes:**
  - **AU** — applied 3-Sep-2024; **in-principle 7-Aug-2025**; NOFHC condition replaced (Q4 FY26); **final-licence application Mar-2026; pending** [V/V-sec].
  - **Ujjivan** — applied **4-Feb-2025** [V-snip company PR]; reported **returned 13-Apr-2026** for MFI concentration [V-sec, single secondary; our primer (29-Jul-26) says "RBI has asked for a more diversified loan mix before it will consider" — consistent].
  - **Jana** — applied **Jun-2025**; **returned 28-Oct-2025** for not meeting eligibility (BS) [V-snip]; the April-2026 report also lists Jana [V-sec] — possibly a second application or a mis-report; FLAG.
  - **Equitas** — eligible on paper (GNPA ~2.75%, NNPA ~0.92% at Dec-2025) but **deliberately paused** after peer rejections [primer]; note FY26 profit was only ₹103 Cr with a Q1 FY26 loss — "net profit in each of last two years" is met on a full-year basis.
- **What conversion changes:** PSL 60% → 40%; CRAR 15% → 11.5%; no ≤₹25 lakh rule; higher exposure limits; full forex/AD-I; brand → deposit cost. AU's deck lists "stronger perception as a universal bank helps attract and retain best-in-class talent at a lower cost" and cheaper funds as benefits [V]. AU says no change in strategy for 3–5 years and no large-corporate push [V-sec].

### 7.3 Microfinance regulation
- **RBI Microfinance Directions (Mar-2022, effective Apr-2022)** [K]: microfinance = collateral-free loan to a household with annual income ≤ ₹3 lakh; repayment obligations (all loans) capped at **50% of household income**; pricing deregulated (board-approved policy) for all REs. MFIN (Jun-2026) wants the ₹3 lakh threshold revised and complains that bank MF loans are being reported to retail bureaus instead [V].
- **NBFC-MFI qualifying-asset norm cut from 75% to 60% (Jun-2025)** [K] — relevant to SFBs' NBFC-MFI competitors.
- **MFIN Guardrails** (SRO; members = NBFC-MFIs, banks, SFBs): Guardrails 1.0 (Jul/Aug-2024) capped lenders per borrower at **4** and total MFI indebtedness at **₹2 lakh**; Guardrails 2.0 from **1-Apr-2025** cut lenders to **3** (unsecured retail counted), barred lending to borrowers with PAR 60+ over ₹3,000 elsewhere, etc. [K; Ujjivan deck confirms "disbursement allowed up to 60 DPD with other lenders" rule V]. Effect visible at Ujjivan: borrowers with 4+ lenders fell to 0.4% of the GL book; "unique to Ujjivan" rose from 51.0% (Sep-25) to 57.7% (Sep-26) [V]; Jana 97% of new customers have ≤2 MFI lenders vs 91% industry [V].
- **State laws:**
  - **Karnataka Micro Loan and Small Loan (Prevention of Coercive Actions) Ordinance, Feb-2025** (later enacted) after reports of coercive recovery and suicides; registration and penalties for unregistered lenders; RBI REs largely carved out but collections disrupted [K]. **Karnataka is still the worst MFI state for Ujjivan: GL PAR 90+ 5.4% vs 3.2% book average (Sep-26)** [V].
  - **Tamil Nadu** Money Lending Entities (Prevention of Coercive Actions) Act, 2025 [K].
  - **Bihar Micro Finance Institutions (Regulation of Money Lending and Prevention of Coercive Actions) Bill, 2026** — passed by the Assembly **26-Feb-2026**; s.2(2) excludes RBI-regulated entities; borrower-protection and fair-recovery provisions apply to all lenders [V MFIN].
  - Earlier precedent: **Andhra Pradesh MFI Ordinance, Oct-2010** — the event that destroyed the AP MFI industry [K].
- **Credit guarantees:**
  - **CGFMU** (Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units, NCGTC) — covers MUDRA-eligible micro loans (≤₹10 lakh; ₹20 lakh "Tarun Plus" from 2024 [K]); used by AU (96% of MFI book), Jana (~80% of unsecured), Ujjivan (₹1,306 Cr cover) [V]. It is a portfolio guarantee with a first-loss and a cap, paid by annual premium [K, mechanics approximate; FLAG].
  - **CGTMSE** for MSE loans (up to ₹10 Cr from 2025 [K]); Jana cites "CGFMU/CGTMSE" [V].
  - **CGSMFI 2.0** — government guarantee on bank loans *to* NBFC-MFIs; extended till **Aug-2026** [V MFIN].

- **Expected Credit Loss (ECL) framework** — RBI's draft (Oct-2025) proposes ECL for banks from **1-Apr-2027** [K, verify]; SFBs' unsecured books would need higher stage-2 provisions — a FY28 risk.
- **Labour codes** — one-time provisions in Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27 (AU ₹20 Cr pre-tax; Equitas ₹29.5 Cr) [V].
- **Gold-loan directions (2025)** — LTV slabs (up to 85% for small tickets) [K].
- **Deposit insurance:** DICGC ₹5 lakh (since Feb-2020) [K]; discussions on raising it periodically.

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **2010–2014: AP crisis and the MFI → bank idea.** The Oct-2010 Andhra Pradesh ordinance froze MFI repayments; the Malegam Committee (2011) led to NBFC-MFI regulation [K]. Bandhan got a universal licence (2014) and the RBI created SFBs (2014 guidelines) to give MFIs and local NBFCs deposits [K].
2. **2015–2017: licence round and launch.** Ten in-principle licences (Sep-2015); banks opened 2016–17 (Ujjivan Feb-2017; AU Apr-2017; Equitas Sep-2016; Jana Mar-2018) [K; V AU deck "commenced operations as an SFB 2017"].
3. **2016–2019: demonetisation.** The Nov-2016 note ban hit cash-collected MFI books; Janalakshmi — then India's largest MFI — nearly failed, needing emergency equity; Jana lost ₹2,504 Cr in FY18 and ₹1,949 Cr in FY19 [V screener].
4. **2020–2022: COVID moratoria and restructuring.** Ujjivan lost ₹415 Cr in FY22 [V screener]; Equitas' profit dipped (FY22 ₹281 Cr); AU stayed profitable (₹1,130 Cr) [V].
5. **FY23–FY24: the boom.** Post-COVID MFI credit boom; MFI GLP peaked ~₹4.4 lakh Cr (Mar-2024) [K]; **record PAT** — Ujjivan ₹1,281 Cr (FY24, RoE 27%), Equitas ₹799 Cr, Jana ₹670 Cr (incl. DTA), AU ₹1,535 Cr [V screener]. Listings: Utkarsh (Jul-2023), ESAF (Nov-2023), Jana and Capital (Feb-2024) [K].
6. **Apr-2024: AU–Fincare merger** effective 1-Apr-2024 (all-stock; Fincare promoter infused ~₹700 Cr pre-merger [K]) — added ~₹16,000–17,000 Cr of loans (heavily MFI/micro-LAP/gold) and ~1,200 touchpoints [K/E] just as the MFI cycle turned. AU: "Expanded distribution in last 2 years, aided by Fincare acquisition"; 176 MFI branches came with it; touchpoints 727 → 1,909 (deposits) [V].
7. **FY25–H1 FY26: the MFI stress cycle.** Over-leverage (borrowers with 4+ lenders), post-election cash disruption, heatwaves, Karnataka coercion crisis (early 2025) [K]. Industry PAR 31–180 rose from 2.5% (Jun-24) to **6.3% (Mar-25)** [V]. SFB GNPA 2.4% → **3.6% (Mar-25) → 3.9% (Sep-25)** (ICRA universe) [V]. Members: Ujjivan PAT −43% (FY25), Equitas −82% (FY25) and a Q1 FY26 loss, Jana FY26 PAT −35% vs FY25; ESAF and Utkarsh into losses [V].
8. **2025: rate cuts and regulatory relief.** Repo −125 bp, CRR −100 bp; PSL 75% → 60%; AU in-principle UB licence (Aug-2025) [V-snip/V].
9. **H2 FY26–H1 FY27: recovery.** MFI PAR 31–180 down to 1.6% (Jun-26); disbursements back; members' Q1 FY27 PAT up 158% YoY; Ujjivan record Q2 FY27 [V].
10. **Oct-2026: first rate hike since 2023** (repo 5.50%) — deposit-cost tailwind ending [V-snip].

## 9. Unit economics and native KPIs (benchmarks in §F)

### 9.1 The KPI stack (definitions)
- **Gross loan book / GLP / AUM** — loans outstanding incl. off-book (assigned/securitised/IBPC) per company definition (trap 1).
- **Disbursement** — new loans in the period; MFI disbursement growth is the earliest cycle indicator (Ujjivan Q2 FY27 MF disbursement −2% YoY while secured +58% [V]).
- **Secured share %** — secured loans ÷ gross book. AU ~92% [E: 1 − (7,488 + 3,145) ÷ 1,44,250]; Equitas ~85–86% [E/primer]; Jana 72.8%; Ujjivan 52% [V].
- **MFI share %** — JLG (+IL) ÷ book: AU 5.2%, Equitas 13% (incl. DA), Jana ~9% group + 18% individual = 27% unsecured, Ujjivan 25% JLG / 48% micro banking [V].
- **Yield on advances** — interest on loans ÷ average loans (AU 13.7%, Equitas 15.74%, Jana 16.4%, Ujjivan 17.4%) [V].
- **Cost of funds (CoF)** — interest expense ÷ average interest-bearing liabilities (AU 6.48%, Ujjivan 6.9%, Equitas 7.05%, Jana 7.4%) [V].
- **NIM** — NII ÷ average interest-earning assets (AU 5.9%, Equitas 7.24%, Jana 7.5%, Ujjivan 8.4%) [V] (trap 2).
- **CASA %** — (CA + SA) ÷ deposits (AU 29%, Ujjivan 26%, Equitas 25%, Jana 19%) [V].
- **Retail / stable deposit share** — trap 11 (AU stable 79%; Equitas retail 66%; Ujjivan retail TD + CASA 67%; Jana retail 64.9%) [V].
- **CD ratio** — advances ÷ deposits (ex-refinance variant) [V].
- **Opex/ATA and cost-to-income** — AU 4.0% / ~58%; Equitas 5.61% / 68.4%; Jana ~5.8% / 67.0%; Ujjivan ~6.6% / 62% [V/E].
- **Credit cost** — provisions ÷ ATA or ÷ average advances (trap 3).
- **GNPA / NNPA / PCR** — trap 4–5. **Slippage ratio** — fresh NPAs ÷ opening standard advances (Equitas net slippage 1.43% annualised; AU slippages ₹798 Cr in Q1 FY27 ≈ 2.2% annualised on GLP [E]).
- **PAR 0 / PAR 30 / PAR 90** (MFI books) — % of portfolio with any amount overdue > N days. **Collection efficiency (X-bucket)** — collections from current borrowers ÷ demand (Ujjivan 99.71%, Jana B0 >99%) [V].
- **RoA / RoE** — annualised PAT ÷ ATA / average equity.
- **CRAR / Tier-1 / LCR** — §4.1.

## 10. Global lens

- **Global analogues:** Grameen-style microfinance banks — **BRAC Bank** (Bangladesh), **Bank Rakyat Indonesia (BRI)** micro-segment and its PNM/Pegadaian holding, **Compartamos Banco** (Mexico; famously ~70%+ portfolio yields at its 2007 IPO), **Mibanco** (Peru, part of Credicorp), **Khan Bank** (Mongolia), **TBC/Bank of Georgia** retail-micro [K].
- **Comparisons (indicative, [K/E]):** BRI's micro book runs NIMs of ~7–8% with RoA ~3% and trades at ~2–3× book; Compartamos (Gentera) NIM >30% on tiny tickets but RoA ~3–5% after high opex and losses, ~1–1.5× book; Mibanco RoA ~2%. **Indian SFBs earn lower RoA (1–2.4%) on similar NIMs because of (a) higher funding cost (no state deposit franchise), (b) 15% CRAR + 18% SLR + 3% CRR drag, and (c) political/state-law collection risk.**
- **India vs global on regulation:** Indian microfinance has an explicit household-income cap (₹3 lakh), a 50%-of-income repayment cap, a credit-bureau-checked lender cap (MFIN guardrails) and state anti-coercion laws — among the most borrower-protective regimes globally [K/E].
- **India vs Indian universal banks (the relevant valuation lens):** large private banks: NIM ~3.5–4.3%, opex/assets ~2–2.5%, credit cost ~0.5–1%, RoA 1.8–2.1%, RoE 15–17%, P/B 2–3× [K/E]. **AU's RoA (1.6–1.7%) and RoE (14–16%) are already close to a mid-size private bank; its 3.5× P/B prices the universal-bank re-rating in advance.**

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Digital lenders and fintech-NBFC partnerships** compete for the same small-ticket borrowers (BharatPe/Unity, slice, Kreditbee, Navi); slice's acquisition of NE SFB shows fintechs want a banking licence too [K].

3. **AI in origination and collections** — all four claim it: AU (agentic-AI gold loan LOS live; 50+ AI use cases; ~70% AML alerts resolved by AI; >90% transactions on AU 0101) [V]; Equitas (collection voice AI) [V]; Jana (90%+ digital uptake, RPA, headcount flat QoQ) [V]; Ujjivan (cashless collection ~45–50% of micro banking) [V]. ## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **The microfinance credit cycle (2024–26) — now in recovery.** Every 4–6 years Indian MFI books blow up (AP 2010, demonetisation 2016, COVID 2020–21, over-leverage 2024–25). The 2024–26 episode took industry PAR 31–180 from 2.5% to 6.3% and back to 1.6%, shrank GLP for seven quarters (₹4.4 → 3.15 lakh Cr) and wiped out 1–2 years of SFB earnings (Equitas FY25 PAT −82%, Q1 FY26 loss; Ujjivan FY25 −43%) [V/K]. The cycle defines earnings volatility, valuation multiples and regulatory posture. **It has turned:** Q1 FY27 PAT of the four is 2.6× a year earlier.
2. **Funding disadvantage vs universal banks.** SFBs pay 150–250 bp more for money (CoF 6.5–7.4% vs ~5% at large private banks [V/K]) with CASA of 19–29% and CD ratios of 88–97%. Deposit franchise building — branches, brand (Ujjivan's R. Madhavan campaign), digital, ASBA — is the long-run moat. The rate cycle (−125 bp in 2025, +25 bp in Oct-2026) moves NIM with a 2–4 quarter lag because liabilities are fixed-rate TDs.
3. **The secured-mix migration.** Secured share is the market's single most-watched KPI: Jana 55.8% (FY23) → 72.8% (Jun-26), Ujjivan ~30% (Mar-24) → 52% (Sep-26), Equitas MFI 12–13% [V/primer]. It lowers yield and NIM (AU 5.9% vs Ujjivan 8.4%) but cuts loss volatility and risk weights — and is the gate to a universal-bank licence (diversification test).
4. **Universal-bank transition as the re-rating event.** RBI's 2024 framework created an exit from the SFB rulebook. AU is the only one through the in-principle gate (Aug-2025), now awaiting a final licence; Ujjivan's and Jana's applications were returned. The prize: PSL 60% → 40%, CRAR 15% → 11.5%, cheaper deposits and a valuation closer to private banks.
5. **Opex intensity and scale.** SFBs run at 4–6.6% opex/ATA and 58–68% cost-to-income [V]. AU's scale (₹1.9 lakh Cr balance sheet, 2,920 touchpoints) gives it 4.0% cost-to-assets; the MFI-heritage trio are at 5.6–6.6%. Operating leverage + AI is the second RoA lever after credit cost.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Lender equation:**
`PAT = Average assets × [ (Yield × Earning-asset share − CoF × Liability share) = NIM-on-assets + Fee income/ATA − Opex/ATA − Credit cost/ATA ] × (1 − tax)`
`RoE = RoA × Leverage (ATA ÷ average equity)`

**Worked example 1 — AU SFB, FY26 (₹ Cr) [V deck; E arithmetic]:**
- Average total assets ≈ (1,57,846 + 1,91,797) ÷ 2 = **₹1,74,822 Cr** [V screener balance sheets].
- Interest income ₹18,636 Cr (10.7% of ATA) − interest expense ₹9,524 Cr (5.4%) = **NII ₹9,113 Cr (5.2% of ATA; NIM 5.65% on interest-earning assets incl. off-book)**.
- + Other income ₹2,978 Cr (1.7%) = net total income ₹12,091 Cr (6.9%).
- − Opex ₹6,982 Cr (4.0%) = PPoP ₹5,109 Cr (2.9%).
- − Provisions ₹1,615 Cr (0.9%) = PBT ₹3,494 Cr (2.0%).
- − Tax ₹838 Cr (24%) − exceptional ₹15 Cr = **PAT ₹2,641 Cr → RoA ≈ 1.5% (company 1.6% on monthly averages)**.
- Leverage ≈ 1,74,822 ÷ avg equity 18,570 = 9.4× → **RoE ≈ 14.2%** [V company 14.2%].

**Worked example 2 — Ujjivan, Q2 FY27 annualised (₹ Cr) [V deck/screener; E]:**
- Gross loan book ₹45,699 Cr; ATA ≈ ₹60,000 Cr [E: (57,541 + 62,635) ÷ 2 ≈ ₹60,088 Cr].
- NII ₹1,244 Cr/quarter → ₹4,976 Cr p.a. = 8.3% of ATA (NIM 8.4%).
- + Other income ₹296 Cr/quarter → ₹1,184 Cr (2.0%).
- − Opex: C/I 62% → ~₹3,820 Cr p.a. (6.4%) [E] → PPoP ₹594 Cr/quarter = ₹2,376 Cr (4.0%) [V PPoP].
- − Credit cost 0.7% of ATA ≈ ₹420 Cr → PBT ≈ ₹1,960 Cr (3.3%); screener Q2 PBT ₹491 Cr ×4 = ₹1,964 Cr ✔.
- − Tax 26% → PAT ₹365 Cr × 4 = ₹1,460 Cr → **RoA 2.4%; equity ~₹7,437 Cr → RoE ~19.7%** [V].
- **Sensitivity:** each +1 pp of credit cost on the micro-banking book (₹22,037 Cr) = −₹220 Cr PBT = **−0.37 pp RoA** [E]. A FY25-style year (credit cost 2.45% of loan book [primer] ≈ 1.9% of ATA) takes RoA back to ~1.3%.

**Worked example 3 — why SFB NIMs lag the repo [E, illustrative]:**
- Assume 70% of liabilities are TDs with an average residual tenor of ~12 months, 25% CASA (SA rates sticky), 5% refinance. A 125 bp repo cut lowers marginal TD rates by ~75–100 bp; the blended CoF falls by ~25 bp per quarter for 3–4 quarters → **−60 to −75 bp over a year** — matching AU (−60 bp YoY), Ujjivan (−71 bp), Jana (−57 bp) and Equitas (−44 bp) in Q1 FY27 [V].
- Asset side: MFI/vehicle/gold loans are fixed-rate (yield moves only as the book turns over, 12–24 months); EBLR-linked MSME/home loans reprice immediately (AU yield fell 14.1% → 13.7% YoY [V]). **Net: a cut first hurts AU-type banks (more floating assets) and helps MFI-type banks (fixed-rate assets).** The Oct-2026 hike reverses this slowly.

**D7. CGFMU economics per ₹100 of covered MFI loans [V NCGTC; E illustration]**
- **Fee:** ₹1.0 p.a. on sanctioned amount in year 1, then on outstanding. Jana pays ~₹80–100 Cr a year; AU says MFI credit cost is "~2.5% including guarantee".
- **Payout if ₹10 defaults:** lender bears the first ₹0.30 (3% of default); NCGTC pays 75% of ₹9.70 = **₹7.28**; the lender's net loss is **₹2.73** (27%).
- **Cap:** total payout ≤ 15% of the crystallised portfolio. **The cap binds only in a catastrophic year** (default > ~20% of the pool).
- **Read:** CGFMU turns a fat-tailed MFI loss distribution into a fixed ~1% cost plus ~27% of losses. That is why AU, Jana and Equitas (87%) all buy it, and why the MFI-heritage banks can again talk of "normal" 2–2.5% MFI credit costs.

## 4.5 New insights and new primer issues

**Insights the first pass missed (expert-level)**
1. **The funding tailwind ended in Q1 FY27 — before the RBI hiked.**
   - All four managements said in July 2026 that CoF had bottomed: AU "effectively bottomed out"; Jana "the opportunity to drop cost of funds is now over"; Ujjivan "repricing benefit… almost done"; Equitas CoF already +11 bp.
   - All four raised savings or term-deposit rates during **Q1 FY27 (Jun-2026 for Jana and Ujjivan)**, after tight liquidity in April–May and a deposit war.
   - The 7-Oct-2026 hike therefore lands on books already repricing upward. **Peak SFB NIMs were Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27; expect 10–20 bp of compression per quarter in H2 FY27** (Equitas guides 7.24% → ~7.1%) [V; E].
2. **The FD premium is only ~110–125 bp; the CoF gap is 150–200 bp.** The rest is CASA share and bulk/institutional funding. Equitas now draws ~30% of term deposits from co-operative banks and government bodies; AU 21% from government and interbank; Jana lost state-government CASA in Q4 FY26 (−8% QoQ). **SFB funding is less granular than "retail-deposit" labels suggest, and government/co-op money is the most rate-sensitive and politically mobile** [V].
3. **CGFMU has changed MFI economics from a tail risk to a toll.** At a 1% fee plus ~27% of losses net (3% first loss, 75% cover, 15% cap), the guarantee turns MFI credit cost into a predictable ~2–2.5%. That is why AU (96%), Jana (~80%) and Equitas (87%) cover their books, and why all three now cap MFI at ~10% of loans or ~10–12% growth instead of exiting. **The cap binds only if a vintage loses >~20%** — a 2010-AP-style event would still hurt [V; E].
4. **The universal-bank gate is qualitative, slow and sequential.**
   - AU is still waiting **13+ months after in-principle approval** (AU's own 24-Sep-2026 deck); its NOFHC condition had to be removed first.
   - RBI returned Jana's application (Q3 FY26) and Ujjivan's (13-Apr-2026) on diversification, not on the numeric tests. Ujjivan was told to show "further progress"; Jana cited "shortages".
   - Equitas, which meets the numbers, is deliberately slow.
   - **Expect no second UB conversion before FY28; AU's final licence is the only FY27 catalyst** [V; E].
5. **State anti-coercion laws do not legally reach SFBs.** Karnataka's Act (s.1(3)) explicitly excludes SFBs, as Bihar's Bill excludes RBI-regulated entities. Yet Karnataka is still Ujjivan's worst MFI state (GL PAR 90+ 5.4%). **The damage runs through borrower behaviour and field-collection climate, not legal liability** — a point the report should make precisely [V].
6. **Equitas's de-risking cost ~230 bp of NIM (9.00% FY23 → 6.69% FY26, old basis), with no better through-cycle RoA yet** (FY25–26 RoA 0.30% / 0.19%). Ujjivan has kept NIM at 8.1–8.5% while getting to 52% secured. The contrast shows migration pays only if opex falls with ticket size — Ujjivan's MSME ticket-size lift is an explicit opex-for-yield trade [V].
7. **Jana's earnings quality depends on a ₹185 Cr DTA and carried-forward losses.** No DTA was added in FY26, so its zero tax is loss set-off. FY27's "PAT +80%" is pre-normalisation. Its **PCR fell from 68% to 63% over five quarters** while guarantee cover rose — Jana is substituting guarantees for provisions [V].
8. **AU is quietly building universal-bank plumbing.** Contingent liabilities rose 77% (₹7,370 → ₹13,047 Cr), mostly forward FX (₹5,644 Cr) and new derivatives (₹519 Cr). There is also a "zero-forex-margin" remittance product, GIFT City/OFCB funding, and a CRO hired from HDFC Bank. Ujjivan (₹1,375 Cr) and Equitas (₹314 Cr) show smaller FX books from FCNR(B) launches after the RBI's June-2026 FX-inflow measures [V].
