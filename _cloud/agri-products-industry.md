## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Four archetypes, one label.** Never compute "sector margin" or "sector P/E" without the subgroup split.
   - Basmati (LT + KRBL) is **67% of FY26 revenue** (₹17,044 of ₹25,532 Cr) but **49% of m-cap**.
   - Manorama is **5.4% of revenue but 25.8% of m-cap**.
   - Kaveri's margin structure (24% OPM, 600-day inventory, three loss quarters a year) has nothing in common with GAEL's (8% OPM, 70-day inventory).
2. **Consolidated vs standalone differ for three members.**
   - **Kaveri:** consolidated revenue is *lower* than standalone in the season quarter. Q1 FY27 consolidated ₹742 Cr vs standalone ₹815 Cr; Q1 FY26 ₹859 vs ₹945 Cr. The likely cause is intra-group elimination of sales to subsidiaries. FY26 consolidated ₹1,392 Cr / PAT ₹296 Cr vs standalone ₹1,301 Cr / PAT ₹283 Cr [V screener both]. The **primer and the company's investor update use standalone** (₹815 Cr Q1; ₹1,304 Cr FY26). This file uses consolidated in Part 3 and quotes standalone where the KPI only exists there (crop-wise revenue).
   - **Manorama:** FY26 consolidated ₹1,367 Cr / OP ₹361 Cr / PAT ₹225 Cr vs standalone ₹1,358 Cr / OP ₹368 Cr / PAT ₹233 Cr. The African/Brazilian subsidiaries lose a little money. The company's own consolidated deck shows ₹1,366.7 Cr revenue, EBITDA ₹361.3 Cr (26.4%) [V]. **The primer's ₹1,358 / ₹368 / ₹233 Cr is standalone.**
   - **GAEL:** its Q1 FY27 deck is **standalone** (PAT ₹176.93 Cr); consolidated is ₹176.77 Cr [V-snip]. The difference is immaterial.
   - **LT Foods and KRBL** report consolidated as primary.
3. **"EBITDA" has three bases: company EBITDA incl. other income vs screener operating profit vs PBT.**
   - **KRBL FY26:** company EBITDA **₹973 Cr (15.8%)** includes other income of ₹70 Cr. Screener OP is ₹902 Cr (14.8%); PBT is ₹873 Cr [V KRBL deck; V screener]. **The primer's "EBITDA ~₹873 Cr" is actually PBT.**
   - **GAEL FY26:** company EBITDA **₹574 Cr (10.02%)** includes other income. Screener OP is ₹468 Cr (8.2%) [V]. The primer's "EBITDA ~₹458 Cr / ~8%" is near screener OP but labelled as derived.
   - **Manorama Q1 FY27:** company EBITDA ₹106.2 Cr (26.3%) *excludes* other income (₹16.2 Cr) and matches screener OP ₹106 Cr [V].
   - **LT Foods FY26:** company EBITDA ~₹1,236 Cr [primer] vs screener OP ₹1,159 Cr. Company revenue includes other income (₹11,023 Cr vs screener sales ₹10,946 Cr; the ₹77 Cr gap ≈ other income ₹83 Cr).
   - **Rule for this file:** Part 3 uses screener OP throughout. Company figures are quoted in Part 2 with their basis.
4. **LT Foods' "revenue" includes other income.** The deck footnote reads: "*Revenue includes Revenue from Operations and Other Income." Q1 FY27 is ₹3,161 Cr on the company basis vs **₹3,151.8 Cr revenue from operations** [V LT deck; screener ₹3,152 Cr]. Its FY26 "₹11,023 Cr" likewise exceeds screener's ₹10,946 Cr.
5. **KRBL's market-share number changed basis.** The primer's "India Gate ~42% vs Daawat ~30%" came from industry commentary. KRBL's own Q1 FY27 deck (Nielsen/RMS, MAT Jun-26) gives **37% in general trade, 38% in modern trade, 41% in e-commerce** (internal, Jun-26 exit). LT Foods' deck gives **Daawat 23.1% (MAT Jun-26)** [V both decks]. Together that is roughly **60%** of packaged basmati in India, not ~72%.
6. **Kaveri earns its year in one quarter.** Q1 (Apr–Jun) carries ~95–110% of annual PAT. Q2 is usually small or negative: Q2 FY26 consolidated PAT was **−₹16 Cr** and Q2 FY25 −₹1 Cr [V screener]. **The primer's illustrative "Q2 ~₹40 Cr profit" is wrong.** Q3 is small positive and Q4 negative. Its Q2 FY27 (board 28-Oct) will be a small number either way; do not annualise it.
7. **Inventory days mean different things.**

8. **Basmati export data come in several overlapping series.**

- **Five companies, four businesses, one shared exposure: Indian farm output priced against world markets.** All five buy a crop (basmati paddy, forest seed, maize/soy, or grower-contracted seed) in a short seasonal window, carry it for months, and sell into markets where policy (export bans, MEPs, MRP caps, import duties, ethanol allocation, foreign tariffs) can reset prices overnight.
  - **Basmati pair (LT Foods + KRBL):** FY26 revenue **₹17,044 Cr (+19.4%)**, OP ₹2,061 Cr (12.1%), PAT ₹1,273 Cr; m-cap ₹21,186 Cr; **16.6× FY26 PAT** [E: sums of screener 24-Oct-26].

### 1.2 Value chains (inputs → customer)

1. **Basmati (LT/KRBL model).**
   - Farmer grows basmati paddy (kharif; transplanted Jun–Jul, harvested Sep–Nov). No MSP or state procurement for basmati: **100% private buying** in Punjab/Haryana/UP mandis, with arhtiya commission plus mandi fee and RDF (Punjab mandi fee + RDF ≈ 6% [K]).
   - **Stage 1:** buy a full year's paddy in Oct–Jan. KRBL Q1 FY27 inventory ₹2,994 Cr; LT Foods ~₹4,000 Cr+ [primer, approx.].
   - **Stage 2:** dry, store and **age 12–24 months** (as paddy or as rice).
   - **Stage 3:** mill (dehusk, whiten, polish, colour-sort; steam/parboil for "sella"). Yield ~62–67% head rice plus broken, bran and husk [K].
   - **Stage 4:** pack and brand, then distribute. Domestic: GT (3.3 lakh outlets for KRBL [V]), MT, e-com, quick-com. Exports: bulk, buyer's brand or own brand through overseas subsidiaries.
   - Value is captured in ageing (the price premium of aged over new crop), brand (the premium over loose) and channel (developed-market retail realisations).
   - By-products: husk for biomass power, bran for rice-bran oil or de-oiled bran, broken rice for feed/ethanol or domestic sale.
2. **Specialty fats (Manorama model).**
   - **Sal seed** falls May–July in the forests of Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Jharkhand and MP. It is hand-collected by tribal households and aggregated by village heads and purchase centres (a network of ~18,000 villages [primer]).
   - **Shea nuts** come from West Africa (subsidiaries in Ghana, Burkina Faso, Togo, Benin, Nigeria, Côte d'Ivoire, Chad [V deck lists Ghana, Togo, Burkina Faso, Chad entities, a UAE trading LLC and Manorama America LTDA (Brazil)]).
   - **Mango kernel** is a by-product of the pulp industry.
   - **Processing** at Birkoni (Chhattisgarh): solvent extraction (~90,000 TPA of seeds [primer]) → crude butter → refining (~45,000 TPA [primer]; a 90,000 TPA refinery is planned [V deck]) → **fractionation (47,500 TPA [V deck])** into stearin (the CBE-relevant fraction) and olein → blending to customer spec → packing (30,000 TPA newly acquired packing capacity [V deck]).
   - **Value** lies in procurement access (low-cost seed), fractionation know-how, and 3–4-year customer qualification.
3. **Maize wet-milling (GAEL model).**
   - Inputs: kharif and rabi maize (Bihar rabi maize for Malda; Karnataka/Maharashtra/MP for Hubli/Chalisgaon; Uttarakhand/UP for Sitarganj).
   - Process: steep kernels → separate germ (corn oil), fibre and gluten (feed) → starch slurry → native starch, or hydrolyse to glucose → dextrose/sorbitol/maltodextrin → or ferment glucose into sodium gluconate.
   - Yields: ~**62–66% starch, 7–8% germ, 5–6% gluten, 10–12% fibre/feed** [K, industry norms].
   - Value grows with each step up the ladder. Pharma-grade dextrose/sorbitol and gluconate carry the best margins.
4. **Oilseed crushing (GAEL "Other Agro").**
   - Soybean (MP/Maharashtra/Rajasthan, Oct–Dec harvest) → solvent extraction → crude oil (~18%) + meal (~80%) [K] → meal to domestic feed or export; crude oil refined or sold.
   - India imports ~55–60% of its edible oil [K], so domestic oil prices follow palm/soy world prices plus duty.
   - **Crush margin = meal value + oil value − seed cost − processing.**
5. **Seeds (Kaveri model).**
   - Breed parent lines (germplasm) → produce foundation seed → **contract thousands of grower-farmers** (Telangana, AP, Karnataka, Gujarat) to produce hybrid seed a season ahead (cotton hybrids require hand-emasculation and pollination) → condition, treat and pack → dealers/distributors → farmers buy each sowing season.
   - For Bt cotton, the trait (Bollgard-II, Bayer/Mahyco Monsanto Biotech) is licensed. The trait fee is capped inside a government MRP under the Cotton Seeds Price (Control) Order, 2015 [K].
   - Value is captured in germplasm (yield and pest tolerance), brand and dealer trust. Production is capital-light; working capital (season-ahead seed inventory, dealer credit) is heavy.

### 1.3 Where value is captured (one line each) [E]
- **Basmati:** gross margin 25–36% (KRBL FY26 28.3%, Q1 FY27 36.3%; LT basmati ~33–34%). EBITDA 12–16%. The paddy-price "carry" can swing gross margin ±5–10 points year to year.
- **Specialty fats:** gross margin ~43–47% (Manorama FY26 46.3%, Q1 FY27 43.6%), EBITDA 25–27%. Value sits in fractionation plus captive seed.
- **Maize derivatives:** maize segment margin 7% (FY26) to 17.6% (Q1 FY27). The swing is the maize price lag. **Oilseed crush** segment margin ~6% with big swings.
- **Seeds:** EBITDA ~24% for the year, ~35–40% in Q1. The farmer pays for genetics; the seed's physical cost is small.

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets (with numbers)

1. **Gulf food security and diaspora demand (basmati exports).**
   - Middle East = 71% of basmati exports by volume [V KRBL deck].
   - Saudi Arabia is the anchor. LT Foods made **₹17 Cr revenue from Saudi Arabia in Q1 FY27** as it builds the market with **SALIC** (Saudi Agricultural & Livestock Investment Co., a strategic shareholder) [V deck].
   - Demand is price-inelastic. Payment risk (Iran) and logistics risk (Hormuz/Red Sea) are elastic.
2. **India's shift from loose to packaged rice.**
   - KRBL: India Gate reaches **1.2 crore households** via **3.3 lakh outlets** [V deck].
   - LT: Daawat household penetration **64.4 lakh (MAT Mar-26) vs 45.6 lakh (MAT 2023)** [V deck].
   - E-com/quick-com share: KRBL 41% e-com share; LT claims "e-Comm/Quick-Comm leadership" [V decks].
3. **US/UK mainstreaming of basmati and jasmine.** Royal holds **60%+ of North American basmati import share**; Golden Star is the **#1 US jasmine brand**; Daawat targets **£100 m UK revenue in 4 years** [V LT deck].
4. **Chocolate cost inflation → CBE substitution** (Manorama).
   - EU, India (FSSAI) and others allow **up to 5% CBE in finished chocolate** [V Manorama deck].
   - Compound coatings, fillings and tropical-market chocolates (heat stability) use far more [V deck].
   - The 2024–25 cocoa spike forced reformulation. Once qualified, CBE stays. Q3-2026 Asian grindings +29% [V tradingeconomics] point to strong Asian chocolate demand, a CBE-heavy market.
5. **Natural and traceable cosmetics** (shea, kokum, mango butters). EUDR/palm-free traceability helps forest-seed fats [primer].
6. **Processed food, pharma and construction for maize derivatives** (GAEL): dextrose/sorbitol for pharma; maltodextrin for infant food/dairy; sodium gluconate as a concrete admixture ("Green admixtures for infrastructure" [V deck]).
7. **Ethanol blending.** E20 was reached in 2025 [K]. Grain ethanol (maize + FCI rice + damaged grain) now supplies a large share. **Maize = ~36% of ethanol production in ESY 2025-26 to June** [V-snip]. This raises maize demand (GAEL's input cost) and creates a maize-seed demand pull (Kaveri's maize seed).
8. **Feed demand (poultry)** for maize, soymeal and DDGS.
9. **Hybrid adoption in seeds.** Hybrid rice and single-cross maize keep displacing saved/open-pollinated seed. Kaveri: **new single-cross hybrids >20% of maize portfolio**; new cotton products **22% → 37%** of cotton; **hybrid paddy launches KRH7344/KRH7227 = 62% of the new-product bucket**; bajra new hybrids **61% → 65%** [V deck].
10. **Monsoon (seeds, paddy and maize supply).** Deficient 2026 (−12.6%) → Kaveri's Q1 hit. It also threatens kharif maize and soy output (input inflation for GAEL) and basmati yields. Irrigated Punjab/Haryana are less exposed; NW India was only −5.8%.

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Rice export policy (the defining lever)
- **Basmati MEP:**
  - **25/26-Aug-2023:** Centre set a **Minimum Export Price of US$1,200/t** on basmati (to stop non-basmati being shipped as basmati during the white-rice ban).
  - **25-Oct-2023:** lowered to **US$950/t**.
  - **13/14-Sep-2024:** **MEP removed entirely**; APEDA retained floor-price monitoring of contracts [K: DGFT/APEDA notifications; primer confirms Sep-2024 removal].
- **Non-basmati restrictions and their removal:**
  - **8/9-Sep-2022:** export of **broken rice banned**, and a **20% export duty** on non-basmati white and brown rice (husked) [K: DGFT notification 31/2015-20]. *(The task brief said "Jul-2022 broken rice"; the correct month is **September 2022** [K].)*
  - **20-Jul-2023:** **non-basmati white rice exports banned** (with government-to-government exceptions) [K].
  - **25-Aug-2023:** 20% export duty on **parboiled** rice (to 31-Mar-2024, then extended) [K].
  - **28-Sep-2024:** white-rice ban lifted with a **MEP of US$490/t**; parboiled duty cut to 10% (27-Sep) and **to zero (22-Oct-2024)**; **MEP on white rice removed 23-Oct-2024** [K].
  - **7-Mar-2025:** **broken-rice exports allowed again** [K].
  - **24-Sep-2025:** APEDA **prior registration made mandatory for non-basmati exports** [V-snip procurementresource].
- **What this did:** non-basmati exports fell from ~17.8 mt (FY23) to ~11 mt (FY24), then recovered to ~14–15 mt (FY25–FY26) [K/V-snip]. Global prices spiked in 2023 (Thai 5% broken ~$650/t, a 15-year high) and fell back through 2024–25 as India returned [K]. **Rule of thumb: India's export policy *is* the world rice price.**

### 7.2 Importing-country rules
- **EU pesticide MRLs:** the EU cut the **tricyclazole MRL to 0.01 mg/kg** (default) from **1-Jan-2018**, and later tightened buprofezin, carbendazim and others [K]. Punjab banned the sale of 10 pesticides (incl. acephate, buprofezin, chlorpyrifos, hexaconazole, propiconazole, thiamethoxam, profenofos, isoprothiolane, carbendazim, tricyclazole) for basmati in recent seasons (2022–2025 notifications) [K]. Europe is 10% of basmati exports [V KRBL deck] but a high-realisation market. LT's Europe revenue was **−12% in Q1 FY27** (cutting non-strategic B2B) [V].
- **US tariffs:**
  - Reciprocal/IEEPA tariffs of 25% + 25% (Aug-2025) hit Indian rice in FY26, contributing to the stock fall in Dec-2025 [V-snip Business Standard].
  - IEEPA was struck down 20-Feb-2026; Sec 122 10% to 24-Jul-2026; **Sec 301 10% from 24-Jul-2026** (proposed 12.5%; India contested; legal challenge) [V-sib; V-snip].
  - Separately, US rice-state lawmakers asked USTR for a **rice-specific Sec 301 investigation** (Crawford letter) [V-snip crawford.house.gov].
  - **LT Foods exposure: North America = 50% of revenue (Q1 FY27)**; Royal is imported basmati, so tariffs are passed through to retail prices. The FY26 margin dip (11% vs 12%) was attributed by the primer to tariff pass-through plus freight reclassification.
- **US CVD on organic soybean meal:** the rate on LT's subsidiary **Ecopure** was revised down **from 340% to 75%**; appeal filed [V LT deck]. This is the reason for the organic "remodelling" (Q1 organic revenue −13%, EBITDA margin 4%).
- **Gulf:** Saudi SFDA labelling/quality rules; Iran's import licensing and rupee-rial payment channel (UCO Bank vostro, built 2012–2019 on Iranian oil-for-rupee; dried up after US sanctions on Iranian oil in May-2019) [K].

### 7.3 Specialty fats
- **EU Directive 2000/36/EC** allows up to 5% non-cocoa vegetable fats (illipe, palm, sal, shea, kokum, mango kernel) in chocolate [K; V deck "up to 5% CBE"]. **FSSAI** allows the same in India [V deck].
- **EUDR (EU Deforestation Regulation):** covers cocoa and palm (not sal/shea), but its implementation was delayed twice to Dec-2025/Dec-2026 [K]. It helps traceable non-palm fats [primer].
- **Minor Forest Produce MSP scheme** (TRIFED/Van Dhan) sets floor prices for sal seed and other forest produce [K].

### 7.4 Maize, ethanol, edible oil
- **Ethanol:** E20 achieved; E27/E30 roadmap discussed [K].
  - OMC ethanol prices for **maize-based ethanol ~₹71.86/litre (ESY 2024-25)** [K].
  - FCI rice to distilleries: **₹2,250/qtl in 2025, ₹2,320/qtl to 31-Oct-2026, ₹2,390 from 1-Nov-2026** [V-snip ruralvoice]. FCI dispatched **6.35 mt of rice worth ₹14,597 Cr** to ethanol plants in a 12-month period at ~40% below acquisition cost [V-snip Outlook/Moneylife].
  - **Implication for GAEL:** cheap FCI rice for distillers *reduces* maize competition at the margin. A policy switch back to maize-only would tighten maize.
- **Maize import:** TRQ of 0.5 mt at 15% duty; 50% duty otherwise; GM ban [K].
- **Edible-oil duty:** crude oils cut **20% → 10%** basic in 2025 [V-snip SOPA/Motilal]. SOPA wants it raised. Refined oils ~32.5% basic [K]. Soymeal export competitiveness depends on domestic soybean vs Argentine/Brazilian meal.

### 7.5 Seeds
- **Cotton Seeds Price (Control) Order, 2015:** annual MRP notification for Bt cotton seed.
  - **BG-I ₹635** unchanged since 2016.
  - **BG-II: ₹730 (2019-20, trait value ₹20) → ₹767 (2020-21) → ₹810 (2022-23) → ₹853 (2023-24) → ₹864 (2024-25) → ₹901 per 475 g packet (2025-26)** [V-snip DTE/cropgpt; earlier steps K].
  - **2026-27 expected unchanged** [V-snip cropgpt, Mar-2026].
  - Trait value has been ~₹0–20 since 2018 — effectively zero for Bayer/MMB [K/V-snip].
- **GM approvals:** no new cotton trait commercialised since **Bollgard-II (2006)**. **HTBt (herbicide-tolerant Bt, BG-III type) is unapproved.** Illegal HTBt packets are sold widely in Maharashtra, Telangana and Gujarat (industry estimates 15–25% of the cotton seed market in some states) [K/E]. Kaveri's Q1 FY27 deck cites "challenges from illegal cotton" [V]. **GM mustard (DMH-11)** environmental release (2022) is still before the Supreme Court [K].
- **Seeds Bill** (draft 2019/2025) and the **Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001** [K]. State-level price regulation for some vegetable/maize seeds in a few states [K].
- **Pink bollworm:** resistant to BG-II across central and south India since ~2015–17 [K]. Punjab/Haryana/Rajasthan (north zone) is now also affected (2021–25) [K]. Kaveri says it has "established a strong foundation in the northern markets of Haryana, Punjab and Rajasthan" [V deck].

### 7.6 Working capital and credit
- Basmati houses fund paddy with working-capital limits (KRBL: ICRA AA(Stable) on **₹1,540 Cr** working-capital limits + A1+ on ₹515 Cr [V screener announcement 21-Oct-2026]).
- The RBI repo rise to 5.50% (7-Oct-2026) raises carry costs for LT (interest ₹40 Cr/quarter, up because of "supplier funding arrangements" [V deck]) and Manorama (interest ~₹38 Cr/yr).

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **1990s–2008: the branded basmati era begins.** KRBL launches India Gate (1993). LT Overseas builds Daawat and buys Royal's US owner (Kusha Inc.) around 2007–08 [primer; K]. The Pusa 1121 variety (released 2003) transforms basmati supply: higher yield, extra-long grain [K].
2. **2008–2013: the rupee and Iran.** The 2008 non-basmati ban and the basmati MEP ($1,200/t in 2008, cut later) create the first policy shock. Iran becomes the #1–2 basmati buyer, paid via the rupee vostro (2012+) [K].
3. **2014–2017: the paddy price crash.** Basmati paddy halved (₹4,000+ → ₹2,000–2,500/qtl in 2014–15) after export overhang and Iran payment delays. Inventory write-downs; LT and KRBL margins hit [K].
4. **2018–2019: EU MRL shock + Iran sanctions.** The tricyclazole MRL (Jan-2018) cut EU basmati shipments sharply. US sanctions (May-2019) froze Iranian rupee payments [K].
5. **2020–2022: COVID pantry demand, then the FY22 edible-oil boom** (GAEL FY22 PAT ₹475 Cr, OPM 15%) [V screener].
6. **2022–2024: India's rice export restrictions.** Broken-rice ban (Sep-2022), white-rice ban (Jul-2023), basmati MEP $1,200 → $950 (Aug–Oct 2023), all unwound Sep-2024 to Mar-2025 [K]. Basmati paddy hit ~₹4,500–5,000/qtl in 2023 [K].
7. **2024–2025: the cocoa super-spike.** ICE cocoa rose from ~$4,000 to ~$12,900 (Dec-2024) [K]. Manorama's revenue rose from ₹457 Cr (FY24) to ₹1,367 Cr (FY26) [V screener].
8. **2025–2026: tariffs, war and recovery.** US reciprocal tariffs on India (Aug-2025, 50% total) → struck down (Feb-2026) → Sec 122/301 10%. Iran unrest (Jan-2026, ₹2,000 Cr stuck); West Asia war (Feb-2026) → Hormuz, CIF→FOB shift. KRBL exports −50% in Q1 FY27 while domestic realisation rose 11%. Cocoa collapse to ~$2,846 (Mar-2026) and partial rebound. Deficient monsoon (−12.6%) → Kaveri Q1 −14%.
9. **M&A/listing waves:**
   - Manorama listed on BSE SME (Oct-2018), migrated to the main board, and listed on NSE later [K: SME listing; VERIFY dates]; QIP ₹500 Cr (Jul-2026).
   - LT Foods: SALIC stake (2019–21) [primer]; Golden Star (US jasmine) acquisition (FY26) [V deck].
   - Kaveri: buybacks of ₹125.65 Cr (2022) and ₹325 Cr (2024, ₹725/share) [primer].
   - GAEL: equity capital doubled from ₹23 Cr to ₹46 Cr in FY24, consistent with a 1:1 bonus/split-type action [V screener equity line; nature of the action not verified].

### 9.3 Basmati ageing-carry arithmetic [E composite]
- Buy 1121 paddy at ₹4,000/qtl (Oct-2025) → milled rice ~₹62–65/kg of input cost (at a 65% yield, plus ~₹3/kg milling).
- Hold 12 months at a ~9% working-capital cost plus storage ~1%/yr → +₹6–7/kg.
- Sell aged sella in Oct-2026, when new-crop paddy is ~₹4,800/qtl (+20%) → aged rice reprices to ~₹85–95/kg wholesale (aged premium ~10–15% over new crop).
- Spread ~₹18–25/kg (~20–25% gross) vs ~₹10–12/kg in a year when paddy falls 20%.
- **This is why KRBL's Q1 FY27 gross margin jumped 10.6 points:** it sold aged stock bought cheaper into a rising market (management: "price increase, better sales mix") [V deck].

### 9.4 CBE spread arithmetic [E composite]
- Cocoa butter typically trades at **2.0–3.0× the cocoa-bean price** (the "butter ratio") [K].
- At $5,500/t beans and a ratio of ~2.2 → cocoa butter ~$12,000/t ≈ ₹11.4 lakh/t (₹1,140/kg).
- CBE sells at a 30–50% discount → ~₹570–800/kg.
- Manorama's blended realisation is implied at ~₹300–350/kg [E: revenue ₹1,367 Cr ÷ ~40–45 kt]. Its mix includes cheaper butters, olein and by-products, so blended realisation is far below CBE list prices.
- **Sensitivity:** cocoa at $3,000 makes the CBE discount ~₹250–350/kg narrower in absolute terms → less substitution urgency. Qualified CBE volumes are contractual and sticky over 1–2 years [primer].

### 9.5 Maize-derivative spread [E composite]
- 1 t maize at ₹24,000 → ~0.64 t starch + by-products (gluten ~₹55–65/kg, germ ~₹35–40/kg, fibre/feed ~₹12–15/kg).
- Native starch ~₹32–36/kg; dextrose/sorbitol ₹45–60/kg.
- A ₹2,000/t fall in maize (−8%) raises the maize-segment margin by ~4–6 points if product prices lag (GAEL Q1 FY27: 7.0% → 17.6% partly this, partly mix).

### 9.6 Seed packet economics [E composite]
- BG-II cotton: MRP ₹901/475 g. Dealer margin ~10–15% → company realisation ~₹750–780. Production cost (grower payment ~₹350–450/packet-equivalent + processing/packing ₹50–70) → gross margin ~40–50%; trait fee ~₹0–20.
- Hybrid maize: 4 kg bag at ~₹1,200–1,800; gross margin ~55–60%.
- Vegetables: highest per-gram value, ~60–70% gross margin.

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Precision-fermented and cell-based cocoa butter.** Start-ups (e.g. California Cultured, Celleste Bio, Planet A Foods/ChoViva cocoa-free chocolate, WNWN, Voyage Foods) make "cocoa-free" chocolate or cultured cocoa fats [K]. Scale is tiny today. They target the same cost-inflation demand Manorama rides. **Medium-term threat to the CBE premium** [E].
2. **Enzymatic interesterification / structured fats from palm** (AAK, Bunge) compete directly with wild-seed CBE on cost [K].
3. **Gene-edited and new GM traits in seeds.** India exempted SDN-1/SDN-2 genome editing from GM rules (Mar-2022) and released two gene-edited rice varieties (May-2025, ICAR: DRR Dhan 100 Kamala, Pusa DST Rice 1) [K]. Public-sector gene-edited varieties could compete with private hybrids. **HTBt approval** (if it ever comes) would reset the cotton market. Illegal HTBt is a share-stealer today.
4. **Direct-seeded rice (DSR) and short-duration basmati** (PB1509, 1692, 1847/1885/1886 resistant varieties from IARI) change the paddy supply curve and pesticide residues [K].
5. **Quick-commerce and D2C** shift brand economics: high trade margins for platforms, but faster share gains for top brands (KRBL e-com share 41%) [V].
6. **Ethanol technology:** 2G ethanol and the feedstock mix (cane vs grain) decide maize demand [K].
7. **Climate:** heat stress during basmati grain-filling, an El Niño monsoon (2026), and erratic sal flowering [E].
8. **Blockchain traceability/EUDR** — an opportunity for Manorama, a cost for smaller sourcing players [primer].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert leads with)

1. **Policy sets the price.** India's own export rules (bans, MEPs, duties) and importers' rules (US tariffs/CVD, EU MRLs) have moved basmati and rice prices more than any harvest in the last four years. The same is true of Bt cotton MRPs (seed), edible-oil duties (crush) and FCI's ethanol rice pricing (maize). **Every archetype here is a policy-arbitrage business.**
2. **Inventory carry is the hidden P&L.** Basmati houses buy a year's paddy in 3 months and age it 12–24 months. Manorama buys a year's sal in 6–8 weeks. Kaveri produces seed a season ahead. **Whoever finances the carry cheaply and calls the crop price right earns the margin:** KRBL's Q1 FY27 GM +10.6 pts is pure carry; LT's interest bill rose on supplier funding; Manorama's inventory days run 370–520.
3. **West Asia is the demand centre for basmati and the source of 2026's shocks.** It takes 71% of basmati exports [V]; Iran payments, Hormuz, freight and insurance, CIF→FOB. KRBL (Gulf-heavy exports) vs LT (US-heavy exports) diverged in Q1 FY27 for exactly this reason.
4. **Commodity-indexed demand in a specialty wrapper (cocoa → CBE; maize ↔ ethanol).** Manorama's addressable demand is cocoa-indexed; GAEL's margin is maize-price-indexed, which in turn is ethanol-policy-indexed.
5. **The monsoon and seasonal windows.** Kaveri's single selling season and the kharif crops decide the year. 2026 was the weakest monsoon since 2015.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**General form (per archetype):**
- **Basmati house:** Revenue = Σ_channel (tonnes × realisation/kg). Gross profit = tonnes × (realisation − paddy cost/kg ÷ milling yield − packaging) ± ageing carry gain/loss. EBITDA = GP − A&P − freight − staff − overheads. PAT = EBITDA − interest on paddy WC − D&A − tax.
- **CBE maker:** Revenue = tonnes fractionated × utilisation × realisation (indexed to cocoa butter × (1 − discount)) + butters. EBITDA = revenue − seed cost (₹/kg × kg seed per kg fat ≈ 1/oil yield) − energy/solvent − opex.
- **Maize processor:** Revenue = grind TPD × days × utilisation × Σ(product yields × prices). EBITDA = revenue − maize cost − conversion cost (steam, power, chemicals).
- **Seed company:** Revenue = Σ_crop (packets or kg × net realisation). EBITDA = revenue − grower payments − processing − R&D − selling. PAT ≈ EBITDA + treasury income − D&A (tax ~2–5%).

### C.1 Worked example 1 — KRBL Q1 FY27 vs Q1 FY26 (company consolidated) [V deck, E bridge]
- Revenue ₹1,496 Cr (−6%) = domestic ₹1,221 Cr (+14%; realisation +11%, so volume ~+3%) + exports ₹244 Cr (−50%) + power ₹31 Cr.
- COGS ₹995 Cr (−17%) → **gross profit ₹566 Cr (36.3%)** vs ₹415 Cr (25.7%). **+₹151 Cr of gross profit on ₹88 Cr less revenue** — the aged-stock carry plus price.
- Employees ₹55 Cr; other expenses ₹139 Cr; other income ₹64 Cr → EBITDA ₹372 Cr (23.8%) → D&A ₹23 Cr, interest ₹1 Cr → PBT ₹348 Cr → tax ₹87 Cr → **PAT ₹261 Cr (+73%)**.
- Read: one quarter's gross-margin swing (+10.6 pts) = ₹158 Cr of EBITDA ≈ 60% of a normal quarter's PAT. **That is the paddy carry.**

### C.2 Worked example 2 — one tonne of branded basmati exported to the US (LT/Royal) [E composite]
- Retail price $2.2–2.6/lb for 10–20 lb bags → importer/distributor price ~$1,800–2,200/t FOB-equivalent at LT's US subsidiary level ≈ ₹1.7–2.1 lakh/t.
- Costs: aged 1121 rice ~₹85,000–95,000/t; packaging ₹5,000; ocean freight + insurance ~₹8,000–12,000 (higher after Hormuz/Red Sea); **10% Sec 301 duty on customs value (~₹9,000–10,000/t)** + MFN rice duty (~1.4–2.1¢/kg [K]); US warehousing/distribution/trade spend ₹25,000–35,000.
- Gross margin ~30–35%; EBITDA ~12–14% (LT basmati segment EBITDA 13% [V]).
- If the duty is passed through, the margin % dilutes ~1 pt while rupee EBITDA is broadly kept. That matches the primer's FY26 narrative of "tariff pass-through dented reported margin".

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed

   - India exported 6.52 mt (+8%), but value was flat at ₹50,138 Cr: per-tonne realisation fell ~7% after the flood-damaged, weak-priced Oct-2025 opening.

   - Both basmati houses guide margins **down** for the rest of FY27 (KRBL GM ~30% vs 36%; LT basmati 13–14% ±1). The writer should not extrapolate Q1.
2. **The two basmati houses now disclose comparable tonnes.**
   - LT sells ~2.26 lakh t a quarter (≈9 lakh t a year) and holds ~5.1 lakh t of rice and paddy.
   - KRBL holds ~4.6 lakh t. Its sales tonnes are undisclosed, but at a FY26 revenue of ₹6,098 Cr and ~₹90–100/kg it is ~6–7 lakh t a year [E].
   - On these numbers **LT is the larger rice seller by volume**, and its book cost is lower (₹56/kg vs ~₹64/kg rice).
3. **KRBL's "ED stake" is a customer's frozen block, not a governance verdict on KRBL's promoters.** The 6.26% belongs to the Balsharaf Group. The PMLA complaint does name KRBL, KRBL DMCC and a JMD, so the overhang is real, but the shares are not promoter stock. The equity story should separate (a) the free-float overhang from (b) the legal risk to the company (Dhuri attachment ₹15.3 Cr; the trial).
4. **Manorama's two-customer concentration (40.5%) is the risk the valuation ignores.**
   - At 43× earnings, two chocolate/confectionery buyers (unnamed; the customer slide lists Ferrero, Mondelez, Mars, Hershey, Nestlé, Barry Callebaut) account for ~₹550 Cr of revenue on 9–12-month contracts.
   - Add the customs/CEPA episode (**palm mid-fraction imports**: Manorama blends imported palm fractions, not just forest seeds) and utilisation capped at ~80% of 52,000 TPA until FY28. FY27 growth is volume-capped at roughly +10–20% [E].
5. **Maize is no longer scarce in India, but it is getting dear again.** Output jumped 27% to 55 mt, and ethanol now absorbs ~10–11 mt (41.8% of ethanol). Yet maize rose from ~₹16–17/kg (start of 2026 kharif) to ~₹27–28/kg (August, per Kaveri), and FAO maize rose 5.6% in September. GAEL's 17.6% maize margin was struck on low-cost grain; **the spread is at risk in H2 FY27**, while Kaveri's rabi maize seed demand benefits from the same price.
6. **Kaveri has quietly become a non-cotton company, but not a growth company.**
   - Non-cotton was 81% of FY26 standalone revenue (maize ₹403 Cr, +40%). R&D is 7.2% of turnover.
   - Yet PAT (~₹300 Cr) equals 2014-15's, and FY26 CFO was −₹6 Cr with inventory up ₹200 Cr.
   - Contingent "legal claims" jumped fivefold to ₹96.6 Cr, while ITAT keeps upholding the agricultural-income exemption (₹197 Cr of demands set aside across AY2020-21/22-23/23-24).
7. **India's grain glut is the macro backdrop for every member.** Rice stocks are ~3× the norm, with ~60 mt of rice equivalent in FCI hands. Non-basmati export policy stays liberal; FCI rice keeps feeding distillers (222 crore litres); domestic common-rice prices stay capped. That is why basmati's premium (₹76,900/t vs ₹34,600/t per export tonne) is the sector's margin pool.
8. **The US tariff saga produced a hidden asset for LT.** LT is "eligible" for refunds of the struck-down IEEPA duties (it paid up to 50% in Q4 FY26), has received part, and has booked **no income** yet. Any recognition would be a one-off in Q2–Q4 FY27; the writer should flag it as a possible "other income" spike.
