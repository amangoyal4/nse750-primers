## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **PhysicsWallah has at least five "EBITDA" numbers. Always name the basis.**
   - **Company "EBITDA" (Ind AS, after ESOP, includes other income):** FY26 **₹549 Cr (14.1%)**; FY25 ₹193 Cr; FY24 −₹829 Cr [V-sec Outlook]. It equals screener operating profit + other income: FY26 ₹370 + ₹179 = ₹549 Cr; FY25 ₹73 + ₹120 = ₹193; FY24 −₹975 + ₹146 = −₹829 [V screener; E reconciliation]. **This is the number our primer calls "~14% EBITDA / operating margin" — it is inflated by treasury income.**
   - **Screener operating profit (EBITDA ex other income):** FY26 **₹370 Cr (9.5%)**, TTM ₹389 Cr (9.5%) [V screener].
   - **Company "Adjusted EBITDA"** = pre-Ind AS EBITDA + lease payments + **other income**, before ESOP and exceptionals. Q2 FY26 ₹269.7 Cr (25.7%); H1 FY26 ₹296.2 Cr (15.6%); Q1 FY27 **₹135 Cr (12.9%)** vs ₹26.5 Cr [V Q2 FY26 letter; V-sec Inc42].
   - **Pre-Ind AS EBITDA** (rent treated as an operating cost, no other income — the closest to cash operating profit): Q2 FY26 ₹160.9 Cr (15.3%); Q1 FY26 −₹88.7 Cr; H1 FY26 ₹72.2 Cr (3.8%); FY26 ~₹300 Cr (one source) [V letter; V-snip]; Q1 FY27 **−₹44 Cr vs −₹88 Cr** [V transcript].
   - **Segment EBITDA (new from Q1 FY27):** online FY26 margin "around 27%" (CFO on call); offline pre-Ind AS EBITDA Q1 FY27 **−₹106 Cr (−22%)** vs −₹113 Cr (−26%) [V transcript; V-sec Inc42].
   - **Rule for the writer:** use screener OP (₹370 Cr, 9.5%) in tables, pre-Ind AS EBITDA for "cash" economics, and quote the company's ₹549 Cr only with the words "including ₹179 Cr of other income".
2. **PW's "paid users" are not the same thing in different documents.**
   - "Total paid users" = **unique transacting users (online, by unique mobile number)** + **offline student enrolments** [V letter definitions].
   - FY25: 4.46 M (online 4.13 M + offline 0.33 M); FY26: **5.34 M (online 4.87 M + offline 0.47 M)**; H1 FY26: 3.62 M; Q1 FY27: **2.49 M (online 2.15 M + offline 0.35 M)** [V letter; V-sec Outlook/Inc42].
   - Period figures are cumulative within the period, so **a quarter's users cannot be compared with a year's**. Category "enrolments" (4.80 M in FY25) exceed unique users (4.13 M) because one user buys several courses; the "overlap" was 0.66 M in FY25 [V letter].
3. **PW's offline ARPU is period-based and swings with the calendar and mix.** Offline ARPU was **₹40,405 in FY25**, ₹21,985 in H1 FY26 (−4.3% YoY on mix: more short courses in CA/UPSC/govt exams/tuition), and **₹12,633 in Q1 FY27 (+6.9% YoY)** [V letter; V-sec Inc42]. FY26 implied ≈ **₹37,700** (₹1,774 Cr ÷ 0.47 M) [E]. Our primer's "~₹36,600/yr" is in the right range for a full year. Never compare a quarterly ARPU with an annual one.
4. **PW's online "ACPU" is collections, not revenue.** ACPU = payments collected from online users in the period ÷ unique online users: FY25 ₹3,683; H1 FY26 ₹3,918; **Q1 FY27 ₹4,311 (+9.7%)** [V letter; V-sec]. Revenue per online user, FY26, ≈ ₹4,012 (₹1,954 Cr ÷ 4.87 M) [E]. Online fees are paid **upfront for the year** → negative working capital and a Q4→Q1 cash build of ~₹600 Cr [V transcript].
5. **"Centres" at PW come in four kinds, and the mix shifts.** Sep-25: Vidyapeeth 117, Pathshala 75, PW Other 53, subsidiary centres (Xylem, Utkarsh, etc.) 69 = 314. Jun-26: Vidyapeeth 165, Pathshala 70, other + subsidiary 131 = **366** [V letter; V-sec Inc42]. Mar-26: 353 [V-sec]. **Pathshala fell from 84 (Mar-26) to 70 (Jun-26) because mature Pathshalas were converted into Vidyapeeths, not closed** [V transcript]. Vidyapeeth is ~65–70% of offline revenue [V letter; V transcript].
6. **Crizac's revenue is gross commission; about two-thirds is paid straight out to agents.** Revenue FY26 ₹1,042 Cr; **cost of services (agent payouts) ₹703 Cr = 67.5%** [V deck]. The company's "take" (net revenue) is ~₹339 Cr (32.5%) [E]. Screener calls the agent payout "manufacturing cost" (68%) [V screener]. Comparing Crizac's gross-revenue margin (27% EBITDA) with an agent-net peer like IDP is apples to oranges. On net revenue Crizac's EBITDA margin is ~83% [E: 282 ÷ 339].
7. **Crizac's history has two bases.**
   - **Company/RHP (restated, includes Crizac UK, acquired 2024):** revenue FY23 ₹473 Cr, **FY24 ₹635 Cr**, FY25 ₹849.5 Cr, FY26 ₹1,042 Cr; EBITDA FY24 **₹72.6 Cr (11.4%)**; PAT FY24 **₹118.9 Cr** [V deck].
   - **Screener (as-reported consolidated):** FY24 revenue **₹530 Cr**, OP ₹145 Cr (27%), PAT ₹116 Cr [V screener].
   - The FY24 EBITDA (11.4%) being far below PAT implies large non-operating/one-off items in the restated FY24 [E]. **Use screener for FY23–FY24 in the aggregates; quote the company's FY24 only with the note.** From FY25 the two bases agree (FY25 ₹849 / FY26 ₹1,042 Cr).
   - **Standalone vs consolidated:** Crizac Ltd (India) standalone revenue fell from ₹344 Cr (FY24) to ₹187 Cr (FY25) and ₹267 Cr (FY26) at 80%+ margins — the agent-facing business moved into **Crizac UK** after the 2024 acquisition; standalone is now largely an intra-group service fee [V screener standalone]. Always use consolidated.
8. **Crizac's "applications", "unique applicants" and "enrolments" are three different funnels.** FY26: **3,94,629 applications**, **24,697 enrolments** (+13.8%), conversion ~10% of unique applicants [V deck; V call]. Q1 FY27: 1,03,539 applications (−6.2%), **47,488 unique applicants**, **4,751 enrolments (+15.0%)**, enrolment rate 10% [V deck]. Revenue tracks enrolments × revenue per enrolment, not applications.
9. **Crizac's UK share has three published versions.** Primer: "~90–95%+". Management on the call: "around 97%" historically. **Q1 FY27 deck: UK 98.7% of destination-country revenue** (Ireland 0.9%, Dubai 0.2%, other 0.2%) [V deck]. Use **~97–99%** and cite the deck. The **source-country** mix (India 50.2%, Asia ex-India 27.2%, Africa 22.3%) is a different split and is diversified [V deck].
10. **UK visa statistics: calendar "year ending" vs Crizac's fiscal years, and main applicants vs total.**
    - Home Office (YE Jun-2026): **383,455 sponsored study visas** (total incl. dependants, −11%), **365,868 main applicants (−12%)**; India **82,523 main applicants (−16%)**, China 86,368 — **China is again the No. 1 source** [V gov.uk].
    - Crizac's deck (FY = Apr–Mar): total UK study visas **FY24 4,46,029 → FY25 4,03,257 → FY26 3,91,572**; Indian UK study visas **1,16,215 → 93,445 → 90,425**; and says "India becomes the largest source country in Mar'26" (12 months to Mar-26, India 90,425 vs China ~88,776 on its chart) [V deck].
    - Both can be right on their own windows; India and China swap the No. 1 spot quarter to quarter. Do not mix windows in one sentence.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Two members, two opposite economic engines, one customer: the aspirational Indian family.**
  - **PhysicsWallah (domestic test-prep, consumer-paid):** FY26 revenue **₹3,900 Cr (+35%)**, screener OP ₹370 Cr (9.5%), reported PAT **−₹24 Cr**; TTM revenue ₹4,106 Cr, TTM PAT **+₹15 Cr**; Q1 FY27 revenue ₹1,054 Cr (+24.4%), PAT −₹88 Cr [V screener]. Market cap **₹36,231 Cr at ₹125** (IPO ₹109; 52-week range ₹77.7–162) → **~9.3× FY26 sales, ~7.5× TTM EV/sales** [V screener; E].
  - **Crizac (outbound student recruitment, university-paid):** FY26 revenue **₹1,042 Cr (+22.7%)**, OP ₹286 Cr (27%), PAT **₹219 Cr (+41%)**, ROE ~37–40%, net cash ₹570 Cr (Jun-26), FY26 dividend ₹140 Cr (63.9% payout) [V screener; V deck]. Market cap **₹2,488 Cr at ₹143** — **a 52-week low (₹142), −42% vs the ₹245 IPO price and −57% from the ₹333 high**; **P/E 11.3×, EV/EBITDA ~6.8×, dividend yield 5.6%** [V screener; E].
  - **Combined (screener consolidated, FY26): revenue ₹4,942 Cr (+32.3%), OP ₹656 Cr (13.3%), PAT ₹195 Cr; m-cap ₹38,719 Cr** [E: straight sums, Part 3]. **Crizac is 21% of revenue, 112% of PAT (PW lost money) and 6% of m-cap.** The market pays ~165× combined TTM PAT for the pair — entirely because of PW's growth-option valuation.
- **The demand base is huge but the gates are narrowing.**
  - **Test prep:** NEET-UG 2026 registrations **22.79 lakh** (2025: 22.76 lakh — flat) [V-snip DH]; JEE Main 2026 Session 1 **~14.1–14.5 lakh, "highest ever"**, both sessions >16 lakh applications [V-snip Allen/Careers360]. Redseer sizes Indian test prep at **~$14.8 bn (≈₹1.4 lakh Cr at ₹94.9) in FY26, growing 12–15% to $23–26 bn by FY30**; UG-entrance alone $3.8 bn, ~70% of it JEE+NEET [V-snip Outlook/Redseer, Aug-2026].
  - **Study abroad:** Indians in higher education abroad fell for the first time in four years to **12.54 lakh (2025) from 13.35 lakh (2024)** (MEA, Parliament) [V-snip PIE]. UK main-applicant study visas to Indians **82,523 in YE Jun-2026, −16%** [V gov.uk]; US F-1 issuance to Indians **−60% in 2025 vs 2024** [V-snip PIE]; Canada's 2026 study-permit cap **408,000 (−16% vs 2024's 485,000 target)** [V-snip CIC News]; Australia NPL **295,000** new commencements for 2026 (+25,000) [V-snip education.gov.au].
- **PW's story is a profitability turn that keeps getting interrupted.**
  - FY24 PAT −₹1,131 Cr → FY25 −₹243 Cr → FY26 −₹24 Cr; quarterly PAT in FY26: Q1 −₹127, Q2 +₹70, Q3 +₹102, Q4 −₹69 Cr; Q1 FY27 −₹88 Cr [V screener]. **Strong seasonality**: Q1 (and Q4) are loss quarters because expenses (faculty increments, marketing for the new academic year, new centres) are front-loaded while revenue from upfront fees is recognised over the course [V letter Q11].
  - **2026's specific shock: NEET-UG 2026 was cancelled by NTA on 12-May-2026 after a coordinated paper-leak and re-held on 21-Jun-2026**; results 16-Jul-2026 [V-snip]. PW's **online NEET collections fell 28% in Q1 FY27**; collections from 1-Jul to 13-Aug rose ~51% YoY [V-sec Inc42; V transcript]. Paid users grew only **2.4%** in Q1 [V-sec].
  - **Guidance (Aug-2026): FY27 revenue +30%, EBITDA +100%, offline "near profitability" this year** [V transcript]. Offline expansion has been throttled to "opportunistic", with new centres "decided over the next 2 quarters" [V transcript] — vs "75+ new centres identified" in the Dec-2025 letter [V letter].
- **Crizac's story is a superb toll-booth on a corridor that is being narrowed by policy.**
  - Enrolments **17,842 (FY24) → 21,710 (FY25) → 24,697 (FY26)**; share of all UK study visas **3.5% → 4.8% → 6.0%**; share of Indian UK study visas **9.0% → 12.4% → 13.9%** [V deck]. It is **gaining share of a shrinking pie**.
  - But Q1 FY27 revenue **−4%** (constant currency −4%), Q2 guided "a bit down", **FY27 guided flat on FY26**, EBITDA margin 25–27% [V call]. **Revenue per enrolment** ~₹4.2 lakh (≈ £3,380) [E: ₹1,042 Cr ÷ 24,697].
  - **Concentration is extreme on three axes:** UK ~98.7% of revenue; **top-10 universities 68.7% of Q1 revenue (top-3 41%, top-5 51%)**; top-10 counselling partners 24.5% of payouts [V deck].
- **Valuation divergence is the single most striking fact for the writer.** PW trades at ~9× sales on near-zero earnings; Crizac trades at ~11× earnings with a 5.6% dividend yield and 37%+ ROE. One is priced as a platform with a long runway; the other as a policy-exposed cyclical with a melting corridor. The writer's job is to explain both prices, not to call either "cheap" or "dear".

### 1.2 Value chains (inputs → end user)

1. **Online test-prep (PW model).**
   - Inputs: **faculty** (lecture, content, doubt-solving), studios and production, the app/cloud stack, marketing (YouTube is the top of the funnel), payment gateways, books/print.
   - Flow: free YouTube lecture → app download (78 M+ downloads, 3.5 M+ DAU, 103 min/day engagement, Sep-25; DAU **4.03 M in Q1 FY27, +26%**) → paid batch (₹4,000) → value-added batches, test series, books → offline centre or adjacent category [V letter; V-sec].
   - "Mega classrooms capable of hosting over 200,000 students live in a single session" [V letter] — the marginal cost of one more online student is close to zero.
2. **Offline coaching (Allen/Aakash/PW Vidyapeeth model).**
   - Inputs: leased buildings (rent), local faculty (the most important and most poachable asset), academic heads, counsellors, non-academic staff, marketing (newspapers, hoardings, results ads), study material, hostels (third-party in Kota/Sikar/Delhi).
   - Customer: Class 11–12 and dropper students (JEE/NEET), Class 6–10 (Foundation), graduates (UPSC/govt exams).
   - Economics: utilisation of seats × fee − (rent + faculty + overhead). PW's seat utilisation measure (students ÷ seats on an annual basis, counting batches) rose **1.77 → 1.99**, and improves ~5–7% a year [V transcript]. Student-teacher ratio "used to be in the 80s", rising [V transcript].
3. **International student recruitment (Crizac/IDP/ApplyBoard model).**
   - Source side: ~17,400+ registered counselling partners (agents) in 85+ source countries; 4,032 active in Q1 FY27 [V deck; V screener announcement 14-Oct-2026].
   - Platform: application screening, document verification, compliance checks (fraud is the universities' biggest fear), routing to universities, offer and CAS tracking, visa support.
   - Destination side: 450+ partner universities; 110 generated revenue in Q1 FY27 (198 in FY26) [V deck].
   - Money flow: student pays tuition to university → after census date/enrolment the university pays Crizac a commission → Crizac pays the agent its share (~67.5% of revenue in FY26) [V deck]. Crizac's receivables (₹239 Cr, 84 days) are roughly offset by agent payables (₹234 Cr) [V screener schedules] — **net working capital ~1.7 days of revenue (FY26), −29 days in Q1 FY27** [V deck].

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **In test prep, value sits with whoever owns the student's trust at the lowest acquisition cost.** PW's founder-teacher brand made CAC low: marketing was **9.6% of revenue in FY25**, guided to 8–9% in FY26; Q1 FY27 marketing ₹128 Cr (12.1% of a seasonal quarter's revenue) [V letter; V transcript]. Offline incumbents spend on results advertising and star faculty.
- **Faculty is the scarce input and the main cost.** PW employee cost **48.9% of revenue in FY26 (~₹1,906 Cr incl. ESOP)**; FY24 59.7% [V screener schedules]. Faculty attrition 25–27% overall, 15–17% for lecture faculty [V letter].
- **In recruitment, value sits with whoever controls the university relationship and the compliance record.** Universities pay ~15–25% of first-year tuition as commission [K: UK sector norms; not disclosed by Crizac]; the platform keeps ~a third and passes ~two-thirds to agents [V deck]. Crizac's revenue per enrolment ≈ ₹4.2 lakh ≈ £3,380 [E] — consistent with ~15–20% of a ~£16–22k one-year master's fee [E].
- **The state captures value at the gate.** NTA sets the exam calendar (2024 and 2026 NEET disruptions), MoE/state laws set who may be coached, and the UK Home Office/IRCC/DHA/US State Department set how many students may go abroad. Neither member controls its volume driver.

### 3.2 Study-abroad demand (Crizac)

- **Push factors (source side):** aspiration, India's weak graduate job market, migration intent, rising middle-class incomes, education loans (NBFCs like Auxilo, Avanse, HDFC Credila). **Pull factors (destination side):** post-study work rights, dependants, PR pathways, university quality.
- **2024–26 every pull factor was cut** (§7.4): UK dependants ban (Jan-2024) and Graduate Route cut to 18 months; Canada caps; Australia NPL and higher visa fees; US interview pause and social-media vetting (2025).
- **The rupee:** at ₹94.9/$ and ~₹125/£, a £25,000 UK master's year (tuition + living) costs ~₹31 lakh vs ~₹25 lakh at ₹100/£ in 2022 [E]. Crizac's call: "the continued strength of both the US dollar and the pound sterling increases the effective cost of education" [V call]. **For Crizac the GBP is a double-edged sword:** commissions are earned in GBP and translated into more rupees (analyst on the call: revenue per student +18% FY24→Q1 FY27 while GBP rose ~22% vs INR), but the same rupee weakness reduces the number of Indian families who can afford the UK [V call].
- **Crizac's source mix diversifies the push side** (India 50.2%, Asia ex-India 27.2% — Nepal, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Vietnam, China; Africa 22.3% — Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya; LatAm small) [V deck], but its pull side is one country.

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Coaching regulation in India (PW)

- **Ministry of Education, "Guidelines for Registration and Regulation of Coaching Centre 2024" (issued 16/18-Jan-2024)** [V-snip Careers360/Drishti; details K]:
  - **No enrolment of students below 16 years / before the secondary school examination** [V-snip].
  - Tutors must be at least graduates; no one convicted of moral turpitude [V-snip].
  - A "coaching centre" = more than 50 students; **each branch registers separately** [V-snip].
  - No misleading advertisements or guaranteed ranks/marks; fee, course and refund policies to be published [V-snip].
  - Pro-rata **fee refund within 10 days** if a student leaves mid-course (incl. hostel/mess) [K]; penalties **₹25,000 (first), ₹1 lakh (second), registration cancelled (third)** [K]; ~1 sq m per student, classes not over 5 hours a day, not too early or late, mental-health counselling [K].
  - Status: guidelines are a model for states (education is on the Concurrent List); enforcement is by states [K].
- **State laws:**
  - **Rajasthan Coaching Centres (Control and Regulation) Bill, 2025:** introduced 19-Mar-2025; referred to a Select Committee 24-Mar-2025 after criticism; as introduced: authority + registration for centres with 50+ students; **penalties ₹2 lakh (first), ₹5 lakh (second), cancellation (third)**; pro-rata refund **within 10 days**; fees in at least **four instalments**; mandatory receipts; the under-16 age bar was dropped from the bill [V-snip Business Standard/TheWeek/Allen]. **Passed by the Assembly in Sep-2025 [K — verify the passage and notification date before printing].**
  - **Assam Coaching Institutes (Control & Regulation) Act, 2025:** cabinet approval 9-Mar-2025; registration, fee disclosure, faculty/infrastructure standards, ban on guaranteed-selection ads, counselling [V-snip Careers360].
  - Earlier state laws: Bihar (2010), Goa (2001), Karnataka (2001), Manipur (2017), UP (2002) coaching acts exist but are lightly enforced [K].
  - **Kota district "Kota Cares" 2025-26:** no hostel security deposit, fixed maintenance fee, anti-suicide fan devices, CCTV and biometric attendance [V-snip]. Kota student suicides 17 in 2024 vs 26 in 2023 [V-snip Swarajya].
- **Dummy-school crackdown:** CBSE has inspected and de-affiliated/show-caused schools where coached students were enrolled but did not attend; from 2025-26 CBSE enforces the **75% attendance rule for board-exam eligibility**, and Rajasthan/Delhi authorities have acted against dummy schools [K — no 2026 source fetched]. This hits the integrated "school + coaching" model hardest and pushes demand to after-school coaching (online/Pathshala-type).
- **GST:** coaching is taxed at **18%** (not an exempt "education service") [K]. PW's ~₹4,000 online fee and ~₹40,000 offline fee are inclusive/exclusive by product; the 18% rate is a cost the unorganised tail often evades [E].
- **NEP 2020** promises reduced board-exam stakes, two board-exam attempts (CBSE from 2026 for Class 10 [K]) and multiple entry points; the stated aim is to reduce the coaching "need", but entrance exams (JEE/NEET/CUET) remain the gate [K].

  - **Read-through for PW:** a 5–7 week shift in the NEET calendar delayed PW's biggest enrolment cycle, cut Q1 FY27 online NEET collections 28% and paid-user growth to 2.4% [V-sec; V transcript]. ### 7.4 China's 2021 "double reduction" — the policy-shock precedent

- On **24-Jul-2021** China's State Council banned for-profit tutoring in core school subjects (K-9), barred new licences, foreign investment and IPOs for such firms, and restricted weekend/holiday tutoring [K].
- **New Oriental, TAL Education and Gaotu lost ~80–95% of market value within weeks**; New Oriental closed K-9 tutoring, cut ~60,000 staff and pivoted to livestream e-commerce (East Buy) and overseas test prep/study-abroad consulting [K].
- **Lesson for India:** India's regulation targets **welfare and conduct** (age, fees, refunds, mental health, advertising) rather than banning for-profit coaching. A China-style ban is not on the agenda [E]. But the NEET/NTA cycle shows how a single government decision can move a quarter.

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

- **GenAI cuts the cost of content and doubt-solving** — PW is embracing it: "AI-first model" across teaching, counselling, operations and product [V-sec Outlook]; **Ask AI**, an **AI companion for children** and a **one-to-one Socratic AI tutor "at a very affordable price" (in beta)** [V transcript]; AI use in content creation, doubt resolution and grading is already changing faculty roles (content/doubt faculty attrition) [V letter]. Employee cost ex-ESOP fell 2.6% YoY in Q1 FY27 while revenue rose 24% [V transcript].
- **GenAI also threatens the content model itself:** Chegg's collapse (revenue −39% in 2025, m-cap ~$0.1 bn) shows what happens when students substitute a free chatbot for paid answers [V-snip]. PW's defence is live teaching, community, results, and physical centres — products that are about **discipline and accountability**, not just information [E].
- **YouTube is both funnel and substitute:** free content is PW's CAC advantage, but it also caps what students will pay online (₹4,000 ACPU). Pi (₹300–400) is a bet on monetising the free tier [V letter].
- **Recruitment tech:** universities' direct digital recruitment, AI application-screening and B2C platforms could disintermediate agents. But regulators are moving the other way — the UK's BCA/AQF regime favours scaled, auditable intermediaries [V deck; K]. Crizac positions itself as "AI native" (EduMentor, ForeignAdmits) [V deck].
- **Exam-format risk:** more computer-based testing (NTA reform) or a move to aptitude-style tests could shift coaching content but not demand [E].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **The state controls the gate, and in 2026 it slammed two of them.** NEET-UG 2026 was cancelled for a paper leak and re-held seven weeks later; every major English-speaking destination is restricting student visas. PW's Q1 FY27 paid-user growth fell to 2.4%; Crizac's Q1 revenue fell 4% and FY27 is guided flat. Neither company can grow faster than the exam calendar or the visa quota allows — they can only take share inside them.
2. **Affordability beats premium in Indian test prep.** Byju's (premium, sales-led, debt-funded) is insolvent; Unacademy sold for ~6% of its peak value; Allen's profit fell 70% and Aakash makes ~1% EBITDA. PW, charging ~₹4,000 online and ~₹40,000 offline, grew revenue 35% and turned FCF-positive. The edge is a near-free audience (YouTube, 4 M DAU) that keeps marketing at ~8–10% of revenue.
3. **Offline is where the money is, and where the losses are.** Offline is ~45% of PW's revenue from ~9% of its users, but the network still lost ₹106 Cr (pre-Ind AS) in Q1 FY27. Whether mature centres reach 12–15% margins — and whether PW stops opening new loss-making ones — decides PW's earnings path. Management has now throttled expansion.
4. **Crizac's economics are excellent; its concentration is extreme.** A ~27% EBITDA margin on gross commissions, ~37% ROE and net cash — but ~99% UK revenue, 69% from ten universities, in a market where UK visas to Indians fell 16% and 36% of English universities run deficits. It is gaining share (6% of all UK study visas), so its fate is market volume × share × revenue per student.
5. **The rupee is a wedge between the two.** A weaker rupee (₹94.9/$; ~₹125/£) makes studying abroad dearer (fewer Crizac students) but translates each GBP commission into more rupees; for PW it pushes families towards cheaper domestic coaching.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Test prep (PW):**
Revenue = (online users × ACPU) + (offline enrolments × offline ARPU) + others
EBITDA (pre-Ind AS) = revenue − faculty & staff − rent (lease payments) − marketing − direct/content costs − tech/overhead
Offline centre profit = seats × utilisation × ARPU − (rent + centre faculty + centre overhead), with a 2–3-year ramp.

**Recruitment (Crizac):**
Revenue = enrolments × average tuition × commission rate (× GBP/INR)
EBITDA = revenue × (1 − agent split) − opex
Enrolments = destination visa issuance × Crizac share; share depends on BCA/AQF compliance and the agent network.

### Insights (what an expert would add)

1. **PW is a treasury company with a test-prep business attached, for now.**
   - Q1 FY27 other income (₹109 Cr) was larger than adjusted EBITDA *minus* other income (₹26 Cr). FY26 other income (₹232 Cr) was 77% of pre-Ind AS EBITDA (₹300 Cr).
   - With ₹2,041 Cr of IPO money in FDs at ~6.8% plus ~₹3,500 Cr of other treasury, other income alone can run at ~₹350–400 Cr a year [E]. That will carry a large part of the "EBITDA +100%" guide on the company's definition.
   - **The writer should judge FY27 on pre-Ind AS EBITDA (₹300 Cr base), not on headline EBITDA.**
2. **PW's IPO money is being used for overheads faster than for the stated growth objects.**
   - By Jun-26, 64% of utilised funds (₹674 Cr of ₹1,059 Cr) was "general corporate purposes" — salaries, professional fees, GST.
   - Only 7% of the ₹460.6 Cr fit-out budget and 9% of the ₹710 Cr marketing budget had been spent.
   - Combined with "opportunistic" offline expansion, **the ₹460 Cr fit-out object may end up re-purposed** (it needs shareholder approval if the variation exceeds 10%). It is a disclosure to watch in each quarterly monitoring report.
3. **Offline's FY27 "near profitability" target needs a ~₹200 Cr swing that Q1 did not start [E].**
   - The company's cohort data are the bull case: 2022–24 centres are EBITDA-positive, and hyperlocal centres break even within ~24 months.
   - The bear case is the arithmetic: 155 of the 353 centres (44%) were net additions in FY26 alone (198 → 353), so a large part of the network is still on the loss side of its J-curve [E].
   - The Pathshala → Vidyapeeth conversions (84 → 70) and the subsidiary drag (Utkarsh enrolments down on scarce Rajasthan exam notifications; 59k vs 80k) are the swing factors.
4. **PW is quietly consolidating its acquisitions with cash.** It has forward contracts to buy the rest of Utkarsh (24.5%), Xylem (22.75%) and Sarrthi (to 85% by FY31), plus a ₹339 Cr NCI-buyout liability on the balance sheet. This is where "selective inorganic" money is already committed.
5. **Crizac's FY24 hides the clearest governance signal in the sub-sector.** In the year before the IPO:
   - the listed group paid a ₹74.9 Cr break fee to a promoter-owned UK company (Crizac Informatics) to take over its university contracts and impose a 10-year non-compete;
   - it bought ₹103.6 Cr of IT software from a promoter-owned Indian company (Crizac Technologies);
   - the listed company still holds shares in Crizac Technologies.

   All of this is disclosed and arm's-length per management. But the 100% OFS IPO, plus these transfers, means **the promoters monetised the business three ways before public shareholders came in**. The −42% share price and the institutions' exit (FII+DII 10% → 4%) should be read with this in mind.
6. **Crizac's diversification is moving the wrong way, while its university concentration is improving.**
   - UK share: 96.4% (FY23) → 95.1% (FY25) → 98.7% (Q1 FY27).
   - Top-10 universities: 82.4% → 68.7%.
   - The platform is broadening *within* the UK, which is the BCA-driven consolidation trade, not a geographic hedge. The <60%-UK target needs either a large non-UK acquisition or a UK collapse.
7. **UK applications are now falling faster than UK grants** (−19% YE Sep-2026 vs −12% grants YE Jun-2026). With the Graduate Route cut on 1-Jan-2027 and the levy from 2028-29, the January-2027 and September-2027 intakes are at risk.
   - Crizac's enrolment growth (+14–15%) has so far come from share gains of ~1 percentage point a year of UK visas. If the market shrinks ~20% (IDP's planning case), Crizac needs ~+25% share gains just to stay flat [E] — hence "flattish" guidance.
8. **University credit risk is unprovided.** Crizac's receivables run on 90–180-day terms with nil ECL provision, while the OfS forecasts 42.7% of English providers in deficit in 2025-26. Receivables from a single top-3 university (top-3 ≈ ₹82 Cr of Q1 FY27 revenue) could approach a quarter's PAT (~₹47 Cr) if it failed or delayed payment [E].
