## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Ind AS 116 EBITDA vs pre-Ind-AS ("rent-paid") EBITDA — the central trap.**
   - Under Ind AS 116 (from FY20), fixed-rent mall leases are capitalised. Rent leaves operating expenses and reappears as **ROU depreciation + interest on lease liabilities**, which inflates EBITDA.
   - **FY26:** reported EBITDA **₹2,278.9 Cr (33.4% of reported total income)** vs company pre-Ind-AS EBITDA **₹968.0 Cr (14.4%)**. The ₹1,310.9 Cr gap is essentially the fixed rent that was capitalised [V Q4 FY26 PPT Annexure 2].
   - **Screener's "operating profit" of ₹2,095 Cr (32%)** is the Ind AS 116 figure *excluding other income* — a third number. Our primer quotes "EBITDA ~₹2,095 Cr, ~31%", which is the screener basis.
   - The primer's pre-Ind-AS estimate of **"₹700–800 Cr, roughly 11–12%" is wrong**. The company figure is ₹968 Cr / 14.4%. See §C and Part 2 for the full reconciliation. **Always use pre-Ind-AS EBITDA in prose** and say so.
2. **Net debt: three numbers.**
   - **Company net debt ex-leases:** ₹1,430 Cr (Mar-23) → ₹1,294 (Mar-24) → ₹952 (Mar-25) → ₹162 (Mar-26) → **net cash ₹81 Cr (Jun-26) → net cash ₹141 Cr (Sep-26)** [V PPTs]. "Cash" in the company figure includes investments, other bank balances and bank deposits.
   - **Screener "borrowings" include lease liabilities.** Sep-26 borrowings of ₹6,309 Cr = ₹501 Cr bank debt + **₹5,808 Cr lease liabilities** [V PPT Annexure 3; V screener]. Screener-basis net debt (borrowings − cash) ≈ ₹5,667 Cr, which is an Ind AS 116 number.
   - **Use the company ex-lease figure** for net debt and leverage. Show the ₹5,808 Cr lease liability separately and pair it only with Ind AS 116 EBITDA.
3. **Industry box office: Ormax CY vs "Ormax + company estimates" FY quarters.**
   - Ormax: **CY2025 ₹13,395 Cr; H1 CY2026 ₹6,398 Cr (+10% YoY); YTD Sep-2026 ~₹11,000 Cr** [V Ormax].
   - PVR INOX Q1 FY27 deck: "Jan–Jun'26 India box office **₹6,665 Cr, +20%** vs ₹5,570 Cr" [V PPT]. That is **₹267 Cr higher and double the growth rate** of Ormax's own H1 figure.
   - The two agree better for Jul–Sep: Ormax months Jul ₹1,582 + Aug ₹1,768 + Sep "over ₹1,100" ≈ ₹4,450 Cr [E], vs PVR deck Q2 FY27 **₹4,443 Cr**. In the call, Bijli said "₹4,433 Cr", a transcription or slip.
   - **Prefer Ormax for CY industry totals.** Use PVR's FY-quarter industry series only for PVR-share calculations and label it "Ormax + company estimates".
4. **Gross vs net box office; ATP incl. vs excl. GST.**
   - Ormax "gross box office" and PVR "GBOC" are **gross of GST**.
   - PVR's reported **"sale of movie tickets" is net of GST** (and of any local entertainment tax) but **gross of the distributor share**, which is booked as a cost (FHC, "film hire cost").
   - **PVR ATP is gross of GST.** Check: FY26 150.1 mn admits × ₹280 = ₹4,203 Cr gross; ÷ 1.18 ≈ ₹3,562 Cr vs reported net ticket revenue ₹3,530 Cr [E arithmetic].
   - The trade press quotes "net" collections (ex-GST) for films — e.g. Sacnilk "net" vs "gross". Never mix the two.
5. **Industry ATP vs PVR ATP.**
   - Ormax all-India ATP was **₹161 in CY2025** (₹134 in CY2024, +20%) [V Ormax 2025]. PVR INOX's FY26 ATP was **₹280** [V].
   - The gap reflects the single-screen and tier-3 mix, South India's lower prices (Tamil Nadu has regulated ceilings) and PVR's premium formats. Do not compare PVR's ATP with an industry ATP without saying this.
6. **SPH-to-ATP ratio claims.**
   - Management said in the Q2 FY27 call: "we are at about 60% of the ATP… in Western countries food sales or SPH is equal to the ATP" [V transcript].
   - On the reported (gross-of-GST) ATP the ratio is lower: FY26 147/280 = **52.5%**; Q2 FY27 153/298 = **51%** [E]. It reaches ~60% only against **ATP net of GST** (280/1.18 = 237 → 62%) [E].
   - The "parity in the West" claim is **not supported by AMC's Q3-2026 8-K**: US F&B per patron $8.85 vs US ATP $13.55 = **65%**; consolidated $7.87 / $12.73 = **62%** [V SEC]. India's SPH/ATP gap to the US is therefore small. Flag this for the writer and do not repeat the "parity" claim as fact.
7. **Admits/occupancy exclude management (FOCO) screens; screen count includes them.**
   - Screen count includes **71 management screens in 19 cinemas** (Mar-26 onward; 42 screens / 12 cinemas at Mar-25). PVR earns only a management fee on these.
   - Admits, occupancy, ATP, SPH and weighted-average screens **exclude** them [V PPT footnotes].
   - Weighted average screens (Q2 FY27: 1,717) ≠ period-end screens (1,797).
8. **Restated comparatives (Zea Maize).**
   - FY25 total income was **₹5,874.6 Cr originally vs ₹5,791.8 Cr restated**; FY25 F&B was ₹1,827.0 Cr originally vs ₹1,747.0 Cr restated; FY25 pre-Ind-AS EBITDA was ₹470.3 Cr originally vs ₹484.8 Cr restated [V Q4 FY25 and Q4 FY26 PPTs].
   - The 4700BC sales to third parties were in F&B and other income. Use the restated figures for FY25→FY26 growth and the original ones for FY24→FY25.
9. **PAT basis.**
   - FY26 PAT has four versions: reported ₹332.8 Cr, which matches screener's ₹333 Cr; pre-Ind-AS ₹386.8 Cr; **continuing operations reported ₹176.1 Cr**; continuing pre-Ind-AS ₹230.6 Cr.
   - The difference is the Zea Maize gain plus the ~₹48 Cr labour-code exceptional charge (−₹48.3 Cr pre-tax) [V].
   - **Use the reported total in tables, with continuing ops in a separate column.** Q2 FY27 has no discontinued item: reported PAT ₹263.1 Cr, pre-Ind-AS ₹273.3 Cr.
10. **Screens vs properties vs cities, and closures.**
    - Gross openings ≠ net additions. FY25: 77 opened, net +5 → **~72 closed** [E arithmetic: 1,718 + 77 − 1,723]. FY26: 93 opened, **18 closed**, net +75 [V]. H1 FY27: 24 opened, **25 exited**, net ≈ −1 (1,798 → 1,797) [V].
    - The analyst on the Q2 FY27 call cited "1,890 screens declared in Q4 FY23". The company's own Mar-23 count was ~1,689 [K], so this is a transcription slip; Mar-24 was 1,718 [V].

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **India is a high-volume, low-price, under-screened, content-volatile market. In FY26–H1 FY27 it was in a genuine up-cycle.**
  - **CY2025 gross box office was a record ₹13,395 Cr** (CY2023 ₹12,226 Cr; CY2024 ₹11,833 Cr; pre-Covid CY2019 ₹10,948 Cr) [V Ormax 2025; V PVR Q4 FY26 PPT citing Ormax].
  - But **footfalls fell 6% to 83.2 Cr** (832 mn). Growth came from **ATP +20% to ₹161** [V Ormax 2025].
  - 2026 is running hotter: **Ormax H1 2026 ₹6,398 Cr (+10%), footfalls 37.8 Cr (+5%); YTD Sep ~₹11,000 Cr**, with Ormax projecting a first ₹15,000 Cr year [V Ormax].
  - **Jul–Sep 2026 was the first ₹4,000 Cr+ quarter in Indian history** (₹4,443 Cr, +23%), led by **English films +87% to ₹1,024 Cr**: Spider-Man: Brand New Day ₹608 Cr, India's highest-grossing English film ever; Nolan's The Odyssey ₹233 Cr, the first film to pass ₹100 Cr from IMAX screens alone [V PVR Q2 PPT; V-snip t2online].
- **Footfalls are structurally lower than pre-Covid. The industry now monetises fewer, richer visits.**
  - **Footfalls by year:** 146 Cr in 2019 (MAI-EY) → 94.3 Cr in 2023 (post-Covid high, Ormax) → 88.3 Cr in 2024 → 83.2 Cr in 2025 [V EY; V Ormax].
  - MAI-EY: **footfalls −41% 2019→2024; footfalls per screen −44%; theatrical revenue CAGR −0.2% (2019–24)**. Filmed entertainment shrank from ₹19,100 Cr to ₹18,746 Cr [V EY].
  - **PVR INOX is the exception that proves the premiumisation thesis.** Its FY26 admits of 150.1 mn are close to FY24's 151.4 mn, but ATP (₹280) and SPH (₹147) are records. **Q2 FY27 ATP ₹298 (+14%), SPH ₹153 (+14%), admits 46.3 mn (+4%), occupancy 30.4%** [V].
- **Screens: about 10,000 for 1.45 bn people, flat for six years.**
  - MAI-EY counts **9,527 screens (2019) → 9,927 (2024)**; density fell from **7.6 to 6.8 per million**, vs **US 109, UK 66, China 64** [V EY].
  - FICCI-EY 2026: 2025 screens **+1% (240 added, 124 shut — mainly single screens; South −1%, rest of India +3%)**, ~10,033 total [V-snip].
  - PVR management: "India has a total of about **10,500**… South… **45–50%**… still **50% of the screens are single screens**" [V transcript Q2 FY27].
  - **16,350 of ~19,000 PIN codes have no cinema screen** [V EY].
- **PVR INOX is the industry's price-setter and consolidator.**
  - **1,797 screens / ~360 cinemas / 114 cities** (Sep-26), ~**17–18% of India's screens** [E: 1,797 / ~10,000–10,500].
  - About **31% of India's gross box office** [E: FY26 admits × ATP = ₹4,203 Cr ÷ FY26 industry ₹13,519 Cr; Q2 FY27 46.3 mn × ₹298 = ₹1,380 Cr ÷ ₹4,443 Cr = 31%].

## 1. What the sub-sector does: products, value chain, where value is captured

### 1.1 The product
Film exhibition sells **time-bound access to a seat in a darkened auditorium for a specific show**, plus everything sold to the person in that seat. Six revenue streams (PVR INOX FY26, pre-Ind-AS, continuing ops) [V Q4 FY26 PPT]:

| Stream | FY26 ₹ Cr | Share of total income | FY25 (restated) ₹ Cr | YoY | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sale of movie tickets (net of GST) | 3,530.1 | 52.4% | 2,954.9 | +19.5% | Gross ticket ÷ (1+GST); distributor share is a cost |
| Food & beverages | 2,088.4 | 31.0% | 1,747.0 | +19.5% | Popcorn, cola, hot food; ~78% gross margin (COGS 22.5%) |
| Advertising | 463.6 | 6.9% | 447.5 | +3.6% | On-screen pre-show and intermission, in-foyer, digital/brand tie-ups |
| Convenience fees | 241.0 | 3.6% | 211.1 | +14.2% | Online booking fee (own app/web and aggregators); online penetration 72% in Q2 FY27 |
| Other operating income | 323.1 | 4.8% | 339.4 | −4.8% | Of which **film distribution ₹222.4 Cr** (PVR INOX Pictures), VPF (virtual print fee) income, management fees from FOCO screens, gaming etc. |
| Other income | 96.4 | 1.4% | 91.9 | +4.9% | Treasury, provision reversals |
| **Total income** | **6,742.6** | 100% | **5,791.8** | **+16.4%** | |

Mix in H1 FY27: tickets 52.9%, F&B 32.5%, ads 6.3%, convenience 3.9%, other op 3.2%, other income 1.3% [V Q2 PPT; E shares]. **F&B is gaining share; advertising is losing share.** Ad revenue grew only 1.3% in H1 FY27 while tickets grew 17%.

### 1.2 Value chain (inputs → audience)
1. **Producer/studio** finances and makes the film. Funding sources: theatrical, satellite, digital/OTT, music and overseas rights (pre-sales).
2. **Distributor** (often the producer itself, or a studio — Yash Raj, Dharma, AA Films, Pen Marudhar, Hombale, Mythri, Sun Pictures; Hollywood majors Disney, Sony, Warner, Universal/Paramount via local arms) licenses theatrical rights territory by territory. India has **14 film-trade "circuits"** (Mumbai, Delhi-UP, East Punjab, CP-Berar, CI, Rajasthan, Nizam, Mysore, etc.) [K].
3. **Digital cinema service providers (DCSPs)** — **UFO Moviez** (incl. Scrabble Digital) and **Qube Cinema** dominate — lease projection systems and deliver encrypted content (DCP/KDM) via satellite/hard drive. Exhibitors pay a **VPF (virtual print fee)**, or distributors pay per screen. In the Hollywood DCI model the VPF flows to the exhibitor (PVR INOX books "VPF income" in other operating income: Q4 FY26 ₹14.5 Cr vs ₹16.1 Cr) [V Q4 FY26 PPT footnote]. **CCI fined UFO, Scrabble and Qube ₹2.70 Cr (Apr-2025)** for anti-competitive equipment-leasing practices [V-snip].
4. **Exhibitor** — multiplex chain or single-screen owner — programmes shows, sets ticket prices (subject to state rules), sells F&B and advertising, and pays the distributor a **revenue share of net box office**.
5. **Aggregators** — BookMyShow (Big Tree Entertainment), Paytm/District (Zomato) — sell tickets for a convenience fee, part of which is shared with the exhibitor or retained [K]. PVR INOX also sells on its own app and website. ITAT Mumbai held that no TDS under s.194H applied to convenience fees retained by BookMyShow (PVR INOX appeal) [V-snip Taxscan].
6. **Audience** — gross ticket price = net price + GST (5% if ≤₹100, 18% if >₹100 since 22-Sep-2025) + any state/local entertainment tax (e.g. Tamil Nadu LBET) [V Storyboard18; see §7].

### 1.3 Where value is captured — "who keeps what from a ₹300 ticket and a ₹150 snack" [E, worked from PVR INOX FY26 disclosures]

| Line | ₹ | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Gross ticket (ATP) | 300.0 | Above ₹100 → 18% GST |
| − GST @18% | −45.8 | 300 × 18/118 |
| = Net ticket | 254.2 | This is "sale of movie tickets" |
| − Film hire cost @ ~45% of net | −114.4 | FY26 FHC 45.4%; Q2 FY27 44.5% [V] |
| = Exhibitor's ticket gross profit | 139.8 | |
| F&B spend (SPH, as reported) | 150.0 | |
| − COGS @ ~22% | −33.0 | FY26 COGS 22.5% of F&B [V] |
| = F&B gross profit | 117.0 | Not shared with the distributor |
| + Advertising + convenience + other, per admit | ~69 | FY26 (₹463.6 + 241.0 + 323.1 Cr) ÷ 150.1 mn admits ≈ ₹68.5 [E] |
| = Gross profit per admit | ~326 | |
| − Fixed costs per admit (rent, CAM, staff, power, other) | ~−235 | FY26 fixed ex-distribution ₹3,528.9 Cr ÷ 150.1 mn ≈ ₹235 [E] |
| = Pre-Ind-AS EBITDA per admit | ~₹90 | FY26 actual: ₹968 Cr ÷ 150.1 mn = **₹64.5** (lower ATP/SPH than this example, plus distribution costs) [E] |

**Read:** for each admit, F&B gross profit (₹100–117) is roughly 80% of the ticket gross profit (₹120–140), on about 60% of the revenue. This is the single most important economic fact in the sector. The primer's claim that "the snacks and the ads out-earn the movies" holds only when convenience fees and advertising are added to F&B [E].

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Content supply and quality (dominant driver).**
   - In CY2025, 37 films crossed ₹100 Cr (22 in 2024) [V Ormax].
   - In H1 2026, six films crossed ₹200 Cr (four in H1 2025) but only 13 crossed ₹100 Cr (17). Box office concentrated: the top-15 share rose to 58% from 49% [V].
   - Mid-budget revival in FY26 (PVR deck): the ₹100–200 Cr band rose from 12% to 20% of GBOC, and dependence on ₹500 Cr+ blockbusters fell from 30% to 19% [V].
   - Sleeper hits: **Hanuman Ansh (budget ~₹2–2.5 Cr, India GBOC ₹351 Cr)**; Mahavatar Narsimha (₹301 Cr in Q2 FY26) [V transcript; V PPT].
   - Streaming IP crossing over: **Mirzapur: The Movie (₹267 Cr)**, the first Hindi web series turned into a feature [V].
2. **Hollywood normalisation after the 2023 WGA/SAG strikes.** Q2 FY27 English GBOC +87% [V]. Dubbed versions take English films beyond the metros: ~36% of Spider-Man's GBOC came from dubbed versions, ~₹200 Cr from the Hindi dub [V PPT; transcript].
3. **Premiumisation and format demand.**
   - IMAX/4DX/recliner/luxury formats lift ATP. PVR's premium-format screens are 286 (16% of the circuit) [V Q4 FY26 PPT].
   - Q2 FY27 ATP +14% came partly from IMAX-heavy Hollywood [V transcript].
4. **Value pricing to rebuild frequency.**
   - Weekday morning shows ₹99 (falls in the 5% GST slab), Friday first show ₹149, front rows from ₹79 [V Q2 PPT].
   - Five National Cinema Days / Cinema Lovers' Days in FY25 drew **34 lakh admits** [V Q4 FY25 PPT].
   - Re-releases: Avengers Endgame Encore ₹45 Cr in 4 weeks (₹28 Cr in-quarter in the deck) [V].
5. **Alternate content.** Live sport, concerts and events. Q1 FY27 alternate content was 1% of PVR GBOC: **2.5 lakh admits at ATP ₹409** [V Q1 FY27 PPT].
6. **Urbanisation, malls and tier-2/3 penetration.**
   - Tier-2/3 cities hold 22% of PVR's screens (Tier II 13%, Tier III 9%) [V Q2 PPT].
   - The SMART format targets ~300 cities / ~1,000 screens [V].
7. **Substitutes: OTT and the window.**
   - 81% of cinemagoers prefer theatres, but **81% believe films reach streaming within 8 weeks**, and **34% would wait** if the window were short [V EY survey of 14,688 cinemagoers, mid-2025].
   - Piracy: 51% of media consumers access pirated content [V].
8. **Pricing regulation (demand-side ceiling).** South India's ATP is ~25% lower than the rest of India; Tamil Nadu is the lowest. Higher occupancy in the South "more than makes up" for it [V transcript].

**End-market mix at PVR INOX (Sep-26)** [V Q2 PPT]:
- **By city tier:** metro 49%, tier I 29%, tier II 13%, tier III 9% of screens.
- **By region:** South 34% (incl. Sri Lanka), North 27%, West 20%, Central 11%, East 8%.
- **Largest states by screens:** Maharashtra 264, Karnataka 236, UP 145, Tamil Nadu 142, Telangana 132, Gujarat 119, Delhi 114, Haryana 100.

## 5. Industry structure

- **Concentration.**
  - By screens: PVR INOX ~18% of ~10,000; Cinépolis ~5%; all other multiplex chains combined perhaps 15–20%; single screens ~50% [E/K].
  - **By gross box office: PVR INOX ~31%** [E, §0]. The top-2 organised chains likely take ~40% of national GBOC [E].
  - Multiplexes take a disproportionate share of Hindi and English GBOC. Single screens and local chains dominate Tamil, Telugu and mass-market Hindi in smaller towns. That is why PVR's Q2 FY27 box office grew 18% against industry growth of 23%: "Hanuman Ansh, Mirzapur and regional films did very well in mass markets in Tier 2–4 towns where single-screen penetration is more than multiplex presence" [V transcript].
- **Organised vs unorganised.** Single screens are largely family-owned, often in old buildings. MAI-EY / FICCI-EY report steady closures (124 screens shut in 2025, mainly single screens) [V-snip]. Many survive by converting to 2–3-screen "mini-plexes" or joining FOCO/franchise networks (Connplex, Movietime, PVR's own FOCO/management model) [K].
- **Unlisted and MNC players.**
  - **Cinépolis India** — subsidiary of Mexico's Cinépolis (global top-4 by screens). Brands Cinépolis, Cinépolis VIP, Fun Cinemas (acquired from Essel/Zee in 2014) [K]. ~490 screens [V-snip]. Has entered on-screen and DOOH advertising (350-screen ad network) [V-snip indiantelevision]. **Financials (MCA filings) not retrieved — GAP.**
  - **Miraj Entertainment** — Rajasthan-based (Miraj Group). Unlisted [K].
  - **Movietime Cinemas** — Delhi NCR. Unlisted [K].
  - **Rajhans Cinemas** — Gujarat. Unlisted [K].
  - **Mukta A2 Cinemas** — subsidiary of the listed **Mukta Arts** (NSE MUKTAARTS, a micro-cap). This is a small **listed adjacent exhibitor that the brief did not mention** [K; verify listing status].
  - **Carnival Cinemas** — expanded by acquisition (Big Cinemas from Reliance Media Works, 2014; HDIL's Broadway; Stargaze) to ~450 claimed screens, then collapsed into insolvency and closures (2020–22) [K]. It is the industry's cautionary tale about debt-funded rollups.
  - **SPI Cinemas (Sathyam, Chennai)** — acquired by PVR in 2018 for ~₹633 Cr (71.7%) [K].
  - **DT Cinemas** (DLF) — acquired by PVR in 2016 [K].
  - **Big Cinemas** — sold to Carnival in 2014 [K].
- **Listed adjacent.**
  - **UFO Moviez India** (digital cinema distribution and in-cinema advertising to single screens): still trading (~₹68, Jul-2026) [V-snip]. The 2017 UFO–Qube merger scheme was abandoned [K]. In 2023 the two formed two 50:50 JVs (advertising, content services), whose termination was later approved [V-snip, undated]. Penalised by CCI in Apr-2025 [V-snip].

- **Imports/China.** Not applicable (exhibition is a non-tradeable service). Equipment — projectors (Barco, Christie, NEC), IMAX systems and seating — is largely imported [K].

## 6. Business models inside the sub-sector (archetypes)

| Archetype | Capex by exhibitor | Revenue booked | Cost booked | Economics | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Leased mall multiplex (traditional)** | 100% fit-out (~₹2–3 Cr/screen) | All (tickets, F&B, ads, conv. fee) | All (rent MG/revenue share, CAM, staff, power) | Highest absolute EBITDA. Payback 3–5 years in good catchments. Fixed-rent exposure creates operating leverage | Most of PVR INOX's estate; Cinépolis |
| **2. Asset-light (developer co-funds)** | 20–60% (developer funds 40–80%) | All | All | Same P&L as (1) but lower capital employed → higher ROCE. Rent terms often more revenue-linked | PVR INOX: 86 screens / 20 cinemas signed (May-26); **64% of FY27 openings** [V] |
| **3. FOCO / management contract** | 0% (developer/franchisee funds 100%) | **Only a management fee** (share of property revenue) | None (operating costs sit with the owner) | Pure fee income. Tiny revenue, near-100% margin. Brand and programming leverage. Shows up in "other operating income" | PVR INOX: 71 screens / 19 cinemas operating; 52 screens / 14 cinemas in pipeline [V] |
| **4. Owned property** | Land + building + fit-out | All | No rent | Highest margin per screen, but capital-heavy. PVR INOX owns a few properties and has put asset sales on hold since it is net cash [V transcript] | Some legacy PVR/INOX sites |
| **5. Single screen (owner-operated)** | Owned/inherited | Tickets, small F&B | Staff, power, maintenance | Low ATP (~₹100–150), weak F&B, little advertising. Survives on mass hits. Upgrade capex rarely economic | ~5,000 screens, mostly family-owned [E] |
| **6. Tier-3/4 compact format ("SMART")** | 70–75% of a normal screen's capex; opex −25% | All | All | **ATP ~₹190** (vs ₹298 circuit), "hero-item" F&B menu, payback ≤3–4 years targeted | PVR INOX Muzaffarpur (Sep-26); Rampur, Bharatpur in fit-out; ~100 more signed for the next 12–18 months [V] |

Adjacent model: **film distribution** (PVR INOX Pictures). FY26 income ₹222.4 Cr against distribution and print costs of ₹316 Cr (the cost line also includes prints/VPF), so it is a low-margin, volatile add-on. H1 FY27 distribution income fell, hence "other operating income −20.5%" [V].

**Revenue accounting by model (CFO, Q2 FY27 call):** "On the asset-light, we account the entire P&L in our books… On the franchise, we just account the management fees… The operating cost sits with the franchise owner." [V transcript]. **Implication:** a shift to FOCO lowers reported revenue per screen and admits per screen (FOCO admits are excluded) while raising ROCE.

## 8. History and cycles (defining events)

- **1997:** PVR (Priya Village Roadshow JV) opens India's first multiplex, PVR Anupam, Saket, Delhi [K].
- **1999–2002:** INOX Leisure opens its first multiplex in Pune (1999/2002). States grant entertainment-tax holidays to multiplexes, the trigger for the first wave [K].
- **2006–07:** PVR (2006) and INOX (2006) list; Adlabs/Big Cinemas, Fame, Cinemax expand [K].
- **2008–10:** Digitisation (UFO/Scrabble/Qube) collapses print costs and enables wide releases [K].
- **2012–14:** Consolidation. PVR buys Cinemax (2012); INOX buys Fame (2010) and Satyam (2014); Carnival buys Big Cinemas (2014); Cinépolis buys Fun Cinemas (2014) [K].
- **2016–18:** PVR buys DT Cinemas (2016) and SPI Cinemas (2018) [K].
- **Jul-2017:** GST at 28%/18% on tickets subsumes state entertainment tax (except local body levies). Tamil Nadu theatre strike over a 30% LBET [K; V-snip].
- **Jan-2019:** GST cut to 18%/12% [K].
- **Mar-2020 to 2021:** COVID closures. India box office falls to ₹2,056 Cr in CY2020 and ₹3,772 Cr in CY2021 [V]. Direct-to-OTT releases; windows compress to ~4 weeks [K].
- **2022:** Recovery to ₹10,637 Cr, led by South Indian pan-India hits (KGF 2, RRR) while Hindi struggles [V/K].
- **27-Mar-2022:** PVR–INOX merger announced. **NCLT sanction Jan-2023; effective 6-Feb-2023** [K].

- **2024:** ₹11,833 Cr (−3%); Hindi −13%; Pushpa 2, Kalki 2898 AD and Stree 2 the only ₹500 Cr+ films; Malayalam crosses ₹1,000 Cr [V-snip]. **PVR INOX FY25: admits −9.5%, pre-Ind-AS EBITDA margin 8.4%, loss** [V].

### 9.3 What drives each KPI

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **OTT substitution and window compression.** The core risk. Partly mitigated: Hindi windows have held at 8 weeks for national chains; streamers have retreated from aggressive direct-to-OTT buying ("better sense has now taken over") [V transcript]; FICCI-EY shows OTT rights values falling 4% a year (2022–25) [V]. Risk persists in South windows (4 weeks) and on dubbed versions (Jailer 2) [V-snip].
2. **Piracy.** 51% of media consumers use pirated sources [V EY]. Cinematograph (Amendment) Act 2023 penalties [K].
3. **Premium large-format (PLF) technology.** IMAX laser, 4DX, ScreenX, Dolby Cinema, LED screens (Samsung Onyx; PVR has 2 LED screens [V/E]) raise ATP but need capex and licensor revenue shares.
4. **Digital advertising shift.** Cinema ad revenue has stagnated at ~₹450–465 Cr for PVR INOX since FY24 [V]. Management is building a **digital "pay-per-eyeball" ad platform, ~8–10 months away**, to monetise sleeper hits (Hanuman Ansh, Saiyaara, Mahavatar Narsimha) that draw crowds but are not pre-sold to advertisers [V transcript].
5. **Data/loyalty and dynamic pricing.** The own app/web drives online penetration (72%) and convenience fees. Personalised F&B offers from the booking database [V]. Aggregator dependence (BookMyShow/District) is a margin risk [K].
6. **Projection technology life.** Projector life reassessed from 10 to 15 years (Ind AS 8, Q2 FY27) [V]. A watch item: is this justified, or does it defer a re-equipment cycle?
7. **Content technology/AI.** Cheaper VFX and AI-assisted production could lift film supply. AI-generated content could also flood OTT. Effect on cinemas unclear [E].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert leads with)

1. **The content cycle is the demand curve.**
   - Exhibitors do not make films, and one quarter's slate swings EBITDA by ±40%.
   - CY2024 (Hindi −13%, Hollywood still strike-hit) produced PVR INOX's FY25 loss. CY2025–26 (Dhurandhar, Saiyaara, Kantara: Chapter 1, Spider-Man, The Odyssey, Hanuman Ansh) produced record margins.
   - The **language mix** matters as much as the total. Hindi and English skew to multiplexes; Tamil, Telugu and mass Hindi skew to single screens. In Q2 FY27 industry GBOC rose 23% but PVR's only 18% [V].
2. **Fewer visits, more money per visit (ATP + SPH premiumisation).**
   - India footfalls are ~40% below 2019 [V EY]. Box office is at records because ATP rose ~20% in 2025 [V Ormax].
   - At PVR INOX, ATP + SPH = ₹451 per head in Q2 FY27 vs ₹396 a year earlier (+14%) [V/E].
   - The tension: affordability, price caps and the ₹100 GST threshold push the other way. Value pricing (₹79–₹149 seats) is the industry's attempt to rebuild frequency without diluting the premium core.
3. **Fixed-cost operating leverage, disguised by Ind AS 116.**
   - Rent + CAM + staff + power ≈ ₹3,529 Cr a year at PVR INOX (FY26) [V], almost all of it fixed.
   - Pre-Ind-AS EBITDA margin swings from 8% (FY25) to 22% (Q2 FY27) on modest revenue changes.
   - Reported (Ind AS) EBITDA margins of 29–38% hide this. The pre-Ind-AS lens is mandatory for understanding cyclicality, leverage and valuation.
4. **Under-screening plus the capital-light pivot (supply side).**
   - 6.8 screens per million [V], flat screen count, ~50% single screens [V], 16,350 PIN codes with no screen [V].
   - After Carnival's collapse and the COVID debt overhang, growth has shifted to **developer-funded models** (asset-light 64%, FOCO 21% of FY27 openings) and **compact tier-3/4 formats (SMART)**. ROCE is up from −0.3% (FY25) to 16.5% (TTM) [V].
   - Management now guides 150–175 screens a year from FY28 [V].
5. **The window and OTT bargaining.**
   - The 8-week Hindi window survives for national chains [V-snip]. Streamers have pulled back from direct-to-OTT, and OTT rights values are falling ~4% a year [V].
   - Exhibitor scale (PVR INOX ~31% of GBOC) is the enforcement mechanism: chains refused the Jailer 2 Hindi dub on a 4-week window [V-snip].
6. **The policy wedge: GST, local taxes, price caps, convenience fees.**
   - Each attacks a specific profit line: 18% GST above ₹100, Tamil Nadu LBET and price ceilings, the stayed Karnataka ₹200 cap, convenience-fee bans.
   - The courts have so far protected exhibitors: Bombay HC on fees (Jul-2025), Karnataka HC stay (Sep-2025) [V-snip].
7. **F&B and advertising as the margin engine.**
   - F&B is 31–33% of income at a ~78% gross margin and is not shared with distributors [V].
   - Advertising is ~6–7% and has stagnated since FY24 [V]. A digital ad platform is in development [V].
8. **Consolidation and balance-sheet strength.**
   - PVR INOX is now net cash, has started capital returns ([V] buyback; 30% payout policy) and is ~4× Cinépolis.
   - Weaker chains and single screens are closing or seeking FOCO/franchise partners — a structural share gain for the leader [E].

## C. Revenue / profit equation, with a worked example

**Exhibitor revenue** = Admits × [ATP_net + SPH + (Ad + Conv + Other) per admit]
where Admits = Screens × Seats per screen × Shows per day × 365 × Occupancy, and ATP_net = ATP ÷ (1 + GST).

**Pre-Ind-AS EBITDA** = Admits × [ATP_net × (1 − FHC%) + SPH × (1 − COGS%) + Ad/Conv/Other per admit] − Fixed costs (rent incl. variable + CAM + staff + power + other) − distribution costs.

**Worked example — PVR INOX FY26 (actuals, pre-Ind-AS) [V inputs; E arithmetic]:**
- Admits 150.1 mn; ATP ₹280 gross → ATP_net ≈ ₹235 (reported ticket revenue ₹3,530.1 Cr ÷ 150.1 mn).
- Ticket revenue ₹3,530.1 Cr × (1 − 0.454 FHC) = **₹1,927 Cr ticket gross profit**.
- F&B: 150.1 mn × ₹147 = ₹2,206 Cr. Reported F&B is ₹2,088.4 Cr; the gap suggests SPH includes some GST or items not in F&B revenue [E — FLAG]. × (1 − 0.225) = **₹1,619 Cr F&B gross profit**.
- Ads ₹463.6 + convenience ₹241.0 + other operating ₹323.1 + other income ₹96.4 = **₹1,124 Cr**.
- **Gross profit ≈ ₹4,670 Cr.**
- Fixed costs ex-distribution **₹3,528.9 Cr**; distribution/print **₹316.0 Cr** → **₹3,845 Cr**.
- **EBITDA ≈ ₹825 Cr vs reported ₹968 Cr.** The ~₹143 Cr difference is because FHC% and COGS% are company ratios on bases slightly different from reported ticket/F&B revenue (FHC is likely on net box office after some deductions). The equation is directionally exact; label the output INDICATIVE.

**Worked example — a single 5-screen multiplex, metro, leased (ILLUSTRATIVE) [E]:**
- 5 screens × 220 seats × 5 shows × 365 days = 2.0 mn seats a year. At 30% occupancy → **602,250 admits**.
- ATP ₹300 → net ₹254 → ticket revenue ₹15.3 Cr; FHC 45% → ticket gross profit ₹8.4 Cr.
- SPH ₹150 → F&B ₹9.0 Cr; 78% margin → ₹7.0 Cr.
- Ads/convenience/other ₹70/admit → ₹4.2 Cr.
- Gross profit ₹19.6 Cr.
- Fixed costs: rent ₹0.8 Cr/screen ×5 = ₹4.0 Cr; CAM ₹1.1 Cr; staff ₹2.6 Cr; power ₹1.1 Cr; other ₹1.7 Cr → ₹10.5 Cr.
- **Pre-Ind-AS EBITDA ≈ ₹9.1 Cr (~32% of ~₹28.5 Cr revenue).**
- Capex ₹2.5 Cr/screen × 5 = ₹12.5 Cr → **payback ~1.5–2 years at 30% occupancy**. At 20% occupancy, EBITDA ≈ ₹2.6 Cr and payback ~5 years. **This is the operating-leverage lesson.**

- **Screens:** "on track to open 90–100 new screens in FY27, with the bulk in H2, mainly capital-light". **FY28 onward "150, 175… every year, could be even more"**; an analyst's "~500 over 3 years" was confirmed ("Yes"). Openings models FY27: 64% asset-light, ~21–23% FOCO, ~13–15% lease (the Q1 and Q2 slides differ slightly). Regions FY27: South 39%, North 36%, West 13%, East+Central 12% (Q1 slide) [V].

- **Capex:** FY27 ₹300–330 Cr (H1 ₹132 Cr); FY28 ">₹350 Cr".
- **Depreciation:** ₹35–40 Cr a year lower after the useful-life change.

- **Advertising:** H1 weak for lack of pre-marketed tentpoles; expects "a fairly healthy growth number" for the full year; digital pay-per-eyeball platform in ~8–10 months.
- **F&B:** SPH growth split 60% price / 40% volume; 85% strike rate; popcorn + Pepsi = 60% of F&B sales; gross margin up from ~74% to 77–78% (4PL logistics, wastage reduction); expanding home delivery, Grab & Go, outdoor catering, in-house brands (F&B beyond cinemas = 1.2% of gross F&B sales in Q1 FY27).

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed, and new primer issues

### 4.5-A Insights an expert would add
1. **Footfalls depend on whose count you use, and the multiplex/single-screen split explains it.**
   - Ormax says 2025 footfalls fell 6% (−19% vs 2019); Comscore says they rose 1.4%. Both agree GBO hit a record on price.
   - Comscore's split shows **multiplexes sell 31% of tickets but take 51% of the money, at 2.3× the single-screen ATP (₹251 vs ₹110)**. Multiplex ATP rose 17% in 2025 vs 7% for single screens.
   - So "fewer, richer visits" is really "**more of the money migrating to multiplexes**". PVR (~55% of multiplex admits, ~61% of multiplex GBO [E]) is the main beneficiary.
2. **PVR's market share is a content-mix variable, not a structural constant.**
   - Quarterly share ranged 26.8–32.8% over FY25–FY27; it falls when South/mass films lead (Q4 FY25, Q4 FY26, and Q2 FY27's Hanuman Ansh/Mirzapur).
   - Its state map explains why: PVR holds **59% of Delhi's screens and 24–33% in Maharashtra, Karnataka, Haryana and Punjab, but only 4–5% in Andhra Pradesh, Kerala and Bihar and 12% in Tamil Nadu** (D5).
   - The AP/Telangana price approvals that lifted South ATP 22% in 2025 mostly accrued to single screens.
   - This is the strategic logic of the South push (35–40% of new screens) and of SMART.
3. **Guidance has been cut three times in nine months, and the market did not penalise it.**
   - FY27 openings went 150 → 120 → 90–100; capex ₹350–400 Cr → ₹300–330 Cr.
   - Part of the cut is mix (FOCO = zero capex) and part is execution: licence delays meant zero openings in Q1, and SMART went from 28–30 screens to 2–3 cinemas.
   - Meanwhile the FY28+ ambition was raised (150–175 a year; "1,000 in five years").
   - The writer should present delivery risk on the 150–175 figure honestly: FY26 delivered 93 against 100–110 guided; FY27 is tracking 90–100 against 150 first mooted.
4. **The SPH-parity claim is backwards.**
   - US F&B per patron is **78% of ATP at Cinemark** and **65% at AMC** (US), vs PVR's 51% on gross ATP (~61% net of GST).
   - The headroom argument still holds, but because US chains run *above* India's ratio, not at "parity".
   - India's lower ratio partly reflects price: PVR's ATP is 1.74× the all-India average, and the denominator is inflated by 18% GST.
5. **The real fixed charge is the lease cash, ~₹1,180–1,311 Cr a year — 1.2–1.35× pre-Ind-AS EBITDA.**
   - Undiscounted future lease commitments are ₹8,280 Cr, ~5× FY26 pre-Ind-AS EBITDA.
   - Ind-Ra's lease-adjusted leverage (net debt incl. leases / EBITDAR) of **2.2×** is the right "credit" metric. It explains why PVR is AA, not AAA, despite being "net cash".
6. **Governance and related-party points the primer lacks:**
   - a recurring **INOX brand royalty (₹19 Cr, +8.6% a year) to the Chairman personally**;
   - rent to a Bijli-family company (₹4.7 Cr);
   - executive pay of ~₹39 Cr for the two Bijli-side executives (~4% of pre-Ind-AS EBITDA);
   - **~40% of the MD's personal stake still pledged** (personal borrowing), even after the Nov-2026 release.
   - Also: the promoter group is only 27.6%, and ~17% of it sits with GFL/INOX — the true control is a two-family pact.
7. **Two new regulatory exposures:**
   - **CCI's VPF probe** (Sep-2025; commitments offered). VPF is small (~₹58 Cr a year at the Q4 FY26 ₹14.5 Cr run-rate [E]), but the "discriminatory pricing" finding would be a reputational and legal precedent for a ~31%-share player.
   - **The Supreme Court's Nov-2025 remarks on F&B prices** ("₹700 for coffee"), made while hearing the Karnataka cap matter, are a headline risk for the F&B margin engine (78% gross margin).
8. **Advertising is a share-of-market problem, not a cyclical blip.**
   - India's in-cinema ad market grew just 3% to ₹930 Cr in 2025 (FICCI-EY), and PVR already takes about half of it.
   - Growth must come from eyeball-based/digital selling (web/app inventory ₹2–3 Cr a year so far) rather than share gains.
   - Pre-COVID quarterly ad revenue (~₹146 Cr, analyst) is still above today's ~₹110–131 Cr.
