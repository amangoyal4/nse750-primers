## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **"HEG" now means two different companies.** (See "Still listed" above.)
   - **HEG Advanced Materials (HEGAM, listed)** = TACC + BESS + hydro/solar (incl. merged BEL).
   - **HEG Limited (new, ex-HEG Graphite)** = the electrode business. Listing awaited.
   - Every FY26 number in our primer and on screener describes the **pre-split whole**.
   - **For the electrode economics use the standalone FY26 numbers** (net sales ₹2,551.77 Cr, EBITDA ₹497 Cr, PAT ₹180.72 Cr [primer, from the company release]). They are the closest proxy for the new HEG Limited. They include treasury income that may be split between the two entities; the allocation is not public [E].
   - HEGAM's market cap cannot be put next to the FY26 P&L.
2. **Screener "operating profit" vs company "EBITDA" differ hugely in this sub-sector.**
   - **Graphite India FY26:** screener OP **₹202 Cr (7%)** vs primer EBITDA **~₹375 Cr (~13%)**. The primer figure probably adds part of other income or excludes inventory write-downs. Q1 FY27: screener OP ₹144 Cr (17%) vs a press-reported "EBITDA margin 17.1%" [V-snip Sahi]. Another summary says "28.6%" [V-snip], probably including other income.
   - **HEG FY26:** screener consolidated OP ₹399 Cr (16%) vs company standalone EBITDA ₹497 Cr (19%) [primer]. Q1 FY27: screener OP ₹151 Cr (22%) vs a press-reported "standalone EBITDA margin ~29%" [V-snip quartr].
   - **RHIM FY26:** screener OP ₹443 Cr (11%) vs company adjusted EBITDA ₹477 Cr (~11.9%) [V-snip constructionworld]. Q1 FY27: screener OP ₹138 Cr vs company operating EBITDA ₹147 Cr (14.5%) [V PR]. The ~₹9 Cr gap is "other income" that RHIM counts as operating.
   - **Rule:** tables use screener OP (one basis). Prose may quote company EBITDA, labelled.
3. **Treasury income is a large share of electrode-maker profit.**
   - Graphite India's FY26 other income of ₹162 Cr = **66% of PBT (₹244 Cr)**. TTM other income ₹322 Cr vs TTM OP ₹90 Cr [V screener].
   - HEG's FY26 other income of ₹259 Cr = **63% of PBT (₹408 Cr)** [V screener].
   - Graphite India's Q2 FY24 other income of ₹1,045 Cr (Sep-2023 quarter) is a one-off gain [V screener]. It is probably a fair-value/realised gain on investments; the nature is not verified. It inflates FY24 PAT (₹805 Cr), so **FY24 is not a "good electrode year"**: FY24 OP was −₹143 Cr.
   - HEG's Q4 FY26 standalone loss (₹163 Cr) came from **MTM losses on foreign investments + rupee depreciation** (~₹189 Cr hit), not from the furnace [primer].
   - **Always show "core" (OP-based) and "reported" PAT side by side.**
4. **Graphite India capacity: 98 kt nameplate, ~80 kt operating, two Indian plants.**
   - ICRA (30-Jan-2026): "installed capacity of around 98,000 MTPA (**including 18,000 MT capacity in Germany which is currently non-operational**)… domestic capacity of ~80,000 MTPA… two operating plants at **Durgapur (West Bengal) and Nashik (Maharashtra)**" [V ICRA].
   - The Bengaluru electrode plant no longer operates [E from ICRA wording; it closed ~2020–21 [K]].
   - Our primer's "about 98,000 tonnes a year across its Indian plants" is **wrong**.
   - Utilisation figures (~93% H1 FY26 per ICRA; 104% Q4 FY26 and 97% Q1 FY27 per company summaries) are on the **~80 kt domestic** base [E].
5. **HEG capacity: 100 kt at one site; 115 kt by early 2028.** Q1 FY27 utilisation was ">90%" (vs ~80% a year earlier) [V-snip sahi]. Management says there is "no significant volume growth beyond this due to operational limitations" [V Arthneeti].
6. **RHIM: shipments ≠ production. FY26 shipment growth was ~+5%, not +9.4%.**
   - Q1 FY27: **production 81 kt vs shipments 122 kt** (Q4 FY26: 79 vs 117; Q1 FY26: 85 vs 129) [V RHIM presentation]. About a third of what RHIM ships is not made in its own Indian plants: traded or parent-sourced product and mechanism/flow-control parts.
   - FY26 shipments **523 kt, "+5%"** [V-snip constructionworld]; our primer says +9.4%.
   - **Q1 FY27 shipments fell 5% YoY (122 kt vs 129 kt) while revenue rose 6%.** That is price/mix, not volume. The press release's "+5%" cover KPI is **quarter-on-quarter** (122 vs 117).
7. **RHIM's "loss" years are impairments of acquired goodwill/intangibles, three years running.**
   - FY23 other income −₹646 Cr; FY24 −₹322 Cr (Q4 FY24); FY26 −₹529 Cr (Q4 FY26 exceptional ₹557 Cr = goodwill impairment on "RHIM IR", i.e. ex-Dalmia) [V screener; V presentation].
   - Reported PAT: FY23 −₹466 Cr, FY24 −₹100 Cr, FY26 −₹383 Cr. Pre-exceptional profit is ~₹170–270 Cr a year [E].
   - Screener's P/E of 38.7 is on a basis that excludes the exceptional (TTM reported PAT is −₹354 Cr) [E].
   - Amortisation of acquired intangibles is **₹21 Cr a quarter (~₹84 Cr/yr)** [V presentation]. Company "EBITA" adds it back.
8. **"Spread" means GE price minus needle coke price per tonne. It is not a margin.**
   - ICRA's downgrade trigger for Graphite India: "industry spread between GE and needle coke (CNC) prices … below **$1,600 per tonne** on a sustained basis" [V ICRA]. That is a useful anchor for "normal".
   - Conversion cost (power, pitch, labour, depreciation; ~$1,000–1,500/t [E]) comes out of the spread. So a $1,600 spread is roughly breakeven-to-thin, not healthy.
   - Spreads in 2018 were ~$8,000–10,000/t [K approx].
9. **Two electrode "price" series that do not match.**
   - **Chinese domestic UHP**: RMB 16,200–17,756/t incl. 13% VAT (~$2,200–2,450/t) in May–Jun 2026 [V BigMint/Mysteel].
   - **Western/Indian export prices**: GrafTech realised **~$3,900/t** in Q3-2026 [V GrafTech 8-K]. Indian implied realisations are ~₹2.8–3.2 lakh/t (~$3,200–3,600/t) [E].
   - Chinese electrodes are mostly smaller-diameter/HP grades at the margin. The UHP ≥600 mm market outside China is priced separately.
   - Trade cases (US, EU) keep the two apart.
10. **Refractory "market share" claims use different denominators.**
    - RHIM says "30%+ market share in India" [V presentation] and targets ~40% by 2029 [primer].
    - Screener's summary says "30% market share" [V].
    - Our primer says "mid-teens-to-thirties" depending on segment.
    - India's refractory market size is not officially published. Implied market ≈ ₹12,000–13,500 Cr [E: ₹4,020 Cr ÷ 0.30–0.33].

# PART 1 — INDUSTRY DOSSIER

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Two businesses under one screener label.** "Electrodes & Refractories" pairs:
  - **(a) Graphite electrodes:** Graphite India, HEG. A global, oligopolistic, deeply cyclical spread business, ~60–70% exported. Its demand is EAF steel output.
  - **(b) Refractories:** RHI Magnesita India. A domestic, fragmented, steady consumables business priced on tonnes of steel/cement produced × kg per tonne. Its costs track Chinese magnesia/alumina/graphite.
  - **The only real link is the customer (steel mills).** The writer should treat them as two sub-industries with a shared end-market.
- **Group size (screener consolidated FY26):** revenue **₹9,440 Cr** (+12.6% YoY), OP **₹1,044 Cr (11.1%)**, reported PAT **₹129 Cr**. PAT is **~₹685 Cr ex RHIM's ₹556 Cr goodwill impairment** [E: sums].
  - Electrode pair: revenue ₹5,420 Cr, OP ₹601 Cr (11.1%), PAT ₹512 Cr, of which **other income ₹421 Cr**.
  - Group m-cap ₹27,272 Cr on 23-Oct-2026. **Of this, ₹6,556 Cr is the HEG stub that no longer owns electrodes.** The valuation-clean m-cap (Graphite India + RHIM) is ₹20,716 Cr [E].
- **The electrode cycle turned in 2026, after three years of oversupply (2023–25).**
  - **GrafTech** (the Western price-setter) announced a price increase of **$600–1,200/t in Mar-2026** and then an **immediate minimum 30% increase on 8-Sep-2026**, saying prices "had declined over the past three years while raw material, energy and logistics costs have risen" [V-snip nasdaq/steelmarketupdate; V-snip Sahi].
  - Its Q3-2026 realised price is still only **~$3,900/t (−7% YoY, flat QoQ)**. Cash COGS is **$3,653/t**, adjusted EBITDA $8.6 m on 28.8 kt (≈ **$300/t**) and utilisation **64%**. It is **closing Monterrey (Mexico)**, cutting annual capacity to ~127 kt, and guides "**meaningfully higher realized pricing in 2027**" with Q4-26 pricing +mid-single-digit % QoQ [V GrafTech 8-K 23-Oct-2026].
  - HEG says price hikes of **5–7% are done, with a further 5–10% expected**, to offset a 10–15% input-cost rise (needle coke +$200–300/t). It is locking **~70% of capacity into 3–5-year contracts** [V Arthneeti; V-snip sahi].
  - **Graphite India's stock rose 18.5% on 9-Sep-2026** on GrafTech's announcement [V Sahi].
- **Indian electrode makers are running full and earning again, but most "profit" is still treasury income.**
  - Graphite India Q1 FY27: revenue **₹842 Cr (+27%)**, OP **₹144 Cr (17%)** vs ₹43 Cr, PAT **₹171 Cr** [V screener]. Standalone utilisation **97%** (82% a year ago) [V-snip].
  - HEG (pre-split) Q1 FY27: revenue **₹681 Cr (+11%)**, OP **₹151 Cr (22%)**, PAT ₹122 Cr [V screener]; utilisation >90% [V-snip].
  - Electrode-pair Q1 FY27 OP **+98% YoY** to ₹295 Cr; OPM 19.4% vs 11.7% [E].
  - FY26 was a weak, volume-led year: Graphite India OPM 7% with a **Q4 operating loss of −₹139 Cr** (inventory write-downs), and HEG Q4 OP −₹148 Cr [V screener].
- **Trade remedies are now a two-edged sword for India.**
  - **The US is investigating Indian electrodes as well as Chinese ones.** The large-diameter (>425 mm) AD/CVD case was filed by Resonac Graphite America and Tokai Carbon GE (LDGE Fair Trade Coalition).
  - **Preliminary CVD (27-Jul-2026): China 103.49% (AFA); Graphite India 3.68%, HEG 6.99%, all-others India 5.87%** [V trade.gov].
  - **Preliminary AD: Graphite India 12.22% margin, 9.88% cash deposit** (company disclosure 29-Sep-2026) [V screener].
  - Final determinations are due **8-Dec-2026** [V trade.gov].
  - The US market for these electrodes from India is small: **792 t, $2.25 m in 2025** under HTS 8545.11.0020 [V trade.gov]. The case matters more as a precedent than for volume.
  - EU: definitive ADD on Chinese graphite electrode systems was extended in 2022 (Implementing Reg. 2022/558) [V-snip eur-lex title]. India's electrodes enter the EU without such a duty.
  - India: **no Indian ADD on Chinese graphite electrodes was found** (gap, §H).
- **Refractories: volume-light, price-led recovery in FY27 under a 13% margin ceiling.**
  - RHIM Q1 FY27: revenue ₹1,014 Cr (+6%), operating EBITDA **₹147 Cr (14.5%, +3.7 pp)**, PAT ₹65 Cr (+83%), **shipments 122 kt (−5% YoY)** [V PR/presentation]. FY27 margin guidance remains **~13%** [V-snip sahi].

### 1.2 Value chains (inputs → customer)

1. **Graphite electrodes (Graphite India / HEG model)**
   - **Inputs per tonne of finished electrode [K, industry norms; E ranges]:**
     - ~0.8–1.0 t **calcined needle coke** (petroleum-based from decant/slurry oil, or coal-tar-based). This is the critical input, ~40–60% of cash cost.
     - ~0.25–0.35 t coal-tar **pitch** (binder and impregnation).
     - **Electricity ~4–6 MWh** (graphitisation at ~2,800–3,000°C is the bulk).
     - Natural gas/fuel for baking.
   - **Process:** mix & extrude "green" electrode → bake (~800–1,000°C, 2–4 weeks) → pitch impregnation → re-bake (1–3 cycles) → graphitise (Acheson or lengthwise LWG furnaces) → machine/thread → pin assembly.
   - **The cycle takes 2–6 months.** Graphite India says ~2 months per batch; HEG ~6 months of WIP [primers].
     - This is why **inventory days are 320–390** (FY26: Graphite India 321, HEG 389 [V screener]) and why price falls strand high-cost coke in WIP (FY26 Q4 losses).
   - **Customers:** EAF mini-mills (rebar, long products, increasingly flat via EAF+DRI), ladle-furnace operators at BF-BOF plants (a small share), ferro-alloy/silicon-metal makers (submerged-arc furnaces use mostly carbon/Söderberg electrodes; graphite is a minority).
   - **Value capture:**
     - Needle coke producers capture value in shortages (2018: CNC rose to ~$3,000–4,000+/t long-term contract [K approx]).
     - Electrode makers capture value when electrode supply is short (2017–18).
     - Steel mills capture value in gluts (2019–2025).
     - **Integrated players (GrafTech with Seadrift; Resonac/Showa Denko via Nippon Steel Chemical & Material–linked supply [K]) earn a structural hedge.** HEG and Graphite India buy all their CNC.
2. **Needle coke**
   - **Petroleum needle coke:** from FCC decant oil, via delayed coking with special feed prep, then calcination.
     - Producers [K]: Phillips 66 (Lake Charles US, Humber UK), GrafTech/Seadrift (US), Sumitomo Corp/Petrocokes Japan, ENEOS, Shandong Yida/Jingyang and other Chinese makers, Indian Oil (planned).
   - **Coal-tar (pitch) needle coke:** Mitsubishi Chemical, C-Chem (Nippon Steel Chemical & Material), POSCO Future M/PMC Tech (Korea), many Chinese makers [K].
   - **India: none commercially.** Indian Oil approved (Stage-1, Sep-2020) a **56 ktpa CNC grassroot needle coker at Paradip, ₹1,268 Cr**, using in-house R&D technology. IOCL then put India's requirement at **80–100 ktpa, met entirely by imports** [V-snip iocl.com 2020]. **No 2026 commissioning status was found** (§H).
   - **Competing use: battery anodes.** Synthetic graphite anode demand pulls on "green" needle coke/premium coke. This was a 2017–18 squeeze factor and a structural one now (China's anode market "tilting toward synthetic graphite" [V-snip fastmarkets]).
3. **Refractories (RHIM model)**
   - **Raw materials** (much imported, mostly from China) [primer; K]:
     - Dead-burned and fused **magnesia** (Liaoning province dominates [V-snip USGS]; China's fused magnesia export needs a MOFCOM licence, HS 25199010 [V-snip htshub]).
     - Calcined **bauxite**/brown & white fused alumina, **flake graphite** (for MgO-C bricks), zirconia, spinel, silicon carbide, **quartzite** (own mines at Raigarh, Chhattisgarh and Bhikampali, Odisha [V presentation]), resins/pitch, antioxidants (Al/Si metal powders).
   - **Process:** crush/grade → mix with binders → press (shaped) or blend (monolithics) → fire/temper → install (often by the supplier).
   - **Customers [K approx, global split]:** steel ~65–70%, cement & lime ~10–15%, non-ferrous ~5–8%, glass ~3–5%, energy/chemicals/environmental the rest.
   - **Refractory cost as a share of customer cost (RHIM):** steel **c.3%**, cement **c.0.5%**, non-ferrous **c.0.2%**, environmental/chemicals c.1–1.5% [V presentation]. Small spend, huge consequence of failure: this is the pricing-power argument.
   - **Value capture:** technology/flow-control and service contracts (sticky, margin-rich) versus commodity bricks (price-led, China-input-exposed). **RHIM's material cost was 58–63% of revenue** in the last five quarters [V presentation], so gross margin is ~37–42%.

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

### 3.1 Electrodes: EAF steel output (not capex)
- **Equation:** GE demand ≈ EAF steel tonnes × specific consumption (kg/t) + ladle-furnace use.
- **Specific consumption has fallen** from ~6–7 kg/t (1980s) to **~1.2–2.0 kg/t** on modern UHP furnaces. That is a **~1–2%/yr structural headwind** [K; primer].
- **World EAF share:** ~28–30% of crude steel (2023–24) [K worldsteel]. China ~10%; **US ~70–72%**; EU ~43–45%; Japan ~25%; Turkey ~70%; Middle East (Iran, Saudi, UAE, Oman, Qatar) ~85–90% EAF/DRI [K].
- **Drivers of the EAF shift [K unless noted]:**
  1. **Decarbonisation economics:** BF-BOF ~2.0–2.3 t CO₂/t vs scrap-EAF ~0.4–0.7 t (grid-dependent); DRI(gas)-EAF ~1.0–1.4 t.
     - **EU CBAM** definitive phase from **1-Jan-2026**: importers buy certificates for embedded emissions, phasing in as free allocation falls (2026–2034) [K].
     - The **EU steel import regime from 1-Jul-2026** (Reg. 2026/1384; quotas −47% vs 2024, 50% out-of-quota duty, ~18.3 Mt ceiling, "melt-and-pour" origin) [V-snip EC/SMM] raises EU mill utilisation, and European mills are ~45% EAF.
  2. **New EAF capacity:** "roughly 60 million metric tons" of new EAF capacity planned for 2026–28 [V Arthneeti, HEG call]. Examples [K]: US (Nucor, Steel Dynamics, BlueScope, Big River 2, US Steel Big River), EU (Thyssenkrupp/ArcelorMittal DRI-EAF; several delayed), Middle East/North Africa, India (JSW/AM/NS and Tata Ludhiana EAF), SE Asia.
  3. **US EAF share ~70%** with Sec 232 at 50% supporting domestic output; "US steel production is up 6% year-to-date through August" 2026 [V 8-K].
  4. **China:** EAF share stuck at ~10% because of scrap/power costs. Policy aims for 15%+ but has repeatedly slipped. China's steel output fell 4.4% in 2025 [V-snip worldsteel]. Weak Chinese domestic demand = more Chinese electrode exports.
- **India specifically:**
  - Crude steel **168.4 Mt FY26** [V-snip]; capacity **~221.9 mtpa (May-2026)** [V-snip NBM]; National Steel Policy 2017 target **300 mtpa capacity / 255 Mt production by 2030–31** [K].
  - **Route mix FY25: BF-BOF 63 Mt (41%), IF 57 Mt (38%), EAF 32 Mt (21%)** [V-snip BigMint].
  - Induction furnaces do not use graphite electrodes. **Electrode-relevant Indian EAF output ≈ 32–36 Mt** [E] (but IF→EAF upgrades are a long-run upside).
  - Most new Indian capacity (2025–30) is BF-BOF (JSW, Tata Kalinganagar, AM/NS Hazira, SAIL, JSPL Angul). **India's EAF share may fall before it rises** [E; primer optimism should be tempered].
  - **India Green Steel Taxonomy (12-Dec-2024):** steel below **2.2 t CO₂e/t finished steel** can be rated green; 5-star <1.6, 4-star 1.6–2.0, 3-star 2.0–2.2; reviewed every 3 years; NISST verifies [V-snip BigMint/ClearIAS]. This favours scrap-EAF/gas-DRI over coal-DRI/IF.
  - India's carbon market (CCTS) intensity targets for steel are notified [K, verify].
- **Middle East:** the largest export region for Indian electrodes (HEG ~20% of FY26 sales [primer]). The **West Asia war (from 28-Feb-2026)** disrupted logistics (HEG cited it in Q4 FY26) and also **blocked Chinese shipments** [V BigMint]. Net effect on Indian makers: logistics costs up, competitive position in the Gulf better.

### 3.2 Refractories: tonnes produced × kg/t × price × share
- **Steel (~70% of RHIM [E]):** India crude +10.7% FY26, +3.0% Q1 FY27 [V-snip]. RHIM: "Steel producers reported a strong operational quarter … blast furnace ramp-ups and higher utilization" [V presentation].
  - **BF-BOF steel consumes more refractory per tonne** (BF hearth, torpedo ladles, BOF, RH degassers) than EAF. India's BF-led capex is good for refractories even if it is not for electrodes [K/E].
- **Cement (~10–15% [E]):** India cement capacity ~690–700 mtpa (FY26) [K approx]; kiln relines are mostly annual. RHIM lost share in cement (FY26 broker note: "loss of market share in the cement business") [V-snip MNCL]; Q1 FY27 cement volumes were "maintenance-led" [V].
- **Iron-making/DRI/pellet:** ~₹1,800 Cr market [V presentation]. India's DRI output (largest in the world, ~50–55 Mt [K approx]) and pellet plants are growth niches.
- **Glass, non-ferrous, oil & gas:** small but higher-margin. RHIM "drove growth across non-cement industrial segments (Oil & Gas and Glass)" [V].

## 5. Industry structure

### 5.1 Graphite electrodes: a global oligopoly with a Chinese fringe
- **Concentration:** "the GE capacity is with five to six major players globally, resulting in high-capacity concentration" [V ICRA]. Outside China the top six (Resonac, GrafTech, HEG, Graphite India, Tokai Carbon, EPM/Nippon Carbon) hold ~85–90% of non-China capacity [E].
- **India is a duopoly:** HEG and Graphite India are "among the only two graphite electrode manufacturers in the country" [V ICRA]. There are no unlisted Indian majors. Small carbon-electrode makers serve ferro-alloys.
- **China:** dozens of producers led by **Fangda Carbon** [primer; K] plus Kaiyuan Oriental, Jilin Carbon, Shida/Dantan, Shanxi Juxian (the two named in the US CVD case [V trade.gov]). Chinese capacity grew strongly in 2018–23. China is the **marginal exporter and the price-setter in HP/small-UHP grades**. Exports were ~300–360 kt/yr in 2023–25 [K approx]; **Apr-2026 UHP exports were 25,344 t (−30.8% YoY)** [V BigMint].
- **Global peers and owners [K]:**
  - **GrafTech International** (US; NYSE: EAF; Brookfield exited 2023). Vertically integrated via **Seadrift Coke** (Texas). Gross debt **$1,225 m** (Sep-2026), liquidity $242 m, net loss $46.8 m in Q3-26 [V 8-K]. Plants: Calais (France), Pamplona (Spain), St Marys (US, partly idle); Monterrey (Mexico) closing [V].
  - **Resonac Holdings** (ex-Showa Denko; bought SGL's GE business in 2017). Plants in Japan, US (Ridgeville SC — petitioner), Germany (Meitingen), Spain, Austria, Malaysia [K]. Resonac has been reviewing/divesting GE assets [K, verify].
  - **Tokai Carbon** (Japan; US unit Tokai Carbon GE, Charlotte NC — petitioner). Closed some plants in 2024–25 [K approx].
  - **Nippon Carbon**, SEC Carbon (Japan); **EPM Group** (Russia, Novocherkassk/Chelyabinsk); **SANGRAF** (US/Italy, ex-Graftech/SGL assets) [K].
- **Customer power:** EAF mills buy on 6–12-month (sometimes quarterly) contracts. HEG is now pushing **3–5-year contracts for ~70% of capacity** [V Arthneeti], a structural change if it succeeds.

### 5.2 Refractories: fragmented, consolidating around MNC subsidiaries
- **India's top players [primer; K]:**
  - RHI Magnesita India (~30%+; parent RHI Magnesita N.V., 56.07% [V screener]).
  - **TRL Krosaki Refractories** (Krosaki Harima/Nippon Steel ~77%, Tata Steel minority [K approx]).

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **1960s–70s, foundation:** Graphite India incorporated 1974 (originally with Great Lakes Carbon, US) [primer; K]. HEG's Mandideep plant started 1977 ("the most recent global entrant in 1976" [V screener key points]). Both used Western licences; India became a GE exporter in the 1990s–2000s.
2. **2000s, consolidation abroad:** UCAR → GrafTech; SGL Carbon; Showa Denko; Tokai. Graphite India bought Carbon Electrodes Germany (Nürnberg) ~2000s [K approx; ICRA lists the Bavaria Electrodes GmbH subsidiary].
3. **2015–16, the trough:** China's steel glut; electrode prices ~$2,000–2,500/t; Western closures (SGL sells GE to Showa Denko 2016–17; GrafTech bought by Brookfield 2015) [K].
4. **2017–18, the super-cycle:**
   - China shut ~100 Mt of induction-furnace capacity (2016–17) and replaced it with EAFs. Environmental shutdowns hit Chinese electrode and needle-coke plants. Battery-anode demand diverted needle coke [primer; K].
   - UHP 600 mm spot prices went from ~$2,500/t to **$10,000–15,000/t**; contracts ~$8,000–10,000/t [K approx].
   - **FY19: Graphite India revenue ₹7,858 Cr, OP ₹5,020 Cr (64%), PAT ₹3,396 Cr; HEG revenue ₹6,591 Cr, OP ₹4,662 Cr (71%), PAT ₹3,026 Cr** [V screener].
   - GrafTech IPO'd (Apr-2018) and signed 3–5-year take-or-pay contracts at ~$9,700/t [K].
5. **2019–21, the bust:**
   - Chinese capacity returned and expanded; spot prices fell to ~$3,000–4,000/t by 2020 [K].
   - FY21 OP: Graphite India −₹215 Cr, HEG −₹53 Cr [V screener].
   - Both had banked the windfall. Graphite India's investments rose from ₹631 Cr (FY17) to ₹2,590 Cr (FY19) [V screener]; HEG paid large dividends and did buybacks [K].
6. **2021–23, restock and capex:**
   - Restocking in FY22 (OPM 15%/24%).
   - **HEG expanded to 100 kt** (FY22–23 capex; CWIP peaked at ₹696 Cr in FY22 [V screener]).
   - China built more. Needle coke spiked in 2022 (energy crisis) [K].
7. **2023–25, oversupply + the needle coke lag:**
   - Electrode prices drifted down for three years ("prices have declined over the past three years" — GrafTech [V-snip]).
   - **Graphite India FY24 OP −₹143 Cr** (inventory losses), rescued in reported terms by a ₹1,045 Cr other-income gain in Q2 FY24 [V screener].
   - ICRA: H1 FY26 realisations −12% YoY [V].
   - Western closures: Tokai, Resonac, GrafTech (St Marys idled, Monterrey) [K/V].
8. **2026, the turn?**
   - GrafTech hikes (Mar-2026 $600–1,200/t; Sep-2026 ≥30%).
   - US AD/CVD on China and India (prelims Jul–Sep 2026).
   - EU steel import regime (Jul-2026).
   - The West Asia war disrupts Chinese supply to the Gulf.
   - Indian makers at 90–100% utilisation.
   - **Structural events at the companies:** **HEG demerges electrodes (1-Sep-2026)**; Graphite India shuts its German specialities for six months and backs Godi; Graphite India and HEG both commit capital to battery anodes.
9. **Refractories timeline:**
   - RHI + Magnesita merge (2017) → Orient Refractories becomes the Indian listed arm.
   - Successive mergers 2019–22.
   - **Dalmia OCL refractory acquisition (₹1,708 Cr share swap, deal announced Nov-2022 [V-snip business-standard/ventureintelligence])**.
   - Goodwill/intangible write-downs in FY23, FY24 and FY26.
   - Record cash generation FY26.
   - **Dalmia exit (Sep-2026)**.

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Falling specific consumption:** better electrodes, furnace automation (AI-regulated arcs), DC-EAFs and foamy slag cut kg/t ~1–2%/yr [primer; K]. Volume growth must outrun it.
2. **H₂-DRI + EAF is good for electrodes, but HBI/DRI-heavy charges change consumption.** Open-slag-bath furnaces (OSBF) and **submerged-arc "electric smelting furnaces"** (Thyssenkrupp/BlueScope/Rio Tinto pilots for low-grade ore) use **Söderberg or prebaked carbon electrodes**, not UHP graphite [K]. This is a long-run substitution risk if ESFs scale (2030s).
3. **Induction furnaces in India:** they use no electrodes. Policy pressure to move IF→EAF (quality, emissions) would add electrode demand [K/E].
4. **Battery anodes compete for needle coke.** The synthetic-graphite tilt in China's anode market [V-snip fastmarkets] means the battery boom bids for the same premium coke. HEG's TACC and Graphite India's SGAM are hedges and also competitors for their own feedstock [primer].
5. **Indian needle coke (IOCL Paradip 56 ktpa)**, if built, would cut import dependence and freight. Status unknown [V-snip 2020].
6. **Refractories:**
   - Robotics (RHIM "operating 2 robots at largest integrated steel plant of India" [V presentation]); digital lining monitoring; **recycling of spent refractories** (MINPRO JV) cutting raw-material cost and CO₂.
   - Longer campaign lives reduce kg/t. Shift from bricks to monolithics.
   - Low-carbon steel routes change the lining mix (less BF hearth/torpedo refractory, more EAF/DRI) [K].
7. **CBAM-type carbon costs on the electrode makers themselves:** graphitisation power is coal-heavy in India. ICRA flags environmental risk; Graphite India cut water use 50% at Durgapur [V ICRA]. EU buyers may demand low-carbon electrodes [E].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **The electrode spread, set by Chinese supply and Western discipline.**
   - Indian electrode earnings = tonnes (capped near 180 kt between the two) × (realisation − needle coke − conversion).
   - Realisation fell for three years (2023–25) on Chinese oversupply. It is turning in 2026 because the high-cost West is shutting capacity (GrafTech Monterrey, Tokai/Resonac closures) and raising prices (+$600–1,200/t Mar-26; ≥30% Sep-26).
   - ICRA's $1,600/t spread floor marks "trouble" [V].
2. **EAF decarbonisation outside China, minus falling kg/t.**
   - ~60 Mt of new EAF capacity in 2026–28 [V Arthneeti]; the US ~70% EAF; EU CBAM + the 2026 import regime; Middle East DRI-EAF.
   - Electrode demand grows only ~1–3%/yr net of kg/t gains [E]. The tailwind is real but slow.
   - **India's own growth is BF- and IF-led, so the domestic electrode tailwind is weaker than primers imply.**
3. **Trade policy now cuts both ways.**
   - US AD/CVD now names **India** (CVD 3.68–6.99%; GI AD 12.22% prelim), not just China (103%+).
   - The EU keeps Chinese electrodes out (2022 extension).
   - India has no ADD on Chinese electrodes [gap].
   - Trade remedies split the world into a protected West (high price) and a Chinese-priced rest. India is in between.
4. **Treasury books and capital allocation: from cash-hoarding to battery anodes.**
   - Graphite India's ₹3,900 Cr cash and investments (≈29% of m-cap) and HEG's ₹858 Cr treasury generate 60–100% of trough-year PBT.
   - Both are now committing that cash to synthetic graphite anodes (TACC ₹2,200–3,100 Cr; Graphite India SGAM ₹4,330 Cr phased).
   - HEG went further and **legally separated the anode/power business from electrodes** (Sep-2026).
5. **Refractories: India steel tonnes × consolidation × China raw materials.**
   - RHIM rides BF-led Indian steel growth (refractory-heavy) with 30%+ share and a services model.
   - Its margin is capped by Chinese magnesia/alumina pricing and by new competitors. Shipments −5% in Q1 FY27 even as steel grew 3%.
   - Its accounts are distorted by ~₹1,500 Cr of cumulative write-downs on acquisitions (FY23 + FY24 + FY26) [E from V].

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Electrodes:** Revenue ≈ Capacity (t) × Utilisation × Realisation (₹/t); EBITDA ≈ Volume × [Realisation − CNC cost × t-CNC/t − Power − Other conversion]; reported PAT ≈ (EBITDA − D&A − interest + **treasury income**) × (1 − tax).

Worked example: HEG-like plant, FY27E [E, illustrative; not guidance]
- Capacity 100,000 t × utilisation 92% = **92,000 t**.
- Realisation $3,600/t × ₹94.9 = ₹3.42 lakh/t → revenue ≈ **₹3,140 Cr** (+ ~₹100 Cr other products).
- Needle coke 0.95 t × $1,500 = $1,425/t; pitch & materials $200; power 5 MWh × ₹6.0 = ₹30,000 (~$316); other conversion & freight ~$600 → cash cost ≈ **$2,540/t ≈ ₹2.41 lakh/t**.
- EBITDA/t ≈ ₹1.01 lakh ($1,060) → EBITDA ≈ **₹925 Cr (~29%)**. This is consistent with HEG's "maintain 28–29% EBITDA margin" commentary [V Arthneeti].
- Less D&A ~₹210 Cr; plus treasury ~₹60 Cr [E]; tax 25% → PAT ≈ **₹580 Cr**.
- **Sensitivity:**
  - **±$100/t realisation = ±₹87 Cr EBITDA (±9%)** [E: 92,000 × $100 × ₹94.9].
  - ±$100/t needle coke = ∓₹83 Cr.
  - ±5 pp utilisation = ±₹50 Cr.

**Refractories:** Revenue ≈ Customer steel tonnes × kg/t × ₹/kg × market share (+ cement/industrial). EBITDA ≈ Revenue × (1 − material % − employee % − other %).

Worked example: RHIM FY27E [E]
- Shipments 500–520 kt (Q1 run-rate 122 kt × 4 = 488 kt, plus seasonal H2) × ₹80,000/t ≈ **₹4,000–4,160 Cr**.
- Material 59% → gross margin 41%; employee 10%; other 18% → EBITDA ~13% ≈ **₹520–540 Cr** (guidance ~13% [V-snip]).
- D&A ~₹200 Cr (incl. ~₹84 Cr amortisation); interest ~₹38 Cr; tax 26% → PAT ≈ **₹210–225 Cr** (no impairment).
- **Sensitivity: ±1 pp material cost = ±₹40 Cr EBITDA; ±₹2,000/t price = ±₹100 Cr revenue.**
