**Units, conventions and FX**
- ₹ crore (Cr) = 10 million. ₹ lakh crore = ₹1 trillion. ₹ bn = ₹100 Cr.
- **MSM = million square metres** (tile industry's unit; also "mn sq m", "msm"). 1 MSM ≈ 10.76 mn sq ft.
- Gas: **₹/SCM** (standard cubic metre) is how Indian tile makers quote gas; LNG/benchmarks are in **$/mmbtu**. Gujarat Energy's own Morbi formula equates **$28.294/mmbtu = ₹90/SCM at ₹96/$**, i.e. ~**30 SCM per mmbtu** [V deshgujarat 3-Sep-2026]. Rule of thumb used in this file: **₹1/SCM ≈ $0.35/mmbtu at ₹96/$** [E]. Propane is quoted by the industry in ₹/SCM-equivalent (heat-equivalent) or $/t (Saudi CP).
- FY26 = Apr-2025 to Mar-2026. Q1 FY27 = Apr–Jun 2026. **Q2 FY27 = Jul–Sep 2026.** All Indian tile companies here have a March year-end. RAK Ceramics (UAE) and Mohawk (US) report on calendar years.
- **FX: ₹96 = US$1** (the rate in Gujarat Energy's Sep-2026 Morbi gas formula [V]; a sibling dossier used ₹94.9 on 19-Oct-2026 [V-sib]). Earlier-period USD figures are converted at that period's rate only where stated. **AED 1 ≈ ₹26.1** [E, at the AED 3.6725 peg].

## READ THIS FIRST: eleven definitional traps

1. **Three different "volume" numbers at Kajaria — always say which.**
   - **Sales volume** (what investors quote): FY26 **118.52 MSM** = own plants **62.85** + subsidiaries/JVs (consolidated, majority-owned Morbi/Telangana units) **26.26** + **outsourced** from independent Morbi units **29.41** [V Q4 update].
   - **Production volume** (own + subsidiaries only): FY26 **84.59 MSM** (FY25 87.90) [V].
   - **Capacity**: **82.70 MSM excluding the 50% Nepal JV**; **87.80 MSM including Nepal's 5.10 MSM** [V Q2 / Q4 updates]. The Nepal JV (Kajaria Ramesh Tiles) is **equity-accounted**, not consolidated — its output is in neither sales nor production volume; its profit appears as "share of JV" (₹2.61 Cr in Q2 FY27) [V].
   - Management's own shorthand on the Q2 call: "overall capacity is about 88 million square meter" (= incl. Nepal) and "this year we'll be selling about 130 million…the rest 42 million…outsourced" [V]. That "42 MSM outsourced" bundles **subsidiary + outsourced** volume against an 88 MSM capacity that includes Nepal — it is **not** comparable to the 29.41 MSM "outsource" line in the investor update. **Do not mix these.**
2. **Kajaria revenue: company "Total Sales" ≠ screener "Sales".** The company's investor updates **include plywood** (discontinued) for comparability: FY26 ₹4,832.50 Cr; FY25 ₹4,683.24 Cr [V]. Screener shows FY26 ₹4,830 Cr and **FY25 ₹4,635 Cr** (restated ex-plywood) [V screener]. Company FY26 EBITDA ₹861.95 Cr (17.84%) vs screener OP ₹869 Cr (18%). PAT: company ₹485.41 Cr (after ₹44.02 Cr exceptional) vs screener ₹487 Cr. Use screener in peer tables, company figures in prose, and say so.
3. **Realisation (₹/sq m) is blended across own, subsidiary and outsourced tiles** and is my arithmetic (tile revenue ÷ sales volume) from the company's revenue-by-source and volume-by-source charts [E from V]. FY26 **₹361/sq m**; Q2 FY27 **₹406/sq m** (+11.4% YoY). In Q2 FY27 the **outsourced realisation (₹423) exceeded own-plant realisation (₹408)** for the first time in years — because Morbi-made tiles rose ~35–40% in price after the gas shock while Kajaria's North/South plants raised only 12–13% (management: "mainly because of inflation…not seeing any incremental margin") [V Q2 call]. The primer's "~₹364/m²" is FY26; it is now stale by ~11%.
4. **Industry size: four incompatible numbers circulate.**
   - **₹60,000 Cr** for FY27 = **domestic ~₹48,000 Cr + exports ~₹12,000 Cr**, inflated by price hikes (Kajaria chairman, Q2 FY27 call) [V].
   - **₹53,100 Cr (FY25E)**, ₹51,700 Cr (FY24), ₹36,000 Cr (FY19) → **₹76,900 Cr FY29P** (9.7% CAGR) — the market sizing in **Varmora Granito's DRHP**, reproduced by PL Capital (5-Mar-2026) [V PL pdf]. This appears to be a **domestic** market value.
   - **~₹75,000 Cr ("Rs 750 bn")** — "India's ceramics industry" in a BBC-sourced Business Today piece (19-Mar-2026), which **includes sanitaryware** and is Morbi-centric [V].
   - **US$10–11 bn** — our primer's "domestic market" [primer]; at ₹96/$ that is ₹96,000–1,06,000 Cr, **too high** against every other source. Treat as unsupported.
   - **Use the Varmora-DRHP series for history and the Kajaria ₹48,000 Cr for FY27 domestic**, and state the basis.
5. **Export value: sources disagree by up to ~20% for the same year.**
   - PL Capital chart (commerce data): FY20 ₹10,000 Cr → FY24 **₹20,200 Cr (peak)** → FY25 **₹18,000 Cr (−11%)** → Apr–Dec FY26 ₹14,300 Cr (PL's text says ₹12,000 Cr for 9M FY26 — internal conflict) [V PL pdf].
   - Kajaria management: FY26 **₹16,000 Cr**; FY27 run-rate **₹1,000 Cr/month → ~₹12,000 Cr** [V Q1 & Q2 calls]. A Kajaria Jul-2025 analyst deck (snippet) said exports "declined by 20% in FY 2025 to Rs. 16000 crore" [V-snip] — which conflicts with PL's ₹18,000 Cr for FY25.
   - Morbi wall-tile association: "₹18,000 Cr last year → ~₹12,000 Cr this year" [V-snip indiaseatradenews]; Gujarat government (Vibrant Gujarat): **Morbi** exports ~₹15,000 Cr in 2024-25 (80–90% of India's) [V ThePrint].
   - CAPEXIL (export promotion council) headline: **US$2,115 mn in 2025-26, a "record"** [V-snip] — ≈ ₹18,000–18,500 Cr at FY26 average FX (~₹86–88/$) [E]. That contradicts Kajaria's ₹16,000 Cr and the "second consecutive year of fall" narrative.
   - **Recommendation:** treat FY24 ≈ ₹20,000 Cr as the peak, FY25 ≈ ₹16,000–18,000 Cr, FY26 ≈ ₹16,000–18,000 Cr (flat-ish; the HS 6907 series was **not fetched** — FLAG), FY27E ≈ ₹12,000 Cr (−25–30%). Quote ranges and name the source.
6. **"Morbi units" — the count depends on who is counting and what a "unit" is.** 400–500 "plants" (Kajaria chairman, Q2 call) [V]; ~600 (Morbi Ceramic Association membership) [V ThePrint]; 670 (BusinessLine via Pulse) [V-snip]; ~800 (deshgujarat, Sep-2026) [V]; **~900 = 370 gas-only + ~530 dual-fuel** (PL, Mar-2026) [V]; ~1,200 (Gujarat government figure in ThePrint, likely including sanitaryware, ancillary and frit/glaze units) [V]; "1,800-plus kilns" (our primer — kilns, not units). **Use "roughly 800–900 tile units" and cite PL's split**; never compare a kiln count with a unit count.
7. **Gas prices: ₹/SCM vs $/mmbtu, landed vs ex-VAT, contracted vs spot.** GEL's Morbi tile price was **₹90/SCM excluding 6% VAT** from 2-Sep-2026 (₹82 for long-term users and sanitaryware) [V]. Kajaria's North (GAIL via Delhi CGD) and South (CGDs) prices are blended contract prices (Q2 FY27: N ₹70.56, S ₹63, W/Morbi ₹75; average ~₹70) [V]. When Kajaria's MD said "spot prices…$28 to $30 per SCM" he meant **$/mmbtu** [V transcript wording; E interpretation].
8. **The "price gap" statistic.** Kajaria's chairman: before 28-Feb-2026 a Kajaria tile from its North/South plants sold at ~**40%** more than a comparable Morbi-made tile sold by a Morbi dealer; after Morbi's ~35–40% price increases vs Kajaria's 12–13%, the gap is **20–25%** [V Q1 & Q2 calls]. This is **management's estimate**, not measured data. Somany says the same thing qualitatively (Morbi price hikes "double those taken by SOMC") [V Arihant].
9. **GST on tiles is 18% and did NOT change under GST 2.0 (22-Sep-2025).** Ceramic, vitrified and porcelain tiles (HSN 6907) are 18% before and after [V-snip housing.com/bajajfinserv/busy.in, consistent]. A blog claim that tiles moved "from 28% to 18%" in Sep-2025 is wrong: tiles were cut from **28% to 18% on 15-Nov-2017** (GST Council, 10-Nov-2017) [K]. What GST 2.0 did cut was **cement 28%→18%** and **raw marble/granite blocks 12%→5%** [V-snip] — the latter marginally helps natural stone vs tiles. **Sanitaryware (HSN 6910) remains 18%** [K].
10. **Anti-dumping duty: India's ADD on Chinese vitrified tiles is alive but expires around Feb-2027.** Notification **09/2022-Customs (ADD), 24-Feb-2022**, continued the duty on glazed/unglazed porcelain/vitrified tiles (<3% water absorption) from China for **five years** after a sunset review (case 7/39/2020-DGTR, petitioners: Gujarat Granito Manufacturers Assn, ICCTAS, Morbi Ceramics Assn, Sabarkantha District Ceramics Assn); first imposed by Notification 29/2017 (14-Jun-2017) [V studycafe; V-snip taxguru/DGTR]. **Rates are in a table I could not read**; a snippet gives "₹155 per sq m" and another "$1.87 per sq m" — **unverified, FLAG**. **No 2026 sunset-review initiation was found** [V-snip, negative search]. Separately, India's tiles **face** duties abroad: **US CVD ~3.06–3.45%** (order 16-Jun-2025; AD finding negative), **GCC ADD renewed for five more years from 28-May-2026** (China and India) [V antidumping.vn], Oman national duties from 29-May-2025 [V-snip]. Our primer's "fresh Mexican probe from 2025" is unverified.
11. **ROCE: company vs screener.** Kajaria reports **ROCE 29.87% and ROE 23.68% for Q2 FY27** (quarterly, annualised; capital employed **excluding cash & bank**; average of preceding FY-end and quarter-end) [V Q2 update definitions]. Screener's ROCE **23.4%** (FY26) includes cash in capital employed. Use screener in peer tables; quote the company figure only with its definition.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **India is the world's #2 tile producer and the #1 or #2 exporter by volume, but 2026 has turned it from an export story into a domestic-share story.**
  - World tile output 2024: **~14,950 MSM** (−6.2%); 2023 15,937 MSM; Asia ~10,900 MSM (72.8%); Europe 1,615 MSM (10.9%); China fell ~820 MSM in 2024 [V-snip Ceramic World Review/MECS; page blocked by Cloudflare]. MECS forecasts **India's production growing ~5.9% a year to 2028 (+800 MSM)** — the fastest of any major producer — while China shrinks [V ctasc mirror of CWR "forecasts to 2028"].
  - India's production is **~2,500–2,600 MSM** (≈ 16–17% of world) [E: ThePrint's "2.55 billion square metres" is labelled exports but can only be production; MECS growth arithmetic implies a 2023 base ~2,600 MSM]. Domestic consumption ~**1,800–2,000 MSM** [E: production − exports of ~550–650 MSM]; IndexBox puts Indian consumption at 844 MSM, which is implausibly low [V-snip, low reliability].
  - **Morbi makes 70–75% of India's tiles** (PL, Mar-2026) [V] — other sources say "~80% of India's ceramics" (BT) [V] or "~90%" (our primer). Use **70–80%**.
- **The 2026 West Asia war hit the industry through gas, not demand.**
  - Morbi PNG: ~₹48/SCM pre-crisis → **₹86–88 (Mar/Apr)** → ₹79 (Aug) → **₹90/SCM ex-VAT from 2-Sep-2026** (+88%) [V Kajaria Q1 call; V deshgujarat]. Propane (40–65% of Morbi's fuel) was ~₹41/SCM-eq in Mar-2026 and is now rising toward PNG parity (gap narrowed from ~15% to ~5% by 26-Oct) [V PL; V Q2 call].
  - **Morbi shut from ~5-Mar to ~15-Apr-2026**; 10% of plants restarted 16-Apr [V Kajaria Q1 call]. Peak shutdown estimates: ~550 factories / ~80% of manufacturers (BT, 19-Mar) [V]; ~62% of units (PL, 5-Mar) [V]; 430 units for 20–25 days, output −64% (BusinessLine via Pulse) [V-snip].
  - **As of late Oct-2026, ~40–50% of Morbi's plants are still not working** (Kajaria chairman) [V Q2 call]; Somany thinks **~15–17% of Morbi capacity is unlikely to restart** [V Arihant Q1 note].
  - ICRA (Jun-2026): power & fuel ≈ **25% of production cost**; gas prices ~**1.7×** pre-conflict; India gets **50–60% of its LNG via Hormuz**; large players (~40% share) better placed than small (~60%) [V ICRA].
- **Exports are collapsing for the third time in four years.** Peak ~₹20,000 Cr (FY24) → ₹16,000–18,000 Cr (FY25/FY26) → **~₹12,000 Cr FY27E** (freight 1.5–2×, container shortage, Gulf disruption, US CVD + 10% Sec 301, GCC ADD renewed, Chinese JV plants in Saudi/Jordan/Kenya) [V calls; V-snip]. ICRA's Jun-2026 base case was only −5–10% [V ICRA] — management's run-rate implies −25%.
- **The branded players are the winners of the shock.** With Morbi's costs up ~35–40% and Kajaria's North/South plants on contracted GAIL/CGD gas up only ~10–25%, the **brand-vs-Morbi price gap fell from ~40% to ~20–25%** [V]. Kajaria Q2 FY27: **volume +10%** vs an industry Kajaria estimates at **+5–6%**, revenue +24%, EBITDA margin 19.5%, PAT +43% [V]. Somany Q1 FY27: revenue +23%, EBITDA +68%, margin 10.9%, but volume only +3% [V Arihant].
  - Organised listed tile makers' Q1 FY27 (Kajaria, Somany, AGL, Orient Bell; screener): revenue **+25.7%**, operating profit **+49.2%**, margin 14.1% vs 11.8% [E sums of V screener; §3.2].
- **Kajaria is the industry's price and profit leader by a distance.** FY26 screener: revenue ₹4,830 Cr (**47.5%** of the four listed tile pure-plays' ₹10,169 Cr), OP ₹869 Cr (**68.9%** of their OP), PAT ₹487 Cr (**82.3%**), m-cap ₹17,430 Cr (**81.0%** of their ₹21,528 Cr) [E sums of V screener]. Its FY26 margin 18% compares with Somany 9.2%, AGL 5.1%, Orient Bell 5.8%, H&R Johnson ~7.3% [V screener; primer].

### 1.2 Value chain (inputs → customer)

1. **Raw materials (body):** ball clay, china clay (kaolin), feldspar, quartz/silica sand, talc; from Rajasthan (Bikaner, Bhilwara), Gujarat (Kutch, Sabarkantha), Kerala/AP for some clays. Typically **~1 t of body mix per 40–50 sq m of 8–10 mm tile** [E: 17–22 kg/sq m]. **Glazes, frits, digital inks** (largely Spanish/Italian/Chinese chemistry; frit made in Morbi), **packaging** (cartons, pallets).
2. **Process:** wet grinding in ball mills → **spray drying** (gas/coal-fired hot air — a major fuel user) → hydraulic pressing (SACMI/System/Chinese presses; 3,000–7,000 t) → drying → glazing + **digital inkjet printing** → **roller-hearth kiln firing at 1,100–1,220 °C** for 40–70 minutes (the other major fuel user) → polishing/squaring (PVT, GVT) → sorting, packing.
   - Fuel: natural gas (PNG) or propane in kilns; **spray dryers can use coal/biomass/coal gas** where permitted. Kajaria: biofuel is ~30% of fuel at its three North plants (coal not allowed there), biofuel also in the South, **coal in the West**; **Nepal JV runs on coal gas** [V Q2 call]. Morbi was ordered off coal gasifiers by the NGT in 2019 [K].
3. **Plant economics:** a modern GVT line of 5–6 MSM used to cost ₹150–160 Cr; Kajaria now spends **₹340 Cr for 12.5 MSM (Srikalahasti, incl. a shed that can house a second line to 25 MSM)** and **₹180 Cr for 11 MSM (Gailpur)** — "earlier 5 million square meters used to cost INR150 crores…now in INR370 crores we'll get almost 22 million" [V Q1 & Q2 calls]. Capex per MSM: ~₹27 Cr (Srikalahasti incl. shed and warehouse) and ~₹16 Cr (Gailpur) vs ~₹28 Cr historically [E]. Somany: 9+ MSM South GVT for ~₹220 Cr (~₹24 Cr/MSM) [V Arihant].
4. **Distribution:** manufacturer → **dealers** (Kajaria ~1,800 dealers, ~450 exclusive; plans +100 incl. 50 exclusive; "we don't have distributors, we have dealers") [V calls] → sub-dealers/retailers ("touchpoints") → contractors/masons/architects → homeowner. **Projects** (builders, institutions, government) sold direct; Kajaria claims "a major breakthrough with two of the very, very big builders of India" (Q1 FY27) [V]. Tiles are heavy (~20 kg/sq m [E]) — **freight is 8–12% of the landed price over 1,000 km** [E], which is why Kajaria's North/South plants enjoy a freight edge in those regions vs Morbi.
5. **Customer:** new residential (~45–55% of demand), **replacement/renovation (~25–35%)**, commercial/institutional and government housing (~15–25%) [E composite; industry commentary; no primary split found — FLAG].

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **The brand-and-distribution layer captures most of the profit pool.** Kajaria FY26 OPM 18% and ROCE 23% while sourcing ~25% of its sales volume from independent Morbi units [V]; Morbi units earn low-single-digit to ~10% EBITDA in normal years and losses in gas spikes [E; ICRA sample commentary].
- Kajaria's realisation by source (FY26, ₹/sq m) [E from V]: **own plants ₹370, subsidiaries ₹344, outsourced ₹359** → outsourced tiles sell for ~97% of own-plant prices; the brand margin on bought-in tiles is the difference between ~₹359 and the Morbi ex-factory price (≈ 25–35% lower than the brand price pre-war, ≈ 20–25% lower now) [E from management's price-gap statements].
- **Machinery and chemistry** (Italian/Chinese presses and kilns, Spanish inks/frits) capture technology rent; **gas suppliers** (GEL, GAIL, CGDs) capture the energy rent in shocks.
- **Adjacencies with higher value per customer:** tile adhesives (Kajaria FY26 ₹136 Cr → FY27E ~₹240 Cr → FY28E ~₹400 Cr, "margin very good" and partly outsourced) [V Q2 call]; bathware (₹414 Cr FY26; margin "used to be 14–15%", now low because new sanitaryware plants run at 42–54% utilisation) [V].

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Residential new construction (largest; lag 12–24 months from launch).** Tiles go in at finishing, so tile demand follows **completions/possession**, not launches. Sibling realty report [V-sib]: ICRA expects area sold in seven cities **+2–5% in FY27** (value +8–11%), launches +4–7%; Knight Frank 8-city Jan–Sep 2026 sales show **sub-₹50 lakh homes −14% YoY (47,660 units) and ₹50 lakh–1 Cr −6% (69,380)**, while ₹1–2 Cr +7% (77,087); RBI house price index +3.6% YoY (Q1 FY27). **Implication:** the premium end (large-format GVT, slabs — Kajaria's target) is healthier than mass housing (ceramic floor, Morbi's domestic core). Management: "project demand overall in India is picking up…a lot of construction is happening" [V Q2 call].
2. **Replacement and renovation (~25–35% of demand [E]).** Steadier; driven by India's ageing post-2000 urban stock and the shift from mosaic/marble/ceramic to vitrified. Kajaria's 10% Q2 volume growth was "across all regions…retail sales also, project sales also" [V].
3. **Rural / tier-2/3 demand.** PMAY-Gramin and PMAY-Urban 2.0 (1 crore urban homes approved 2024) [K]; low-cost ceramic floor tiles from Morbi dominate. Kajaria is "going deeper down to the Tier 2, Tier 3 cities…more exclusive showrooms" [V Q2 call].
4. **Commercial, institutional, hospitality, airports, metro stations, hospitals** — project sales; Kajaria targets a rising project share; Somany expects projects to add **3–4 percentage points** to its mix [V Arihant].
5. **Format and finish upgrade (value driver).** The 600×1200 mm ("four by two") GVT is now "the most prevalent size which is sold in the market" [V Q2 call]; slabs/surfaces growing "but not at a very high pace" [V].
6. **Adhesives pull-through.** Large, low-absorption tiles need polymer adhesives, not cement mortar — Kajaria adhesive revenue **+69% YoY in Q2 FY27 (₹54 Cr)**; FY27E ~₹240 Cr; FY28E ~₹400 Cr [V].
7. **Exports (~22–28% of Indian production by value in FY24–FY26, falling to ~20% in FY27E)** [E: ₹16–18k Cr exports ÷ ₹60–70k Cr total]. When exports fail, Morbi tonnage is diverted home and **domestic prices fall** — the defining mechanism of FY24–FY25 (PL: "inventory accumulation and dumping in the domestic market, thereby intensifying pricing pressure on organized players") [V PL].
8. **Seasonality:** Q1 (Apr–Jun) weakest (heat, labour migration, monsoon onset); Q4 (Jan–Mar) strongest (dealer stocking ahead of April price lists). Q1 FY27 was distorted: Morbi shut, dealers pre-bought in March ahead of 1-Apr price hikes, labour shortage (state elections and LPG shortage sent workers home) [V Q1 call].

**Demand numbers to quote [V unless noted]:** Kajaria volume growth FY21–FY26: FY21 75 MSM (−3.4%), FY22 92 (+21.5%), FY23 102 (+11.0%), FY24 108 (+6.3%), FY25 114.69 (+6.1%), FY26 118.52 (+3.3%) [PL chart; V company for FY25–26]; FY27 guidance ~130 MSM (+~10%), FY28 ~145 MSM [V Q2 call].

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Indirect tax
- **GST 18% on tiles (HSN 6907), unchanged by GST 2.0 (22-Sep-2025)** [V-snip, consistent]; history: 28% at GST launch (Jul-2017) → **18% from 15-Nov-2017** [K]. **Cement cut 28% → 18% in GST 2.0** [V-snip] (helps construction cost marginally). Marble/travertine/granite blocks 12% → 5% [V-snip] — a small relative advantage for natural stone.
- GST and e-invoicing (thresholds lowered to ₹5 Cr turnover in 2023 [K]) are the main formalisation lever cited for organised share gains (under-invoicing by small units narrowed the price gap pre-2017) [primer; K].

### 7.2 Trade remedies — inbound (protecting India)
- **ADD on Chinese glazed/unglazed porcelain/vitrified tiles (<3% water absorption):** first imposed **14-Jun-2017** (Notif. 29/2017-Cus(ADD)); extended to 28-Feb-2022 (54/2021); **continued for five years by 09/2022-Cus(ADD) dated 24-Feb-2022** after sunset review 7/39/2020-DGTR [V studycafe; V-snip]. Exclusions: micro-crystal, full-body, thin panels <5 mm [V]. **Expiry ~23/24-Feb-2027**; no 2026 sunset-review initiation found [V-snip negative] — **FLAG; this is a live 2027 event**. Rates: not read (image) — snippets say "₹155 per sq m for most exporters, nil for named producers" and "$1.87/sq m" (the latter matches the 2017 top rate [K]) — **unverified**.
- **BIS Quality Control Order:** "Ceramic Tiles, Sanitary Wares and Laboratory Wares (Quality Control) Order, 2023" mandating **IS 15622:2017** (pressed ceramic tiles); BIS circular of **2-Apr-2026** on implementing **Amendment No. 2 to IS 15622, last date 28-Jun-2026** [V-snip BIS circular listing; alephindia]. Effective date of the QCO itself not confirmed [FLAG]. A QCO acts as a non-tariff barrier to low-quality imports and a compliance cost for the smallest units.

### 7.3 Trade remedies and tariffs — outbound (hurting Morbi)
- **United States:** CVD order on ceramic tile from India — final CVD rates **Antiqa Minerals 3.45%, Win-Tel/Theos 3.06%, all-others 3.18%** (POI Apr-2023–Mar-2024); final AD determination **negative** (no AD order); ITC affirmative on **threat** of injury (9-Jun-2025); order published **16-Jun-2025** [V-snip trade.gov/Federal Register 2025-06908, 2025-11052]. Earlier ICRA (Oct-2024) had feared AD margins of 328–489% (petition rates) — they did not materialise [V-snip]. On top: the 2025–26 US tariff saga — IEEPA tariffs (incl. the 50% on India in Aug-2025 [K]) **struck down 20-Feb-2026**; Sec 122 10% to 24-Jul-2026; **10% Sec 301 since** [V-sib]. US was ~9% of India's tile exports (FY24) [V-snip ICRA].
- **GCC:** ADD on ceramic and porcelain tiles from **China and India** — original GCC duties (complaint by Saudi Ceramic Co and Alfanar, 2018 investigation; Indian rates reported 40–106%, most Morbi exporters ~42.9% vs China ~24%) [V-snip]; **extended for five more years effective 28-May-2026** [V antidumping.vn 18-May-2026]. The UAE carve-out under CEPA is reported but unclear [V-snip]. **Oman** national duties from 29-May-2025 [V-snip Oman Observer].
- **Taiwan** considered ADD on Morbi ceramics (date unclear) [V-snip headline]; **Mexico** probe (primer, unverified).
- **FTAs (tailwinds):** UAE CEPA (2022) → UAE share of India's tile exports 5.2% → 7.6% [V PL]; **UK CETA signed Jul-2025; Oman CEPA Dec-2025; EFTA TEPA in force Oct-2025; India–EU FTA signed Jan-2026; NZ finalised Dec-2025** [V PL table].

### 7.4 Energy and environment
- **NGT (2019) ordered Morbi units off coal gasifiers** → forced migration to PNG/propane [K] — the origin of Morbi's gas dependence.
- **March-2026 emergency gas allocation order** capped industrial/commercial CGD customers at **80% of their six-month average** use [V-sib gas-distribution]; GEL invoked **force majeure**, cut Morbi supply ~50% and terminated certain MGO-linked agreements [V PL].

- **Gujarat state support:** ₹115 Cr assistance to 2,200 Morbi beneficiaries over two years (data dated 6-Jan-2026); association met CM on 9-Mar-2026 [V ThePrint].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; timeline in §G)

1. **1958–1990s: the Johnson era.** H&R Johnson (India) pioneered ceramic tiles in 1958 [primer Prism]; Kajaria's first plant at Sikandrabad (UP) in **1988** with Spanish collaboration [primer]; Somany (1968) [K]; Nitco, Orient Bell (Orient Ceramics / Bell Ceramics merger) [K].
2. **2000s: vitrified arrives and Morbi industrialises.** Polished vitrified tiles (PVT) displace marble/mosaic in urban floors; Morbi grows from wall-tile workshops to a cluster of hundreds of units supplied by Italian/Chinese machinery and cheap coal gas [K].
3. **2010–2016: digital printing and GVT.** Inkjet glazing makes marble/wood looks cheap; **GVT** becomes the growth product; Kajaria adds Morbi JVs (Kajaria Vitrified/Jaxx, Kajaria Infinity/Cosa) and South plants; enters **bathware (2014)** [primer]. Chinese imports rise → **ADD on Chinese vitrified tiles (2016–17)** [V-snip].
4. **2017–2019: GST and NGT.** GST at 28% then **18% (Nov-2017)**; **NGT bans coal gasifiers in Morbi (2019)**, shifting Morbi to PNG/propane and raising its costs [K]. GCC ADD on Indian tiles (2019–20) [V-snip]. Demonetisation/GST hit small units' cash economy.
5. **FY21–FY24: the export boom.** Post-COVID global demand, Chinese anti-dumping abroad (US, EU, GCC on China) and Indian cost advantage push exports from **₹10,000 Cr (FY20) to ₹20,200 Cr (FY24)** [V PL]; Kajaria volume 75 → 108 MSM (FY21–FY24) [V PL].
6. **FY23: the first gas shock.** Russia–Ukraine → spot LNG >$40/mmbtu (Aug-2022) [K]; Morbi PNG ~₹65–70/SCM [K]; Kajaria manufacturing cost 31.8% of sales and **PAT −10% to ₹346 Cr** (FY23) despite revenue +18% [V screener]. Morbi shut several hundred units for a month (Aug-2022) [K].
7. **FY24–FY25: the freight shock and domestic price war.** Red Sea crisis → **Drewry WCI $3,493 (Feb-24) → $5,806 (mid-2024)** per 40 ft [V PL chart]; US CVD case; exports −11% (FY25) and Morbi "dumping in the domestic market" [V PL]; Kajaria margin 13.5% (FY25; −190 bp), Somany 8.3% [V PL]; Kajaria PAT −31% to ₹300 Cr [V screener].
8. **FY26: Kajaria's internal reset.** **Apr-2025 "unification"** of ceramic/PVT/GVT verticals into one tiles organisation ("a lot of people went") [V Q1 FY27 call]; flat 9M volumes, then **+11% in Q4**; plywood exited; promoters forgo salary; leadership re-designation (Oct-2025); buyback ₹297 Cr; FY26 PAT **+65% to ₹485 Cr**, margin 17.8% as Morbi gas fell to ~₹37–45 [V].
9. **FY27: the second gas shock — and the branded windfall.** War from **28-Feb-2026**; Morbi shut ~5-Mar to 15-Apr; PNG ₹48 → ₹90; Morbi prices +35–45%; brand-vs-Morbi gap 40% → 20–25%; Kajaria Q2 volume +10%, margin 19.5%; GEL replaces Gujarat Gas; GCC renews ADD (May-2026); exports heading to ~₹12,000 Cr [V].

**Cycle pattern [E]:** Kajaria's margin troughs coincide with Morbi gas spikes **only when exports are healthy** (FY23: Morbi could pass cost to export buyers) or with **export collapses** (FY25: Morbi dumped domestically). In 2026 the gas spike coincided with an export collapse **and** Morbi shutdowns — so domestic supply fell and branded players gained. The writer should frame the cycle as **gas × exports**, not gas alone.

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Large-format slabs and thin panels** (continuous presses; 1,600×3,200 mm, 6–20 mm) compete with granite/quartz countertops and natural marble; Morbi has invested heavily; Kajaria has two capable lines and makes slabs "as and when we need to" [V Q2 call]. Risk: slab overcapacity in Morbi → price war in the premium end.
2. **Energy transition in kilns:** hydrogen-blend and electric kilns are being trialled in Spain/Italy (EU ETS pressure) [K]; Indian makers are shifting spray dryers to **biomass** (Kajaria ~30% biofuel at North plants) [V]; shorter firing cycles are Kajaria's near-term fix [V Q2 call]. Carbon border taxes (EU CBAM does **not** currently cover ceramics [K]) could matter for exports later.
3. **Substitutes for floors:** SPC/LVT vinyl planks and laminates (fast in commercial interiors abroad; still niche in India) [K]; engineered wood; natural stone (now with 5% GST on blocks) [V-snip]; polished concrete/microtopping. Low threat to Indian residential floors near term [E].
4. **Digital printing commoditises design:** any Morbi unit can copy a Kajaria design within weeks — the moat moves from design to **distribution, consistency and trust** [primer; E].
5. **Distribution disruption:** online/D2C tile sales remain tiny (heavy, breakable, must be seen); the real channel shift is to **exclusive branded showrooms** and **project sales to large developers** [V calls; E].
6. **Adhesive/grout systems** become part of the tile sale (Kajaria, Somany, Pidilite's Roff, Ardex Endura, MYK Laticrete) — an adjacency that rewards brands [V; K].
7. **Gas-supply disruption as a structural risk:** 50–60% of India's LNG via Hormuz [V ICRA]; Morbi has a single gas supplier (GEL) [V Kajaria Q1]; propane supply chains run through Kandla from the Gulf [V ThePrint]. The 2026 shock may push organised capacity out of Gujarat permanently (Kajaria and Somany both building in the South) [V].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert leads with)

1. **Morbi gas economics (the cost curve is set in Gujarat, by crude).** Morbi makes 70–75% of India's tiles on GEL PNG (MGO-linked; ₹48 → ₹90/SCM in six months) or Gulf propane (Kandla). At ~1.6–2.0 SCM/sq m, every ₹10/SCM adds ₹16–20/sq m (6–8% of a Morbi ex-factory price). Branded players with plants on GAIL/CGD gas in the North and South (Kajaria's 80%+ of capacity outside the West) sit lower on the cost curve when Morbi gas spikes [V; E].
2. **The export valve (Morbi's surplus goes abroad or comes home).** ~20–28% of Indian output by value is exported. When freight, ADD (GCC, US CVD), tariffs (US Sec 301) or war shut the valve, Morbi tonnage floods the domestic market (FY25: Kajaria margin 13.5%). 2026 is unusual: the export valve **and** Morbi's gas supply failed together, so domestic supply shrank [V PL; V calls].
3. **The price gap = the share-gain engine.** Organised share rises when the gap between a branded tile and a Morbi tile narrows. It went from ~40% to ~20–25% in 2026, and Kajaria grew volume 10% vs market 5–6%. Kajaria is deliberately **not** taking further price increases ("we need to go more for volumes"; "not greedy for margins…focus on absolute EBITDA") to lock in the gap [V Q2 call].
4. **Asset-light flexibility vs insourcing.** ~25% of Kajaria's volume is outsourced; ICRA says 20–30% for organised players. In 2026 outsourced tiles cost more (Morbi inflation) and supply was uncertain, so Kajaria is building +23.5 MSM of its own GVT capacity outside Gujarat and expects outsourcing to fall from ~42 to ~36–37 MSM (its broad definition) by FY28 [V].
5. **Demand: completions, renovation and mix, not launches.** Volume grows ~5–6% a year; value grows faster through GVT/large formats (GVT from 29% to ~45% of market value FY19→FY29P). Mass housing is weak in 2026; premium housing and projects are stronger [V PL/DRHP; V-sib].
6. **Distribution reach and brand trust.** ~1,800 dealers (450 exclusive) plus project sales to large builders; a homeowner pays ~20–25% more for a Kajaria tile (management's estimate of the current gap) [V].
7. **Trade protection at home is thin and expiring.** India's ADD on Chinese vitrified tiles lapses ~Feb-2027 unless reviewed; BIS QCO (IS 15622) is the other barrier [V/V-snip].
8. **Balance-sheet strength as a weapon.** Kajaria's ₹635 Cr net cash (Sep-26) funds capex (~₹400 Cr in FY27), a buyback (₹297 Cr) and treasury loans to NBFCs (₹155 Cr) [V]; Morbi units are working-capital constrained and shut first in a shock.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Tile maker revenue = sales volume (MSM) × realisation (₹/sq m)**, where
- sales volume = own production (capacity × utilisation) + subsidiary production + outsourced purchases;
- realisation = f(mix: ceramic/PVT/GVT/slab; format; region; brand-vs-Morbi gap).

**EBITDA = volume × (realisation − variable cost/sq m) − fixed costs**, where variable cost/sq m = body + glaze + packaging + **fuel (SCM/sq m × ₹/SCM)** + power + freight + (for outsourced tiles) the purchase price.

### C.1 Worked example 1 — Kajaria Q2 FY27 (company data) [V inputs; E arithmetic]
- Volume **31.81 MSM** × realisation **₹405.8** = tile revenue **₹1,290.75 Cr**; + bathware ₹123.59 Cr + adhesives ₹53.92 Cr = **₹1,468.26 Cr**.
- EBITDA ₹286.50 Cr (19.51%) → **EBITDA per sq m of tile ≈ ₹80–85** if tiles carry ~92–95% of EBITDA [E].
- Below EBITDA: depreciation ₹43.10, finance ₹5.28, other income +₹15.76 → PBT (pre-JV) ₹253.88; JV +₹2.61; exceptional −₹1.55; tax ₹64.22; minority ₹1.20 → **PAT ₹189.52 Cr** [V].

### C.2 Worked example 2 — one square metre of 600×1200 GVT, Kajaria North plant vs a Morbi unit (Sep-2026) [E composite; FLAG]

| ₹ per sq m | Kajaria (Gailpur, GAIL gas ₹70.56) | Morbi unit (GEL ₹90 + 6% VAT) |
|---|---|---|
| Realisation (ex-factory, net of GST) | 410 (brand) | 310 (unbranded, post ~35–40% hike) |
| Fuel (1.3 vs 1.8 SCM/sq m) | 92 | 172 |
| Body + glaze + ink + packaging | 80 | 70 |
| Power, labour, stores | 45 | 35 |
| Freight to a Delhi dealer | 15 | 45 (Morbi→Delhi ~1,100 km) |
| A&P, selling, overheads | 50 | 10 |
| **EBITDA** | **~128 (31% at plant level; group 19.5% after bathware, overheads, lower-margin outsourced/sub tiles)** | **~−22 to +10 (loss-making unless at full utilisation)** |

### C.3 Worked example 3 — the price gap arithmetic (pre-war vs now) [E from V management statements]
- Pre-war: Morbi tile at ₹100 (index) → Kajaria ₹140 (gap 40%).
- Morbi +37.5% (midpoint of 35–40%) → ₹137.5; Kajaria +12.5% → ₹157.5 → **gap 14.5%** by this arithmetic — **narrower than the 20–25% management quotes**, which implies Kajaria's North/South increases or the Morbi increases were partly reversed by Q2 (Morbi offered "discounts and price reductions" in Sep when propane was 20–25% cheaper — analyst on the Q2 call) [V]. Use management's 20–25%.

### C.4 Worked example 4 — Kajaria FY28E bridge (management guidance) [E]
- Volume ~145 MSM (FY27E ~130) × ~₹400/sq m (flat; "value growth will be almost equal to volume growth") ≈ ₹5,800 Cr tiles + bathware ~₹550 Cr + adhesives ~₹400 Cr ≈ **₹6,700–6,800 Cr revenue**; at ~19% ≈ **₹1,280 Cr EBITDA**. (A Kajaria sales-meet slide reportedly showed "₹6,000 Cr" — an analyst reference on the Q1 call, not company guidance [V].)

### C.5 Company-level reconciliation [V]
- FY26 tiles ₹4,280.29 Cr = own ₹2,322.89 + subs ₹902.20 + outsourced ₹1,055.20; + bathware ₹413.64 + adhesives ₹136.43 + plywood ₹2.14 = **₹4,832.50 Cr** (company) vs screener ₹4,830 Cr.
