**Units, conventions and FX**
- ₹ crore (Cr) = 10 million; ₹ lakh crore = ₹1 trillion.
- **TMT** = thousand metric tonnes; **kl** = kilolitre = 1,000 litres; **mn L** = million litres.
- Lubricant density ≈ **0.87–0.90 kg/L** [K]. Rule of thumb: **1 TMT ≈ 1.13–1.15 mn L**; 1 mn L ≈ 0.88 TMT [E].
- PSU oil companies report lubricant sales in **TMT**. Castrol does not publish litres (only proxies). Kline and Gulf quote **million tonnes**.
- **Castrol India reports on a calendar year (Jan–Dec).** In its own filings it calls CY2026 "FY 2026" (e.g. "3Q FY 2026" = Jul–Sep 2026). Everyone else here (Gulf, Veedol, Savita, IOCL/HPCL/BPCL, PPAC, FADA) uses **April–March**: FY26 = Apr-2025 to Mar-2026; Q2 FY27 = Jul–Sep 2026. **Always say "CY" for Castrol.** Castrol's Q3 CY26 is the **same calendar quarter** as Gulf's Q2 FY27.
- **FX: ₹94.9 = US$1** (late Oct-2026 working rate, per task brief; the rupee was ~₹86 in Dec-2025 [E] and fell ~5.5% in calendar 2025 [V Castrol AR25]). Used for all USD conversions unless stated.

## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Calendar year vs fiscal year.**
   - Castrol "FY 2026" = **CY26 (Jan–Dec 2026)**. Its "3Q FY 2026" is Jul–Sep 2026.
   - Gulf/Veedol/Savita "FY26" = **Apr-2025 to Mar-2026**.
   - A Castrol CY25 vs Gulf FY26 comparison is offset by one quarter. That is acceptable for annual structure, but **not for anything touching the 2026 base-oil shock**: Gulf's FY26 includes Jan–Mar 2026; Castrol's CY25 does not.
   - Castrol's CFO also said on the 26-Oct call that the Stonepeak deal was "announced back in **December '24**". That is a slip. It was **December 2025** [V AR25, PA dated 24-Dec-2025].
2. **Volume share vs value share.**
   - **IOCL Servo is #1 by volume:** 855 TMT in FY26 [V-snip], roughly **4× Castrol's litres** (Castrol ~241 mn L ≈ 212 TMT [E]).
   - **Castrol is #1 by value / in branded automotive retail.** The company says "#1 market share across automotive categories" [V Q2 CY26 PPT]. Our primer's "~20% of the overall market" is unsourced and has no stated basis.
   - The only share table found is old and undated: IOCL 27%, Castrol 18%, HPCL 17%, BPCL 10%, Gulf 5%, Tide Water 4%, Shell 4% [V-snip, undated, probably early 2010s]. **Never print a market share without saying "volume" or "value" and the year.**
3. **"Lubricants & greases" in PPAC is not the finished-lubricant market.**
   - PPAC apparent consumption of L&G was **4,961 TMT in FY26** [V]. Kline puts finished-lubricant demand at **2.6 mn t (2023)** [V Gulf PPT citing Kline].
   - The ~2 mn t gap is base oils used in process oils, white oils, transformer oils, rubber-process oil, re-refining flows, and refinery/marketing reclassification [E].
   - PPAC's series is also **noisy**: FY22 4,540 → FY23 3,737 → FY24 4,087 → FY25 4,581 → FY26 4,961 TMT.
   - Use PPAC for **direction and the 2026 shock** (H1 FY27 −7.6%). Use Kline for the **level**.
4. **Castrol does not disclose volume.** Every litre number is a proxy:
   - "8 litres sold every second" (Q2 CY26 PPT) → ~252 mn L/yr [E].
   - BRSR water intensity: 0.17 KL water per KL of output on 40,994 KL withdrawn → **~241 mn L in CY25**; 0.20 on 45,210 KL → ~226 mn L in CY24 [E from V AR25; intensity is rounded, so ±5%].
   - Primer: "~230 M litres" for CY25 (also "well over 200 million litres").
   - Quarterly, management gives only words: ">5% volume growth" in Q2 CY26 (PPT); "healthy" between Q2 and Q3; "ahead of a 3–4% market" over 9M [V transcript]. Analysts on the Q3 call explicitly complained about this.
5. **Margin % vs margin per litre (the 2026 trap).**
   - Q3 CY26 EBITDA margin was **15%**, against **26%** in Q2 and the **21–24%** guidance frame. Management says **margin per litre** is "steady to slightly higher" because revenue per litre rose with ~60% raw-material inflation [V transcript].
   - The arithmetic does **not** support "steady per litre" on a gross-profit basis. Q3 gross profit was **₹634 Cr vs ₹679 Cr** a year earlier (−6.6%) [E from V results]. Either Q3 volume fell ~7%+, or "margin per litre" is a different metric (e.g. a contribution figure after variable selling costs). **Flag; do not repeat management's claim as fact.**
   - 9M CY26 gross profit **+12.3%** (₹2,343 vs ₹2,087 Cr) is the better read [E].
6. **Castrol's "investments" are not cash.**
   - The **₹442.8 Cr non-current investment** (Dec-25; ₹487.5 Cr in Dec-24) is **0.001% CCPS plus equity in Ki Mobility Solutions Pvt Ltd** (the restructured TVS aftermarket/servicing business Castrol bought into in 2022), carried at FVTOCI [V AR25 note 4.6].
   - Real liquidity at Dec-25: **cash ₹426.6 Cr + other bank balances ₹714.4 Cr = ₹1,141 Cr**, against only lease liabilities (screener borrowings ₹63 Cr) [V AR25; V screener]. **Net cash ≈ ₹1,078 Cr** [E].
   - Do **not** use screener's "investments" in net cash.
7. **Royalty: rate vs effective cost.**
   - Royalty to Castrol Ltd (UK) was **₹132.49 Cr in CY25** (₹129.31 Cr CY24) [V AR25 note]: **2.3% of sales** and **~10.2% of pre-exceptional PBT**.
   - The secondary-source formula "3.5% of turnover capped at 10% of PBT" [V-snip TradingView] fits: **the PBT cap binds**. Royalty therefore tracks profit, not sales.
   - The primer says the rate is "not independently confirmed". It now is (amount [V]; formula [V-snip]).
8. **A&P is two lines.**
   - "Advertising" ₹163.9 Cr + "Sales promotion fees" ₹315.4 Cr = **₹479.4 Cr = 8.4% of CY25 sales** (CY24: ₹420.3 Cr, 7.8%) [V AR25].
   - Much of the "sales promotion" line is **trade/mechanic incentive spend** (loyalty points, FastScan rewards), economically closer to a price discount than to media [E].
   - Freight (₹187.4 Cr, 3.3%) and "legal, professional and contract" (₹166.5 Cr, which likely includes BP-group shared services) are separate [V].
9. **Exceptional items and dividends.**
   - CY25 PBT ₹1,282 Cr is **after** a ₹22.5 Cr exceptional item (new labour codes; Gulf booked ₹22.6 Cr for the same) [V].
   - CY24's 149% payout reflects a **₹4.50 special dividend** (CY24 DPS ₹13.00 = interim 3.50 + final 9.50 incl. special) [V AR25]. CY25 DPS **₹8.75** (3.50 + 5.25) = 91% payout.
   - Do not call CY24 "the new normal".
10. **"Tide Water" is now "Veedol Corporation".** Same company (Andrew Yule-promoted; Veedol and ENEOS brands); screener lists it as Veedol Corporation Ltd [V]. "Savita" trades as SOTL. Savita is **mainly transformer and white oils (73% of sales)**; lubricants are only ~26% [V Savita Q1 FY27 PPT]. It is a base-oil-to-specialty processor, not a branded-lube peer.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **India is the world's #3 lubricant market and one of the few still growing in volume:**
  - ~**10% of global demand** and **21% of Asia-Pacific** (Mordor, cited in Castrol AR25) [V].
  - Kline: **2.6 mn t in 2023 → 3.6 mn t in 2033**; volume CAGR **3–4%**, value CAGR **6–8%**; industrial **3–4%**, automotive **2–3%** (Gulf Q1 FY27 PPT citing Kline Global Lubricants 2023) [V].
  - Castrol's MD: "market growth in a regular year … 3% to 4%" [V transcript].
  - At ~₹200–250/L blended trade value, the market is roughly **₹55,000–70,000 Cr (~US$6–7 bn)** [E]. Third-party sizing ranges **US$4.8–9.7 bn**; these figures are mutually inconsistent and come from paid-report landing pages [V-snip]. **Use ranges.**
- **It is an oligopoly of two kinds of player:**
  - **(a) PSU refiners with captive base oil and forecourts:** IOCL Servo **855 TMT** (FY26, +15%), HP Lubes **686 TMT** (FY26, vs 704), BPCL MAK **428 TMT** (FY26) [V-snip].
  - **(b) Brand-and-distribution private/MNC blenders:** Castrol (~212 TMT [E]), Gulf, Veedol, Shell, Valvoline Cummins (JV), ExxonMobil/Mobil, TotalEnergies, Jio-bp (and Castrol's own Jio-bp forecourt tie-up) [primer; K].
  - The PSUs win **tonnes**; the brands win **rupees per litre**.
- **The listed universe is small and Castrol-dominated.**
  - Four listed lubricant-led companies: last-FY revenue **₹16,307 Cr**, OP **₹2,334 Cr (14.3%)**, PAT **₹1,669 Cr**, m-cap **₹30,480 Cr** [E: sum of screener 1-Nov-26].
  - **Castrol is 61% of the m-cap and 57% of the PAT.**
- **Castrol's economics are FMCG, not oil:**
  - CY25 revenue ₹5,722 Cr, OP ₹1,348 Cr (**23.6%**), PAT ₹950 Cr; **ROCE 60%, ROE 46%**; payout 91% [V screener].
  - Negative-to-zero working capital (CCC 0 days CY25) [V screener].
  - Per litre (CY25, on ~241 mn L) [E]: realisation **~₹237/L**; gross margin **~₹116/L**; A&P **~₹20/L**; royalty **~₹5.5/L**; EBITDA **~₹56/L**.
  - Peers: Gulf OPM **12–13%**, ROCE 26%; Veedol OPM **10%** (16% in Q1 FY27), ROCE 24%; Savita OPM **4–6% normal**, 25% on inventory gains in Q1 FY27, ROCE 13% [V screener].
- **2026 is a base-oil shock year:**
  - Indian **base-oil (LOBS) import unit value $1,116/t in H1 FY27 vs $864/t a year earlier (+29%)**, peaking at **$1,423–1,487/t in Jun–Jul-2026**; import **tonnage −18%** (1,305 vs 1,600 TMT) [V PPAC, value/qty arithmetic].
  - PPAC L&G consumption **−7.6%** in H1 FY27, with **June −28%** [V PPAC].
  - **Castrol:** priced early (Q2 revenue +25%, OPM 26% on low-cost inventory). Then the squeeze hit: **Q3 revenue +27%, EBITDA −19%, OPM 15%, PAT −23%**, with "a 60-plus percent increase in raw material bill" and **COGS +61% YoY** [V results; V transcript].
  - **Gulf:** volume **+16%** (Q2 FY27) and +17% (Q1) while holding a **~12% OPM** [V]. Gulf is **taking share** in the disruption.
- **The structural debate is EVs vs the parc:**
  - **Electric 2Ws were 10.7% of 2W retail in Aug-2026** (7.7% a year earlier); 6.54% for FY26; 9.79% in Mar-26 [V autocarpro/FADA; V-snip FY26].
  - ePV 7.6%, eCV 5.18%, e3W 65% (Aug-26) [V].
  - The parc is **~328 mn vehicles (Mar-26), 243 mn of them 2Ws** [V Castrol Q2 PPT]. Cumulative e2W stock is ~**5 mn ≈ 2% of the 2W parc** [E].
  - The ICE parc is still growing, slowly. The *rate* of engine-oil pool erosion is now ~0.6–1.0 pp/yr in 2Ws and rising [E].
- **The ownership event:** Castrol India is about to get a **private-equity-controlled parent** (Stonepeak + CPPIB, BP 35%).
  - The open offer is at **₹194.04 + 10% p.a.** (≈ **₹214 if the DPS lands 31-Dec-2026; ≈ ₹216 at end-Jan-2027** [E]). The stock trades at **₹188**.
  - The question for minorities: does a PE owner with a **US$10.1 bn EV to service** change the royalty, payout or brand-licence terms? See §B3.

### 1.2 Value chain (crude → mechanic)

1. **Crude → vacuum distillate → base oil (refinery).**
   - **Group I** (solvent-refined; VI 80–120; still a large share of Indian mineral oils).
   - **Group II** (hydrocracked; low sulphur; the workhorse for BS-VI-era 15W-40/20W-40).
   - **Group III** (severely hydrocracked; VI >120; synthetics 0W/5W).
   - **Group IV** = PAO; **Group V** = esters [K].
   - Castrol "predominantly" uses **Group II and III**; 0W/5W synthetics (Magnatec, Power1) depend on **Group III + PAO** [V transcript].
   - India produces mostly Group I/II at PSU refineries (IOCL Haldia/Panipat/Koyali, HPCL Mumbai, CPCL) [K] and **imports ~3.2 mn t/yr of base oil** (FY26 LOBS imports **3,178 TMT, $2.71 bn**) [V PPAC].
   - **Group III comes largely from Korea (SK, S-Oil, GS Caltex) and the Gulf (Bahrain Bapco/Nynas, UAE ADNOC Ruwais, Qatar)** [K]. Gulf Group III supply sits **behind Hormuz**, hence the 2026 shock.
2. **Additives (10–20% of the blend).** Detergents, dispersants, anti-wear ZDDP, VI improvers, pour-point depressants. Bought as packages from the **"big four" additive houses: Lubrizol, Infineum, Chevron Oronite, Afton** [K]. Priced in USD and largely imported → **rupee exposure**. For Castrol the global group sets formulations and approvals [V transcript: "products … approved globally"].
3. **Blending and packaging** (low capex).
   - Castrol: **3 plants** (Patalganga, Silvassa, Paharpur), **1 tech centre**, **14 depots/DCs**, **630+ employees** [V Q2 CY26 PPT]. Utilisation **70–80%** [V transcript; CY25 AR said "very high"]. Maintenance + growth capex **₹100–150 Cr/yr** [V transcript].
   - Gulf: Silvassa and Chennai plants [K; Chennai V PR].
   - Veedol: **5 Indian plants** [V screener].
   - Packaging (HDPE bottles, cartons) is a meaningful cost line: "raw and packaging material" [V transcript].
4. **Route to market (where value is captured).**
   - **B2C bazaar/retail:** distributor → sub-distributor/rural stockist → spare-parts retailer / workshop → **mechanic** (the decision-maker).
     - Castrol: **~167,000 outlets incl. 46,000 rural** (Q3 CY26); **400+ distributors**, ~850 sub-distributors [primer]; **1,100+ rural distributors, 45K rural outlets, ~950 Rural Service Express** (Q2 PPT); **700K mechanics** in its ecosystem; **180,000 "verified" mechanics** on FastScan (+24% YoY); **~64K servicing network** [V].
     - Gulf: **100,000+ touchpoints**, 330+ auto distributors, 2,100+ rural stockists, ~13,500 Gulf Bike/Car Stops in 522 cities [V Gulf PPT].
   - **Workshops / IWS:** Castrol has **37,000 independent bike workshops, 16,000 multi-brand car workshops, ~870 Castrol Auto Service** outlets (Q3 CY26) [V].
   - **OEM:** factory-fill (first fill, low margin, strategic) and **franchise workshops (FWS)**, where the OEM's "genuine oil" is often a co-branded or OEM-labelled product supplied by a blender. Castrol has **90+ OEM customers** (Tata, Maruti, JCB named) [V]; Gulf has **50+ OEM partnerships** [V].
   - **B2B industrial / fleet / mining / marine:** direct or via industrial distributors. Some contracts have **raw-material price-flex clauses** [V transcript]. Castrol runs **23 chemical-management (CMS) sites** [V PPT].
   - **PSU forecourts:** Servo at IOCL pumps (~33,000+ outlets [V-snip]), HP and MAK at their own pumps. Castrol sells via **Jio-bp forecourts** [primer].
   - **E-commerce / modern trade:** Castrol Auto Care is in ~50,000 stores plus e-com [V].

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **The vehicle parc, not new sales.**
   - Parc **~328 mn (Mar-26): ~243 mn 2Ws, ~50 mn 4Ws, ~12 mn CVs** [V Castrol Q2 PPT; primer's "~350 mn / 250 mn" is rounder and older].
   - Parc grew **~4%** in FY25 [V AR25/CRISIL].
   - FY26 auto retail was a record **2.97 crore units** [V-snip FADA/autocarpro]. 2W retail in Aug-2026 was **17.15 lakh (+19.7%)** [V-snip].
   - Lifetime arithmetic: a motorcycle is drained ~30–40 times over ~15 years [primer]. Annual lube per vehicle ≈ **2W 2–3 L; car 4–6 L; LCV 15–25 L; HCV 60–120 L; tractor 15–25 L** [K/E ranges].
   - **Implication:** one HCV ≈ 30–50 2Ws of oil. That is why CV/freight cycles (and diesel) matter more than headline 2W EV penetration.
2. **Freight and diesel activity.**
   - HSD consumption FY26 **94,708 TMT (+3.6%)**; H1 FY27 **47,938 vs 47,677 (+0.5%)**; Oct-26 diesel **+11.7%** [V PPAC].
   - CV sales declined in FY25 on lower government capex [V AR25].
   - Castrol's Q2 is seasonally strongest (agri movement → freight/CV oil) [V transcript].
3. **Rural motorisation.**
   - Rural ≈ **55% of 2W retail** (FADA) [V AR25]. 2.24 mn used cars were sold in rural markets in 2025 [V AR25].
   - Only ~**5 cars or 60 bikes per 100 rural households** vs 23 cars/64 bikes urban; 90%+ of households have no car; 129 mn households have no 2W [V Castrol Q2 PPT citing PRICE/Tata research].
   - Gulf: 8% of households own cars, 47–50% own 2Ws [V].
   - Castrol's rural network: ~40,000 (CY25) → **46,000 outlets** (Q3 CY26) [V].
4. **Industrial production.** Steel, cement, auto/components, machinery growth **7–10%** (Castrol PPT citing Mordor/IBEF) [V]. Castrol industrial: double-digit volume growth for ~1.5–2 years; **~400 net new customers** in 9M CY26 [V transcript].
5. **Agriculture/tractors.** Tractor sales **+18.7% FY25**, expected **+10–15% FY26** (monsoon, MSP, **GST cut 12% → 5%** on tractors) [V AR25 citing CRISIL]. Tractor oils are a Servo/Castrol/Gulf battleground; Gulf cites "Agri" as a driver of OEM FWS growth [V].
6. **Premiumisation (value, not volume).**
   - SUVs, BS-VI engines and turbo-petrols need 0W-20/5W-30 synthetics.
   - Gulf/Kline: "fast adopting lighter viscosity engine oils & synthetics"; middle class 14% (FY05) → 31% now → 60–63% by 2047 [V Gulf PPT].
   - Castrol launched **full-synthetic Activ (2W) and GTX** in Q2 CY26 [V PPT].
7. **Data centres / new energy (nascent).** Castrol's thermal-management fluids: pilots plus "a bit of business"; two formulations **NVIDIA-certified** ~Sep-2026; "wouldn't form a substantial part of our revenue and profit today" [V transcript]. Treat as an option, not a driver.

## 7. Regulation and policy

1. **Emission and engine norms.** **BS-VI from 1-Apr-2020** and BS-VI phase 2 (RDE, OBD-II) from Apr-2023 [K]. These require lower-SAPS oils (API SN/SP, ACEA C-class, CK-4) and longer drain intervals. The effect is premiumisation (value up) but fewer litres per vehicle per year.
2. **E20 fuel.** Nationwide E20 petrol from 2025 [K]. "MS consumption includes ethanol blending" [V PPAC]. Ethanol affects oil dilution and corrosion → new specs (Castrol: "lubricant for flex fuel and hybrid vehicles" [V PPT]).
3. **GST.** 18% on lubricants [K]. The GST 2.0 rationalisation (Sep-2025) cut GST on small cars/2Ws ≤350cc and tractors (12% → 5% for tractors) [V AR25 for tractors; K for vehicles]. That boosts parc additions, not lube tax.
4. **Base-oil customs duty.** Import duty on base oils is ~5% (BCD) plus AIDC/SWS [K — VERIFY]. **No anti-dumping duty on base oil found** [E]. Policy lever: any duty change moves the PSU-vs-private cost gap.
5. **Used oil / EPR.** The Hazardous and Other Wastes (Amendment) Rules introduced **Extended Producer Responsibility for used oil from FY2024-25** [K]: producers/importers of base and lube oil must ensure re-refining targets. This is a cost for blenders and an opening for RRBO. Castrol's RRBO engine oil and Tata Motors MoU are relevant [V].
6. **BIS / quality.** Engine-oil QCOs: none confirmed [E — FLAG]. OEM approvals (Maruti, Tata, Ashok Leyland, Cummins) act as de facto standards.
7. **SEBI takeover code (for Castrol now).** The indirect-acquisition open offer (SAST Reg 5(1)) and price enhancement at **10% p.a.** (Reg 8(12)) apply. The DPS is due ≤5 working days after the underlying transaction completes (proviso to Reg 13(4)) [V PA]. Minimum public shareholding (25%) may force a post-offer sell-down if acceptance is high [K/E].
8. **Related-party / royalty rules.** SEBI LODR Reg 23: material RPTs need shareholder approval. Royalty above 5% of turnover (brand) requires MoE approval only in old FEMA regimes [K]. Castrol's royalty RPT limit was ₹150 Cr approved vs ₹62 Cr used in H1-2024 [V-snip NSE RPT filing]. **Any post-Stonepeak change to the licence/royalty would need minority approval as a material RPT** [E/K].
9. **EV policy.** FAME-II ended in 2024 and was replaced by **PM E-DRIVE** (Oct-2024 to Mar-2026; later extended?) [K — VERIFY], plus state EV policies and PLI for ACC batteries and autos [K]. EV penetration has accelerated through the 2026 oil shock (e2W **10.7%**, Aug-26) [V].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

- **1990s–2000s:** Liberalisation lets MNC brands (Castrol, Shell, Mobil, Gulf, Valvoline Cummins, Total) take on PSU lubes. Castrol builds the 2W/PCMO bazaar franchise and becomes India's most profitable lube company. The PSUs keep tonnage via forecourts and institutional supply.
- **2000:** BP acquires Burmah Castrol, so BP becomes Castrol India's ultimate parent [primer; K].
- **2008–09:** Crude spikes to $147 then crashes. Base-oil prices whipsaw and lubricant margins compress then expand [K]. This set the template for the "margin lag" behaviour seen again in 2022 and 2026.
- **2014:** Gulf Oil Lubricants India is demerged and listed (Hinduja). It begins a decade of ~9% volume CAGR and share gains [V growth; K listing].
- **2014–2016 (oil collapse):** Base oil falls with crude. Castrol's OPM peaks at **~30% (CY16, CY19)** [V screener]. ROCE was >100% then (on a smaller capital base).
- **2015:** BP sells **11.5% of Castrol India for ~₹2,075 Cr**, cutting to 51% [V-snip epcworld headline; date K ~2015 — VERIFY].
- **2017:** 1:1 bonus (equity ₹247 → ₹495 Cr) [V screener].
- **2020:** BS-VI; COVID. CY20 revenue −23% to ₹2,997 Cr; OPM 27% [V].
- **2021–22:** Russia-Ukraine; base oil spikes. Castrol OPM falls from 27% (CY20) to **23% (CY22)** [V]. Castrol invests ~₹325 Cr in **TVS Automobile Solutions** (now Ki Mobility) in 2022 [V screener investments 0 → 325].
- **2023–24:** Castrol Auto Service expands. EV fluids and data-centre cooling start. Gulf diversifies into EV charging (Tirex).
- **Feb-2025:** BP launches a strategic review of Castrol [K]. **Dec-2025:** sale of 65% to Stonepeak; Castrol India open offer [V].
- **2025:** Castrol posts a record CY25 (₹5,722 Cr, +8% volume) [V].
- **28-Feb-2026:** West Asia war. Base oil import unit value peaks at ~$1,490/t in Jul-2026 [V PPAC]. **Q2 CY26:** Castrol OPM 26% on early price hikes and inventory. **Q3 CY26:** OPM 15%. Gulf grows volume 16–17% at a steady 12% OPM. Savita books a 25% OPM on inventory gains (Q1 FY27).

**The cycle pattern [E]:** base-oil price spikes compress branded-lube margins for 1–3 quarters. Price hikes then restore them, and falling base oil subsequently *expands* margins (CY15–16, CY20, CY23). **Branded players therefore earn above-normal margins in the down-leg.** The 2026 spike is the third such episode in six years.

5. **Hydrogen ICE, CNG, flex-fuel, hybrids.** These sustain lube demand (hybrids still need engine oil). Castrol is "experimenting" with H2-ICE products [V PPT]. CNG is growing in CVs/cars [V PPT chart].
6. **Data-centre cooling (upside).** NVIDIA-certified fluids [V]. Material revenue is unlikely before 2028+ [E].
7. **Re-refining / circularity.** RRBO under EPR could commoditise part of base-oil supply [K/E].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Base oil is a pass-through with a lag, and the lag is the P&L.**
   - Base oil is ~70% of COGS, crude- and Group-III-linked, and largely imported (3.2 mn t; $2.7 bn FY26) [V PPAC].
   - Branded blenders reprice in steps and win back margin when base oil falls. 2026 is the textbook case: Castrol OPM went 21% (Q1) → 26% (Q2) → 15% (Q3) [V].
   - Normalised margin, not quarterly margin, is what to value.
2. **Brand and distribution, not chemistry, earn the returns.**
   - Castrol's ~₹116/L gross margin vs ~₹56/L EBITDA: the ₹60/L in between is A&P, trade and network [E].
   - ~167,000 outlets, 700K mechanics, FastScan [V]. The mechanic decides, which makes the moat "trust plus reach". Castrol's 60% ROCE vs Gulf's 26% is this force in one number.
3. **Volume is mature (3–4%); value grows on premiumisation.**
   - Kline: 3–4% volume, 6–8% value [V]. Drain intervals lengthen as products premiumise, so litres per vehicle fall while ₹ per change rises.
   - Winners grow **realisation per litre and share**, not the market.
4. **EVs erode the 2W engine-oil pool first, and 2026 accelerated it.**
   - e2W penetration 10.7% (Aug-26) [V]. The parc delays the impact by ~10 years, but the **direction is now visible in sales**.
   - CV, tractor, industrial and marine are the durable pools, which is why every player is tilting B2B/industrial (Castrol industrial double-digit [V]; Gulf B2B/OEM double-digit [V]).
5. **Ownership and competition are reshuffling at once.**
   - Castrol moves from an oil major to a **PE-led JV (Stonepeak)**. HPCL plans to **demerge HP Lubes**. Gulf is **taking volume (+16–17%)**. PSUs (Servo +15%) used captive base oil to win in the 2026 shortage.
   - Share is moving in a way it hasn't for a decade.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Revenue = Σ_segments (parc × litres per vehicle per year × brand share) × realisation per litre**

**EBITDA = Volume × (realisation/L − base-oil-and-additive cost/L − packaging/L) − A&P − network/logistics − royalty − overheads**

Equivalently: **EBITDA = Volume × EBITDA/L**, where EBITDA/L moves with (price-hike timing − base-oil cost/L change).

### C.1 Worked example 1 — Castrol CY25 [E from V]
- Volume ~241 mn L × realisation ~₹237.4/L = **₹5,721.5 Cr** revenue [V].
- COGS ₹2,919 Cr = ₹121/L → **gross profit ₹2,802 Cr (49.0%) = ₹116/L**.
- Less: A&P ₹479 Cr (₹19.9/L); employees ₹296 Cr (₹12.3/L); freight + stock-point ₹229 Cr (₹9.5/L); royalty ₹132 Cr (₹5.5/L); other ~₹318 Cr (₹13.2/L).
- → **EBITDA ₹1,348 Cr (23.6%) = ₹55.9/L**.
- D&A ₹101 Cr, other income ₹45–67 Cr, interest ₹9 Cr, exceptional −₹22.5 Cr → PBT ₹1,282 Cr → tax 26% → **PAT ₹950 Cr** [V].

### C.2 Worked example 2 — Castrol Q3 CY26, "the squeeze" [E from V]
- Revenue ₹1,734.7 Cr (+27.3%). COGS ₹1,100.8 Cr (+61%) → gross profit ₹634.0 Cr (36.5%) vs ₹678.9 Cr (49.8%) a year earlier.
- Opex: employees ₹87.1 Cr + other expenses ₹284.8 Cr = ₹372 Cr vs ₹356 Cr → **EBITDA ₹262 Cr (15.1%) vs ₹323 Cr (23.7%)**.
- If Q3 volume was flat YoY (~60 mn L), GM/L fell from ~₹113 to ~₹106. If volume fell 7%, GM/L was flat at ~₹113. Management's "steady to slightly higher" implies **volume down ≥7%** [E].

### C.3 Worked example 3 — Gulf Q2 FY27 [V, with E]
- Revenue ₹1,379 Cr (+44%) on volume +16% → realisation/L **+24%** [E: 1.44/1.16].
- EBITDA ₹163 Cr (+37%) → EBITDA/L **+18%** [E: 1.37/1.16].
- Gulf **grew per-litre profit while growing volume** in the same quarter Castrol's EBITDA fell 19%. Different fiscal labels, same calendar quarter (Jul–Sep 2026).

### C.4 Worked example 4 — what a ₹10/L base-oil move does [E]
- Castrol: ₹10/L × 241 mn L = ₹241 Cr ≈ **18% of CY25 EBITDA**.
- Gulf (volume unknown; assume ~180 mn L [E]): ~₹180 Cr ≈ **35% of FY26 EBITDA**.
- **Lower-margin players are proportionally twice as sensitive.** That makes Gulf's 2026 margin stability notable.

### C.5 Worked example 5 — one 2W rider over 10 years [K/E]
- 1 L every 3,000–4,000 km at ~9,000 km/yr ≈ 2.5 changes/yr × 1 L × ₹420 MRP ≈ **₹1,050/yr** of oil at retail.
- Castrol's net is ~₹590/yr (at ₹237/L) and its EBITDA ~₹140/yr.
- An e2W replaces this with ~₹100–200 of e-fluids/grease over the decade.

   - No quarterly number. "In the 9-month period, we continue to track volume growth, which is ahead of the market growth rate" (market 3–4% in a regular year; "this year … may well be a bit different").

   - Industrial grew double-digit in Q3 and has done so for ~1.5–2 years (steel, cement, metalworking fluids, localisation). Industrial margin is "on track to be higher than … last year".
   - "Overall volumes may remain soft in the short to medium term" (CFO).

Some B2B/institutional contracts have cost-flex clauses. Other levers: localisation of inputs, reformulation to cut bill-of-materials cost, a diversified supplier pool.

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed

1. **Gulf discloses litres, so the "per-litre" debate can be settled for Gulf and not for Castrol.**
   - Both managements switched to "per-litre margin" language in 2026.
   - Gulf's numbers check out: EBITDA/L rose from ~₹31 (FY26) to ~₹35 (Q1–Q2 FY27) while volume grew 16–17%.
   - Castrol's claim cannot be checked, and its own gross profit (−6.6% YoY in Q3) fits it only under generous H1-volume assumptions.
   - **The disclosure gap is now a competitive-narrative gap.**
2. **Castrol's real trade spend is ~18% of gross sales, not 8%.**
   - AR25 shows ₹679 Cr of rebates netted from revenue on top of ₹479 Cr of advertising and sales-promotion fees.
   - The "brand premium" is partly bought back by the trade every year. That is why a challenger with a cheaper ladder (Gulf) can take volume when base-oil inflation forces list-price hikes.
3. **The royalty is a profit share, not a sales tax, and that limits a PE parent's options.**
   - Castrol's royalty has sat at ~10% of PBT every year checked; Gulf's is a flat ~1.1% of sales. A Stonepeak-era increase would have to be a renegotiated licence (a material RPT needing minority approval).
   - Castrol India buys almost nothing from the group (₹92 Cr), so there is no transfer-pricing back door.
   - **Dividends (₹656 Cr to Castrol Ltd in CY25) are the main up-streaming channel.** Expect payout to stay at 80–90%+.
4. **PSU "lube sales" are a tonnage concept that includes base oil.**
   - HPCL's 428 TMTPA lube refinery and IOCL's new Group III LOBS make the PSUs base-oil **suppliers to the private blenders** as well as competitors.
   - In a Hormuz-type shock their pricing power over Group I/II feedstock matters more than their retail brands.
   - Gulf's CFO said Gulf is "in touch with all the NOCs in India who are producing base oils".
5. **Valvoline Cummins is the hidden #3 private brand.**
   - ₹3,011 Cr of FY26 revenue (+28%) puts it ahead of Veedol and close to Gulf's lubricant revenue. It grew faster than both in FY26.
   - The Cummins engine link anchors it in the EV-resistant DEO/CV pool. Any "listed peers" framing misses it.
6. **Veedol's growth is now offshore; India is flat.** Standalone revenue +1.3% with "flat domestic volumes" and deferred capex. Overseas subsidiaries supplied almost all FY26 growth and ~40% of PBT. The ENEOS franchise fee (₹311 Cr ≈ 20% of standalone revenue) makes Veedol's domestic economics far thinner than its consolidated 10–12% OPM suggests.
7. **Castrol's leadership churn coincides with the ownership change.** Three MDs and three CFOs between Oct-2024 and Jul-2025 (AR25 KMP list), plus a new auditor from the 2027 AGM. **Execution risk during the transition is under-discussed.**
8. **The open offer is economically a ~10% p.a. carry trade, conditional on completion.** At ₹188 vs ₹213.9 (DPS end-Dec-26) the gross spread is ~13.8%. The downside if the deal fails is that no offer happens at all (PA §1). Acceptance may be pro-rated if more than 26% is tendered [K]. The binding constraint is MPS: tendering above ~24% forces a promoter sell-down within 12 months.
