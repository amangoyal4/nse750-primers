## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **"Vedanta" means four different things. Name the basis every time.**
   - (a) The **pre-demerger group** (FY26: revenue ₹1,74,075 Cr, EBITDA ₹55,976 Cr) [primer]. It no longer exists as one listed entity.
   - (b) **Screener VEDL consolidated, restated**: continuing operations FY26 ₹78,437 Cr revenue, ₹23,183 Cr OP [V]. But "Net Profit" ₹25,096 Cr includes discontinued operations and minorities.
   - (c) **Residual VEDL as the company reports it from Q1 FY27**: revenue ₹23,456 Cr, EBITDA ₹8,469 Cr, PAT ₹5,294 Cr (Q1 FY27) [V-snip angelone]. Screener's Q1 shows ₹24,205 / ₹8,501 / ₹7,918 Cr. The PAT gap (₹7,918 vs ₹5,294–5,474 Cr) is **HZL's 39.29% minority interest** [E: screener EPS ₹14.00 × 391 Cr shares ≈ ₹5,474 Cr attributable].
   - (d) **The "Vedanta group" of five listed companies** (combined m-cap ₹2.55 L Cr) under Vedanta Resources. Management still guides at this level: **FY27 group EBITDA US$9.5–10 bn** [V-snip cofacto VEDL Q1 call].
   - **Rule for the writer:** sector tables use (b)/(c). Aluminium economics belong to VAML, a **reference** company, not a member.
2. **Attributable vs total profit.** Screener's VEDL FY26 Net Profit (₹25,096 Cr) is **before minority interest**. FY26 EPS ₹44.47 × 391 Cr shares ≈ **₹17,390 Cr attributable** [E]. Our primer calls ₹25,096 Cr "PAT (attributable)" — wrong label (see Primer issues). HZL is 39.29% owned by others: Government of India 27.92% plus the public.
3. **VEDL's valuation is a holdco discount on HZL.**
   - Vedanta's 60.71% of HZL is worth **₹1,27,234 Cr** at HZL's ₹496 (m-cap ₹2,09,576 Cr) [E from V screener].
   - **VEDL's whole m-cap is ₹88,570 Cr — 70% of the HZL stake alone.** The market ascribes **negative** value to Zinc International + copper + FACOR + ports + ventures, net of VEDL's own debt.
   - Screener's P/E of 8.05 on VEDL does not reconcile with its own TTM EPS (₹50.33 → 4.5×) [V screener; E]. Use the HZL look-through instead of P/E.
4. **Two storeys of debt — now three.**
   - **VEDL (residual):** net debt/EBITDA **0.30×** at Jun-26; cash ₹19,922–19,992 Cr; net debt fell ₹2,223 Cr in Q1 [V-snip]. Screener basis: borrowings ₹32,947 Cr − investments ₹15,418 Cr = ₹17,529 Cr (Mar-26, continuing ops) [V; E]. **Prefer the company 0.30× for prose.** Show the screener figure only as a balance-sheet item.
   - **Demerged entities:** VAML net debt **₹29,532 Cr** (Jun-26; Mar-26 ₹33,013 Cr), 0.9× EBITDA [V-snip VAML release].
   - **Parent Vedanta Resources (VRL, unlisted, London):** net debt **~US$4.9 bn (Mar-26)** — about ₹46,500 Cr at ₹94.9 — down from ~$8.9 bn five years earlier [V-snip outlookbusiness]. Management said in July 2026: "$10 bn → $5 bn over three years", with a target of **$3 bn by end-FY27** [V-snip cofacto].
   - Never add VRL debt to VEDL's. VRL services it from dividends and brand fees received from the five listed companies.
5. **Recyclers' revenue is mostly metal value passing through; margins are per tonne, not %.**
   - Jain: FY26 OP margin 5.9%; Q1 FY27 **3.97%**. Gravita: FY26 10.3%; Q1 FY27 7.5% (screener) / 9.8% (company "adjusted EBITDA") [V; V-snip].
   - Compare recyclers on **EBITDA per tonne** and volume, never on margin %. A copper-heavy mix mechanically lowers margin % because copper is ~5–7× the value of lead per tonne [E: LME Cu ~$14.2k vs Pb ~$1.9k].
6. **Gravita's "EBITDA" has two bases.** Q1 FY27 company adjusted EBITDA **₹145 Cr** vs screener OP **₹110 Cr**. The ₹35 Cr gap is roughly screener's "other income" of ₹48 Cr, which likely holds hedging/forex gains and treasury income on QIP money [E]. Gravita's guided per-kg spreads (lead ₹19–20/kg etc.) are on the company basis.
7. **Gravita's "volume" = sales volume of recycled product, and "MTPA" is metric tonnes.** Q1 FY27 volume **55,455 t (+4%)**. Installed capacity is **4.97 lakh tpa** (i.e. ~45% utilisation on the volume run-rate), with a target of **>8 lakh tpa by FY29** [V-snip investywise/NSE PPT].
   - Our primer's "~230k+ T FY26 volume" is plausible (~2.1–2.3 lakh t [E: 4 × ~55k]) but unverified.
   - Gravita's long-range plan is labelled "Vision 2028" (primer), "Vision 2029" and "Vision 2030" (Q1 FY27 deck/Quartr) in different sources. **Use "Vision 2030 (formerly Vision 2028/2029)" and quote the FY29 capacity target.**
8. **Jain's FY25 base differs between screener and the primer.** Screener FY25 revenue **₹6,429 Cr** vs primer **~₹7,126 Cr**. FY26 growth is therefore **+48% (screener)**, not "+34%" (primer) [V]. Use screener. The primer's figure may be gross of something or standalone — unexplained.
9. **Zinc CoP has two definitions.**
   - HZL reports zinc CoP **excluding royalty**: Q2 FY27 **$703/t** (−29% YoY); Q1 FY27 $851/t; FY26 $959/t [V-snip; primer HZL].
   - Royalty (~8–10% of LME zinc plus a ~2% DMF/NMET top-up) adds ~$350–400/t at today's prices [E].
   - The silver by-product is credited separately, not netted in CoP. **Always say "ex-royalty".**
10. **Aluminium "cost of production" = hot-metal cost** (alumina + power + carbon + conversion, before casting/value-add premium).
    - VAML: Q1 FY27 **$1,698/t** (−3% QoQ, −4% YoY); FY27 guidance **$1,650–1,700/t**; FY26 **$1,752/t** (a five-year low) [V-snip; primer].
    - VAML EBITDA/t Q1 FY27 **$1,797/t (Emkay) / $1,804/t (company call summary)**, vs $1,511/t in Q4 FY26 [V-snip].
    - Hindalco reports "CoP" on a different basis ($1,926/t Q4 FY26, with EBITDA/t $1,756) [primer hindalco]. **Do not compare CoP across companies without the basis.**

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Three members, two economic species, one theme: India's non-ferrous metal supply, primary vs secondary.**
  - **Vedanta (residual) = a primary miner/holdco.** FY26 continuing revenue ₹78,437 Cr, OP ₹23,183 Cr (29.6%); TTM ₹88,898 Cr / ₹27,496 Cr (31%) [V screener]. Its value is mostly **60.71% of Hindustan Zinc**, a first-decile zinc-lead-silver miner whose Q2 FY27 EBITDA hit a record **₹9,285 Cr at a 62% margin**, with silver ~45–46% of profit [V-snip HZL release]. Cash is upstreamed to the leveraged unlisted parent VRL.
  - **Gravita + Jain = secondary (scrap-based) converters.** FY26 revenue ₹13,808 Cr (+34%), OP ₹1,002 Cr (7.3%), PAT ₹725 Cr; m-cap ₹19,894 Cr [E: sums of V screener]. They earn a per-tonne conversion spread on lead, copper, aluminium, plastics and rubber. They are hedged on the LME, so volume × spread matters, not price.
  - **All three: FY26 revenue ₹92,245 Cr (+26.3%), OP ₹24,185 Cr (26.2%), PAT ₹25,821 Cr (screener basis; VEDL incl. discontinued and minorities); m-cap ₹1,08,464 Cr on 23-Oct-2026** [E: sums of V screener]. VEDL is 85% of revenue and 82% of m-cap. **Any "sector" figure is a Vedanta figure unless the recyclers are split out.**
- **2026 was a metal-price boom for the miner, and a margin squeeze for the recyclers.**
  - LME, latest available:
    - copper **$14,215/t (22-Oct-2026)**, after a record **$14,850 on 17-Aug-2026** (2025 average $9,947) [V-sib copper];
    - zinc ~**$3,724/t** (15-Oct settle) [V-snip S&P corrections notice];
    - aluminium ~**$3,222/t** (28-Aug spot; alloy contract ~$3,190–3,200 mid-Oct) [V-snip cbonds; S&P];
    - lead ~**$1,879/t** (28-Aug) [V-snip cbonds];
    - silver at multi-decade highs (HZL realised $53.1/oz in FY26, +75%) [primer HZL].
  - **VEDL Q1 FY27:** OP +99% YoY to ₹8,501 Cr (35% margin) [V screener]. HZL H1 FY27 EBITDA ₹17,359 Cr (+108%) [V-snip].
  - **Recyclers Q1 FY27:** revenue **+62%** but OP only **+14%**. OP margin fell from 7.4% to **5.2%** [E: sums of V screener].

    - Gravita's OP margin fell to 7.5% from 9.7% as low-margin RMIL copper/brass was consolidated [V screener; E].
  - Rising, volatile metal prices inflate recyclers' working capital and widen hedging gaps. Scrap discounts to LME narrow when everyone wants scrap.
- **Valuation splits the same way:**
  - **VEDL: ~3.9× EV/TTM OP on the screener basis** (₹1,06,099 Cr EV ÷ ₹27,496 Cr) [E]. Adding HZL's minorities (₹82,342 Cr) as a claim gives **~6.9×** [E]. Dividend yield **15.0%** trailing (screener) [V], which management says has "normalised" to 6–7% and targets ~5% [V-snip].

- **Policy is the recyclers' tailwind; governance is Vedanta's discount.**
  - Recyclers' tailwinds:
    - **Battery Waste Management Rules 2022** (EPR, recovery targets, certificate price band, recycled-content mandates from FY28) [V-snip].
    - **Nil customs duty on copper scrap since Feb-2025.** Copper scrap imports were **467 kt in FY26 (+35%)** [V-sib copper].

  - Vedanta's discount:
    - The parent's dependence on upstreamed cash.
    - **Viceroy Research's 9-Jul-2025 report** calling VRL a "parasite" on a "dying host". Vedanta called it "false propaganda"; a Supreme Court PIL was withdrawn in Oct-2025 after the bench declined it; no SEBI finding is known [V-snip moneylife/outlook].

### 1.2 Value chains

1. **Integrated zinc-lead-silver (HZL inside VEDL).**
   - Underground mines in Rajasthan (Rampura Agucha, Sindesar Khurd, Zawar, Rajpura Dariba, Kayad) → concentrator → smelters (Chanderiya, Dariba, Debari) with captive coal power and renewables → refined zinc/lead/silver sold domestically and exported [primer HZL; K].
   - **Mined metal 271 kt in Q2 FY27** (+5%), H1 539 kt; refined 264 kt (+7%) [V-snip].
   - Value is captured **at the mine**: ore grade, depth and the silver credit put HZL in the first cost decile. Smelting adds little standalone margin, but integration removes TC/RC exposure.
2. **Copper custom refining (VEDL Silvassa).**
   - Imported anode/blister → electrorefining → cathode → CCR rod. Margin = TC/RC + by-product + rod premium.
   - **TC/RC collapsed to a benchmark of zero for 2026** (spot ~−$235/dmt) [V-sib copper]. **Vedanta's copper business made an EBITDA loss of ₹48 Cr on ₹31,069 Cr revenue in FY26** [V-sib copper].
   - Copper is ~half of residual VEDL's revenue but ~0% of its EBITDA. This is why the company quotes "EBITDA margin 57%" (ex-copper) against an all-in ~36% [V-snip angelone; E].
3. **Lead-acid battery recycling (Gravita, Jain).**
   - Collection (dealers, OEM take-back, scrap aggregators, imports of "lead scrap/drained batteries") → breaking/separation (lead paste, grids, PP casing, acid) → smelting (rotary/short-rotary furnace) → refining/alloying (Ca, Sb, Sn alloys to battery-maker specs) → ingots back to battery makers.
   - EPR certificates are a separate, tradable revenue stream for registered recyclers [V-snip BWMR].
   - A lead-acid battery is ~60%+ lead by weight [primer Gravita]. Secondary lead is the majority of Indian lead supply [K; see §2].
4. **Copper scrap recycling (Jain, Gravita/RMIL).**
   - Imported #1/#2 copper, birch/cliff, cable scrap and brass (HS 7404) → sorting → fire-refining in anode furnaces → anodes → electrorefining or direct rod/busbar/profile casting.
   - Jain commissioned a **2nd copper anode furnace (800 t/month) in Jul-2026**. **Copper wire rod (600 t/month)** and **busbar/profiles (1,500 t/month)** are due in Q3 FY27 [V-snip cofacto/scanx].
5. **Aluminium scrap remelting (Gravita, Jain; many unlisted).**
   - Imported mixed scrap (80–85% of India's requirement is imported) → remelt → secondary alloy ingots for die-casters [V-snip MRAI].
6. *(Reference) Primary aluminium (VAML, Hindalco, NALCO):* bauxite → Bayer refinery (alumina, ~1.9–2.0 t per t Al) → Hall-Héroult smelter (~14–15 MWh/t) → billets, wire rod, flat [K].

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **Mine with a by-product credit** (HZL): ~50–62% EBITDA margins at today's prices [V screener HZL OPM 46–61% over 13 quarters].
- **Integrated smelter with captive power and alumina** (VAML): ~48–50% margin in Q1 FY27 at LME ~$3,000+/t [V screener; V-snip].
- **Custom copper refiner without a mine** (Silvassa): ~0% margin in FY26 [V-sib].
- **Recycler:** 4–10% margin on revenue, but ROCE of 16–26% from asset turns of 2.5–3.5× [V screener: Gravita ROCE 16%, Jain 25.7%].
- **The informal recycler** captures value by avoiding GST, pollution-control and labour costs. This is the arbitrage EPR is meant to close.

### 5.3 Imports, China and the global scrap trade

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Battery Waste Management Rules (BWMR) 2022 and amendments [V-snip impriindia/IEA/indiaenvironmentportal]
- **EPR obligation:** producers (battery makers and importers) must ensure collection and recycling/refurbishment of waste batteries equal to a set % of batteries placed on the market in prior years, keyed to battery life. They comply via in-house recycling or by **buying EPR certificates from registered recyclers** on the CPCB portal.
- **Minimum material-recovery targets:**
  - automotive/industrial (lead-acid) **50% → 60% by 2026-27**;
  - EV and portable **70% → 90% by 2026-27**.
- **2024 amendment:** CPCB fixes an **EPR-certificate price band: ceiling 100%, floor 30% of the Environmental Compensation (EC)** for non-fulfilment. This puts a floor under recyclers' certificate revenue.
- **Second amendment (June 2024): minimum recycled content in new batteries from FY2027-28**, rising to FY2030-31+. This is structurally positive for organised secondary lead.
- **EC** is calculated on collection/transport/processing cost. Paying EC does not discharge the obligation; the money goes into an escrow for collecting uncollected batteries.
- A specific 2025 amendment text was **not** found (FLAG: verify on the MoEFCC gazette).
- Gravita's/Jain's EPR-certificate revenue is not disclosed in the sources found (FLAG).

### 7.2 E-waste and other EPR regimes [K]
- E-Waste (Management) Rules 2022 (EPR for electronics, with recovery targets for copper, aluminium, gold, etc.).
- Plastic Waste Management Rules (EPR for packaging). Relevant to Gravita's plastics.
- Tyre EPR, notified 2022 under the Hazardous & Other Wastes Rules. Relevant to Gravita's rubber.
- The **Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management & Transboundary Movement) Rules 2016** govern lead-scrap imports (permissions needed) [K].

### 7.3 Scrap policy and customs duties
- **Copper scrap BCD cut to nil** in Budget 2025-26 (1-Feb-2025). FY26 imports +35% [V-sib].
- **Aluminium scrap BCD 2.5%**; primary aluminium 7.5%; downstream products 7.5–10%. Budget 2026-27 **rejected industry's ask for a flat 15%** on aluminium including scrap [V-snip alcircle]. MRAI asked the PMO in **Jul-2026** to remove the 2.5% scrap duty: aluminium scrap is "the only major non-ferrous scrap still dutiable" [V-snip knnindia].
- **Budget 2026-27:** exempted capital goods for critical-mineral processing; no change to copper metal duties [V-sib].
- **National Non-Ferrous Metal Scrap Recycling Framework** (Ministry of Mines, 2020): voluntary framework for registered recycling and scrap standards [K]. **No statutory successor found** (FLAG).
- **Steel Scrap Recycling Policy 2019** (ferrous; context) [K].
- **BIS QCOs:** a QCO keeps uncertified foreign **copper cathode** out [V-sib]. **No notified QCO on aluminium scrap** was found; AAI is lobbying for one [V-snip]. Aluminium product QCOs exist for some grades [K; FLAG].

### 7.4 Critical minerals and recycling incentives [V-sib copper]
- **National Critical Mineral Mission (NCMM)** — ₹16,300 Cr (plus PSU spending; launched Jan-2025 [K]).
- **₹1,500 Cr critical-mineral recycling incentive scheme** (guidelines **2-Oct-2025**) for capacity to recover critical minerals from e-waste, Li-ion batteries and other scrap. Capex and opex subsidies, with large and small/new recycler tranches [K details; FLAG].
- Relevant to Gravita's lithium-ion plans (capex earmarked within ₹1,680 Cr) [V-snip] and to Jain's antimony project (1,000 t lead-antimony bullion, Q3 FY27) [V-snip]. **Antimony is on India's critical-mineral list** [K].

### 7.5 Mining regulation (VEDL/HZL)
- MMDR Act: royalty on zinc/lead (% of LME), DMF (30% of royalty for pre-2015 leases) and NMET (2%) [K].
- The **Supreme Court's 25-Jul-2024 judgment** allowed states to tax mineral rights/land, with retrospective arrears from 2005 payable over 12 years. A state-specific levy risk remains [K; FLAG — Rajasthan levy status not checked].
- HZL lease tenure: leases extended to 2030 under MMDR; renewal by auction thereafter (check) [K; FLAG].

### 7.6 Trade: US Section 232 and others
- **Section 232 aluminium: 50% tariff** (raised from 25% in June 2025) on aluminium and listed derivatives [K]. **Copper: 50% on semi-finished products and derivatives from 1-Aug-2025; refined cathode exempt**, with a possible duty from 2027 (15%) / 2028 (30%) undecided [V-sib].
  - Exposure: VAML's US exports (reference); RMIL/Gravita copper-brass exports (~40% of RMIL revenue, destinations not known).
  - India's general US tariff since 24-Jul-2026: 10% Sec 301 on top of MFN (under challenge) [V-sib commodity-chem].
- **EU CBAM** definitive phase from 1-Jan-2026 covers aluminium (and iron/steel). Embedded-emissions certificates hit coal-powered Indian primary aluminium exports to the EU (VAML, Hindalco). Recycled aluminium has far lower embedded emissions [K; V-sib iron-steel].

### 7.7 Corporate/governance rules relevant to Vedanta
- SEBI LODR **related-party transaction** approval thresholds (material RPTs need shareholder approval; brand fees qualify) [K].
- HZL's **₹1,060 Cr brand-fee RPT** to Vedanta was in the headlines in 2026 [primer HZL].

## 11. Technology and disruption risks
1. **Lithium-ion displacing lead-acid.** Two-wheeler EVs, telecom tower and inverter storage migrate to Li-ion (LFP). Over a 10-year horizon this shrinks the future lead-scrap pool. Starting batteries in ICE vehicles and VRLA UPS remain lead-acid near term [K]. Gravita's Li-ion recycling plan and the ₹1,500 Cr scheme are the hedge.
2. **Black-mass/hydrometallurgy for Li-ion** is a different technology from pyrometallurgical lead smelting. Incumbents (Attero, Lohum) and battery OEMs' in-house loops compete [K].
3. **Pollution technology.** Lead smelting emissions (SO₂, lead dust), slag disposal, ETP. Tightening CPCB norms raise the cost floor for everyone and favour organised players [K].
4. **Smelter decarbonisation.** Coal-captive power is VAML/HZL's cost edge and its CBAM/ESG liability. HZL's renewable PPAs (Serentica), and "EcoZen" low-carbon zinc [K].
5. **Copper substitution by aluminium** in cables, at Cu $14k+/t vs Al $3.2k/t: reduces copper demand at the margin, raises aluminium-scrap demand [V-sib cables].
6. **Sensor-based scrap sorting (XRF/LIBS)** lets organised recyclers upgrade low-grade mixed scrap. This is a scale advantage [K].
7. **Silver thrifting in PV paste** — a long-run risk to HZL's silver credit [K].

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Miner (HZL inside VEDL):**
EBITDA = Refined zinc t × (LME Zn + premium − CoP ex-royalty − royalty) + Lead t × (LME Pb − cost) + Silver oz × (silver price − incremental cost) [+ other].
VEDL attributable = 60.71% × HZL earnings + 100% × (ZI + copper + FACOR + ports) − holdco interest − brand fee paid to VRL (if any) [K structure].

**Recycler:**
EBITDA = Σ_metal (tonnes sold × EBITDA/t) + EPR-certificate income + turnkey margin.
Revenue = Σ tonnes × (LME × FX + premium). Revenue is almost irrelevant to profit.
ROCE ≈ (EBITDA − D&A) ÷ (fixed assets + working capital); working capital ≈ revenue × WC days ÷ 365.

### C.1 Worked example 1 — one quarter of HZL zinc + silver (Q2 FY27) [E composite; FLAG]
- Refined metal 264 kt. Assume zinc ~215 kt, lead ~49 kt.
- LME zinc ~$3,700/t (mid-Oct settle $3,724 [V-snip]).
- Zinc CoP ex-royalty **$703/t** [V-snip]; royalty ~10% of LME → $370/t [E].
- **Zinc margin ≈ $3,700 − $703 − $370 = $2,627/t** → × 215 kt = **$565 m ≈ ₹5,360 Cr** [E at ₹94.9].
- Silver 173 t = 5.56 m oz. At ~$50/oz realised [E] → $278 m revenue ≈ ₹2,640 Cr. Near-zero incremental cost (the metal's cost is carried by zinc/lead) → most of it is EBITDA, after silver royalty (~7–8%).
- Lead ~49 kt × (~$1,900 − ~$1,000 cost) ≈ ₹420 Cr [E].
- **Sum ≈ ₹5,360 + ~₹2,400 (silver after royalty) + ₹420 ≈ ₹8,200 Cr, plus other/by-products (sulphuric acid, power) ≈ the reported ₹9,285 Cr** [V-snip].
