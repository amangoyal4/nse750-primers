**Units, conventions and FX**
- ₹ crore (Cr) = 10 million. ₹ lakh crore = ₹1 trillion.
- **mt = million tonnes.** Coal India and the Ministry of Coal write "MT" for million tonnes; we normalise to "mt". **"lakh t" = 0.1 mt** (GMDC reports in lakh MT, so 78.84 lakh t = 7.884 mt). mtpa = million tonnes per annum.
- **mcum / M.CuM = million cubic metres**, used for overburden removal (OBR). The stripping ratio is quoted as **cubic metres of OB per tonne of coal (m³/t)**.
- GCV = gross calorific value, kcal/kg. Indian non-coking coal runs ~3,000–5,000 kcal/kg (grades G1–G17). Indonesian 4,200 GAR is the usual import comparator. Newcastle 6,000 NAR is the global benchmark. Lignite is ~2,200–3,500 kcal/kg [K].

## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Production ≠ offtake ≠ sales, and the gap is the inventory cycle.**
   - CIL FY26: production **768.19 mt**, offtake (raw coal) **744.83 mt**, sales quantity (billing) **743.60 mt** [V AR; Q4 PPT].
   - Pithead stock rose from **107.16 mt (1-Apr-25) to 129.96 mt (31-Mar-26)** [V Q4 PPT].
   - Q1 FY27 reversed this: production **169.63 mt (−7%)**, offtake **197.86 mt (+4%)**, and stock fell to **101.35 mt** by 30-Jun-26 [V Q1 PPT]. By end-September ~63 mt had been liquidated in H1 [V press release], so pithead stock was **~67 mt** [E].
   - **Revenue follows offtake, not production.** A "production down" headline in Q1/Q2 FY27 came with revenue up.
2. **Three different prices, and two different "realisations".**
   - **(a) Notified price**: the grade-wise list price CIL sets, the base for FSA billing and for the e-auction floor.
   - **(b) FSA realisation**: what long-term linkage buyers actually pay. It is lower than the notified price for some grades and includes quality adjustments.
   - **(c) E-auction price**: market-cleared, quoted as **"% increase over notified price"**.
   - CIL reports realisation two ways: **"incl. other charges"** (royalty-linked levies, evacuation/sizing charges etc.) and **"basic" (excl. other charges)**.
   - FY26 FSA: **₹2,083/t incl.** vs **₹1,444/t basic**. E-auction: **₹2,962/t incl.** vs **₹2,260/t basic** [V Q4 PPT].
   - The primer's "FSA ₹2,138/t" is the **Q4 incl.-charges** figure, and its "e-auction ₹2,356/t" matches neither basis.
   - The **company-stated e-auction premium (FY26: 46%, FY25: 48%, FY24: 72%)** [V ARs] is measured against the notified price. It is **not** the gap between the e-auction and FSA realisations (FY26: 42% incl. / 57% basic) [E].
3. **"Revenue" includes ~₹48,000 Cr of levies.**
   - CIL's sale of products (₹1,65,186 Cr FY26) is "inclusive of commercial recoveries (transportation, loading, sizing, royalty, environmental cess, NMET, DMF and other levies) and net of GST" [V AR MD&A].
   - Sale of coal **excluding other charges** was **₹1,16,880 Cr**. The ~₹48,306 Cr difference (~₹650/t) is largely pass-through [V Q4 PPT; E].
   - Margins on reported revenue therefore understate margins on "own" revenue. EBITDA/t is the cleaner KPI.
4. **Three EBITDA numbers for the same year.**
   - **(a) Screener "operating profit"**: FY26 **₹37,172 Cr (22%)**.
   - **(b) Company/MD&A-style EBITDA excluding other income**: PBT 41,923 − JV share 758 + finance cost 1,216 + D&A 10,137 − other income 11,276 = **₹41,242 Cr (24.5%)** [E from V numbers]. This is the primer's figure.
   - The (a)–(b) gap is exactly the **stripping-activity adjustment (₹4,071 Cr)**. Screener nets it differently.
   - **(c) Investor-presentation "EBITDA"**: PBT + finance + D&A, so it **includes other income and the JV share**. Q1 FY27 was ₹14,349 Cr (31%); on that basis FY26 ≈ ₹53,276 Cr [V Q1 PPT; E].
   - **Use (a) in peer tables. Quote (b) for EBITDA/t and say so. Never compare (c) with peers.**
5. **Standalone CIL is a holding company; consolidated CIL is the business.**
   - Standalone CIL earns subsidiary dividends. Q1 FY27 standalone PBT excluding dividends was only ₹211 Cr [V Q1 PPT].
   - All production, cost and margin analysis must use **consolidated** numbers, or the eight-subsidiary table (§2.1).
   - Since Jan/Mar-2026 consolidated PAT includes small minority interests: BCCL 10%, CMPDI 15%.
6. **mt vs MT vs "MTPA" in GMDC's annual report.**
   - GMDC's FY26 board report prints mine production in a column headed "(MTPA)": Mata-no-Madh 32.7, Bhavnagar 25.5, Umarsar 15.2, Tadkeshwar 5.4. These are **lakh tonnes** — they sum to 78.8 lakh t = 7.88 mt, matching CARE's "7.88 MMT sold in FY26" [V].
   - The AR's "Mining Sales 8.34 MT" covers **all minerals**, including bauxite (4.87 lakh t) and silica.
   - **Lignite despatch FY26 = 78.84 lakh t = 7.884 mt** [V AR MD&A].
7. **Lignite is not coal.**
   - Lignite GCV is ~2,200–3,500 kcal/kg [K], vs ~3,500–4,500 for typical CIL G10–G13 coal and ~4,200 for Indonesian GAR.
   - It is high-moisture and cannot be railed economically, so it is a **pithead or regional fuel**.
   - Realisation per tonne is not comparable with CIL. Compare **₹ per million kcal** [E]:
     - GMDC FY26 ~₹2,967/t ÷ ~2,800 kcal ≈ **₹1.06 per 1,000 kcal**.
     - CIL FSA basic ₹1,444/t ÷ ~3,800 kcal ≈ **₹0.38 per 1,000 kcal**.
     - CIL e-auction basic ₹2,260/t ≈ ₹0.59.
     - Imported Indonesian 4,200 GAR at ~$60–70/t CFR ≈ ₹1.4–1.6 per 1,000 kcal.
   - GMDC prices against **imported coal and gas parity** in Gujarat, not against CIL's FSA price.
8. **GMDC has three revenue bases and two profit bases.**
   - Revenue:
     - Standalone revenue from operations **₹2,653.38 Cr** (FY25 ₹2,850.84 Cr) [V AR].
     - Screener consolidated "sales" **₹2,523 Cr** (FY25 ₹2,534 Cr) [V screener]. Screener seems to exclude some other operating revenue — **conflict, flag it**.
     - AR headline **"Revenue ₹3,077 Cr"** (most likely total income including other income [E]).
   - Profit:
     - Standalone PAT ₹990.81 Cr; consolidated PAT ₹957 Cr.
     - Both include a GST input-tax-credit exceptional of **₹522.65 Cr** (press release; CARE says ₹523 Cr) or **₹474.43 Cr** (AR operations section).
     - The two exceptional figures conflict: the larger may include interest/related items. Use **₹523 Cr** when quoting the P&L effect [V].
   - The primer's FY26 revenue ₹2,653 Cr is standalone, labelled "consol.".
9. **Net cash: screener gets both members wrong.**
   - **CIL**: screener shows borrowings ₹14,072 Cr vs investments ₹10,226 Cr, i.e. **net debt ₹3,846 Cr**. That is wrong. The consolidated AR shows **cash ₹8,620 Cr + other bank balances ₹43,955 Cr + current investments ₹4,187 Cr = ₹56,762 Cr** liquid, against borrowings of ₹13,821 Cr, so **net cash ≈ ₹42,941 Cr (Mar-26)** [V AR; E]. Part of "other bank balances" is earmarked (mine-closure escrow etc.); treat ₹40–43k Cr as the range.
   - **GMDC**: screener's ₹464 Cr of investments vs ₹317 Cr borrowings understates cash. CARE: "**unencumbered cash and liquid investments of over ₹1,700 crore** as on 31-Mar-2026", much of it parked with Gujarat State Financial Services at ~7.25%. Net cash ≈ **₹1,400 Cr** [V CARE; E].
10. **"Captive", "commercial", "captive & others" and "non-CIL" are different sets.**
    - MoC reports "captive & commercial" production: FY26 **210.46 mt** [V PIB].
    - The MoC Annual Report table uses "Captive & Others": FY25 197.46 mt [V].
    - SCCL is a separate state-owned company: Telangana 51.1%, GoI 48.9% [V SCCL report].
    - NLC's Talabira and Pachwara coal is in "captive & commercial".
    - **India's FY26 total ≈ CIL 768.19 + SCCL 58.00 + captive/commercial 210.46 = ~1,036.7 mt** [E]. The ministry's own FY26 total release was not found. FY25 was **1,047.52 mt** [V MoC AR].

## PART 1 — INDUSTRY DOSSIER

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **India digs ~1.04 bn tonnes a year and still imports ~0.25 bn. Coal India digs three-quarters and sets the price everyone else is measured against.**
  - FY25 national production **1,047.52 mt**: CIL 781.06, SCCL 69.01, captive & others 197.46 [V MoC AR 2025-26].
  - FY26 **~1,037 mt (−1%)** [E]: CIL 768.19 (−1.6%), SCCL 58.00 (**−15.9%**), captive/commercial 210.46 (**+10.2%**) [V AR, SCCL, PIB].
  - **CIL's share of national output: 77.5% (FY24) → 74.6% (FY25) → ~74.1% (FY26)** [E]. Captive/commercial: 15.5% → 18.9% → ~20.3% [E].
  - Non-coking imports FY26 **160 mt (−5.4%)**; Indonesia 94.6 mt, South Africa 33.7, US 13.7 [V-sec BigMint, 14-Apr-2026]. Coking coal imports ~55–60 mt/yr [K].
- **FY26 was a demand air-pocket. FY27 turned into a squeeze.**
  - **FY26**: coal-fired generation **−3% to 1.28 trillion kWh**; power consumption only +1%; renewables +21%; hydro +13% (above-normal monsoon) [V-sec BigMint].
  - CIL pithead stocks hit a record **~130 mt**; total system stockpiles ~224 mt in early April [V PPT; V-sec BigMint].
  - E-auction premium slid to **46%** (FY25 48%, FY24 72%) [V ARs]. CIL PAT −12% to ₹31,071 Cr [V].
  - **FY27 so far**: a strong El Niño (weak hydro, heat) plus **gas-to-coal switching after the Hormuz LNG cut** [V-sec IEA via OilPrice].
    - September-2026 coal-fired generation **+13.3% YoY**; total generation +11.3%; shortages **560 MU**, the highest since Aug-2023 [V-sec Reuters/Grid-India via OilPrice].
    - Stocks at domestic coal plants fell to **~18.2 mt (10-Oct-2026)**, with **~40% of plants below the 25% "critical" norm** [V-sec Devdiscourse; OilPrice].
    - CIL's **Q2 offtake +12.0%** and the September e-auction premium **94%** [V CIL filings].
    - IEA (Coal Mid-Year Update 2026): Indian coal demand **+4.2% to 1.353 bn t in 2026** [V-sec].
- **The profit lever is price mix, not tonnes.**
  - CIL FY26 basic (ex-levy) sale of coal **₹1,16,880 Cr**: FSA 646 mt × ₹1,444/t = **~₹93,250 Cr (80%)**; e-auction 83.8 mt × ₹2,260/t = **~₹18,940 Cr (16%)**; washed/other 13.8 mt × ₹3,401/t = ~₹4,690 Cr (4%) [V Q4 PPT; E].
  - E-auction is ~11% of volume but ~16% of revenue, and a far larger share of incremental margin.
  - Each 10-point move in the e-auction premium on ~85–100 mt ≈ **₹1,300–1,600 Cr of EBITDA** (~4% of FY26 EBITDA) [E: 10% × ~₹1,550/t notified-equivalent × 85–100 mt].
- **Costs are a fixed-wage machine slowly turning into an outsourcing machine.**
  - FY26 employee cost **₹46,425 Cr = 27.6% of revenue, ~₹604 per tonne produced**. It includes a **one-time ₹1,458 Cr executive pay-scale upgrade** [V Q4 PPT].
  - Manpower **2,12,066 (Mar-26)** vs 3,10,016 a decade earlier [V AR 10-year table].
  - Contractors now remove **89% of overburden and mine 69% of coal** (Q1 FY27) [V Q1 PPT].
  - **NCWA-XI expired 30-Jun-2026.** The JBCCI-12 negotiating committee had **not been constituted by 20-Sep-2026** [V-sec IndianPSU]. Wage-revision provisioning for ~2.4 lakh non-executives is the next earnings overhang.
- **Capital allocation is "dividend first, diversify second".**
  - FY26 DPS **₹26.50** (payout ~53%); **₹10,272 Cr paid to GoI** [V AR].
  - FY26 capex **₹19,607 Cr** (target ₹16,000 Cr); FY27 budget ₹16,500 Cr [V AR].
  - Net cash ~₹43k Cr [E]. Diversification bets: solar (first ₹5.68 Cr of energy sales in Q1 FY27), coal-to-ammonium-nitrate gasification (BCGCL, ₹25,000 Cr), critical minerals, pithead power [V].
- **GMDC is a different animal.**
  - A Gujarat-government regional **merchant lignite** seller: 7.88 mt FY26 to ~2,880 MSME customers in textiles, ceramics, bricks and chemicals [V AR; CARE].
  - Prices are reset every 15 days against imported-coal/gas parity [V CARE], so it is a direct **beneficiary of the West Asia shock**: prices +50–60% by mid-Sep-2026 [V CARE].
  - Optionality: three Odisha coal blocks (Baitarani West, 15 mtpa peak, COD targeted Q4 FY27) and an Ambadungar rare-earth project (BARC tech transfer Jan-2026; IREL MoU approved Jul-2026) [V CARE; AR].
  - **Its stock has halved from the ₹771.90 all-time high to ₹419** [V AR; screener].
- **Valuation (23-Oct-2026)**:
  - CIL **7.8× P/E, 6.7% yield, ~5× EV/EBITDA** on company net cash [E].
  - GMDC **23× P/E** (reported, incl. one-off), ~27× EV/EBITDA FY26 [E].
  - Two-member aggregate m-cap **₹2,57,262 Cr**; aggregate P/E **8.0×** [E; §3.2].
  - Global coal miners: China Shenhua 12.6× P/E / 11.4× EV/EBITDA; AlamTri 6.9× / 5.0×; Glencore 15.8× / 7.9× [V-sec stockanalysis]. **CIL is the cheapest large coal miner on earnings, and the only one with net cash ≈ 18% of market cap.**

### 1.2 Value chain (inputs → customer)

1. **Exploration and planning**
   - GSI (regional), CMPDI (detailed drilling, mine plans; CIL 85%), MECL.
   - India's geological coal resources **4,00,715 mt** (1-Apr-2025): Odisha 1,00,976; Jharkhand 93,254; Chhattisgarh 85,263; West Bengal 34,386 [V MoC AR].
   - **Coking coal only 37,373 mt** (9%) [V].
   - Lignite resources **47,371 mt**: Tamil Nadu 37,524; Rajasthan 6,661; **Gujarat 2,722** [V].
2. **Mine development**
   - Land acquisition (Coal Bearing Areas Act / LARR), environmental clearance (EC), Stage-I/II forest clearance (FC), R&R.
   - CIL needs 12 Stage-II FCs covering ~2,800 ha and ~4,172 ha of land for FY27 targets [V AR].
   - **Opencast is 96.7% of CIL production** (742.6 of 768.2 mt FY26) [V AR].
3. **Extraction**
   - Overburden (OB) removal by shovel-dumper or dragline, then coal winning; surface miners at large OC mines.
   - Increasingly by contractors: OB hiring, "MDO" (mine developer-cum-operator), revenue-sharing MDO for discontinued mines (BCCL gets 9% of revenue at ASGKCC) [V Q1 PPT].
4. **Evacuation**
   - Road (Q1 FY27 ~62 mt of 198 mt offtake) is being replaced by **First Mile Connectivity (FMC)**: conveyor, silo and rapid loading.
   - Nationally **139 FMC projects, ~₹45,000 Cr, 1,319 mtpa**; 65 commissioned (552 mtpa) [V MoC AR].
   - CIL: 72 projects, 843 mtpa; 432 mtpa operational; **994 mtpa mechanised by FY29** [V CIL AR].
   - Rail rakes are the bottleneck in a squeeze: 426/day in Sep-26 → 469/day in early Oct-26 [V-sec].
5. **Sale**
   - (a) FSA/linkage under the SHAKTI policy (revised May-2025: **Window I** at notified price for central/state gencos; **Window II** auction at a premium for anyone, including imported-coal plants, tenure up to 25 years, no PPA needed) [V MoC AR].
   - (b) NRS linkage auctions (steel, cement, sponge iron, "Coal SETU" window for any industrial use plus export, approved 12-Dec-2025) [V].
   - (c) Single-Window Mode-Agnostic (SWMA) e-auction [V].
   - (d) Captive end-use.
   - (e) Commercial-block sale at market.
6. **Consumers**
   - CIL dispatch Apr–Dec FY26: **power utilities 434.1 mt (79.5%)**, captive power 43.3, others 56.0, cement 5.5, sponge iron 4.3, steel 2.6 [V MoC AR].
   - SCCL: 41.2 of 44.2 mt to utilities [V].
7. **Imports**
   - Thermal: coastal imported-coal plants (Adani Mundra, Tata Mundra, JSW, Essar), blending at inland plants, and Gujarat/Maharashtra industry.
   - Metallurgical: steel (JSW, Tata, SAIL, AM/NS).

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **Rent sits with the lowest-cost opencast mines in Odisha, MP and Chhattisgarh.**
  - FY26 PAT/t of offtake [E from V AR table]: NCL **₹777/t** (Singrauli, MP/UP); MCL **₹506/t** (Talcher/IB Valley, Odisha); SECL ₹266/t; CCL ₹389/t; WCL ₹266/t; BCCL ₹39/t; ECL **−₹200/t** (underground-heavy, Raniganj).
  - **NCL and MCL earned ₹21,355 Cr of PAT** — about 69% of group PAT — on 47% of offtake [E].
- **The state captures roughly half of gross revenue.**
  - CIL paid **₹52,299 Cr** to the exchequer in FY26 (royalty, DMF, NMET, GST, cess and other levies) [V AR], plus **₹10,272 Cr** of dividend to GoI [V AR].
  - Royalty is **14% ad valorem** on run-of-mine price; DMF is 30% of royalty; NMET is 2% of royalty [K].
  - The GST compensation cess (₹400/t) was removed and GST on coal and lignite moved **5% → 18% from 22-Sep-2025** [V GMDC AR; CIL AR "GST rationalisation unlocked ₹5,985 Cr of input tax credit"]. This is broadly revenue-neutral to buyers who can take ITC, and a working-capital gain for miners.
  - Jharkhand's **Mineral-Bearing Land Cess rose from ₹250 to ₹450/t**. This is a follow-on to the Supreme Court's July-2024 ruling (*Mineral Area Development Authority v. SAIL*, 9-judge bench, 8:1) that states may tax mineral-bearing land and that royalty is not a tax [K]. It cost CIL **+₹3,635 Cr in rates & taxes in FY26** [V Q4 PPT].
- **Railways capture freight.** Coal is Indian Railways' largest freight commodity (~45–50% of loading) [K]. Rail freight often equals or exceeds the pithead price for distant plants [K]. That is why landed cost — not pithead price — decides imported-vs-domestic.
- **Merchant lignite (GMDC) captures an energy-parity premium.** It sells at ₹2,900–4,300/t (FY22–FY26 average) [E from V AR], close to imported-coal parity per kcal for nearby MSMEs. This is why GMDC's realisation tracks Indonesian coal and LNG, not CIL's notified price.

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Electricity is ~80% of CIL's volumes.**
   - Coal + lignite supplied **69.54% of electricity in Apr–Jun 2026** [V-sec MoP via IndianPSU]. In Sep-2026 the coal-fired share was **66.03%** [V-sec].
   - Coal-fired generation FY26 **1.28 trillion kWh (−3%)** [V-sec BigMint].
   - Installed coal capacity **2,24,158 MW** (30-Jun-2026) of 5,48,858 MW total; non-fossil now 54.18% of capacity [V-sec KNN/ANI, Rajya Sabha reply 4-Aug-2026].
   - Coal + lignite ~230.8 GW [V-sec].
   - **In non-solar peak hours thermal still supplies ~75%** (188.8 of 251.4 GW) [V-sec].
2. **Weather sets the year.**
   - FY26: above-normal monsoon, hydro +13%, RE +21%, demand +1%, coal generation −3% [V-sec BigMint].
   - FY27: strong El Niño, weak hydro, September generation +11.3% and coal +13.3% [V-sec Reuters/Grid-India].
   - One CEA rule of thumb [E]: each 1% of national generation ≈ 18–19 bn kWh ≈ **~12–13 mt of coal** at ~0.68 kg/kWh.
3. **Fuel switching.**
   - Gas is <4% of generation [V-snip Argus], so the LNG cut matters for industrial heat and city-gas-substitution more than for power.
   - Gas-to-coal switching across Asia lifted 2026 demand [V-sec IEA]. Induction-cooking shift estimates (13–27 GW of extra load) appeared during the April-2026 Hormuz blockade [V-snip BusinessToday].
4. **Non-power (NRS)**
   - Sponge iron output 60 mt (+8%) [V-sec BigMint]; cement; captive power for aluminium (Hindalco, Vedanta, NALCO); MSME boilers; brick kilns.
   - CIL NRS supplies were +19.4% in Sep-2026 [V press release].
   - NRS is where the e-auction premium is set.
5. **Steel → coking coal**: ~300 mtpa steel-capacity ambition by 2030 (National Steel Policy) [K]. Domestic washed coking coal is a tiny share, so imports rise.
6. **Coal gasification (new demand)**
   - **National target 100 mt by 2030** [K, MoC Mission].
   - The Cabinet in May-2026 approved a **₹37,500 Cr surface coal/lignite gasification scheme** targeting ~75 mt: incentives up to 20% of plant and machinery, linkage tenure up to 30 years [V GMDC AR FY26 MD&A]. *Verify the outlay against PIB; the source is GMDC's AR.*
   - CIL–BHEL **BCGCL coal-to-ammonium-nitrate** project, **₹25,000 Cr, 6.6 lakh t/yr AN**, foundation stone **20-Jun-2026** [V CIL Q1 PPT].
7. **Exports**: the Coal SETU window (Dec-2025) allows linkage coal for export [V MoC AR]. Volumes are trivial today.

**Demand outlook numbers**
- IEA: India 2026 **1.353 bn t (+4.2%)** [V-sec].
- MoC/CEA planning: thermal capacity requirement **~3,15,000 MW by 2035-36**. 21,080 MW was commissioned Apr-2023–Jun-2026; **47,545 MW is under construction plus 16,000 MW awarded (~63.5 GW pipeline)** [V-sec KNN/ANI, Aug-2026].
  - A March-2026 government statement cited ~307 GW by 2034-35 and +97 GW of coal/lignite planned [V-snip]. Use the August figures.
- The old **"1.5 bn t by 2029-30"** national target is MoC rhetoric from 2023–24 [K]. **No 2026 restatement was found — FLAG.** FY26 output of ~1.04 bn t makes 1.5 bn implausible before 2032 [E].
- Thermal capacity added: FY26 9,470 MW; FY27 to mid-Jul 2,260 MW [V-sec IndianPSU].

**Key economic differences**
- **CIL earns a rent on geology** (Odisha/MP opencast at ~₹600–900/t cash cost [E]) and **gives part of it back by policy** (FSA prices below import parity).
- **GMDC earns a rent on geography**: there is no rail-linked black coal nearby for Gujarat MSMEs, and gas is expensive. Its price is set by the alternatives, but its volume by mine approvals.
- **Commercial miners compete on the e-auction price**, so an e-auction premium collapse hits them first. A spike (Sep-2026) rewards them first.

- **Ministry of Coal** (policy, allocation, CIL/SCCL/NLC administration); **Coal Controller's Organisation** (grading, statistics, mine-opening — prior CCO permission dispensed with by a 2025 amendment to the Colliery Control Rules; company boards now approve mine/seam openings [V MoC AR]).

- **Acts**
  - **Coal Mines (Nationalisation) Act 1973** — monopoly. Eased by the CMSP Act 2015 (after the SC cancelled 204 blocks in 2014) and the **MMDR Amendment 2020/2021**, which opened commercial mining with no end-use restriction and 100% FDI on the automatic route [K; V-sec].
  - **Coal Bearing Areas (Acquisition & Development) Act 1957**.
  - **MMDR Act** for lignite and minerals (GMDC's leases).
  - **Mines Act 1952**.
- **Pricing**
  - CIL fixes notified prices. The government is consulted (de facto approval); historically revisions come every 2–4 years [K].
  - **FY26 FSA billing rose ~4%** on grade/mix while basic FSA price per tonne was +0.8% FY26 and −2% in Q1 FY27 [V PPTs].
  - BCCL raised washed coking prices 0.24% (WPI-linked) from 1-Apr-2026 [V-snip] and raw coking coal 0.1–14% on 13-Oct-2026 [V screener].
  - **No CIL-wide non-coking notified price hike was found in 2026 — FLAG.**

### 7.2 Linkage and sale policy
- **Revised SHAKTI (CCEA 7-May-2025)**:
  - **Window I** (notified price, central/state gencos; 55.2 mt earmarked).
  - **Window II** (premium auction, open to all including imported-coal plants, up to 25 years, no PPA). CIL's first Window-II half-yearly auction began 27-Jan-2026 [V MoC AR].
- **NRS linkage auctions** (tranche-based). **Coal SETU window** (CCEA 12-Dec-2025; guidelines 19-Dec-2025): linkage for any industrial use **and export**, no domestic resale [V].
- **SWMA e-auction** (single window, mode agnostic): CIL offered **142.2 mt and allocated 57.1 mt (40%) in H1 FY27** at a **54% premium**. Sep-2026: 13.0 mt offered, 9.4 mt allocated (72%), **94% premium** [V CIL SWMA].
- **Coal exchange**: draft rules in Dec-2025 for a regulated coal exchange [V GMDC AR MD&A]. It would replace part of the e-auction with screen-based trading. **Status of final rules: FLAG.**
- **Blending**: MoP advisories (2022–24) required 4–10% imported-coal blending by domestic-coal plants; Section 11 kept imported-coal plants running in FY23–FY25 [K]. **No 2026 blending/Section 11 directive was verified — FLAG.**
- **Power-sector measures in the 2026 squeeze**: maintenance of ~10 GW of coal plants deferred by three months [V-snip, via search summary of OilPrice/Reuters]. Plants with critical stock get priority rakes [V-sec].

### 7.3 Fiscal
- Royalty on coal is **14% ad valorem** (since 2012) [K]. DMF is 30% of royalty for pre-2015 leases (10% for auctioned) [K]. NMET is 2% [K].
- **GST**: coal and lignite moved from **5% + ₹400/t compensation cess** to **18% with no cess, effective 22-Sep-2025** (GST 2.0) [V GMDC AR; CIL AR mentions ITC unlock].
  - This ends the inverted duty structure (input GST at 18% vs output 5%).
  - CIL "unlocked" ₹5,985 Cr of ITC. GMDC booked a ₹474–523 Cr exceptional [V].
- **State levies after the Supreme Court's 25-Jul-2024 ruling** (*MADA v. SAIL*, 8:1) [K]: states may tax mineral-bearing land; royalty is not a tax. A follow-up order (14-Aug-2024) allowed recovery of arrears from 1-Apr-2005 in instalments over 12 years from Apr-2026, with no interest/penalty [K].
  - **Jharkhand raised its MBL cess from ₹250 to ₹450/t** and CIL's rates & taxes jumped ₹3,635 Cr in FY26 [V Q4 PPT].
  - Other states (Odisha, Chhattisgarh) may follow. **Arrears exposure is not quantified in what we read — FLAG.**
- **Revenue share on commercial blocks** goes to the states. ~₹47,500 Cr/yr is expected at peak from 147 auctioned mines [V-snip].

### 7.4 Labour
- **NCWA-XI** (non-executives, Jul-2021–Jun-2026): signed 2023, MGB 19% [K]. **Expired 30-Jun-2026.** **JBCCI-12 not constituted by 20-Sep-2026.** Unions held gheraos on 10-Aug-2026, and a Ranchi conference set for 12-Sep was postponed after CIL promised to approach MoC (Apex JCC, 3-Sep-2026) [V-sec IndianPSU, 23-Sep-2026].

- **Executives**: a pay-scale upgradation provision of ₹1,458 Cr was booked in FY26 [V].

### 7.5 Environment, closure, transition
- Mining-plan and mine-closure guidelines for coal and lignite were issued in 2025 [V GMDC AR]. Mine-closure escrow is the reason part of "other bank balances" is earmarked [K].
- **Just-transition**: no binding coal phase-out date. India's NDC targets 50% non-fossil capacity by 2030 — **already exceeded at 54.18% by Jun-2026** — and net-zero by 2070 [V-sec; K].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

- **1971–75 nationalisation**: coking mines (1971–72), non-coking (1973), CIL formed (1975) [K; primer].
- **1993–2000s**: captive mining allowed for power/steel/cement (1993). E-auction introduced (2007). Notified-price regime [K].
- **2010 IPO**: GoI sold 10% at ₹245 — then India's largest IPO [V AR: "IPO price of ₹245"]. CIL has since paid **₹302.25/share in dividends** [V AR].
- **2014 "Coalgate"**: the SC cancelled 204 captive blocks. The CMSP Act 2015 led to re-auction/allotment [K; V MoC AR].
- **2015–19**: the "1 bn t CIL by 2020" target was missed. CIL production rose from 494 mt (FY15) to 607 mt (FY19) [K/V].
- **2020**: commercial coal mining opened (MMDR Amendment, first auction Jun-2020) [K].
- **2021–22**: a Covid rebound plus a weak-import year caused acute plant-stock shortages (Oct-2021 and Apr-2022) [K]. In 2022 the Russia–Ukraine war took Newcastle above $400/t [K]. CIL's e-auction premium went to multi-hundred % (FY23 peaked >300% in some months [K, unverified]). CIL PAT nearly doubled from ₹17,378 Cr (FY22) to ₹31,723 Cr (FY23) [V screener]. Blending mandates and Section 11 followed [K].
- **2023–25 boom**: CIL PAT ₹37,369 Cr in FY24 (record) [V]. National output crossed 1 bn t in FY25 (1,047.5 mt) [V]. Stocks rebuilt.
- **FY26 bust**: demand +1%, coal generation −3%, CIL pithead stock 130 mt, e-auction premium 46%. Then Jharkhand's cess and an executive pay provision: **PAT −12% to ₹31,071 Cr** [V].
- **Capital-market wave 2026**: BCCL IPO (Jan) → CMPDI IPO (Mar) → CIL OFS (May) → NLC OFS (Jun); MCL/SECL IPOs approved in principle [V].
- **FY27 squeeze**: West Asia war (Feb–Apr 2026) → LNG shortfall, Newcastle +40%. El Niño → weak hydro, September generation +11%. Plant stocks fell to ~18 mt and the e-auction premium hit 94% (Sep-2026) [V].

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Renewables + storage eat the incremental demand.**
   - Solar 1,62,152 MW installed (Jun-2026). RE under construction 1,47,720 MW; PSP 15,870 MW/95,220 MWh; BESS 15,754 MW/42,530 MWh under development [V-sec KNN].
   - **Battery-shifted solar into the evening peak is the real threat to coal's ~75% non-solar-peak share** [V-sec].
   - FY26 showed what a good-hydro, high-RE year does to coal: −3% [V-sec].
2. **Flexibilisation**: the CEA's 40% minimum technical load for coal units (2023 regulation) [V-sec] means coal plants cycle more and burn less per MW.
3. **Mine technology** (positive): continuous miners and highwall mining for UG, surface miners, FMC/silos (994 mtpa mechanised loading by FY29), drones/LiDAR OB measurement [V CIL AR]. Outsourcing (MDO) raises productivity but erodes CIL's fixed-cost leverage advantage.
4. **Coal-to-chemicals/gasification**: a hedge (100 mt by 2030 target [K]; ₹37,500 Cr scheme [V GMDC AR]). But first-of-a-kind cost risk (BCGCL ₹25,000 Cr for 0.66 mt AN [V]) is large relative to CIL's ~₹16–20k Cr annual capex.
5. **Carbon pricing**: India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (2023) is intensity-based for industry [K]. The EU CBAM (definitive from 1-Jan-2026) hits Indian steel/aluminium exporters [K] and indirectly coal-intensive captive power.
6. **Rare earths/critical minerals** (GMDC, CIL): a technology-and-partner-dependent pivot. Separation (BARC/IREL) is the bottleneck [V CARE].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert leads with)

1. **Power demand vs RE + hydro, transmitted through inventory.** Coal demand is the residual after renewables, hydro and nuclear. Weather moves that residual ±3–4% a year (FY26 −3%; Sep-2026 +13%). The buffer is stock: CIL pithead 107 → 130 → ~67 mt; plants 42.8 → 18.2 mt. **Stocks, not production, are the leading indicator for CIL's e-auction premium and earnings.**
2. **The two-tier price: policy FSA vs market e-auction.** ~87% of CIL volume sells at an administered price below import parity, so CIL's earnings are an option on market tightness. The e-auction premium swung 72% (FY24) → 46% (FY26) → **94% (Sep-2026)**. Management and MoC decide how much to offer at auction: H1 FY27 offered 142 mt, allocated 57 mt.
3. **Private and captive supply growth erodes CIL's share.** Captive/commercial went from 154 mt (FY24) to 210 mt (FY26) and >228 mt (FY27E), with 147 blocks auctioned and round 16 live. CIL's share fell from 77.5% to ~74%. Commercial miners sell at market and compete directly in the e-auction segment.
4. **Cost inflation from labour, levies and depth.** NCWA-XII is pending; the Jharkhand MBL cess nearly doubled after the 2024 SC ruling; the strip ratio rose 1.9 → 2.6; diesel is up with West Asia. CIL offsets this with outsourcing (89% of OB) and attrition (−4% manpower/yr).
5. **Energy transition and state capital allocation.** India adds ~60+ GW of new coal capacity while RE crosses 50% of capacity. Coal demand likely peaks in the 2030s, not the 2020s [E]. The state uses CIL as a dividend and disinvestment source: ₹10,272 Cr dividend, ₹5,549 Cr OFS and BCCL/CMPDI listings in FY26–27.

## B. Bespoke deep-dive themes (own sections in the report)

### B1. "From glut to squeeze in six months": how FY26's record stocks became FY27's shortage
- FY26: monsoon, hydro +13%, RE +21%, demand +1% → coal generation −3%, CIL pithead 130 mt, e-auction 46%, PAT −12% [V].
- FY27 H1: West Asia (LNG cut, Newcastle +40%), El Niño (weak hydro) → Sep generation +11%, coal +13%, shortages 560 MU, plant stocks 18 mt, 40% of plants critical. CIL liquidates 63 mt; Q2 offtake +12%; Sep e-auction 94% [V].

### B2. "One price for the nation, another for the market": the FSA/e-auction machine
- Volume and price tables FY25–Q1 FY27 (§9.1); notified vs incl.-charges vs basic; Revised SHAKTI Window II and Coal SETU blur the tiers.
- How much premium flows to the P&L: H1 FY27 57 mt at 54% vs FY26 102 mt at 46% [V].
- Sensitivity: ±10 pts of premium ≈ ±₹1,300–1,600 Cr EBITDA [E].

### B3. "Eight companies in a trench coat": CIL's subsidiaries
- MCL and NCL make ~69% of PAT; ECL loses money; BCCL is barely profitable yet listed at ₹13.6k Cr m-cap [V].
- The listing programme (BCCL, CMPDI done; MCL/SECL approved) is value-unlocking but also leaks minority interest from CIL's best assets. If MCL/SECL list 25%, CIL's attributable share of ~₹15.5k Cr of their FY26 PAT falls by ~₹3.9k Cr [E].

### B4. "Gujarat's brown-coal toll booth": GMDC lignite, Odisha coal and the rare-earth option

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Coal India (per year)**

EBITDA = Σ over channels [Q_ch × P_basic,ch] + other operating revenue − (Employee cost [~fixed] + Contractor cost [∝ OBR m³ and coal t] + Materials [∝ t, diesel/AN prices] + Non-levy other expenses + State levies not recovered in price) ± stripping adjustment.

- Channels: FSA (~87% of volume), e-auction (~11%), washed/other (~2%).
- Q_ch is **offtake**, not production. P_basic,ch excludes "other charges", which are mostly recovered pass-through.
- Then PAT = (EBITDA − D&A − finance + other income [interest on ~₹50k Cr deposits] + JV share) × (1 − 26% tax).

| = Sale of products per tonne | 2,221 | V (2,221.43) |
| + Services and other operating revenue | +43 | 3,214 ÷ 743.6 |

**C.2 Sensitivities (FY27, CIL) [E]**
- **E-auction premium**: +10 pts on ~90 mt at a notified-equivalent ~₹1,550/t → **+₹1,400 Cr EBITDA (+3.4%)**.
- **E-auction volume**: +10 mt moved from FSA to e-auction at a ₹800/t basic gap → **+₹800 Cr**.
- **FSA price**: +5% on ~650 mt × ₹1,444 → **+₹4,700 Cr (+11%)**. This is why a notified-price hike is the single biggest lever, and politically the hardest.
- **Volume**: +10 mt offtake × (₹1,572 − variable cost ~₹600) → **+₹970 Cr**.
- **Wages**: a 10% NCWA-XII MGB on non-executive pay (~₹30–32k Cr of the ₹46k Cr employee bill [E]) → **−₹3,000–3,200 Cr/yr**, plus arrears from Jul-2026.
- **Diesel**: +10% → ~−₹250–300 Cr direct (materials), plus contractor price-variation pass-through of ~₹400–600 Cr [E].

**C.3 Worked example — Q2 FY27 e-auction windfall (illustrative) [E]**
- H1 FY27 allotted 57.1 mt at a 54% average premium [V].
- If Q2 e-auction volume ≈ 30 mt at a basic ~₹2,550/t (vs Q2 FY26 ~₹2,150/t [E]), the gain ≈ +₹400/t × 30 mt = **+₹1,200 Cr** YoY.
- Offtake +12% (+20 mt × ~₹970/t contribution) = **+₹1,940 Cr**.
- Against that: diesel/AN costs, cess, and any NCWA provision.
- Q2 FY26 screener OP was ₹6,716 Cr (22%). A clean Q2 FY27 could print ~₹9,000–10,000 Cr OP [E] **if no wage provision is booked**.
