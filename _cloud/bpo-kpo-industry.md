## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Five different "EBITDA"s.**
   - **IKS**: reported EBITDA (FY26 ~34%; Q1 FY27 33.0%, or **35% excluding ~₹20 Cr TruBridge one-time costs**) [primer; V-snip].
   - **Sagility**: **"adjusted EBITDA"** (FY26 25.3%; Q1 FY27 24.0%) excludes ESOP/share-based costs, IPO-related and other one-offs [primer; V-snip]. Statutory EBITDA is lower (gap not quantified in snippets).
   - **Firstsource**: guides and reports **EBIT** (FY26 11.7%; Q1 FY27 12.4%), not EBITDA. Its EBITDA (~15–16% [E]) is not in any snippet. Never put Firstsource's EBIT next to others' EBITDA.
   - **Indegene**: **"adjusted EBITDA"** (FY26 ₹679.3 Cr, 19.4%) [primer]; Q1 FY27 EBITDA ₹179.5 Cr (16.9%) per company-linked summaries vs ₹174.2 Cr (16.4%) per another [V-snip — conflict].
   - **eClerx**: EBITDA (FY26 ₹1,152.6 Cr, ~27% of *total* revenue) [primer]; Q1 FY27 ₹283.1 Cr = 24.2% of total revenue / 24.6% of operating revenue [E].
   - **Rule for the writer:** in peer tables, show each company's own basis with a footnote; in aggregates (Part 3) the "EBITDA" row is a mixed-basis composite and is labelled [E].
2. **Reported vs adjusted PAT — the gaps are large and they go in both directions.**
   - Sagility FY26: reported **₹924.8 Cr** vs adjusted **₹1,130.6 Cr** (gap ≈ intangible amortisation from the HGS carve-out + bolt-ons, ESOP) [V-snip investywise/univest].

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Five listed companies, four archetypes, one common question: what does AI do to a business that bills for people?**
  - **US healthcare operations (IKS, Sagility, + ~34% of Firstsource):** run the admin machine of US providers (IKS — billing, coding, documentation) and payers (Sagility — claims, enrolment, payment integrity). FY26 revenue IKS ₹3,194 Cr + Sagility ₹7,193 Cr = **₹10,387 Cr; adj. EBITDA ~₹2,906 Cr (~28%)** [E: primer basis].
  - **Diversified CX/BPM (Firstsource):** healthcare 34%, banking & financial services (incl. US/UK mortgage, collections) 33%, communications/media/tech 21%, diverse industries 13%; FY26 **₹9,556 Cr, EBIT 11.7%** [primer].
  - **Life-sciences commercialisation (Indegene):** pharma marketing content, omnichannel, medical/regulatory/safety; FY26 **₹3,511 Cr, adj. EBITDA 19.4%** [primer].
  - **High-end KPO (eClerx):** capital-markets operations, digital/content ops, cable & telecom ops; FY26 **₹4,217 Cr (total revenue), EBITDA ~27%** [primer].
  - **All five FY26: revenue ₹27,671 Cr (~$3.13bn at ₹88.3), +23.0% in ₹ on FY25's ₹22,494 Cr; composite EBITDA ~₹6,238 Cr (~22.5%); reported PAT ₹3,428 Cr; adjusted PAT ~₹3,858 Cr; m-cap ~₹1.02 lakh Cr (~$10.7bn at ₹94.9) on latest dated prices** [E: sums in §3.2].
- **Growth is real but flattered by the rupee.** Q1 FY27 aggregate revenue ₹7,815 Cr was **+26% YoY in ₹**, but CC growth was **~12% (Firstsource), ~15% (Sagility), ~12% $ (IKS), ~15% $ (eClerx)**; only Indegene (+39.7% ₹, acquisitions) stands out [V-snip; E]. FY27 guidance converges on **low-double-digit CC**: Firstsource 10–13% CC (EBIT 12.25–12.75%); Sagility "low double-digit CC organic" (adj. EBITDA 24–25%); eClerx EBITDA margin 24–28% [V-snip].
- **The listed Indian group is outgrowing its global peers — at the moment the global CX industry is shrinking.**
  - Concentrix: Q3 FY26 (to Aug-26) revenue **$2.45bn, −1.2% YoY** (CC −0.5%), a **$1.05bn goodwill impairment**, FY26 revenue guide cut twice to **$9.83–9.88bn**; shares fell >21% on the June guidance cut, blamed on client offshoring and spend pullbacks while investors focused on conversational AI [V-snip briefglance/parameter/TNW].

  - By contrast, analytics/AI-led peers are accelerating: **EXL Q2-2026 revenue $594.8m, +15.6%; FY26 organic growth guide raised to 13–14%**; **Genpact Q2-2026 $1.343bn, +7.1%, "Advanced Technology Solutions" +24.1%** [V-snip SEC 8-K/EXL release].
  - **Read:** AI is splitting BPM into (a) voice/contact-centre volume businesses that are deflating, and (b) domain-heavy data/operations/analytics businesses that are growing. All five Indian names sell mostly (b), but Firstsource's CMT book (~21%) and parts of Sagility's member services are exposed to (a).
- **US healthcare is ~55–60% of the group's revenue** [E: IKS ~100% + Sagility ~100% + Firstsource ~34% → ~₹13,600 Cr of ₹27,671 Cr = 49%; adding Indegene's US pharma (~72% of ₹3,511 Cr) → ~58% US-healthcare/life-sciences]. ### 1.2 Value chains (input → customer)

1. **Provider RCM (IKS model)** [primer]:
   - Patient visit → **documentation** (scribe/ambient AI drafts the note; human validates) → **coding** (ICD-10/CPT) → **claim** to payer → **remittance** posted → **denial** worked/appealed → cash.
   - US payers deny roughly **1 in 8** claims on first submission [primer; industry estimates vary 10–15%].
   - Value capture: the vendor that owns the **whole chain** (and increasingly the **EHR**, as IKS now does via TruBridge) captures the most because it controls the data and the outcome metric (net collection rate, days in A/R).
2. **Payer operations (Sagility model)** [primer]:
   - Premium in → member enrolled → **claim received** → auto-adjudicated (most) or **manually adjudicated** (pended claims; the outsourcer's queue) → paid → **payment integrity** review → appeals.
   - Driven by the ACA **medical-loss-ratio (MLR) rule**: insurers must spend ≥85% (large group) / ≥80% (individual/small group) of premium on care and quality, leaving ~15–20 cents for admin + profit [primer; K]. Every admin dollar saved is profit.
3. **CX/BPM (Firstsource model)**: client process (call queue, collections book, mortgage file) → transitioned to vendor → delivered from offshore (India, Philippines), nearshore (Mexico, South Africa, Romania, Trinidad) and onshore (US, UK) sites → billed per FTE/transaction/outcome [primer; V-snip TechMarketView].
4. **Pharma commercial (Indegene model)**: approved drug → brand plan → **content** (thousands of assets per brand, each through **medical-legal-regulatory (MLR) review**) → omnichannel activation (email, web, rep-triggered) → analytics → back to brand team; in parallel, safety (adverse-event case intake → processing → regulatory submission) [primer].
5. **Capital-markets KPO (eClerx model)**: trade executed → confirmation → settlement → reconciliation breaks → reference-data maintenance → regulatory reporting; work is client-system-embedded, often in the client's own platforms [primer].

### 1.3 Where value is captured [E]
- **Labour arbitrage** (the base layer): an Indian delivery FTE costs roughly **₹6–12 lakh/yr all-in for voice/transactional work, ₹10–20 lakh for coding/KPO, ₹15–30 lakh for clinical/medical-writing roles** vs a US equivalent of **$45–90k** [E/K; ranges, not sourced this session]. Billing rates for offshore healthcare BPM are typically **$12–25/hour** vs onshore US costs of $35–60/hour fully loaded [K, industry norms; unverified].
- **Domain + compliance** (HIPAA, SOC2, payer/regulator audits, MLR in pharma) creates switching costs. Top-5 client tenure at Firstsource is **>22 years** [V-snip Firstsource deck].
- **Technology/IP** (platforms, automation, now agents) is where the margin spread is: eClerx ~27% EBITDA, IKS ~34% vs Firstsource ~11–12% EBIT (≈15–16% EBITDA [E]).
- **Outcome ownership** (contingency fees on collections/recoveries, gain-share) is the highest-value layer and the one AI makes more attractive (§B1).

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets (with numbers)

1. **US healthcare admin burden (IKS, Sagility, Firstsource healthcare).**
   - Admin cost estimates ~$950bn–$1tn (above); ~$258bn already avoided via automation (CAQH) [K; V-snip].
   - **Medicare Advantage (MA)** is the most outsourcing-intensive payer line (risk adjustment, Stars quality, prior auth, appeals). **CMS's final 2027 MA rate notice (6-Apr-2026): +2.48% (~$13bn), ~5% all-in with risk-score trend**, vs **+0.09% in the January advance notice**; CMS delayed the risk-model change [V-snip Fierce Healthcare/KFF/TechTarget]. Insurers rallied (UNH +6.6%, CVS +7.3%, Humana +10.4%) [V-snip]. Payer medical-cost pressure "has squeezed industry margins for nearly three years" [V-snip].
   - Read: MA payer margins should improve in 2027 → payer admin budgets loosen in CY2027 → supportive for Sagility's FY28 [E].
2. **Medicaid redeterminations and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, signed 4-Jul-2025).** CBO: **11.8m lose coverage by 2034** from the health provisions (16.9m incl. other policy changes); ~**$1tn** federal Medicaid reduction over 10 years [V-snip ASTHO/KFF/USAFacts]. **Work requirements (80 hours/month for expansion adults) from 31-Dec-2026**, with six-monthly eligibility verification (exemptions possible to end-2028); **provider-tax safe harbour cut from 6% to 5.5% in FY2028, falling 0.5 pp/yr to 3.5% by FY2032**; no new provider taxes; state-directed payments cut 10%/yr from 2028; **$50bn rural health fund over five years** [V-snip].
   - **Two-sided for the group:** (+) more eligibility churn, verification, redetermination and appeals work for payer ops (Sagility, Firstsource) and patient-access/eligibility work for provider RCM; (−) uncompensated care rises, hospital (esp. rural) margins fall → pressure on provider vendor pricing, and rural hospitals are **TruBridge's** customer base (IKS) [E].
3. **Pharma commercial spend (Indegene).** Drivers: shift from reps to digital (~**60% of US physicians restrict rep access**; a rep costs ~**$250–300k/yr**) [V-snip moneylife]; content explosion; patent cliff cost-cutting; AI-readiness. Headwinds: **US MFN drug pricing** — **26 MFN agreements covering ~89% of the branded market by 31-Aug-2026** [V-snip Gibson Dunn/White House]; **Section 232 tariffs of up to 100% on patented drugs** (proclamation 2-Apr-2026; effective 31-Jul-2026 for 17 large drugmakers and 29-Sep-2026 for all branded makers), with MFN-deal companies exempt (0% to 20-Jan-2029 with an onshoring plan) [V-snip Jones Day/EY/Managed Healthcare Executive 29-Sep-2026]; IRA Medicare price negotiation (first 10 drugs' negotiated prices effective 1-Jan-2026 [K]). Indegene's CEO: "Whenever the pharma industry is under pressure, those times are better for us" [V-snip moneylife]. No hard data on 2026 promotional budget cuts was found (gap).
4. **Banks/capital markets (eClerx ~41%, Firstsource BFS ~33%).** Bank cost programmes, T+1 settlement (US May-2024; UK/EU planned Oct-2027 [K]), regulatory reporting. Mortgage: Firstsource's origination-linked revenue rises with US refinancing when rates fall [primer]. US policy rate path in 2026 not re-verified here.
5. **Telecom/cable/media (Firstsource CMT ~21%, eClerx CMT ~26%).** Mature, cost-cutting clients — the vertical most exposed to voice-AI deflation [primer].
6. **Retail/e-commerce/luxury (eClerx ~8% FLR, Firstsource Diverse ~13% incl. Ascensos retail CX).** Cyclical; "Fashion & Luxury" was eClerx's soft spot in FY26 [primer].
7. **Insourcing to GCCs** works against all five: **2,117 GCCs and 2.36m employees** [V-snip]. But GCCs are also customers (vendors run "build-operate-transfer" and specialist towers inside GCCs) [K].

### 9.2 Definitions and drivers
- **Revenue per employee** = revenue ($) ÷ average (or closing) headcount. Driven by onshore mix, skill mix, pricing model and automation. Rising revenue per head with stable margins = productivity captured by the vendor; falling = deflation or mix shift to cheaper geographies.
- **Non-linearity** = revenue growth − headcount growth (pp). FY26: eClerx **~+1 pp** ($ +17.9% vs headcount +16.9%) — essentially linear [primer]; Sagility Q1 FY27 **~−3 pp** in CC terms (+15.2% CC vs +18.5% heads, inflated by CareSeed) [E]; Indegene Q1 FY27 ₹ +39.7% vs heads +14.5% (M&A and FX flatter it) [E]. **No member yet shows clear AI-driven non-linearity in the reported numbers** [E] — the most important negative finding in this dossier.
- **Utilisation** = billable FTE ÷ total delivery FTE; healthy 80–88% in BPM [K]. Not disclosed by most members (gap).

# B. Bespoke deep dives (suggested sections)

### B1. "GenAI and the death of the seat" — the pricing physics
- **Three pricing models** [primer Firstsource §01]: per FTE/seat (revenue ∝ headcount), per transaction (∝ volume), per outcome/gain-share (∝ value). Under FTE pricing, a 30% productivity gain is a 30% revenue cut at renewal unless offset by scope expansion; under transaction pricing, the vendor keeps the gain until the client renegotiates the unit price; under outcome pricing, the vendor keeps it.
- **Worked example [E]:** a 100-FTE offshore claims queue billed at **$22/hour × 1,900 billable hours = $41,800/FTE/yr → $4.18m/yr** (₹39.7 Cr at ₹94.9). Delivery cost ₹9 lakh/FTE + 25% overheads = ₹11.25 lakh ≈ $11,850 → gross margin ~72% before SG&A.
  - AI agents automate 40% of transactions. **FTE-priced:** client cuts to 60 FTE → revenue **$2.51m (−40%)**; vendor gross profit falls from $2.99m to $1.80m.
  - **Transaction-priced** at $4.00/claim × 1.045m claims/yr = $4.18m; vendor redeploys 40 FTE elsewhere, adds ~$0.15m AI run-cost → gross profit **rises** to ~$3.5m (+17%) until repricing.
  - **Outcome-priced** (e.g., 4% of $105m recovered/collected): AI raises recovery 10% → revenue **+10%** with fewer FTE.
- **Member positioning:** IKS (outcome-linked RCM, "AI + human" documentation; Q1 FY27 margin 35% ex one-offs) [V-snip]; Sagility (payer ops mostly FTE/transaction; payment integrity contingency; BirchAI) [primer]; Firstsource (UnBPO → "Intelligence That Operates"; Kairos; AI collections agents) [primer; V-snip]; Indegene (AI-embedded content factory; revenue/head ~$75k as referee) [primer]; eClerx (Roboworx CogniFlows used by ~15,000 staff; first large agentic win Q4 FY26; ACV +25%) [primer; V-snip].
- **What to measure:** revenue per head ($), headcount growth vs CC growth, % revenue non-FTE priced (rarely disclosed — ask management), gross margin per FTE.

### B2. "US healthcare's admin-cost machine — payer vs provider"
- **Provider side (IKS; Firstsource provider RCM):** paid to get money **in** — denial rates, days in A/R, net collection rate. Squeezed when hospital/physician margins fall (Medicaid cuts, site-neutral payment); helped by complexity (more denials, prior auth).
- **Payer side (Sagility; Firstsource payer):** paid to process and control money **out** — claims accuracy, auto-adjudication rate, payment-integrity savings. Driven by MLR economics and MA/Medicaid enrolment; payer margin stress (2023–25) pushed outsourcing; 2027 MA rates help.
- **Adversarial symmetry:** AI on the payer side (auto-denials, AI-driven prior auth) creates more appeals work on the provider side, and vice versa — an "AI arms race" that, so far, **increases** total admin volume [E; CAQH: >50% of plans and 25% of providers already use AI in admin, V-snip].
- **Group exposure [E]:** payer-heavy revenue ≈ Sagility ~₹6,400 Cr (89% of 7,193) + part of Firstsource HC; provider-heavy ≈ IKS ₹3,194 Cr + Sagility ~₹790 Cr + part of Firstsource HC (₹3,209 Cr total HC).

### B3. "The rupee, the margin and the hedge book"
- Mechanics [E]: revenue in $, ~60–70% of costs in ₹/PHP. A 1% fall in the rupee lifts ₹ revenue 1% and ₹ costs ~0.3–0.4% (the $-denominated onshore share) → EBITDA margin +~0.4–0.6 pp at a 25% margin business before hedges. Hedge books (typically 50–70% of 12-month net exposure [K]) defer the gain; hedge losses appear in revenue (eClerx) or other income depending on accounting designation.
- Cross-check from the numbers: FY26 average ~₹88.3 → Q1 FY27 ~₹94.5 = **7% depreciation**; yet margins fell or held (IKS 35% → 33% incl. TruBridge costs; Sagility 25.3% → 24.0%; eClerx ~27% → ~24%; Indegene 19.4% → 16.9%; Firstsource 11.7% → 12.4%). **The FX tailwind was absorbed by wage hikes, acquisition costs, AI investment and hedge losses** [E]. That is the honest finding: don't model a full FX pass-through to margin.
- Pass-through: clients ask for productivity give-backs at renewal (Firstsource "annual productivity" [primer]).

### B4. "Roll-ups and goodwill — how the 2024 cohort was built"

# C. The revenue / profit equation

**Revenue = Σ (billable FTE × utilisation × billable hours × rate per hour) + Σ (transactions × price per transaction) + Σ (outcome base × fee %)**

**EBITDA = Revenue − offshore wage cost (FTE × ₹ cost per FTE ÷ FX) − onshore wage cost − facilities/tech − SG&A**

**PAT = (EBITDA − D&A incl. acquired-intangible amortisation − interest + other income incl. treasury) × (1 − tax ~25%)**

**Worked example in ₹ (an offshore healthcare-ops vendor, illustrative [E]):**
| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| Delivery FTE | 10,000 |
| Utilisation | 85% → 8,500 billable FTE |
| Billable hours/FTE/yr | 1,900 |
| Average rate | $16/hour (blended offshore payer/provider work) |
| Revenue | 8,500 × 1,900 × $16 = **$258.4m** = **₹2,452 Cr** at ₹94.9 |
| Offshore wage cost | 10,000 × ₹7.5 lakh all-in = **₹750 Cr** (30.6% of revenue) |
| Onshore staff (5% of FTE equivalent; 500 × $75,000) | $37.5m = **₹356 Cr** (14.5%) |
| Support/management staff (1,500 × ₹15 lakh) | **₹225 Cr** (9.2%) |
| Facilities, tech, telecom, cloud/AI | **₹196 Cr** (8.0%) |
| SG&A (sales, G&A, corporate) | **₹270 Cr** (11.0%) |
| **EBITDA** | **₹655 Cr (26.7%)** |
| D&A (incl. ₹60 Cr acquired intangibles) | ₹110 Cr |

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed

1. **The group's debt is not where Part 1 said.**
   - Sagility is effectively **debt-free** (net debt 0.06× adj. EBITDA), and its only borrowing is a **promoter loan from Sagility B.V.** that will be repaid in FY27. Repaying the sponsor's loan is a quiet cash transfer to EQT on top of its share sales.
   - Firstsource is the only member with **structural acquisition debt, mostly short-term** (₹2,016 Cr of short-term borrowings at Jun-26).
   - IKS is the only one about to **lever up** (TruBridge, ~₹5,000+ Cr).
   - Ex-IKS's new debt, the five are net cash ~₹1,400 Cr ex-leases.
2. **IKS's client concentration is rising fast while the client count shrinks.**
   - Top-10 share went from 43% to **54%** in a year, top-5 from 32% to **42%**; clients went 650+ → 600+.
   - Land-and-expand inside big health systems (Advocate, a California IDN) is working, but IKS now looks more like Sagility (top-3 60%) than like a diversified RCM vendor.
   - Add the **₹143 Cr upfront "economic value add" guarantee payment to a customer**: outcome-linked contracts now carry balance-sheet risk.
3. **TruBridge is being bought on EBITDA, not revenue.** Management is deliberately shrinking it ($340m → ~$300m: exiting IT services and early-out lines; stricter revenue recognition) to keep the ~$68m EBITDA.
   - Price ≈ **8× EBITDA**, vs IKS at ~26× EV/OP (FY26: EV ≈ ₹27,936 Cr ÷ OP ₹1,084 Cr).
   - Bull case: margin arbitrage (22–23% → early/mid-30s).
   - Bear case: rural-hospital exposure to OBBBA Medicaid cuts, and an EHR franchise that needs re-platforming.
4. **The rupee tailwind is pre-sold through hedge books.**
   - eClerx's FY27 book is at ₹91.16 (~4% below spot). Firstsource's is at ₹92.7/$ and ₹118–120/£. Firstsource's hedge reserve is **−₹345 Cr**, which will flow through revenue.
   - So the gap between ₹ growth and CC growth overstates profit leverage in FY27. The tailwind arrives in **FY28** (eClerx's FY28 book is at ₹96.2).
5. **US policy risk has moved from tax (HIRE Act, stalled) to telecom regulation (FCC NPRM).**
   - The FCC's proposed **offshore call cap (illustrative 30%)** is the first concrete US rule that could force onshoring of BPM voice work.
   - It is narrow (telecom/cable/VoIP/DBS), but it hits exactly the voice-CX segment that AI is already deflating.
6. **Healthcare-payer softness has started at the edges.**
   - Firstsource saw Medicare Advantage clients "recalibrate program scope" and lost a BPaaS payer ramp.
   - Sagility says pricing moves "in the opposite direction".
   - Yet Sagility still grew 15% CC with stable margins.
   - **The 2.48% MA rate (vs 0.09% proposed) and CMS's exclusion of unlinked chart-review diagnoses** cut both ways: more plan revenue, but less risk-adjustment coding upside for payers (relevant to Sagility's and Firstsource's payer analytics).
7. **Labour-cost shocks are now regulatory, not just market-driven.** Karnataka and Telangana minimum-wage hikes cost Sagility ~120 bp of FY27 margin, with 12–18 months to mitigate. Expect spread to other Bengaluru/Hyderabad-heavy BPMs.
8. **Indegene's "no promoter" label hides a concentrated register.** Nadathur Fareast (21.9%), founders (~22%) and Brighton Park (8.7%) hold ~51%. Brighton Park is the next potential seller after Carlyle's exit; DII ownership jumped from 8.7% to 15.8% in the Sep-26 quarter.
