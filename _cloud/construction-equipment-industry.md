## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **"Construction Equipment" here is two companies that are barely construction-equipment companies in the ICEMA sense.**
   - **BEML** is a three-segment defence PSU. FY26 revenue mix by segment: **Mining & Construction 41% (FY25 54%), Defence & Aerospace 35% (27%), Rail & Metro 24% (19%)** [V-snip whalesbook summary of FY26 results; rupee splits below are E]. Applied to screener revenue: M&C ~₹1,784 Cr, Defence ~₹1,523 Cr, R&M ~₹1,044 Cr in FY26, vs ~₹2,172 / ~₹1,086 / ~₹764 Cr in FY25 [E]. So BEML's "CE" revenue **fell ~18%** in FY26 while total revenue rose 8%.
   - BEML's **order book is ~68% Rail & Metro, ~25% Defence, ~7% Mining & Construction** (Q3 FY26, ₹16,300 Cr book) [V psuconnect 23-Feb-2026]. Its forward revenue is a rolling-stock and defence story, not a CE story.
   - **ACE** is a crane company. ICEMA's "construction equipment" is dominated by earthmoving (71% of units, mostly backhoe loaders and excavators), where ACE is a small challenger and BEML sells mostly large mining machines.
   - Neither member makes the industry's volume product at scale: **JCB India alone has ~80% of backhoe loaders and >40% of all CE** [V openthemagazine/CRISIL]. The listed pair covers the **edges** of the industry (cranes; heavy mining machines), not its core.
2. **ICEMA counts units, not rupees, and a mining dump truck = one unit = a backhoe loader.** A 100–200-tonne BEML dump truck can cost ₹15–40 Cr [E, indicative]; a backhoe loader ~₹30–40 lakh; a P&C crane ~₹25–30 lakh (ACE CMCE realisation **₹27.1 lakh/unit** in Q1 FY27 [V ICICI Direct]). Never compare BEML's revenue to ICEMA unit growth.
3. **ACE: "revenue ₹3,390 Cr, EBITDA 18.3%" (primer, company) vs "₹3,280 Cr, OPM 15.4%" (screener).**
   - The company's headline is **standalone total income ₹3,395 Cr** (revenue + other income) and **EBITDA ₹622.36 Cr = 18.33% of total income, including other income** [V ACE Q4 FY26 call transcript, 21-May-2026].
   - Screener consolidated: **revenue from operations ₹3,280 Cr, OP ₹504 Cr (15.4%), other income ₹110 Cr, PAT ₹415 Cr** [V screener]. ICICI Direct's FY26 EBITDA is also ₹504.0 Cr (15.4%) [V].
   - **Use ₹3,280 Cr / ₹504 Cr / 15.4% in all tables.** The 18.3% is "EBITDA incl. other income on total income" and inflates margin by ~3 points.
   - Other income is large for ACE (₹110 Cr FY26 = ~26% of PAT [E]; ₹55 Cr in Q1 FY27 = ~35% of PBT [V-snip marketsmojo]) because of its ~₹1,300 Cr treasury.
4. **BEML: the primer's "EBITDA margin fell from ~25.6% to ~15%" is the Q4 margin, not the year.**
   - Screener quarterly OPM: Q4 FY25 **26%**, Q4 FY26 **15%** [V screener].
   - Full year: OP **₹506 Cr (12.6%) in FY25 → ₹299 Cr (6.9%) in FY26** [V screener]. The primer's "EBITDA ~₹650 Cr" does not exist on any basis we found.
   - Company guidance for FY27 is "13–14% EBITDA, a return to FY25 levels" [V-snip sahi 10-Aug-2026] — that confirms FY25 was ~13%, not 25.6%.
5. **BEML is extremely seasonal: Q1 is always a loss, Q4 is everything.**
   - Q1 OP: FY24 −₹51 Cr, FY25 −₹50 Cr, FY26 −₹49 Cr, **FY27 +₹2 Cr** (company: EBITDA +₹3 Cr, "first positive Q1 EBITDA in ten years") [V screener; V BEML press release 7-Aug-2026].
   - Q4 share of annual revenue: FY24 37%, FY25 41%, FY26 41% [E from screener]. Q4 share of annual OP: FY26 91% (272 of 299) [E].
   - **TTM figures are therefore unreliable mid-year;** a P/E on TTM or FY26 earnings is a P/E on a trough.
6. **Consolidated vs standalone (BEML).** FY26 PAT ₹141 Cr consolidated (screener) vs **₹148 Cr** in one company summary (whalesbook; likely standalone) [V screener; V-snip]. Q1 FY27 net loss ₹27.01 Cr consolidated vs ₹27.26 Cr standalone [V-snip]. Differences are BEML's small subsidiaries; immaterial but quote the basis. (One wire report printed "₹27.26 lakh" — a typo for crore.)
7. **ACE's segments are two, not four.** ACE reports **CMCE** (cranes + material handling + construction equipment, ~92% of revenue) and **Agri Equipment** (~8%), plus a small "Others" line (₹76.55 Cr of miscellaneous exports in FY26) [V ACE Q4 call; V ICICI Direct]. Sub-splits between cranes, forklifts and backhoe loaders are **not disclosed**; the primer's "cranes ~65–70%" is an estimate.
8. **Market-share claims in cranes conflict, and the basis matters.**
   - ACE: "**63%+ of mobile cranes, 60%+ of tower cranes**" (company claim, screener profile) [V screener].
   - Escorts Kubota: "**41.4% pick-and-carry crane share in FY26, India's number one**" (ICEMA basis, company presentation) [V-sib tractors.html].
   - Both cannot be shares of the same market. The likeliest reconciliation is that **ACE is not fully captured in the ICEMA crane dataset** that Escorts quotes, or that the two use different denominators (all mobile cranes incl. Hydra-type vs ICEMA-reported P&C) [E — FLAG; not verified]. The writer should state both claims with their sources and not average them.
   - In **heavy (slew/truck/crawler) cranes**, the market is the opposite: Chinese OEMs hold "nearly 95%" (industry estimate via Economic Times/Outlook Business), and five domestic players (TIL, Kobelco India, Tadano Escorts India, ABG Cranes, Tata Hitachi) have shut crane manufacturing [V-snip outlookbusiness]. SANY India claims **55% of "cranes"** — that is the large-crane market [V-snip themachinemaker].
9. **ICEMA's own history restates.** The FY25 release gave domestic **1,26,961** and exports **13,230** [V-snip constructionworld]; the FY26 release gives FY25 domestic **1,21,301** and FY26 domestic **1,13,229 (−7%)** with exports **+31.5–32%** [V-snip outlookbusiness/knnindia; V nbmcw]. 1,36,995 − 1,13,229 implies FY26 exports of ~23,766 (+26% on the restated base), which does not match "+32%" [E]. Totals (1,40,191 → 1,36,995) are consistent; **the domestic/export split is not**. Quote the totals and the published growth rates; flag the split.
10. **Emission-rule dates are messy.** ICEMA and ICRA (2024 note) say **CEV Stage V from 1-Jan-2025** [V nbmcw; V-snip ICRA]; an ICRA Mar-2026 summary refers to "the CEV-V transition from July 1, 2025"; a 2026 news report says machines **above 56 kW must comply from 1-Oct-2026** [V-snip, single report, unverified]. Use "Stage V phased in from January 2025, by power band; the last bands are still transitioning in 2026" and flag the exact dates.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **India is the world's #3 CE market by units (after China and the US/North America) and roughly a US$10 bn market (FY25)** [V nbmcw quoting ICEMA; ICEMA projects US$14.76 bn by 2030, 8.3% CAGR]. CRISIL sized FY25 at ~₹83,000 Cr [V-snip]. Volumes: **1,40,191 units in FY25 → 1,36,995 in FY26 (−2%)**, with domestic **−7%** and exports **+31.5%** [V nbmcw/ICEMA].
- **The two listed members are a combined ~₹7,631 Cr of FY26 revenue, ₹803 Cr OP (10.5%), ₹556 Cr PAT and ₹27,329 Cr of market value (23-Oct-2026 close)** [E: sums of screener 25-Oct-26]. That is **~2.3× smaller than JCB India alone** (₹19,710 Cr FY26 operating income, provisional [V CRISIL via Open Magazine]). The listed universe is a thin, unrepresentative slice of a market dominated by unlisted MNC subsidiaries (JCB, Tata Hitachi, L&T-Komatsu/LTCMM, CNH/CASE, SANY, Caterpillar, Komatsu, Volvo, Schwing Stetter, Kobelco, Hyundai/HD).
- **FY26 was a down-year for the industry and a margin-down year for both members, for different reasons.**
  - Industry: the CEV Stage V cost step (+12–15% on machine prices [V-snip ICRA; V autocarpro 17-Jul-2026]), the **FY25 pre-buy**, the **2024 election and its after-effects on road awards** (NHAI awards 4,008 km FY25 → **3,124 km FY26** [V-snip Nuvama]), a seven-year low in national-highway construction (~9,380 km [V-sib civil-construction]), slow Jal Jeevan Mission payments and a long monsoon [V-snip desimachines; V ACE call].
  - ACE: CMCE volumes **−19% to 10,853 units** but price/mix held OPM at 15.4% (flat) [V-snip Systematix; V screener].
  - BEML: revenue +8% to a record ₹4,351 Cr, but OP **−41%** (₹506 → ₹299 Cr), PAT **−52%** (₹293 → ₹141 Cr) on cost overruns, a ~₹80 Cr provision for a metro project restart, casting-supply constraints, a 150% jump in R&D and mix [V screener; V psuconnect; primer].
- **H1 FY27 is a recovery off a low base, overtaken by input inflation.**
  - Q1 FY27: ACE revenue **+20.5%**, CMCE volume **+17.2%** (2,740 units), OPM 15.0% [V ICICI Direct]; BEML revenue **+29%**, first positive Q1 EBITDA in a decade, order inflow **+172%** to ₹1,181 Cr [V BEML PR].
  - Escorts Kubota's CE volumes ran **+27.4% in Q1 FY27 and +53.9% in Q2 FY27** [V-sib tractors.html] — the best real-time read on H1 industry demand, since ICEMA's Q1/Q2 FY27 data were **not found** [FLAG].
  - But steel is up ~20% and ACE took **~10–12% of price hikes** in Jan/May/Jun-2026 [V ACE call; V ICICI Direct]; ICEMA guides FY27 domestic **+7%**, ICRA **+3–5%** [V-snip].
- **Valuations price the narrative, not the trough.** BEML **86.4× trailing P/E, 5.3× book, ~53× FY26 EV/OP**; ACE **27.3× P/E, 5.9× book, ~21× EV/OP** [V screener; E]. BEML's multiple capitalises a ₹16,284 Cr order book (3.7× FY26 revenue), defence/metro optionality and a disinvestment/land option; ACE's capitalises a 32% ROCE, net cash and crane dominance.

### 1.2 Value chains

1. **Volume CE (JCB-type, and ACE's CMCE)** — steel plate/sections and castings (~35–45% of cost [E]), bought-in diesel engines (Kirloskar, Escorts Kubota, Cummins, Ashok Leyland, Perkins/Kubota/Yanmar), hydraulics (pumps, valves, cylinders — largely imported from Japan/Europe/China or made by Bosch Rexroth, Danfoss, Kawasaki, Dana), axles/transmissions (Carraro, Dana, ZF), tyres, cabins, electronics/telematics → OEM fabrication, welding, painting, assembly → **dealer network** (JCB 60+ dealers/700 outlets [V]) → buyer financed by NBFC/bank (**85–90% of machines financed** [V ICRA outlook; V ACE Q4 call]) → used by contractor or rented out → spares and service (the after-market annuity) → used-equipment resale.
2. **Mining HEMM (BEML-type)** — Coal India subsidiaries / SCCL / NTPC / SAIL tender → BEML builds to order at KGF (dump trucks, dozers) and Mysore (engines, trucks) → long warranty/maintenance and **MARC (maintenance and repair contracts)** → spares. Increasingly, CIL outsources mining to **MDO (mine developer-operator) contractors**, who buy cheaper imported/private machines; BEML called this shift a reason mining orders were delayed in FY26 [V psuconnect].
3. **Defence vehicles (BEML)** — MoD/Army programme → licensed platforms (Tatra-based HMV 6×6/8×8/10×10/12×12 [K]; armoured recovery vehicles on Arjun/T-72 chassis [K]; bridge systems, mine barriers, gun-towing vehicles, Light Armoured Multipurpose Vehicle [V psuconnect]) → long development + trials → series orders → exports (₹75.58 Cr HMV 12×12/8×8 order from South-East Asia, 9-Oct-2026 [V screener]).
4. **Rolling stock (BEML)** — metro corporations / Indian Railways → car-body (stainless/aluminium), bogies, propulsion (bought in — Mitsubishi/Hitachi/BHEL/Medha), brakes (Knorr, Faiveley) → BEML assembles at Bengaluru (Bangalore Complex) → long warranty and maintenance contracts (BMRCL: ₹0.13 Cr per car per year [V-snip]). Value per car **₹7.74 Cr** (BMRCL Phase 2, 2023) [V-snip indiainfrahub].
5. **Cranes (ACE)** — ACE designs and fabricates booms/chassis at Faridabad/Palwal, buys engines (CEV-V compliant), hydraulics and axles → ~sells through dealers to "retail hirers" (single-machine rental owners), industry and EPC → finance 85–90% [V ACE call].

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **Brand + network + finance access** is the moat in volume CE (JCB's 80% BHL share, ~16% OPM, debt-free [V CRISIL via Open]).
- **Niche dominance** captures margin (ACE CMCE segment margin **18.6%** vs "competition at segment level 8–9%" per ACE management [V ACE Q4 call]; Escorts Kubota CE EBIT **7.9%** FY26 [V-sib tractors]).
- **Government preference** substitutes for brand in BEML's markets (defence indigenisation, PSU procurement) but does not deliver margin (OPM 7–13%).
- **After-market (spares/service)** is the stable profit pool everywhere [K; no Indian disclosure].
- Chinese OEMs capture value through **price** (financing, credit terms and "subsidised" pricing, per ACE [V call]) in excavators and heavy cranes, not in P&C cranes or BHLs.

### 2.2 Value
- **US$10 bn in FY25** (ICEMA) [V nbmcw] ≈ ₹85,000 Cr at FY25's ~₹85/$ [E]; CRISIL **~₹83,000 Cr** (FY25, +4–6%) [V-snip]. Tata Hitachi MD: "US$9–10 bn, growing 10–15% a year" (Oct-2024) [V-snip].

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Emission norms — CEV Stage V (CMVR Rule 115A)
- **What:** Bharat Stage CEV-V (EU Stage V-aligned) for diesel engines in construction equipment vehicles. Requires electronic engines and after-treatment.
- **When:** **1-Jan-2025** (ICEMA, ICRA) for the main bands; <37 kW machines jumped **Stage III → V** directly [V-snip ICRA]. Conflicting dates: ICRA (Mar-2026) refers to the transition "from 1-Jul-2025"; one 2026 report: machines **>56 kW from 1-Oct-2026** [V-snip — FLAG]. CEV-IV had applied from Apr-2021 (>50 hp) and Apr-2024 (<50 hp) [K — FLAG].
- **Price effect:** +12–15% (III → V), +4–6% (IV → V) [V-snip ICRA]; pass-through over 12–18 months. Escorts: +6–7% (BS-III → V) and +3.5–4.5% (BS-IV → V) from Jan-2025 [V-sib tractors]. ACE Hydra cranes +12–15% [V call].
- **Demand effect:** **pre-buy in FY25** (ACE: "FY25… benefited from significant pre-buying ahead of emission norm transition") → **post-norm dip in FY26** (ACE CMCE volumes −19%; ICEMA domestic −7%; old-generation Hydra crane segment, ~60% of cranes, "shrank" until Jan–Feb 2026) [V ACE call; V-snip Systematix].
- **Pending:** ICEMA's request (17-Jul-2026) to exempt armed-forces/BRO machines [V autocarpro]; used-equipment import concerns (autocarpro: "Clean rules for new machines, open doors for old ones") [V-snip headline].
- **Tractors are separate** (TREM V by power band Oct-2026 to Apr-2032) [V-sib tractors] — relevant only to ACE's agri arm.

### 7.2 GST
- **GST 2.0 (56th GST Council, 3-Sep-2025; effective 22-Sep-2025)**: construction and mining equipment (HS 8429: bulldozers, graders, excavators, loaders, etc.) **28% → 18%**; rate schedules per Notification 09/2025-Integrated Tax (Rate) [V-snip equipmentindia editorial; eximpe]. Industry sources say ~15% of products "still remain at 28%" [V-snip]. Treatment of cranes (8426), forklifts (8427), dumpers (8704 10) **not verified** [FLAG].
- **Read:** a 10-point GST cut is worth ~8% on the buyer's invoice (1.18/1.28) — roughly offsetting the Stage V price step for GST-paying buyers, but **input-tax-credit-eligible contractors gain only cash-flow**, so the real demand effect is concentrated on small hirers and non-ITC buyers [E].
- Tractors: 12% → 5% (ACE agri) [V-sib].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **CE volumes follow the *growth rate* of infrastructure spending, not its level.**
   - Union capex grew 25–39% a year in FY21–FY24 and ICEMA units grew ~26% a year; since FY25 capex grows 7–11% and units went +3%, −2% and are guided +3–7% for FY27 [V-sib; V; V-snip].
   - Roads are the swing end-market, and FY26 was the trough: NHAI awards 3,124 km, NH construction ~9,380 km (an eight-year low) [V-snip; V-sib]. FY27 awards are recovering from a tiny base (+69% to ~329 km by August).
   - 85–90% of machines are bought on credit, so the RBI's 7-Oct-2026 hike to 5.50% is a second-order headwind [V; V-sib].
2. **Regulation sets the shape of each year: Stage V and GST 2.0 created a pre-buy, a dip and now a price-led recovery.**
   - CEV Stage V (from Jan-2025) raised machine prices 12–15% for Stage III → V machines (most small machines and Hydra cranes) [V-snip ICRA; V call].
   - FY25 pulled demand forward; FY26 paid for it (ACE volumes −19%; ICEMA domestic −7%).
   - GST 28% → 18% (Sep-2025) partly offset the price step for non-ITC buyers.
   - In H1 FY27, steel (+~20%) and rupee (₹94.9/$) inflation are pushing a **second** round of price rises (ACE ~10–12%) [V ICICI; V call].
3. **China is the competitor that matters, but only in some boxes.**
   - Chinese brands hold ~20–22% of excavators and ~95% of heavy cranes (industry claims) [V-snip]. Five Indian crane makers have exited [V-snip].
   - The DGTR's 24–52% anti-dumping recommendation on Chinese truck/crawler cranes (Sep-2025) was never notified [V-snip; V call].
   - Chinese OEMs do not make pick-and-carry cranes and have not cracked backhoe loaders, which is exactly where ACE and JCB earn their margins [V call; V Open].
4. **The listed members are not a sample of the industry; they are two special cases.**
   - **ACE** is a niche dominator: about 63% of mobile cranes (company claim), 18.6% CMCE segment margin, ROCE 32%, negative working capital, ₹1,283 Cr of investments and no debt [V].
   - **BEML** is a build-to-order PSU whose CE business (~41% of revenue) is shrinking inside it, while rail/metro (~68% of the order book) and defence grow. Its cash cycle is ~421 days and OPM 7% [V; V-snip].
   - JCB India alone (₹19,710 Cr) is 2.6× the two members combined [V].
5. **Execution and cash conversion, not demand, is BEML's binding constraint; mix and price discipline are ACE's.**
   - BEML missed its FY26 revenue guidance (15–20% → actual +8%) and its >₹20,000 Cr order-book target (actual ₹15,896 Cr) [V psuconnect; V].
   - It now guides **+30% revenue and a 13–14% EBITDA margin for FY27** [V-snip sahi]. Its record of delivering such guidance is poor [E].
   - ACE guides only on margin (>15%) and deferred revenue guidance until September [V ICICI].

## B. Bespoke deep-dive themes (own sections in the report)

### B1. "Hydra to Stage V": how an emission rule hit the old-generation pick-and-carry crane — and why that matters more to ACE than any capex number
- About 60% of India's crane market is "old-generation" Hydra cranes bought by retail hirers. In January 2025 they jumped from Tier 3 to Stage V, became electronic for the first time and rose 12–15% in price. That segment "shrank" until January–February 2026 [V ACE call].
- The result: ACE's FY26 CMCE volumes fell 19% (10,853), Q4 FY26 3,458 (−14% YoY, +27% QoQ), then Q1 FY27 2,740 (+17%) [V-snip; V ICICI]. Escorts Kubota's CE swung from −10.6% in FY26 to +27%/+54% in Q1/Q2 FY27 [V-sib].
- The margin held because ACE passed on price. Realisation was ₹27.1 lakh/unit in Q1 FY27 (+4.6%) and CMCE margin 18.6% in FY26 [V].
- Next steps: the >56 kW band (Oct-2026?), the BRO exemption ask, and a further ~10–12% of hikes for steel.

### B2. "Three clocks, one balance sheet": BEML's segment rotation from mining to metro and defence
- Revenue mix moved in one year from M&C 54% / Def 27% / R&M 19% (FY25) to **41 / 35 / 24 (FY26)** [V-snip]. The order book is **~68% R&M / 25% Def / 7% M&C** [V psuconnect]. Q1 FY27 inflow was ₹1,181 Cr (M&C 589 / Def 581 / R&M 11) [V BEML PR].
- Rolling-stock build-out: Bhopal plant (₹1,500 Cr, +300 cars/yr); Bengaluru Phase 2 (66 trainsets/396 cars, ~₹4,000 Cr cumulative; due end-2026); Vande Bharat sleeper (first service early 2026; two BEML rakes to SWR Aug-2026; +₹180.6 Cr ICF order Sep-2026) [V; V-snip].
- **The paradox:** the CE business BEML was founded for is now its smallest forward driver. The sub-sector's "CE" label fits BEML less every year.

### B3. "The working-capital chasm": −20 days vs 421 days
- Same sub-sector, opposite cash models. ACE: dealer and financier pay on delivery; suppliers fund it; FCF ₹330 Cr on ₹415 Cr PAT [V].
- BEML: government buyers pay on acceptance, after trials, with retentions; CCC 358–673 days for a decade; FCF negative two years running [V].
- This, more than margin, explains the ROCE gap (32% vs 8%) and why BEML's P/E is optically 86×.

### B4. "Anti-dumping that never came": India's crane-import policy and the KATO JV
- DGTR found dumping and recommended 24–52% duties (Sep-2025). The MoF let the recommendation lapse [V-snip; V call]. Chinese crane makers responded by starting local assembly (cost +8–10%) [V call].
- ACE's KATO JV is a bet on taking share in heavy cranes from China: >₹300 Cr in 3–4 years without the ADD, ₹700–800 Cr with it [V call].
- Ties to the broader Indian trade-remedy pattern seen in other sub-sectors, where DGTR recommends and the Finance Ministry often does not notify (cf. soda ash, in our commodity-chem dossier).

### B5 (optional). "The listed sliver": why India's #3 CE market has almost no listed CE pure-plays
- JCB, Tata Hitachi, SANY, Komatsu, Cat, Volvo, CASE are all unlisted subsidiaries [K]. Listed exposure exists only through ACE, BEML (partly), Escorts Kubota (15% of revenue), Ajax (concrete), L&T (via LTCMM) and Sanghvi Movers (rental) [V; K].

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Archetype 2 (ACE): Revenue = units × realisation (× mix); EBITDA = revenue × (1 − RM% − employee% − other%) where RM% moves with steel and the price lag.**

### C.1 Worked example — ACE FY26 and Q1 FY27 [E arithmetic on V inputs]
- FY26 CMCE: **10,853 units × ~₹27.2 lakh = ~₹2,950 Cr** (reported CMCE income ">₹2,946 Cr") [V-snip; V call]. Agri ₹251 Cr; Others ₹76.55 Cr. Sum ≈ ₹3,274 Cr vs consolidated revenue ₹3,280 Cr [V screener] — reconciles within 0.2%.
- CMCE segment result **₹548 Cr (18.6%)**; agri ~₹2.5 Cr (1%); less unallocated costs and depreciation → consolidated OP ₹504 Cr (15.4%) [V; E].
- Q1 FY27: **2,740 × ₹27.1 lakh = ₹742.5 Cr** (reported ₹742.4 Cr) [V ICICI]; segment margin 18.2% → ~₹135 Cr segment result (transcript: ₹134.09 Cr) [V-snip]; OP ₹117.9 Cr (15.0%).
- **Price-cost sensitivity per ₹100 of revenue [E]:**
  - Raw materials 67.5, of which steel ~30.
  - Steel +20% adds 6.0 of cost. A 10% price rise lifts revenue to 110.
  - EBITDA = 110 − (67.5 + 6.0) − 4.9 − 12.3 = 19.3, i.e. **17.5%** if the hike sticks fully and the steel rise is the only cost change.
  - At a 5% realised hike: 105 − 73.5 − 17.2 = 14.3, i.e. **13.6%**.
  - **Reading:** ACE needs roughly 8–10% of realised price to hold 15% OPM against a 20% steel rise. That is why it took ~10–12%.

**Archetype 3 (BEML): Revenue = opening order book × execution rate + short-cycle sales (spares, services); EBITDA = Σ segment revenue × contract margin − fixed cost (wages, R&D) − provisions.**

### C.2 Worked example — BEML FY26 → FY27 [E]
- Opening OB (Mar-25) ≈ 15,896 − 5,440 + 4,351 = **~₹14,807 Cr**, ignoring cancellations and price variations [E].
- FY26 execution rate = 4,351 ÷ 14,807 = **29%** [E].
- FY27: the company says ₹5,300 Cr of the Mar-26 book is executable in FY27 [primer]. +30% guidance [V-snip] → **~₹5,650 Cr** revenue, which needs ~₹350 Cr of in-year book-and-bill (spares, short-cycle mining).
- At the guided **13% EBITDA = ~₹735 Cr**. Less D&A ~₹95 Cr and interest ~₹55 Cr, plus other income ~₹25 Cr: PBT ~₹610 Cr, PAT ~₹455 Cr at 25.5% tax [E]. On today's ₹15,419 Cr m-cap that is **~34× FY27E-if-guidance-is-met**, vs 86× trailing.
- **Downside case:** revenue +12% (₹4,870 Cr) at 9% OPM → OP ₹438 Cr, PAT ~₹235 Cr → ~66× [E].
- **Order-book arithmetic:** FY27 inflow guidance ₹20,000 Cr vs FY26 inflow ~₹5,440 Cr (3.7×) and Q1 FY27 ₹1,181 Cr [V; primer]. Pipeline ₹40,000 Cr [V-snip]. Meeting it needs at least one large metro or Vande Bharat award in H2 [E].

### C.3 Worked example — a backhoe loader through the value chain (volume CE, JCB-type) [E composite]
- List price ~₹35 lakh ex-showroom before GST; GST at 18% (post-Sep-2025; was 28%) → ~₹41.3 lakh (was ~₹44.8 lakh) [E].
- Dealer margin ~5–8%. OEM net ~₹32–33 lakh. OEM EBITDA ~16% → ~₹5.2 lakh per machine (JCB-type) [E, from JCB FY26 OPM ~16% V].
- Buyer: 85–90% financed [V]. A ~₹30 lakh loan at 12–14% over 4 years ≈ ₹79,000–82,000 EMI [E, standard annuity]. A rental hirer billing ~₹1,000–1,500/hour × 150–200 hours/month covers it at ~60–70% utilisation [E — FLAG, indicative hire rates].

### C.4 Worked example — one metro car at BEML [E composite]
- BMRCL Phase 2: **₹7.74 Cr per car + ₹0.13 Cr/car/yr maintenance** [V-snip indiainfrahub]. A 6-car trainset is ~₹46 Cr. The Dec-2025 add-on of 6 trainsets (36 cars) for ₹414 Cr = ₹11.5 Cr/car incl. scope changes/inflation [V-snip; E].
- Bought-outs (propulsion, brakes, HVAC, doors, signalling interfaces) ~55–65% of the price. If BEML's contribution is ~15–20% and fixed-cost absorption needs ~250 cars/yr [E], a slip of 3–6 months in deliveries turns a profitable year into a loss quarter. That is the Q1/Q3 pattern and the ₹80 Cr restart provision [V psuconnect].

## 4.2 Company deep-dives

### 4.2.1 BEML Limited (BEML · 500048)

**(a) Management-commentary digest.** Sources: Q1 FY27 call 13-Aug-2026 (P1), investor meet 10-Jun-2026 (P2, P3), CARE 28-Jul-2026 (P5). Paraphrased.

*Guidance and targets*
- **FY27 revenue:** Q1 grew 29%, and the CMD "expects a similar growth in every quarter… high 20s if all goes well" (P1). In June the year opened with **₹5,500 Cr of executable orders, "first time in the history of the company"** (P3). Part 1's "+30%" is the June-meet framing; the August wording is "high 20s".
- **FY27 EBITDA:** "back to at least 13%" (P1). In June the CMD called **~16% "sustainable"**, with **break-even revenue ~₹4,000 Cr**: above that, contribution drops "exponentially" to the bottom line (P3). Long-run aspirations: 20% CAGR, 17–18% EBITDA and lower working capital (P1).
- **FY27 order inflow:** ₹20,000 Cr is a stretch goal "with ~30–40% probability" (P1). Expected mix: Rail & Metro 65–70%, Defence ~20%, international ~5%, mining 5–6% (P1).
- **Live pipeline (P1):**
  - Six metro tenders: Chennai, Delhi RS20 and RS21, UP Metro extension, Maha Metro (Pune/Nagpur), MMRDA Line 12.
  - Metro exports ~₹15,000 Cr (two opportunities).
  - 16 HSR trainsets (B28, 280 km/h), bid submitted.
  - Defence ₹4,000–5,000 Cr: ARV WZT-3 overhaul (DAC cleared 230), command-post vehicles, self-propelled mine burier, tank transporters, LAMV (summer trials cleared), QRSAM support vehicles ₹600–700 Cr. Pinaka is excluded.
  - Mining: L1 in ~₹900 Cr, bidding ₹500–600 Cr more, plus West Asia mining exports.
  - The **MRVC (Mumbai suburban) tender was cancelled.**
- **FY27 capex:** ₹600+ Cr. The "Aditya" HSR complex took ~₹250 Cr. Bhopal ("Brahma", ~₹900 Cr Phase 1) civil tender is to be finalised by ~Oct–Nov 2026; first output 18 months after construction starts (~24 months to completion); debt-funded (P1, P5). The Chhattisgarh cabinet allotted **~80 acres at Mungeli (near Bilaspur)** for a wheeled mining-equipment plant (trucks, sprinklers, graders), close to CIL, which takes ~80% of BEML's mining machines (P1).
- **R&D:** ~7% of revenue, ">₹300 Cr" in FY27 (P1).

*What went wrong in FY26 (P3, P5)*
1. The OB target was ~₹20,000 Cr; the year ended at ₹15,896 Cr. An export order and a MoD order (one signed 27-Mar) spilled into April; HEMM orders where BEML was L1 were not finalised; the 16-trainset HSR order was not placed.
2. M&C revenue fell because CIL did not order during an "unusually long and shifted monsoon".
3. ~₹250 Cr of one-offs: (i) a "one-time correction" in two foreign-currency metro contracts, one won in 2018 at ₹60/$ and €65 without a PVC clause; (ii) a gratuity provision under the new labour codes.

Management expects FX realisation and the PVC clause to recover ≥30% of the hit, and another 10–15% if the rupee keeps weakening. The August call says "20–25% in FY27".

*Business mix and margins (P1, P3)*
- BEML is **exempt from Ind AS 108 segment reporting** (defence-production exemption). It gives only revenue by vertical and growth rates, never segment margins.
- HEMM averaged ~45% of revenue historically (peak 61% in FY17; 40% in FY26). The CMD targets 65–70% Rail & Metro + Defence and ~30% HEMM.
- Margin ranking: **"sustenance" (spares and services) is the highest-margin line; exports are second** (export EBITDA planned ~20%, 25% if the dollar rises).
- Mining product contracts are fixed-price. Long MARC/sustenance contracts (9–12 years) carry annual escalation negotiated with CIL. All newer metro contracts have PVC clauses; the old Mumbai order does not.

*Execution milestones*
- Vande Bharat sleeper: flagged off by the PM on 17-Jan-2026 at Malda (P2); two 16-car BEML rakes commissioned by SWR in Aug-2026; ₹180.6 Cr ICF add-on (Sep-2026) [V-snip]; "two or three more trains can come" (P3).

- HSR prototype: production began ~April 2026; first car body ~Aug-2026; first train ~Jan-2027; testing into ~Aug-2027. Propulsion is local and the IP is 100% BEML's. Seven new corridors (~600 trains): BEML hopes for ~50% (P1).
- Bengaluru Metro Phase 2: CRS inspection done ~12-Aug-2026; 20–25 trains to be supplied in FY27 (P1).
- HAL Light Combat Helicopter fuselages: 48 units over ~3 years (P1).

*Working capital*
- Quarterly revenue runs ~10% / 20% / 30% / 40% (Q4 was ~45% in FY26). The plan is 35% of revenue by H1 and 70% by 9M to cut year-end debtors (P1).

*Guidance*

*Demand*
- Demand was strong until May–June, then the usual monsoon slowdown.
- Growth avenues:
  - the Hydra-crane recovery, after retail hirers' "scepticism" about Stage V electronics "settled in the last 7–8 months";
  - backhoe loaders, forklifts, tower cranes and road machinery;

  - defence execution from August.
- A deficient monsoon can swing the year by 5–10%, visible in Q3.

*Pricing and costs*
- Price increases: January 1–1.5%, March/May 3–4%, June 5–6% (still "work in progress" in July) → ~10%.
- Input inflation is 11–12% ("massive… every possible commodity"). Steel is up ~20%, along with tyres, rubber and plastics. Another ~2% of pricing may follow.
- Q1 gross margin was down ~140 bp YoY.
- If commodities fall back within 6–8 months, part of the price goes back; if they stay up longer, the industry keeps the price.

*Crane-market structure*
- Hydra share: 60–65% in FY25, "a little less than 60%" in FY26, back to ~60% in FY27, ~50/50 in 2–3 years as large industrial users move to safer NG cranes.
- The NG–Hydra price gap is still ~30–35%.
- Mix is moving to higher tonnages (18/20/25-t Hydras), so ASPs "are only going to increase".
- ACE's own volume mix is ~60/40 Hydra/NG.

*Backhoe loaders*
- ~150–160 units a quarter.
- A financing pilot in three or four pockets is "working in the right direction". Management hopes BHL becomes the fastest-growing CMCE line.

- See S14. Upgraded Indian models to KATO standards from Q4 FY27; dedicated KATO-design models in about a year, with a 50–60% localisation target.
- KATO will also source components from India through ACE, an extra revenue stream.

*Crane ADD*
- "No leads so far… still a ray of hope."
- The Ministry of Heavy Industries had encouraged the application. DGTR investigated and recommended duties, and "only in the last leg, the Finance Ministry didn't do it."
- Management attributes this to geopolitics: US tariff pressure, and an India–China thaw after the PM's China visit.

*Strategy*
- The two biggest growth drivers: **inorganic growth** (targets with a moat and "3×–6× growth" potential; "a lot [of cash] available on the books") and **exports**.
- Then domestic growth, the KATO JV (2–3 years), and new demand from data centres (~1.5–2% of revenue), oil storage, nuclear, solar and high-speed rail.

*Supply chain*
- In June, engine and engine-casting suppliers had problems, resolved by July.
- Competitive intensity is "unchanged".

## 4.5 New insights and new primer issues

### New insights the first pass missed
1. **GST 2.0 was not a CE demand stimulus.** Machines in 8426/8427/8429/8430 have been taxed at 18% since Nov-2017. The Sep-2025 cuts hit CE through **cheaper diesel engines, engine parts and tyres** (inputs, all 28% → 18%) and **dumpers/tippers (8704) at 18%** (relevant to BEML rear-dump trucks), plus cement (construction activity). The H1 FY27 recovery is therefore a **Stage V base effect and Hydra normalisation**, not a tax effect. Every "GST offset" sentence in Parts 1–3 should go.
2. **BEML's FY26 earnings collapse is mostly two legacy contracts and a gratuity charge.** Adding back ~₹250 Cr of one-offs to the company's ₹328 Cr EBITDA gives **~₹578 Cr, ~13.3%** — FY25-like [E]. The investment debate is therefore about **execution and working capital**, not a structural margin reset. The new contingent items (other claims +75%; provisional LDs ₹22 Cr) show that the penalty risk has not gone away.
3. **BEML runs with almost no cash.** It had ₹6.7 Cr of cash against ~₹2,990 Cr of receivables and contract assets (~251 days of revenue). It is funded by customer advances, supplier credit (₹1,024 Cr) and ₹292 Cr of short-term debt, and the ~₹900 Cr Bhopal Phase 1 will be debt-funded (CARE). Interest costs and gearing will rise through FY27–FY28 unless collections improve. The ~100% dividend payout in FY26 (₹17.09/share ≈ ₹142 Cr outflow [E]) is a GoI-driven choice that competes with this.
4. **Inside BEML, "construction equipment" is now a spares business.** Machine (EME) sales fell 35% to ₹758 Cr, while spares plus servicing held at ~₹1,000 Cr (23% of revenue) — and management names spares as the highest-margin line. The listed sub-sector's two "CE" companies are a crane maker and a spares-plus-rolling-stock PSU.
5. **Chinese crane imports peaked in FY24 and have since shifted from crawler to truck/AT cranes and tower cranes.** China still supplies ~71% of crane imports and ~51% of CE parts imports. The ADD lapse matters less to ACE's P&C core than the **FY25 tower-crane import surge (US$48 m, 2.6×)**, which hits ACE's ~60%-share tower business directly.
6. **ICEMA's "exports" understate outbound flows.** ~6,400 machines (≈5% of the total) left as non-OEM exports in FY26 — grey or parallel exports, probably of new machines bought domestically [E]. This is also why "domestic" was restated between releases.
7. **The industry turned in Q4 FY26, not Q1 FY27.** Derived Q4 FY26 ICEMA units rose ~4% after −9% in Q3. ACE's revenue turned positive in Q4 FY26 (+7%), and Escorts accelerated from there. Paired with the Union capex series (FY26 RE +4.2% → FY27 BE +11.5%), the writer can date the trough precisely to **Q3 FY26**.
8. **ACE's KATO JV will deconsolidate part of ACE's revenue.** ACE's own truck, crawler and rough-terrain crane business moves into a 50:50 JV, likely equity-accounted. Reported revenue could dip slightly while value is preserved [E — confirm with Q2/Q3 FY27 notes]. Agri remains a drag: 87% material cost and a ₹2.5 Cr result on ₹262 Cr of revenue.
