## 1. What the sub-sector does, the value chain, and where value is captured

**What it is.** Listed Indian "real estate developers" do four economically different things under one NSE sub-sector label:
1. **For-sale residential development** (the bulk of the 19 names by revenue): buy or tie up land, get approvals, register the project under RERA, sell apartments/plots off-plan ("pre-sales" or "bookings"), collect instalments tied to construction milestones, hand over on Occupancy Certificate (OC). Cash comes in years before accounting revenue.
2. **For-sale commercial/strata** (smaller): office floors or retail units sold to investors (common in MMR, NCR — e.g., parts of Sunteck, Anant Raj, TARC, Valor).
3. **Annuity / rent-yielding assets**: Grade-A offices, malls, hotels, data centres, warehousing; held on balance sheet or spun into REITs. Phoenix Mills (malls), NESCO (Bombay Exhibition Centre + IT park), DLF (via DCCDL, a 66.7%:33.3% JV with GIC), Prestige, Brigade, Embassy group (sponsor of Embassy REIT), Oberoi, Anant Raj (data centres) [K, company filings].
4. **Land-bank monetisation / special situations**: companies whose value is an old land bank or a stressed-asset turnaround — Hemisphere Properties (surplus land demerged from Tata Communications/VSNL, owned largely by GoI), Valor Estate (ex-DB Realty), Embassy Developments (ex-Equinox/Indiabulls Real Estate), TARC (ex-Anant Raj demerger), ABREL (ex-Century Textiles' Worli land) [K].

**Value chain (residential).**

| Stage | Who | Typical share of final price [E] | Where margin sits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Land (outright, JDA/JV, society redevelopment, govt auction, SRA) | landowners, farmers, housing societies, mill owners, govt (DDA/MHADA/HSVP/YEIDA) | 15–25% in suburbs/NCR plotted; 30–45% in island-city Mumbai / CBD Bengaluru | **Largest single value pool**; captured either by the landowner (JDA revenue share 25–45%) or by a developer who bought early (DLF Gurugram land at historic cost) |
| Approvals, premiums, FSI/TDR, development charges | state/municipal authorities | 5–8% (pan-India); 12–20% in MMR (fungible FSI, premium FSI, TDR, stamp duty on JDA) | Pure leakage to the state; cut in MMR premiums (Jan-2021, 50% for 1 year) visibly lifted margins [K, Maharashtra GR Jan-2021] |
| Design, construction | EPC contractors (L&T, Shapoorji, Capacite, Ahluwalia, B.L. Kashyap, Tata Projects), in-house construction (Sobha is backward integrated) | 30–40% | Contractor margin 8–12% EBITDA [K, listed EPC filings]; developer captures margin by owning the land and the brand |
| Sales & marketing | channel partners (brokers 2–4% commission), digital, in-house | 3–6% | Brand-led developers cut channel cost; launch-day "sell-outs" lower marketing intensity |
| Finance | construction finance (banks/NBFCs/AIFs at 9–14%), customer advances (interest-free) | 2–5% | Customer advances are the cheapest capital in Indian corporate finance; RERA escrow (70%) ring-fences them |
| Developer margin | developer | 20–35% EBITDA (embedded) | Brand + land cost basis + speed of sale |
| Post-handover: maintenance, resale, rental | facility managers, brokers | n/a | small; some developers retain facility management |

**Where value is captured — the core insight.** In for-sale housing the developer's economic value-add is (a) securing land cheaper than the market (outright buying at the right point in the cycle, or JDA on someone else's land), (b) turning land into cash fast (approvals → launch → sell-out), and (c) brand premium (Godrej/Lodha/DLF/Prestige/Oberoi sell at 10–25% premiums to local developers in the same micro-market [E, broker channel checks widely cited]). The "factory" (construction) is outsourced and earns low margins. Hence the natural KPI set is about land, launches, pre-sales and cash — not revenue and capacity.

In annuity real estate, value is captured by **cap-rate arbitrage**: build at a yield-on-cost of 10–14% and value/REIT at a 7.5–8.5% cap rate (Grade-A office) [E, consistent with REIT valuation reports which use ~7.5–8.0% cap rates for Bengaluru/Mumbai offices, K]. That spread is why Prestige, Brigade, DLF, Phoenix, Embassy group build rental portfolios and monetise via REITs or stake sales.

## 2. Size and growth

**Primary residential sales in the top 7 cities (ANAROCK; MMR, NCR, Bengaluru, Pune, Hyderabad, Chennai, Kolkata).**
- CY2025: ~3,95,625 units (−14% YoY from 4,59,645 in 2024), value **> ₹6.0 lakh crore** (+6% from ~₹5.68 lakh crore), ~US$70bn. Average price ₹9,260/sq ft (+8%). Launches ~4,19,170 units (+2%). Unsold 5,76,617 units (+4%). [V, ANAROCK via Outlook Business]
- CY2023 peak volume: 4,76,530 units (+31%), launches 4,45,770, average price ₹7,080/sq ft (+15%). [V, ANAROCK via Outlook Business]
- 9M CY2026: Q1 ~1,01,675 units (₹1.51 lakh crore) [search snippet, ANAROCK Q1 2026; not fetched]; Q2 ~90,715 units (−6% YoY) [V]; Q3 ~1,00,220 units (+3% YoY), ₹1.55 lakh crore (+2% YoY) [V]. 9M total ≈ **2,92,600 units** [E, sum], ~flat vs 9M CY2025 (~2.87 lakh units per ANAROCK as quoted in press [K]).
- **Value CAGR is much stronger than volume CAGR**: units 2022→2025 grew ~3% CAGR (3.65→3.96 lakh) while value grew from ~₹3.27 lakh crore (2022) [K, ANAROCK Dec-2022] to ~₹6.0 lakh crore (2025) — ~22% CAGR [E]. The 2021–26 upcycle is fundamentally a **price × mix** cycle (luxury share up, average ticket up) rather than a volume boom after 2023.

**Other scopes.**
- Knight Frank (8 cities incl. Ahmedabad): CY2025 sales 3,48,207 units, "broadly steady"; >₹1 crore share 50%; unsold 5,09,815; QTS 5.8 quarters [V]. 9M CY2026: 2,58,238 units (flat YoY), launches 2,79,899 (+4%), QTS 6.1 quarters, >₹1 crore share 55% vs 50% [V].
- ICRA/PropEquity (7 cities, area): FY26 area sold 653 msf (+1.5%); launches 732 msf (+9%); FY27E area sold +2–5% and **sales value +8–11%**, launches +4–7% to ~760–785 msf [V, ICRA 31-Aug-2026]. An earlier ICRA update (Mar-2026) had FY27 area growth of only 0–3% (635–655 msf) and flagged West Asia risk [search snippet, not fetched].
- **Listed developers (28 cos):** FY26 pre-sales ~**₹1.95 lakh crore** (+17% YoY from ">₹1.66 lakh crore") [V, PTI]; FY25 ~₹1.62 lakh crore for 26 cos [search snippet, realtynmore/Shriram Properties PDF]; FY24 ₹1.17 lakh crore for 18 cos (+33% from ~₹0.88 lakh crore in FY23) [V, PTI]. So listed pre-sales roughly **2.2× in three years (FY23→FY26), ~30% CAGR** [E] — far faster than the market, i.e., share gain by listed/branded developers.
- **Whole-sector macro:** Real estate is commonly cited at ~7% of India's GDP, expected to reach ~US$1 trillion market size by 2030 and ~13% of GDP by 2025 (aspirational) [K, IBEF / Knight Frank-NAREDCO]. These are promotional aggregates; treat as indicative. Housing credit outstanding (banks incl. priority-sector housing) was ₹27.2 lakh crore in Mar-2024 vs ₹19.9 lakh crore in Mar-2023 (jump inflated by the HDFC–HDFC Bank merger, Jul-2023) [search snippet of RBI data via Outlook Money; not fetched].
- **Commercial office:** CBRE 2025 leasing a record **82.6 msf** (+~1%) [V]; Cushman & Wakefield 2025 gross leasing ~89 msf; Vestian 78.2 msf absorption [search snippets]. 9M CY2026 CBRE 66.4 msf (+8%, record nine months) [V]; C&W GLV 64.6 msf YTD [V]. India Grade-A office stock is in the ~0.9–1.0 bn sq ft range across the top 7–8 cities [E; see H].
- **REITs:** six listed REITs, combined market cap > **₹2.17 lakh crore** (11-Aug-2026), gross AUM > ₹3.17 lakh crore, > 214 msf, cumulative distributions > ₹34,800 crore [V, Indian REITs Association via FPJ].

**Domestic vs export / India's global share.** Real estate is non-tradable; "exports" exist only as (i) NRI purchases (commonly cited at 10–15% of luxury sales in MMR/Bengaluru/Kerala, [E] from developer commentary) and (ii) foreign capital — GCCs leasing offices (~40% of office demand) and foreign institutional equity in REITs/platforms (Blackstone, Brookfield, GIC, CPPIB, ADIA). India is ~1–2% of global institutional real-estate investment volumes [E]. Listed developers are expanding abroad marginally (Sunteck's proposed Dubai project per PL [V]; Sobha's parent group is Dubai-based; Lodha has London legacy assets) [K].

## 3. Demand drivers (with numbers)

1. **Affordability = income growth vs prices vs mortgage rates.** The 2021–24 boom started from the best affordability in two decades: repo 4.0% (May-2020 to May-2022), home-loan rates ~6.5–7.0%, and prices flat 2014–2020 in real terms [K, RBI; ANAROCK]. The RBI then raised repo 250 bp to 6.50% (May-2022 to Feb-2023), cut 125 bp to 5.25% (Feb-2025 to Dec-2025), held at 5.25% through Apr/Jun/Aug-2026, and **hiked 25 bp to 5.50% on 7-Oct-2026** with a shift to "calibrated tightening" (CPI projected 5.2% for FY27; crude baseline $95/bbl for H2 FY27; West Asia conflict) [V, Motilal Oswal on RBI MPC]. The October hike is the first since Feb-2023 and is a new headwind for FY27 H2 demand in the mid segment.
2. **Price momentum has slowed sharply.** ANAROCK top-7 average ₹6,150/sq ft (2022) → ₹7,080 (2023) → ₹8,590 (2024) → ₹9,260 (2025) → ₹9,714 (Q3 2026, +7% YoY, only +1% QoQ) [V]. RBI HPI (18 cities, base 2022-23=100): 115.9 in Q4 FY26, +4.2% YoY [V, RBI]; Q1 FY27 +3.6% YoY (index 117.5) [search snippets, RBI press 24-Aug-2026]. Registration-based RBI HPI shows far lower growth than consultants' asking-price series — the consultant series are skewed to new launches in premium micro-markets. Prefer RBI for "house price inflation", ANAROCK/KF for "what developers are getting on launches".
3. **Premiumisation / wealth effect.** Homes > ₹1 crore went from ~half to 55% of Knight Frank 8-city sales (9M 2026) [V]; the < ₹50 lakh band fell to 18% (−14% YoY in units) from ~37% in 2022 [V, KF]. ANAROCK: affordable (< ₹40 lakh) share of top-7 sales fell from 38% (2019) to 18% (2024); luxury (> ₹1.5 crore) rose from 7% to 26% [search snippet, ANAROCK via Outlook]. Drivers: equity-market wealth (demat accounts, IPO/ESOP wealth), post-Covid desire for larger homes, start-up/GCC salaries, NRI demand on a weak rupee.
4. **Jobs: IT/ITeS and GCCs.** Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai are IT-employment cities; GCCs took ~42% of 9M CY2026 office leasing (~28 msf) [V, CBRE] and are the top white-collar job creator. Conversely, 2025–26 IT layoffs and "AI-related disruption" are explicitly cited by ANAROCK for the 2025 volume drop and the Q2 2026 wait-and-watch [V].
5. **Urbanisation and household formation.** India's urban population ~36% (2021) heading to ~40% by 2030 [K, UN WUP]; urban housing shortage ~18.8 mn units in 2012 (Kundu committee), ~10 mn by later estimates, overwhelmingly in EWS/LIG — which listed developers do not serve [K, MoHUA]. PMAY-U 2.0 (approved Aug-2024): 1 crore urban houses, ₹2.30 lakh crore central assistance [K, Cabinet release Aug-2024].
6. **Investor demand & "safe asset" behaviour.** Land and plotted products (Gurugram, Dholera, Ayodhya, Goa, Alibaug) attracted speculative money 2023–25; NCR plotted and luxury launches (DLF Privana/The Dahlias, Signature, Godrej, M3M) sold out on launch day [K, company releases]. Budget 2024 changed LTCG on property to 12.5% without indexation (with grandfathering choice for pre-23-Jul-2024 assets of resident individuals) — neutral-to-negative for investors [K, Finance (No. 2) Act 2024].
7. **Geopolitics/sentiment.** ANAROCK cites the West Asia conflict and supply-chain effects for the Q2 2026 −6% YoY [V]; the RBI cites oil at ~$95–100 [V].

**City engines (ANAROCK CY2025 units [V]):** MMR 1,27,875 (32% of 7 cities); Pune 65,135 (16%); Bengaluru 62,205 (16%); NCR 57,220 (14%); Hyderabad 44,885 (11%); Chennai 22,180 (6%); Kolkata 16,125 (4%). In Q3 2026, MMR + Bengaluru = 48% of units [V].

## 4. Supply side and cost structure

**Launches and inventory.** Supply has exceeded sales for 16 consecutive quarters in Knight Frank's 8 cities [V]. ANAROCK top-7 unsold stock bottomed around end-2024 (5,53,073) and rose to 5,76,617 (end-2025) and **~6,30,600 (Q3 2026, +12% YoY)**; months of inventory ~19 overall, Hyderabad ~28 months [V, ANAROCK Q3 2026]. Q3 2026 launches ~1,14,300 (+18% YoY) vs sales ~1,00,200 — gap ~14,100 units [V]. PropEquity shows the opposite for Q3 2026 (launches −15% QoQ to 98,165, below sales of 1,03,170 across 9 cities) [V] — source divergence: ANAROCK counts launches by project phase across 7 metros; PropEquity's 9-city frame splits MMR into Mumbai/Thane/Navi Mumbai. **Both agree on soft volumes; ANAROCK is the more widely-used benchmark and its inventory series is longer, so it is used as the base case here.**
- Supply mix Q2 2026 (ANAROCK): ₹80 lakh–1.5 crore 27%; ₹1.5–2.5 crore 25%; > ₹2.5 crore 22%; ₹40–80 lakh 19%; < ₹40 lakh **6%** [V]. Q3 2026: homes > ₹80 lakh ≈ 69% of launches; > ₹2.5 crore 14%; the article also says affordable < ₹40 lakh was 14% of total supply — inconsistent with the Q2 figure; likely different base (launches vs total supply) [V; flagged].
- **Land.** ANAROCK tracked 126+ land deals for 3,772+ acres in CY2025 vs 133 deals / 2,514 acres in 2024; H1 2025 alone 2,898 acres worth ~₹30,885 crore with ~₹1.47 lakh crore revenue potential and ~233 msf development potential; 17 of 76 H1-2025 deals were JDAs over 782 acres [search snippets, ANAROCK via Outlook Money/TheWeek; not fetched]. Listed developers (Godrej, Prestige, Lodha, Signature, Brigade, Puravankara, Sobha) were the main buyers — the "business development (BD)" arms race that sets up FY26–FY28 launches.
- **Approvals bottleneck.** PL notes Prestige's key FY27 risk is "approval delays (RERA)... not demand"; Oberoi's H2 FY27 launches are "subject to requisite approvals" [V, PL 8-Oct-2026]. Approvals, not demand, are the binding constraint on launch timing for most large players in MMR.

**Construction cost.** Hard construction cost for a Grade-A high-rise ranges ~₹3,500–6,000 per sq ft of saleable area (higher in Mumbai, luxury specs ₹7,000–10,000+) [E, developer commentary; generic house-construction guides quote ₹1,800–3,500/sq ft for low-rise, search snippets]. Inputs: cement ~10–12%, steel ~12–15%, labour ~20–25%, MEP/finishes/façade the balance [E]. GST 2.0 (22-Sep-2025) cut GST on cement from 28% to 18% [K, GST Council 56th meeting] — a ~1% saving on total construction cost because developers cannot claim ITC on residential (GST at 5% without ITC). Construction costs rose an estimated 30–40% between 2019 and 2024 on steel/cement spikes and labour [K, ANAROCK/Colliers commentary; exact figure not fetched — see H]. Price increases (ANAROCK top-7 +51% 2022→Q3-2026) have comfortably outrun cost.

**Capacity/utilisation analogue.** "Capacity" in this business = approved, launch-ready GDV (gross development value) in the pipeline; "utilisation" = absorption (sales/inventory), measured as months/quarters of inventory (MOI/QTS). Top-7 MOI ~19 months (Q3 2026) [V]; Knight Frank QTS 6.1 quarters (9M 2026) [V]; ICRA years-to-sell ~1.4–1.6 years for FY27 [search snippet]. 2014–2019 trough had MOI of 40–50+ months in NCR [K, ANAROCK].

## 5. Industry structure and concentration

- **Extremely fragmented, consolidating fast.** RERA registrations across India: ~84,500 projects and ~64,100 agents by Jun-2022 [K, MoHUA RERA tracker 11-Jun-2022]; a widely-quoted (unverified) 2025 tally of ~1.51 lakh projects, ~1.07 lakh agents and ~1.47 lakh complaints disposed at the launch of the Unified RERA Portal (4-Sep-2025) [search snippet; treat as indicative]. Thousands of developers exist; most are single-city, single-project SPVs.
- **Listed share of the top-7 market [E]:** 28 listed cos' FY26 pre-sales ~₹1.95 lakh crore vs ANAROCK top-7 sales value ~₹6.0 lakh crore (CY2025) → **~30–33%** upper bound (listed pre-sales also include tier-2 cities, commercial and plotted sales outside ANAROCK's frame, so true top-7 share is perhaps 25–30%). In FY20 the equivalent was ~10–15% [E, from FY20 listed bookings ~₹30–35k crore vs ~₹2.6 lakh crore market, K]. Share has roughly doubled since RERA/GST/NBFC crisis squeezed unorganised developers.
- **Top-5 concentration within listed:** Godrej 34,171 + Prestige 30,024 + Lodha 20,530 + DLF 20,143 + Signature 8,250 = ₹1,13,118 crore = **58%** of ₹1.95 lakh crore ("nearly 60%" per PTI) [V]. Godrej alone = 17.5% of listed pre-sales and ~5–6% of top-7 value [E].
- **Large unlisted players** matter in every city: M3M, Tata Housing, Shapoorji Pallonji, Hiranandani, Rustomjee (listed as Keystone), Runwal, Kalpataru (listed 2025), Piramal Realty, Raheja (K Raheja Corp — sponsor of Mindspace REIT), Adani Realty, Hero Realty, Sattva, Salarpuria, Total Environment, My Home (Hyderabad), Aparna, Rajapushpa, Casagrand (Chennai), Bhartiya, Tribeca/Trump-branded projects. NCR luxury in 2023–25 was dominated by DLF, M3M, Smartworld, Signature, Godrej, Elan, Whiteland [K]. MNC developers are rare in for-sale housing (FDI allowed, but foreign capital prefers platforms/JVs: Brookfield, Blackstone, GIC, CPPIB, ADIA, Mitsui Fudosan with Rustomjee/Shapoorji, Sumitomo in BKC offices) [K].
- **"China" angle:** none in demand or supply (no Chinese developers; some Chinese equipment in formwork/lifts/tiles; India's anti-dumping duties on certain steel/glass/ceramic inputs matter at the margin) [E].

## 6. Business-model archetypes and how their economics differ

| Archetype | Listed examples | Land model | Embedded EBITDA margin on pre-sales [E] | Capital intensity / ROE drivers | Key risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pan-India branded residential "BD machine"** | Godrej Properties, Prestige (residential arm), Lodha (MMR + Pune + Bengaluru), Brigade, Sobha, Puravankara, ABREL | Mix: outright purchases, JDAs, JVs, society redevelopment | 22–32% | High asset turns if launch cadence holds; ROE 10–20%; needs constant land replenishment (₹10–20k crore/yr BD for Godrej-scale) | Over-paying for land at cycle peak; launch delays; working capital |
| **Legacy land-bank luxury** | DLF (Gurugram), Oberoi (Mumbai land at old cost), ABREL (Worli), Anant Raj (Delhi-NCR land) | Land on books at historic cost | 40–60% | Very high margins and cash conversion; growth capped by approvals and pace of land conversion | Concentration in one micro-market; luxury demand cyclicality |
| **Luxury MMR / redevelopment** | Lodha (South/Central Mumbai), Oberoi, Sunteck (BKC/Nepean Sea Road), Valor | Redevelopment (society/SRA/cessed buildings), outright | 30–45% | Approvals-heavy; long gestation; high premiums paid to state | Approval risk, high ticket demand risk |
| **Plotted / affordable-premium NCR** | Signatureglobal (Gurugram affordable → mid/premium), TARC (luxury now), Anant Raj | Licensed colonies (DDJAY plotted, affordable housing policy) | 25–35% | Fast cycle (plots deliverable in 2–3 years), high cash conversion | Haryana licensing policy; speculative demand; affordable segment shrinkage |
| **Asset-light JDA/DM specialists** | Godrej (substantial JDA share), Puravankara (redevelopment), Sri Lotus (Mumbai redevelopment boutique), Embassy Developments (JDAs, Embassy group land) | Revenue-share or area-share JDA; development management fees | 15–25% on gross bookings (higher on own share) | ROCE 20–30% because no land capital; but less GDV ownership | Landowner disputes; lower absolute profit |
| **Annuity — offices** | DLF (DCCDL), Prestige, Brigade, Embassy (via REIT sponsor), Phoenix (offices), NESCO (IT park) | Owned land | Rental EBITDA margin 75–90% | Funded by LRD (lease rental discounting) at 8–9%; value realised via cap-rate (7.5–8.5%) and REIT/stake sales | Vacancy, WFH/AI-driven demand change, SEZ rules |
| **Annuity — retail malls** | Phoenix Mills (market leader), Prestige, Brigade, DLF (malls), Nexus REIT | Owned | 60–75% (consumption-linked rent: min. guarantee + revenue share) | Rent tied to consumption growth; long ramp | Consumption slowdown, e-commerce |
| **Land/special-situation monetisers** | Hemisphere Properties (GoI-controlled surplus land, ~739 acres incl. Pune/Delhi/Chennai/Kolkata [K, company]), Valor Estate (ex-DB Realty, Mumbai slum/land assets), Embassy Developments (ex-Equinox), TARC | Old land, legal entanglements | n.m. | Value = land monetisation timeline × legal outcomes | Litigation, governance, slow monetisation |
| **Data centres / warehousing** | Anant Raj (Manesar/Panchkula DCs), Prestige/Brigade small; most DC capacity is outside the sub-sector | Owned | 40–60% EBITDA at maturity | Power-intensive, capex/MW ₹40–60 crore incl. fit-out [E] | Tenant concentration, power cost |
| **Exhibitions/events** | NESCO (Bombay Exhibition Centre) | Owned land | 60%+ | Low capex | Event cycles |

**Why economics differ.** In for-sale housing, ROE is a function of (embedded margin × asset turnover × leverage), where asset turnover is set by how fast land converts to cash. A legacy-land player has huge margins but low turns; a BD-machine has lower margins but can compound if it keeps buying land at 15–25% of GDV and selling within 3–5 years. Annuity players trade growth for predictability and are valued on NAV / cap rates / dividend yield (REIT model).

## 7. Regulation and policy

**Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA).** Passed Mar-2016; partly in force 1-May-2016; fully 1-May-2017 [K, MoHUA].
- Registration mandatory for projects > 500 sq m or > 8 apartments (s.3); no advertising/booking before registration.
- **70% escrow (s.4(2)(l)(D))**: 70% of amounts realised from allottees must be deposited in a separate bank account and used only for land and construction of that project; withdrawals in proportion to completion, certified by an engineer, architect and chartered accountant; annual audit.
- Sale on **carpet area** only; no more than **10% advance** before a registered agreement for sale (s.13); defect liability **5 years** (s.14(3)); refund with interest on delay (s.18); quarterly progress updates on state RERA website; penalties up to 10% of project cost; appellate tribunals.
- Effect: killed "build-with-buyers'-money-across-projects" fund diversion; raised working capital needs; favoured balance-sheet-strong developers → consolidation. Weaknesses: uneven state implementation (some states diluted rules), enforcement of refund orders slow (recovery via revenue authorities). Unified RERA Portal launched 4-Sep-2025 [search snippet].

**Ind AS 115 (Revenue from Contracts with Customers), effective 1-Apr-2018 [K, MCA notification Mar-2018].** Revenue recognised "over time" only if one of the s.35 criteria is met — for Indian residential, typically 35(c): the asset has no alternative use AND the developer has an *enforceable right to payment for performance completed to date*. Because Indian agreements/RERA allow buyer cancellation with limited forfeiture, most large developers (DLF, Godrej, Lodha, Prestige, Oberoi, Brigade, Sobha, Signature) concluded the criterion is not met and recognise revenue at a **point in time — on completion/possession (OC + handover)**, i.e. a "completed-contract" outcome. Transition on 1-Apr-2018 reversed previously-recognised POCM revenue into opening retained earnings (large one-time hits for many developers) [K, FY19 annual reports]. Consequences for analysis:
- Reported revenue/PAT lag pre-sales by **3–5 years** and are lumpy (one big project completion swings a quarter).
- **Pre-sales, collections and operating cash flow** are the real-time indicators; P&L margins reflect land bought 4–7 years earlier.
- Customer advances sit as "contract liabilities"; inventory (work-in-progress) balloons in an upcycle — balance-sheet growth precedes P&L growth.
- Some developers present "embedded EBITDA" or "unrecognised revenue from sold units" to bridge the gap.
- Plotted developments complete faster (2–3 years) → quicker revenue recognition.

**GST.** From 1-Apr-2019: **5% without ITC** on under-construction residential (non-affordable), **1% without ITC** on affordable housing (carpet ≤ 60 sq m in metros / ≤ 90 sq m elsewhere and value ≤ ₹45 lakh); deemed land abatement of 1/3 (so 5% effective on gross value already reflects 7.5% on construction share); completed units with OC exempt; old 12%/8% with-ITC optional only for ongoing projects at the time; 80% inputs must be from registered suppliers or reverse-charge applies [K, GST Council 33rd/34th meeting, Feb/Mar-2019]. Commercial under-construction: 12% with ITC. Rent on commercial property: 18%. GST 2.0 (effective 22-Sep-2025) cut cement 28%→18% [K].

**Direct tax.** Sec 24(b) home-loan interest deduction ₹2 lakh (self-occupied, old regime); Sec 80C principal; Sec 80EEA (expired); Sec 80-IBA 100% profit deduction for approved affordable projects (approvals up to 31-Mar-2022) [K]. LTCG on property: from 23-Jul-2024 12.5% without indexation; resident individuals/HUFs can choose 20% with indexation for assets acquired before that date [K, Finance (No.2) Act 2024]. Sec 43CA/50C safe-harbour 10% circle-rate variance. TDS 1% under s.194-IA on purchases ≥ ₹50 lakh. Sec 54/54F reinvestment exemption capped at ₹10 crore from FY24 [K, Finance Act 2023].

**Stamp duty** (state subject): 5–7% + ~1% registration; women-buyer concessions in Delhi, Haryana, UP, Maharashtra (1%); circle/ready-reckoner rates revised annually. Maharashtra cut stamp duty to 2% (Sep–Dec 2020) and 3% (Jan–Mar 2021) — triggered the MMR sales surge of late-2020 [K, GoM].

**Land & approvals.** RFCTLARR Act 2013 (land acquisition compensation 2–4× market) raised land assembly costs [K]. State-specific: Maharashtra UDCPR 2020 / DCPR 2034 (Mumbai), 33(7)/33(9)/33(11) redevelopment schemes, premium FSI; Haryana DDJAY plotted colonies, affordable housing policy 2013, TOD; Karnataka khata/B-khata regularisation; Telangana TS-bPASS; UP YEIDA. Premium concessions (Maharashtra 50% premium waiver, 2021) lift margins directly.

**Housing finance & credit.** RBI repo path above; banks cannot finance land acquisition by developers (RBI CRE norms) — land is funded by equity, NBFCs/AIFs or customer money; CRE exposure carries higher risk weights; LRD for rented assets. SWAMIH Investment Fund I (Nov-2019, ₹25,000 crore last-mile fund for stalled projects; completed ~50,000+ homes) and SWAMIH-2 (Budget 2025, ₹15,000 crore) [K, MoF].

**PMAY.** PMAY-Urban (Jun-2015) sanctioned ~1.18 crore houses; CLSS interest subsidy (closed Mar-2021/Mar-2022) supported affordable demand; PMAY-U 2.0 (Aug/Sep-2024) — 1 crore houses, ₹2.30 lakh crore central assistance, interest subsidy scheme reintroduced [K, PIB].

**FDI.** 100% automatic route in construction-development (with lock-in of 3 years per phase, relaxed), completed projects for operation; FDI in "real estate business" (land trading, TDR trading) prohibited [K, FDI policy].

**REITs / InvITs.** SEBI REIT Regulations 2014; first REIT (Embassy Office Parks) listed 1-Apr-2019; Mindspace (Aug-2020), Brookfield India (Feb-2021), Nexus Select (May-2023, first retail REIT), Knowledge Realty Trust (Aug-2025, Blackstone–Sattva, largest by area at listing), Bagmane Prime Office REIT (Aug-2026) [K; Bagmane per FPJ V]. Key rules: ≥ 80% of value in completed, rent-generating assets; ≥ 90% of net distributable cash flow distributed (at least half-yearly); leverage cap 49% (above 25% needs AAA and unitholder approval); trading lot 1 unit (2021). SM-REIT framework (Mar-2024): asset size ₹50–500 crore; first SM-REIT scheme (Property Share's PropShare Platina) listed on BSE in Dec-2024 [K]; PropShare Celestia listed Apr-2026 [search snippet]. SEBI reclassified REITs as "equity" for mutual-fund/index purposes from 1-Jan-2026 [K, SEBI board Sep-2025 — verify]. Dividends from REIT SPVs tax-exempt for unitholders if SPV is on old regime; interest and rental distributions taxable; capital repayment taxed via cost reduction from FY24 [K].

**Insolvency.** IBC 2016; SC's *Pioneer Urban* (Aug-2019) upheld homebuyers as financial creditors; 2020 amendment requires ≥ 100 homebuyers or 10% of a project to file; project-wise insolvency approach adopted in *Supertech* (2022) [K].

## 8. History and cycles (with dates)

1. **1990s–2004: unorganised era.** Cash-heavy land transactions, no consumer protection; Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru dominated by local builders.
2. **2005–2008 boom.** Press Note 2 (Mar-2005) opened construction-development FDI; IT-led demand; SEZ mania; DLF IPO Jul-2007 raised ₹9,187 crore (then India's largest IPO) at ~₹525/share; Unitech, Parsvnath, Omaxe, HDIL, Indiabulls RE, Purva, Sobha, Puravankara listed 2006–07. Land-bank valuations peaked in Jan-2008 [K].
3. **2008–09 GFC bust.** Realty index fell >80% from Jan-2008 to Mar-2009; developers launched "affordable" products and raised QIPs at the bottom (2009) [K].
4. **2010–2013 stagflation & governance problems.** 2G-related scandals touched Unitech/DB Realty (2011); interest rates high (repo 8% 2011–13); land acquisition act 2013; launches outran demand; NCR investors and delayed projects (Jaypee, Amrapali, Unitech, Supertech) piled up [K].
5. **2014 volume peak, then 2014–2020 long winter.** ANAROCK top-7 sales 3.43 lakh (2014) → ~3.08 (2015) → ~2.44 (2016) → ~2.11 (2017) → ~2.48 (2018) → 2.61 (2019) → 1.38 (2020) [2014/2019/2020 search snippets of ANAROCK; 2015–18 K, ANAROCK annual releases — see H]. Prices flat in nominal terms; NCR MOI > 50 months.
6. **2016–17 triple reset.** Demonetisation (8-Nov-2016) hit cash-funded land/resale; RERA (1-May-2017); GST (1-Jul-2017). Thousands of small developers exited.
7. **2017–2019 stressed developers.** Jaypee Infratech admitted to IBC (Aug-2017); SC's Amrapali judgment (23-Jul-2019) cancelled RERA registration and appointed NBCC; government allowed to take over Unitech's board (Jan-2020); Supertech twin towers (Noida) demolished 28-Aug-2022 [K].
8. **2018–2020 NBFC crisis.** IL&FS default (Sep-2018), DHFL (Jun-2019), Altico, Reliance Home — construction-finance and land-finance dried up; developers dependent on NBFC structured debt (Indiabulls RE, Omaxe, HDIL via PMC Bank, Sep-2019) suffered. SWAMIH fund (Nov-2019). Listed branded developers gained share.
9. **Ind AS 115 (Apr-2018) & GST rate cut (Apr-2019).**
10. **Covid (Mar-2020) → upcycle start.** Repo to 4.0% (May-2020); Maharashtra stamp-duty cut (Sep-2020 to Mar-2021); work-from-home drove demand for bigger homes; sales troughed at 1.38 lakh in 2020 [snippet].
11. **2021–2023 upcycle.** 2.37 lakh (2021) → 3.65 lakh (2022, beat 2014 peak) → **4.77 lakh (2023, record)** [V/snippet]. RBI hiked 250 bp (May-2022 to Feb-2023) but demand held. Lodha IPO (Apr-2021); listed bookings ₹0.88 → ₹1.17 lakh crore (FY23→FY24) [V].
12. **2024–2025 "value not volume."** 2024 units 4.60 lakh (−4%), value ₹5.68 lakh crore; 2025 units 3.96 lakh (−14%), value > ₹6 lakh crore (+6%) [V]. Luxury >₹1.5 crore share rose to ~26–30% [snippet]. Land-buying spree: 3,772+ acres in 2025 [snippet]. Repo cut 125 bp (Feb–Dec 2025) [K]. Knowledge Realty Trust IPO (Aug-2025) [K].
13. **2026: plateau and new headwinds.** Q1 +7% YoY, Q2 −6% (West Asia conflict, IT/AI job anxiety), Q3 +3% [V]; launches strong (+18% YoY Q3) and unsold stock up to ~6.3 lakh [V]; listed Q1 FY27 bookings −21% YoY on DLF/Prestige launch timing [V]; RBI hike to 5.50% (7-Oct-2026) [V]; office leasing at record 9M pace (66.4 msf, CBRE) [V]; REIT market cap > ₹2.17 lakh crore with Bagmane listing [V].

**Cycle anatomy (author's synthesis [E]).** Indian residential cycles have been ~10–12 years trough-to-trough (2003–2009 boom/bust; 2009–2014 sugar-high; 2014–2020 winter; 2020– upcycle). Leading indicators: (a) affordability (EMI/income), (b) MOI crossing ~24 months, (c) land prices and BD intensity, (d) launch-to-sales ratio > 1.1 for 4+ quarters, (e) price growth decelerating below wage growth. In late-2026: (b) at ~19 months (rising), (d) > 1 for 16 quarters (KF), (e) consultant prices +7% vs RBI HPI +3.6% — **signals consistent with a mature, late-cycle plateau, not a collapse**, because developer balance sheets are far stronger than in 2013 (see §9 net debt).

## 9. Native KPIs — definitions, formulas, ranges

(Ranges are author's synthesis [E] from FY22–FY26 disclosures of the large listed developers; good/average/weak are for a scaled listed developer.)

| KPI | Definition / formula | Good | Average | Weak | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-sales (bookings) value** | ₹ value of agreements signed in the period (net of cancellations), developer's economic share | growth > 20% YoY with ≥ 50% from sustenance | 10–20% | < 0% / launch-dependent | Not revenue; check cancellations and JDA share inclusion |
| **Pre-sales volume (msf / units)** | area or units booked | | | | Value ÷ volume = realisation |
| **Realisation (₹/sq ft)** | pre-sales value ÷ area sold | rising with mix + like-for-like price | flat | falling on mix | Mix-driven: Oberoi ~₹46,000; Lodha ~₹15–20k; Brigade ~₹14,400; Prestige ~₹11–14k (PL Q1 FY27) [V] |
| **Collections** | cash received from customers in period | ≥ 75–90% of pre-sales over a cycle | 60–75% | < 60% | Construction-linked plans lag pre-sales by 2–4 years |
| **Collection efficiency** | collections ÷ (opening receivables + billing due) | > 95% | 85–95% | < 85% | Disclosed by few |
| **Operating cash flow (OCF) / pre-sales** | (collections − construction − overheads − taxes) ÷ pre-sales | > 25% | 15–25% | < 15% | Before land/BD spend; Godrej FY27 guide: OCF ₹9,000 crore on ₹39,000 bookings ≈ 23% (snippet) |

| **Land-bank / BD additions** | GDV of new projects added (outright + JDA) | BD ≥ 1.5× pre-sales | 1–1.5× | < 1× (shrinking) | Godrej added ₹20–30k crore GDV/yr FY24–26 [K] |
| **Land-bank years** | total pipeline GDV ÷ annual pre-sales | 4–7 yrs | 2–4 | < 2 or > 10 (dead capital) | |
| **Unsold inventory / months of inventory (MOI)** | unsold units ÷ trailing average monthly sales | < 15 months | 15–24 | > 30 | Market: ~19 months Q3 2026 (ANAROCK) [V]; KF QTS 6.1 quarters [V] |
| **Launch-to-sales ratio** | units launched ÷ units sold | 0.9–1.1 | 1.1–1.3 | > 1.3 | Market Q3 2026 ~1.14 (ANAROCK) [V] |
| **Net debt / equity** | (gross debt − cash & liquid investments) ÷ equity | < 0.2 / net cash | 0.2–0.6 | > 1.0 | Lodha 0.20× (Jun-2026, snippet); DLF, Oberoi net cash/low [K] |
| **Net debt / collections** | net debt ÷ annual collections | < 0.3× | 0.3–0.8× | > 1× | Better than ND/EBITDA given accounting lag |
| **Cancellations %** | cancelled bookings ÷ gross bookings | < 5% | 5–10% | > 10% | Rising cancellations = stress signal |
| **Rental income / exit rental** | annualised rent from leased area; exit rental = run-rate at full lease | growth > 15% | 8–15% | < 5% | Prestige exit rentals ₹11bn → ₹40bn by FY28/29E (PL) [V] |
| **Occupancy (office/mall)** | leased ÷ leasable area | > 90% | 80–90% | < 80% | Pan-India office vacancy ~11–16% depending on source |
| **Exit rental yield / cap rate** | NOI ÷ asset value | | 7.5–8.5% offices; 7.5–9% malls | | REIT DPU yield 6–7.5% |
| **Trading/consumption (malls)** | tenant sales; rent-to-sales ratio | rent/sales 10–14% | | > 16% (stressed tenants) | Phoenix KPI |
| **NAV / P/NAV** | Σ DCF of projects + land + annuity at cap rate − net debt | | | | Market often pays 1.2–2.5× NAV for BD machines |
| **ROE / RoCE** | PAT ÷ avg equity | > 18% | 10–18% | < 10% | Godrej targets 20% ROE by FY28 (snippet) |

Formula notes:
- **Implied revenue visibility** = Σ (sold-but-unrecognised bookings) — disclosed by some as "revenue to be recognised from sold units".
- **Cash surplus from ongoing projects** = (unbilled receivables from sold units + value of unsold inventory) − (cost to complete). This is the most useful number to test a balance sheet in a downturn.
- **Pre-sales growth decomposition** = Δvolume (msf) × Δrealisation (mix + price).

## 10. Global lens

- **China — the cautionary tale.** Property and related activity were estimated at ~25–30% of China's GDP at peak [K, Rogoff & Yang 2020]. "Three red lines" (Aug-2020) cut leverage; Evergrande defaulted (Dec-2021) and was ordered to liquidate by the Hong Kong High Court (29-Jan-2024); Country Garden defaulted on USD bonds (Oct-2023); new-home sales by value fell >50% from the 2021 peak [K, NBS/press]. Mechanism: pre-sales funds were used for land and other projects (no strict escrow), developers ran 80%+ net gearing, and local governments relied on land sales. **India's RERA 70% escrow is precisely the anti-China rule**; Indian listed developers today run net debt/equity ~0–0.6× vs > 100% for Chinese peers in 2020. India's real estate is ~7% of GDP [K] — far smaller systemic weight.
- **US homebuilders (D.R. Horton, Lennar, PulteGroup, NVR).** Sell completed or near-complete homes, recognise revenue on closing; use lot-option contracts (asset-light land, NVR's model); gross margins ~20–27%, ROEs 15–25%; mortgage rate is the swing factor [K]. India's JDA model is the closest analogue to US lot options.
- **UK housebuilders (Barratt Redrow, Persimmon, Taylor Wimpey).** Hold 5–7-year land banks, margins 15–25%, valued at 1–2× book; Help-to-Buy subsidy ended 2023 [K]. Indian listed developers trade at much higher P/B (3–8×) reflecting growth, consolidation and market share gains [E].
- **Dubai.** Off-plan escrow law (2007) — similar philosophy to RERA; Indian developers (Sobha group's origin, Sunteck's planned project) and Indian NRI buyers link the two markets [K/V].
- **REIT yields.** India listed office REIT distribution yields ~6–7.5% vs 10-yr G-sec ~6.3–6.7% (2025–26) — a spread of ~0–100 bp, compared with US office REIT stress (vacancy ~20% post-WFH) and Singapore S-REIT yields ~6–7% [E/K]. India's office market is the global outlier: record leasing, falling vacancy (Vestian: 13.9% → 10.8% in 2025, snippet), GCC-led demand.
- **Japan/Singapore/HK developers** (Mitsui Fudosan, CapitaLand, Sun Hung Kai) blend development + recurring income — the model DLF/Prestige/Brigade/Phoenix emulate.

## 11. Technology and disruption

- **Construction technology.** Aluminium formwork ("Mivan") cuts slab cycles to ~7 days vs ~14–20 days conventional, enabling 3–4-year high-rise delivery [K, industry practice]; precast/PPVC (Sobha, Godrej Construction, L&T), tunnel formwork; post-tensioned slabs; BIM. Sobha's in-house manufacturing (glazing, interiors, concrete products) is a differentiator.
- **Green buildings.** IGBC/GRIHA/LEED ratings are now table stakes for Grade-A offices (REITs report > 80–90% green-certified portfolios) and increasingly for premium housing; some states give extra FSI for green ratings [K]. Embodied carbon in cement/steel is the next pressure point.
- **Proptech.** Listing portals (99acres/Info Edge, MagicBricks, Housing.com), brokers/aggregators (Square Yards, NoBroker, ANAROCK's own brokerage), digital booking and EOI (expression of interest) campaigns that now drive launch-day sell-outs; channel-partner platforms; CRM/AI pricing. Fractional ownership was pulled under SEBI via SM-REITs (2024).
- **AI as a demand risk.** ANAROCK and others now cite AI-related IT job uncertainty as a reason for Bengaluru/Hyderabad/Pune buyer caution [V]; conversely AI drives GCC and data-centre demand (offices, DCs).
- **Data centres.** India DC capacity ~1.3–1.5 GW (2025), projected 3–5 GW by 2030 [K/E, JLL/CBRE estimates]; real-estate developers (Anant Raj, Lodha's industrial parks, Prestige/Brigade land) supply powered land/shells to hyperscalers.
- **Flex workspaces** (WeWork India, Awfis, Smartworks, IndiQube — all listed 2024–25) are now a top source of office absorption — CBRE says flex is "the largest demand driver" in Q3 2026 [V].

## 12. What to watch (next 4–8 quarters)

1. **RBI path after the 7-Oct-2026 hike** (next MPC 2–4 Dec-2026): bank EBLR resets → EMIs; a second hike would hit sub-₹1.5 crore demand most.
2. **ANAROCK Q4 2026 / CY2026 numbers** (late Dec-2026): is 2026 flat (~3.9–4.0 lakh units) with value up mid-single digit? Unsold stock above ~6.3 lakh and MOI > 20 months would be a warning.
3. **Q2/Q3 FY27 pre-sales vs guidance** (Godrej ₹39,000 crore; Lodha ₹24,000 crore; Prestige +15–20%; DLF "margins over growth"); Oberoi's 360 North (~₹8,000–9,000 crore bookings in Q2 per PL) shows luxury depth.
4. **Launch approvals** in MMR (Oberoi, Lodha, Sunteck) and RERA delays (Prestige).
5. **Luxury fatigue**: KF NCR ₹5–10 crore sales −39% (9M 2026) [V]; Gurugram holds 57% of NCR unsold [V]; NCR prices +23% in 2025 [V] — the hottest market is cooling.
6. **Office**: GCC leasing (42% share) and vacancy trend; REIT acquisitions and new REIT IPOs (RMZ, Bharti, K Raheja retail?) — any sponsor monetisation for DLF/Prestige/Brigade.
7. **Land prices and BD discipline**: over-paying for land in 2025–26 would compress embedded margins in FY29–31.
8. **Policy**: GST on under-construction (any move to ITC regime), state stamp duty changes, affordable definition (₹45 lakh cap unchanged since 2017 — widely lobbied to raise), SWAMIH-2 deployment, PMAY-U 2.0 rollout.
9. **Geopolitics/oil**: West Asia conflict, crude $95–100 → inflation → rates; NRI remittances.
10. **Construction cost**: steel/cement/labour inflation vs price growth (+7% consultants, +3.6% RBI HPI).

## A. The 3–5 defining forces (with reasoning)

1. **Formalisation and consolidation (RERA + GST + NBFC crisis + Ind AS 115).** The 2016–2020 regime change raised the cost of being small (escrow, compliance, no NBFC land money) and handed share to listed/branded developers: listed pre-sales went from ~10–15% of top-7 value (FY20) to ~30% (FY26) [E]. This, not the housing cycle alone, explains why listed pre-sales compounded ~30% p.a. FY23–26 while market units were ~flat. *Reasoning:* consolidation can continue even in a flat market — the most important structural tailwind for the listed universe.
2. **Premiumisation (value up, volume flat).** Top-7 value grew ~22% CAGR 2022–25 on ~3% unit CAGR; > ₹1 crore share 55% (KF 9M 2026) [V]; affordable < ₹40 lakh supply only 6% of Q2 2026 launches [V]. Developers chase luxury because land and approval costs are fixed per sq ft and luxury earns higher absolute margin; but it narrows the buyer base and makes demand more wealth-effect (equity market) sensitive.
3. **Land as the profit pool — the BD arms race.** Margin is made when land is bought. 2024–26 saw record land deals (3,772+ acres in 2025) and large equity raises; JDAs (17 of 76 H1-2025 deals) let capital-light players grow. *Reasoning:* the cycle's risk is shifting from "demand" to "price paid for land" — late-cycle land bought at peak pricing is where future write-downs come from.
4. **Rates and affordability regime turn.** 125 bp of cuts (2025) then a hike (Oct-2026) amid oil/West Asia; house price inflation (RBI HPI ~4%) has slowed to below nominal income growth, which is healthy for affordability but caps realisation growth. *Reasoning:* pre-sales growth in FY27–28 must come from launches/market share, not price.
5. **The annuity pivot and REIT-isation.** Record office leasing (CBRE 82.6 msf 2025; 66.4 msf 9M 2026) [V], GCC demand and a REIT market cap > ₹2.17 lakh crore [V] give developers a second engine and a monetisation exit at 7.5–8.5% cap rates. DLF, Prestige, Brigade, Phoenix, NESCO and Embassy group are partly valued on this.

## B. Deep-dive themes

### B1. Pre-sales ≠ revenue: reading a developer through Ind AS 115
- Bookings → collections (10–20% at booking, the rest construction-linked over 3–4 years; RERA caps pre-agreement advance at 10%) → cash sits in RERA escrow (70%) → revenue and profit recognised only on handover (point in time, for most listed developers).
- Implication 1: **P&L lags by 3–5 years.** FY26 revenue reflects FY21–FY23 pre-sales at FY17–FY21 land prices. PL's numbers show the effect: Prestige FY26 pre-sales ~₹30,000 crore vs FY26 consolidated sales of only ~₹12,700 crore; Oberoi FY26 pre-sales ₹5,447 crore vs revenue ~₹6,000 crore (completion catch-up) [V, PL valuation table — note its column headers read FY25–FY28E but its own "change in estimates" table and known FY25 actuals imply the columns are FY26–FY29E; figures used on that reading, see H].
- Implication 2: **Margins in reported P&L can mislead** (Prestige reported EBITDA margin swings 26–37% quarter-to-quarter in PL's preview [V]).
- Implication 3: the cash flow statement shows the true cycle: operating cash flow before land, then land/BD spend, then net debt. The right leverage metric is net debt/collections or net debt/equity, not net debt/EBITDA.
- Analyst toolkit: pre-sales, collections, OCF (pre- and post-land), embedded margin, unrecognised revenue from sold units, cost-to-complete, cash surplus from ongoing projects.

### B2. The 2021–26 upcycle: volume peaked in 2023, value kept rising — where are we?
- Units: 1.38 (2020) → 2.37 (2021) → 3.65 (2022) → 4.77 (2023) → 4.60 (2024) → 3.96 lakh (2025) → ~3.9–4.0 lakh run-rate in 2026 [V/E].
- Value: ₹3.27 (2022) → ₹5.68 (2024) → ₹6.0+ lakh crore (2025) [K/V].
- Price: ₹6,150 → ₹9,714/sq ft (2022 → Q3 2026), +58% [V]; RBI HPI only ~+4% YoY in FY26 [V] — a big gap between new-launch asking prices (consultant series) and registered transaction prices.
- Inventory: 5.53 lakh (2024) → 6.31 lakh (Q3 2026) [V]; MOI ~19 months; supply > sales for 16 quarters (KF) [V].
- Listed developers grew pre-sales 17% in FY26 despite a −14% unit market in CY2025, but Q1 FY27 −21% YoY [V] shows how launch-dependent they are.
- **Verdict [E]:** mature plateau. Unlike 2013–14, (i) developer leverage is low, (ii) inventory is concentrated in luxury/Hyderabad/Bengaluru suburbs rather than distressed NCR projects, (iii) RERA escrow prevents China-style contagion. Downside scenario: rate hikes + IT layoffs + equity market correction → luxury cancellations and price cuts in NCR/Hyderabad; upside: rate stability, GCC hiring, strong launches by top developers taking share.

### B3. Annuity real estate and REITs as the second engine
- Office: CBRE 2025 leasing 82.6 msf (record), GCCs ~39% of Q4-2025; 9M 2026 66.4 msf (+8%), GCCs 42% (~28 msf), new supply 51 msf (+18%) [V]. C&W Q3 2026: GLV ~22 msf, net absorption 14.1 msf, YTD GLV 64.6 msf (record nine months) [V].
- REITs: 6 listed, market cap > ₹2.17 lakh crore, AUM > ₹3.17 lakh crore, Q1 FY27 distributions ₹3,136 crore to 4.85 lakh unitholders (vs ₹1,559 crore Q1 FY26) [V]; FY26 distributions > ₹8,900 crore [snippet].
- For developers: build at 10–14% yield-on-cost, LRD at 8–9%, monetise at 7.5–8.5% cap → value creation ~40–60% of cost [E]. DLF (DCCDL, ~45 msf rental portfolio [K]), Prestige (exit rentals ₹11bn → ₹40bn by FY28/29E per PL [V]), Brigade (annuity income +17% over FY23–26 per PL [V]), Phoenix (malls).

### B4. Regional markets — five different cycles
- **MMR** (32% of 2025 top-7 units [V]): largest and most expensive (~₹17,000/sq ft avg Q3 2025 [snippet]); redevelopment-driven; premium/approval-heavy; Lodha, Oberoi, Godrej, Sunteck, ABREL, Valor, Sri Lotus. Units −18% in 2025 but Q3 2026 +5% [V].
- **NCR** (14%): plotted + luxury Gurugram; prices +23% in 2025 to ~₹9,300 [V]; KF NCR −11% in 9M 2026, ₹5–10 crore −39% [V]; DLF, Signature, TARC, Anant Raj, Godrej, M3M (unlisted).
- **Bengaluru** (16%): IT/GCC end-user market; unsold +34% YoY in Q2 2026 to ~79,180 [V]; Prestige, Brigade, Sobha, Puravankara, Embassy, Godrej.
- **Pune** (16%): mid-market, high volume, −20% 2025, −6% Q3 2026 [V]; Godrej, Kolte-Patil, Lodha.
- **Hyderabad** (11%): highest MOI (~28 months) [V], but Q3 2026 sales +15% and launches +53% (Q2) [V]; Prestige, Brigade, My Home/Aparna (unlisted).

## C. The revenue/profit equation with a worked ₹ example

**Equations.**
- Pre-sales (₹) = Σ launches (msf) × absorption % + sustenance sales on existing inventory (msf), all × realisation (₹/sq ft).
- Collections (₹) ≈ 10–20% of current-year pre-sales + construction-linked instalments on the prior 2–4 years' bookings.
- Reported revenue (₹) = Σ area handed over in projects that received OC × realisation at which those units were sold (point-in-time, Ind AS 115).
- Reported EBITDA ≈ revenue × embedded margin of the vintage being delivered (land bought 4–7 years earlier).
- Annuity revenue = leasable area × occupancy × rent per sq ft per month × 12 (+ CAM/parking/fit-out income).
- Value creation = (embedded EBITDA of booked + unsold projects, discounted) + (annuity NOI ÷ cap rate − cost) + land at market − net debt.

**Worked example 1 — outright-land residential tower [E].** 1.0 msf saleable in a Bengaluru/Pune premium micro-market, launch price ₹15,000/sq ft → **GDV ₹1,500 crore**.

| Item | ₹ crore | % of GDV |
|---|---|---|
| Land (outright, incl. stamp duty) | 300 | 20% |
| Approvals, premiums, FSI | 120 | 8% |
| Construction (₹5,250/sq ft of saleable area) | 525 | 35% |
| Marketing & brokerage | 60 | 4% |
| Overheads & design | 60 | 4% |
| **Total cost** | **1,065** | 71% |
| **Embedded EBITDA** | **435** | **29%** |
| Interest (construction finance) | 45 | 3% |
| PBT | 390 | 26% |
| Tax @ 25.17% | 98 | |
| **PAT** | **292** | **19.5%** |

Timing: Year 0 land (₹300 crore) + early approvals (₹60 crore) = peak equity ~₹360–400 crore. Year 1 launch sells 60% (₹900 crore pre-sales) — collections ~₹150 crore in year 1 (booking amounts + early milestones, 70% to escrow). Years 2–4 the rest of the stock sells at +5–7%/yr price escalation and construction-linked collections fund construction. **Year 4–5: OC → ₹1,500+ crore revenue and ₹290+ crore PAT recognised in one or two quarters.** Equity IRR ~25–30% [E], but the P&L shows nothing until year 4.

**Worked example 2 — the same project on a 35% revenue-share JDA [E].** Developer share of GDV = ₹975 crore; no land cost, refundable deposit ₹50 crore; developer still bears approvals (₹120 crore), construction (₹525 crore), marketing (₹60 crore) and overheads (₹60 crore) = ₹765 crore → **EBITDA ₹210 crore (21.5% of own revenue, 14% of total GDV)**. Peak capital ~₹100–150 crore → RoCE far higher than outright, absolute profit ~half. This is why "pre-sales" for JDA-heavy developers (often reported on 100% or on own share — check footnotes) are not comparable one-to-one.

**Worked example 3 — 1 msf Grade-A office [E].** All-in cost ₹9,000/sq ft (land + construction + fit-out shell) = ₹900 crore. Rent ₹110/sq ft/month at 92% occupancy → gross rent ~₹121 crore/yr; NOI (87% margin) ~₹106 crore → yield on cost 11.8%. Valued at an 8.0% cap rate → ~₹1,325 crore; value creation ~₹425 crore (47% of cost). Funded with LRD at 8.5% up to ~60% LTV, the developer's equity of ~₹360 crore becomes ~₹785 crore of equity value. This spread is what REITs crystallise.

## F. KPI glossary with ranges (compact; full formulas in §9)

| Term | Meaning | Healthy range [E] |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-sales / bookings / sales bookings | Value of units contracted in the period (net of cancellations) | Growth 15–25% p.a. for a share-gainer; ≥ 40–50% from sustenance (non-launch) sales |
| Collections | Cash received from buyers | 75–90% of pre-sales over 3 years |
| Realisation | ₹ per sq ft achieved = pre-sales ÷ area sold | Rising 5–8% p.a. like-for-like |
| GDV | Gross development value = saleable area × expected price | Pipeline ≥ 2× annual pre-sales |
| Launches | New projects/phases opened for sale (RERA-registered) | Launch-to-sales 0.9–1.1 at market level |
| Sustenance sales | Sales from previously-launched inventory | ≥ 40% of pre-sales |
| Embedded EBITDA margin | Expected project margin on booked sales | 25–35% (pan-India), 40–55% (legacy land/luxury) |
| OCF (pre-BD) | Collections − construction − overheads − taxes | 20–30% of collections |
| Net debt / equity | (Debt − cash) ÷ equity | < 0.3× good; > 1× weak |
| Net debt / collections | Leverage versus cash engine | < 0.5× |
| MOI / QTS / YTS | Months / quarters / years of inventory = unsold ÷ sales rate | < 15 months tight; 15–24 normal; > 30 glut |
| Cancellation ratio | Cancelled ÷ gross bookings | < 5% |
| Carpet area | Net usable floor area (RERA definition), excluding external walls, shafts, balcony | Loading (super built-up ÷ carpet) 1.3–1.45× in older pricing |
| JDA / JV / DM | Joint development (landowner gets revenue/area share), joint venture, development management (fee 8–12% of revenue) | Landowner share 25–45% |
| OC / CC | Occupancy certificate / commencement certificate | OC triggers revenue recognition |
| FSI / FAR, TDR | Floor space index; transferable development rights | Premium FSI costs are a key MMR margin driver |
| LRD | Lease rental discounting — loan against rental receivables | 8–9% cost; LTV ≤ 60–65% |
| Exit rental | Annualised rent at full occupancy of completed assets | |
| Cap rate | NOI ÷ asset value | Grade-A office 7.5–8.5%; malls 7.5–9% |
| DPU / distribution yield | REIT distribution per unit ÷ price | 6–7.5% |
| WALE | Weighted average lease expiry | > 6 years good for offices |
| Occupancy | Leased ÷ leasable | > 90% |
| Trading density / consumption | Mall tenant sales per sq ft / total | rent-to-sales 10–14% |
| P/NAV | Market cap ÷ net asset value | 1.0–2.5× for listed developers |
