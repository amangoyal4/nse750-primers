## READ THIS FIRST: twelve definitional traps

1. **Wholesale (TMA) vs retail (FADA) — two different "market shares".**
   - **TMA** (Tractor and Mechanization Association) data = **wholesale dispatches from factory to dealer**. FY26 domestic **11,60,231 units (+23.47%)** [V autocarpro FY26 analysis; Escorts Q4 PPT "11.60 lakh, +23.5%"].
   - **FADA** data = **retail registrations** (dealer → farmer, via VAHAN). FY26 **10,50,077 vs 8,82,825 in FY25** [V autocarpro]. Retail undercounts because many states do not require registration for farm-only tractors; brand coverage also differs.
   - Gap FY26 = ~1.1 lakh units (wholesale − retail) [E] — partly channel stocking ahead of TREM-V/GST, partly unregistered tractors.
   - **Our Escorts primer's share chart (M&M ~42.6%, Sonalika ~12.8%, TAFE ~11.3%, Escorts ~10.9%, Deere ~9%) mixes bases.** Sonalika 12.80% is FY25 **FADA retail** [V autocarpro Aug-2025]; TAFE "11.3%" is FADA retail **Massey Ferguson brand only**; on the **wholesale** basis CRISIL puts **TAFE at ~18% (9M FY26)** [V CRISIL TAFE rationale 9-Feb-2026] and ICRA puts **Sonalika at 13.2% (FY25)** [V ICRA ITL rationale]. Always name the basis.
2. **Domestic vs export vs "total".** Escorts' 1,33,670 FY26 tractors = **1,26,994 domestic + 6,676 export** [V]. M&M's "5,05,930" is **domestic**; M&M's "total tractors sold" in FY26 was **5,26,403** (so exports ~20,473) [V M&M factsheet; E]. Industry FY26 = 11.60 lakh domestic + **1.056 lakh exports** = **12.66 lakh total** [V Escorts AR FY26]. Market shares are domestic shares unless stated; export shares are separate (Escorts 6.3% of industry exports in FY26 [V]).
3. **Escorts' volumes now include the Kubota brand.** Since the KAI/EKI amalgamation (appointed date 1-Apr-2023, effected FY25), "Escorts Kubota" reports three brands — **Farmtrac, Powertrac, Kubota** — as one number [V AR FY26 "three-brand architecture"]. Pre-FY24 Escorts volume/share series (Farmtrac + Powertrac only) are **not like-for-like** with FY24+ numbers. Kubota-brand India volumes before the merger were reported separately (KAI was a JV, 40% Escorts per the Mar-2020 deal [primer]).
4. **Continuing vs discontinued vs "normalised" PAT (Escorts).**
   - FY26 standalone: continuing PAT **₹1,381.0 Cr**; discontinued (RED) **₹1,027.6 Cr**; reported **₹2,408.6 Cr**; company "normalised" PAT **₹1,378.4 Cr** (after removing the RED gain ₹1,004.4 Cr, land/building gain ₹65.1 Cr, LTCG provision effects −₹91.0 Cr, and adding back labour-code ₹39.3 Cr and impairments ₹20.0 Cr) [V Q4 FY26 PPT].
   - FY26 consolidated (screener): sales ₹11,540 Cr, OP ₹1,496 Cr, other income **₹1,618 Cr (includes the RED gain)**, PAT **₹2,394 Cr** [V screener]. TTM to Jun-26 PAT **₹1,383 Cr** (the RED quarter has rolled out) [V screener].
   - **Use ~₹1,380 Cr as the FY26 earnings base for multiples**, never ₹2,394–2,409 Cr.
5. **Restated comparatives.** Screener's FY25 consolidated sales ₹10,244 Cr (and the quarterly series from Jun-2024) are **continuing operations (ex-RED)**; screener's **FY24 ₹9,804 Cr and earlier still include RED** (railway was ~11% of FY24 revenue per screener's profile) [V screener; E]. So FY24→FY25 growth on screener (+4.5%) understates continuing growth. Our primer quotes FY24 "~₹8,900 Cr" (likely ex-RED) — do not mix.
6. **Standalone vs consolidated (Escorts).** Standalone FY26 revenue ₹11,472.8 Cr vs consolidated ₹11,540 Cr; Q1 FY27 standalone ₹3,178.9 Cr vs consolidated ₹3,207.6 Cr [V]. Differences are small (Farmtrac Tractors Europe, Adico, EKFL). The company's market-share/segment slides are **standalone**; screener is **consolidated**. Pick one per table.
7. **M&M "Farm Equipment" has two bases and includes Swaraj and farm machinery.**
   - **Standalone FES** (Mahindra + Swaraj tractors, farm machinery in M&M Ltd): FY26 revenue **₹36,409 Cr, PBIT ₹7,206 Cr (19.8%)** [V M&M factsheet]. Our M&M primer prints "19.9%".
   - **Consolidated Farm segment** (adds overseas tractor subsidiaries — Mahindra USA, Mitsubishi Mahindra Agricultural Machinery (Japan), Sampo Rosenlew (Finland, harvesters), Erkunt (Turkey) [K] — plus farm machinery subs): FY26 revenue **₹42,568 Cr, PBIT ₹5,517 Cr (13.0%)** [V]. The overseas/farm-machinery tail **loses money or earns little** (consolidated PBIT is ₹1,689 Cr lower than standalone) [E].
   - M&M also quotes "**Core Tractor PBIT margin 19.2%**" (Q1 FY27) — a narrower cut again [V].
8. **"Market share" for Escorts in FY26 FELL, it did not rise.** Escorts' own Q4 FY26 slide: domestic share **11.8% (FY25) → 10.9% (FY26)**, because Escorts grew 14.9% vs the industry's 23.5% [V]. Our primer says "~10.9% … up from ~9.9%" — wrong (see §3.4). Q1 FY27 share recovered to **10.5% vs 10.1%** a year earlier [V]. Note also that Q1 and Q4 shares are seasonally different (Escorts is North-heavy; North buys in rabi/Q4–Q1).
9. **Tractor "realisation" is not ex-showroom price.** Ex-showroom prices are GST-inclusive retail prices (₹5–10 lakh typical [E]); company revenue per tractor is **net of GST and dealer margin** and the "Agri Machinery" segment also includes **non-tractor revenue (19% of segment: implements, engines, spares, harvesters, rice transplanters)** [V]. Escorts FY26 tractor-only revenue per unit ≈ **₹5.9 lakh** [E: ₹9,779.6 Cr × 81% ÷ 1,33,670].
10. **GST 2.0 changed the base mid-year.** Tractors moved **12% → 5% GST, effective 22-Sep-2025** (56th GST Council, 3-Sep-2025); key parts (tyres, tubes, hydraulic pumps, agri diesel engines) **18% → 5%** [V autocarpro; CRISIL 18-Mar-2026]. Ex-showroom prices fell **₹40,000–60,000 per tractor** [V autocarpro]; ICRA put it at **~6%** [V ICRA Sep-2025]. **Sep-2025 to Mar-2026 is therefore an inflated base** for FY27 YoY comparisons — this is why Escorts' Sep-2026 volumes fell 16.7% and M&M's domestic fell 23% [V].
11. **TREM-V is a DRAFT re-phasing, not an implemented rule (as far as fetched sources show).** The original rule applied TREM-V to all tractors from **1-Apr-2026**. A MoRTH **draft** re-phases it: **<25 HP and >75 HP from 1-Oct-2026; the 25–75 HP band (~90% of volume) not until FY2032** [V CRISIL 18-Mar-2026; V autocarpro FY26 analysis]. I did **not** find the final gazette notification → **§H gap**. FY26 wholesale included **pre-buying ahead of TREM-V** (Escorts AR language) [V], which also inflates the FY26 base. Do not confuse TREM (tractors) with **CEV/BS-V (construction equipment)**, which **did** take effect for CE (Escorts passed on 6–7% for BS-III→V models and 3.5–4.5% for BS-IV→V in Jan-2025) [V Q1 FY27 call].
12. **Calendar vs financial years, and "industry" totals from different publishers.** TMA monthly releases are often summarised on a **calendar** basis (e.g. "Jan–Jul 2026 total 7,54,068, +23%") and some outlets quote **domestic+export totals** ("12.66 lakh") rather than domestic (11.60 lakh) [V ruralvoice; V AR]. **FY24 domestic** is quoted as 8,67,237 / 8,74,504 / 8,92,410 by different outlets [V-snip] — use **~8.75 lakh** and flag. CRISIL forecasts are **domestic sales ~1.2 million in FY27** (0–2% growth) [V].

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **India is the world's largest tractor market by units, and FY26 was a once-in-a-cycle peak.**
  - Domestic wholesale **11.60 lakh (+23.5%)**, first year above 1 million; exports **1.056 lakh (+6.9%)**; total **12.66 lakh** [V Escorts AR/PPT]. CRISIL had forecast **9.75 lakh** for FY26 as late as Apr-2025 [V-snip theweek/autocarpro] — the outturn beat it by 19%.
  - Four tailwinds stacked: a ~108%-of-LPA 2025 monsoon [K]; the **GST cut 12% → 5% from 22-Sep-2025** [V]; state subsidies (Gujarat, Maharashtra, Bihar) [V Q3/Q1 calls; autocarpro]; and **pre-buying ahead of TREM-V** [V AR]. RBI also cut **125 bp in 2025** [V autocarpro].
- **FY27 is a high-base, El-Niño year — and Q2 FY27 has just shown the turn.**
  - **2026 south-west monsoon ended at ~87% of LPA (759.4 mm vs 868.6 mm; deficit ~12.6–13%), the weakest since 2015** [V c4scourses; V-snip superkalam/IMD]. IMD had forecast 90% ± 4% [V autocarpro/ICRA]. June was ~65% of normal [V-snip]; kharif acreage was −21% in late June, recovering to −2% by 7-Aug [V autocarpro/ICRA].
  - Wholesale was still **+18.6% in Q1 FY27 (3.39 lakh)** [V Escorts PPT] and **+20.1% in July**, then **+6.5% in August** (retail **+0.8%**) [V autocarpro/ICRA], then a **sharp fall in September** (M&M domestic −23%, Escorts domestic −16.2%) against the GST-cut base and a festive season shifted into October [V].
  - Forecasters for FY27 domestic: **ICRA +1–4%**, **CRISIL 0–2% (~1.2 mn)**, **Escorts "mid-single-digit"** (Aug-2026) [V]. **Wheat MSP for RMS 2027-28 was raised only ₹25 (+1.0%) to ₹2,610/qtl — the smallest hike in ~17 years** [V dtnext; V-snip superkalam]. Terms of trade are turning less supportive into H2 FY27.
- **Structure: one giant, four challengers, a long tail.** Wholesale domestic shares FY26 [E composite of V figures]: **M&M (Mahindra + Swaraj) 43.6%** [V]; **TAFE ~18%** (Massey Ferguson + Eicher; 9M FY26) [V CRISIL]; **Sonalika/ITL ~13%** (13.2% FY25) [V ICRA]; **Escorts Kubota 10.9%** [V]; **John Deere ~7–8%** [E residual]; **CNH/New Holland ~4.6%** (53,225 units) [V autocarpro]; others (VST, Captain, Preet, ACE, Indo Farm, Kartar etc.) ~2–3% [E]. Top-5 ≈ 93–94% [E].
- **Economics: a 13–20% EBITDA, net-cash, cash-rich oligopoly.**
  - **M&M standalone FES PBIT 19.8%** (FY26) [V]; **ITL OPM 18.8%** (FY25 consolidated) [V ICRA]; **Escorts EBITDA 13.2% / Agri EBIT 12.6%** (FY26) [V]; **TAFE OPM 9.6%** (FY25) [V CRISIL]; CRISIL expects sector OPM **13.0–13.5%** in FY27 [V].
  - Every major is net cash: **Escorts ~₹8,300–10,000 Cr** [V screener investments ₹8,281 Cr Mar-26; "almost ₹10,000 Cr" cash surplus cited on the Q1 call]; **ITL ₹7,700–7,800 Cr** liquidity (Sep-25) [V ICRA]; **TAFE ~₹7,600 Cr** liquid surplus (Sep-25) plus a **16.3% AGCO stake worth ~₹13,000 Cr** [V CRISIL].
- **Escorts Kubota is the market's only pure-play listed tractor stock** (M&M's FES is ~18% of M&M group revenue; VST is a niche power-tiller company). At **₹29,390 Cr m-cap (₹2,627, 23-Oct-26), 20.7× screener P/E, ~21× on clean TTM earnings, ~13–14× EV/EBITDA FY26** [V screener; E], it sits **34% below its 52-week high of ₹3,999** [V].
- **The Escorts thesis is Kubota.** Kubota owns 54.07% [V]; ~60% of Escorts' tractor exports go through Kubota's global channel (FY26; 61% in Q1 FY27; 67–68% in Q3–Q4 FY26) [V]; a UP greenfield (overall capex **₹2,000 Cr**, land ₹450–500 Cr in FY27, **first commercial production 2029-30**) is aimed partly at the US [V calls]. Exports are still only **~5% of volume** (6,676 units FY26; 2,794 H1 FY27, −14.8%) [V].

### 1.2 Value chain (inputs → farmer)

1. **Raw materials & components** (~68–73% of revenue — Escorts material cost **69.7% FY26; 72.8% Q1 FY27** [V]): castings and forgings (engine block, transmission housing, axles), steel (sheet, bar), tyres (front/rear — CEAT, MRF, BKT, Apollo farm tyres), hydraulics, fuel-injection (Bosch, Delphi — CRDi above 50 HP since TREM-IV), batteries, plastics, rubber. **Metals + rubber** drive the commodity basket [V Q1 call: "commodity pressure on metal and rubber is still continuing"].
2. **OEM**: engine (most majors make their own; Escorts has its own engine line and uses Kubota engines on 10,000–12,000 tractors/yr [V Q3 FY26 call]), transmission, final assembly. Plants: M&M (Mohali/Swaraj, Jaipur, Nagpur, Rudrapur, Zaheerabad [K]); TAFE (Madurai, Doddaballapur [V], plus Chennai, Mandideep/Eicher [K]); ITL (single plant, Hoshiarpur, **3 lakh/yr three-shift** [V ICRA]); Escorts (Faridabad; tractor CU **~77% FY26, ~84% Q1 FY27** [V]); John Deere (Pune, Dewas [V-snip]); CNH (Greater Noida [K]).
3. **Dealer** (single-brand, typically exclusive): Escorts sold to **2,072 dealers/distributors in FY26** (2,156 in FY25), with **92.5% of sales through dealers** and the top-10 dealers only 8.2% [V AR BRSR]; TAFE **>1,600 dealers, >2,000 outlets** [V CRISIL]. Dealer stock norm ~**30 days** (Escorts, Aug-2026 [V call]).
4. **Financier**: **~95% of tractors are bought on credit** [V autocarpro]. Lenders: NBFCs (Mahindra Finance, Shriram Finance, Cholamandalam, L&T Finance, Tata Capital), banks (SBI, PNB, HDFC, ICICI, regional rural banks), and **captive** arms (EKFL for Escorts — 10–12% penetration in its active states in Q1 FY27, 15%+ in July [V]; Autotrac Finance for ITL [V ICRA]; John Deere Financial India — FY25 revenue ₹706 Cr, PAT ₹152 Cr [V-snip business-standard]).
5. **Subsidy / registration**: state subsidy schemes (SMAM-linked; 40% general / 50% small-marginal, SC/ST, women [V autocarpro]); RTO registration (FADA retail data).
6. **End-use**: own-farm cultivation (tillage, sowing, harvesting with implements), **haulage** (sugarcane, bricks, sand, construction material — **commercial use 12–20% of deployment in a normal year**, an anonymous analyst estimated 45–55% for FY26 [V autocarpro]), and **custom hiring** (75,915 Custom Hiring Centres in 2025; Punjab 11,148 [V autocarpro]).

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **OEM scale and brand** capture most profit: M&M's standalone farm PBIT of **₹7,206 Cr on 5.26 lakh tractors ≈ ₹1.37 lakh per tractor** [E from V]; Escorts' Agri EBIT ≈ **₹1,232 Cr on 1.34 lakh ≈ ₹0.92 lakh per tractor** [E]; ITL ~₹1.2 lakh EBITDA/tractor [E: 18.8% × ₹10,022 Cr ÷ ~1.54 lakh units].
- **Component suppliers** (castings, gears, tyres) earn lower margins and carry commodity risk; tractor OEMs pass on cost with a 1–2 quarter lag and once-a-year January list-price hikes (Escorts CE: "normally we do once in a year price increase in January, and that is the industry practice" [V]).
- **Dealers** earn a margin plus finance/insurance payouts; **financiers** earn NIMs on 4–6-year loans (tractor loans are seasonal-repayment, harvest-linked [K]).
- **Aftermarket** (spares, service, lubes) is the high-margin annuity: the installed base is roughly **8–10 million tractors** [E: ~17 years of sales at 5–11 lakh].

## 4. Supply side and cost structure

### 4.1 Key inputs and price drivers
- **Material cost 69–73% of revenue** (Escorts 69.7% FY26, 72.8% Q1 FY27; M&M absorbed **300–400 bp** of commodity inflation in Q1 FY27 [V]). Drivers: steel (HR coil, bars), pig iron/scrap (castings), aluminium, natural rubber (tyres), copper (wiring), imported components (FX), freight.
- **Q1 FY27 inflation shock**: ~**5% cost impact on Escorts' tractors** (≈4 pp commodity + 1 pp Haryana minimum wages) and a further **1.5–2%** expected in Q2; management hoped for reversal from Q4 FY27; rupee depreciation and West-Asia shipping disruption raised imported-component and logistics costs [V Q1 call].

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Tax: GST 2.0 (Sep-2025)
- **Tractors (and specified agricultural machinery) 12% → 5%**, approved at the 56th GST Council meeting **3-Sep-2025**, **effective 22-Sep-2025**; tyres, tubes, hydraulic pumps, agri diesel engines **18% → 5%** [V autocarpro; V CRISIL]. ICRA: **~6% retail price reduction** [V]. Retail price saving ₹40,000–60,000/tractor [V].
- Before Sep-2025, tractors were at 12% while many parts were at 18–28% → **inverted duty structure** with refund claims [K]; the cut largely resolved this [E].
- Effect: pulled forward demand into Q3–Q4 FY26 (Nov-2025 ~1.26 lakh, the strongest month; Oct-2025 total 1,73,635 incl. exports [V autocarpro; V ruralvoice]) and created the **Sep-2026 base problem** (Escorts Sep-26 −16.7%; M&M domestic −23%) [V].

### 7.2 Emissions: TREM-IV/V (tractors) and CEV/BS-V (construction equipment)
- **TREM timeline** [V ICRA Exhibit 1]: TREM I Oct-1999; II Jun-2003; III Oct-2005; IIIA Apr-2010/2011; **IV Jan-2023 (>50 HP only; CRDi + EGR/SCR; +10–12% price)**; **V originally Apr-2026, all HP (aligns with EU Stage V; DPF)**.
- TREM-IV was originally Oct-2020/Apr-2021, deferred to Oct-2021 and then implemented Jan-2023 [V-snip ICRA/equipmentindia; K]; TREM-V was originally Apr-2024, deferred to Apr-2026 [V ICRA]. TMA asked in Aug-2025 for a further postponement for **25–50 HP** [V ICRA].
- **MoRTH draft (reported by CRISIL Mar-2026): <25 HP and >75 HP from 1-Oct-2026; 25–75 HP from FY2032** [V CRISIL; V autocarpro]. If finalised, the price-shock (15–20% on sub-50 HP) is **removed from the FY27–FY31 window for ~90% of volume** [V CRISIL]. Status of final notification: **not verified (§H)**. The Escorts AR still lists "Emission and safety norms (e.g. TREM-V)" as a key risk [V].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **1945–1990: licence era.** Imported tractors, then joint ventures: **Escorts + Ursus (Poland, 1960s), Escorts + Ford (Ford tractors, 1971)**; Mahindra + International Harvester (1960s); **TAFE + Massey Ferguson (1960–61)**; Eicher (1959); HMT + Zetor; Punjab Tractors (Swaraj, 1974, state-owned) [K; Escorts PPT timeline shows "JV with Ford Motors to make tractors" and "1962 URSUS" [V]].
2. **1990s: brand-building.** Escorts launches **Farmtrac** (premium) and **Powertrac** (value; Escorts PPT: "1995 Launched Powertrac") [V/K]; Sonalika/ITL incorporated 1995 [V ICRA]; John Deere enters (1998, JV with L&T [K]); New Holland (1998–99 [K]).
3. **2000s: consolidation.** **M&M acquires Punjab Tractors (Swaraj) in 2007** [K]; India becomes the world's largest tractor market by volume [K]; FY04–FY08 boom; FY09 dip.
4. **FY10–FY14 boom (3.9 → 6.3 lakh)** on MSP hikes and rural wages; **FY15–FY16 bust** (back-to-back droughts: 2014 88%, 2015 86% of LPA) [K].
5. **FY17–FY19 recovery to 7.9 lakh**; Escorts' margins recover from 1.8% (FY15) to 12% (FY19) [V screener].
6. **COVID (FY21): rural outperforms → 9.0 lakh (+27%)**; Escorts' OPM peaks at **16%** and ROCE 28% [V screener]. **Mar-2020: Kubota buys ~10% of Escorts** (~₹1,042 Cr) [primer].
7. **FY22: Kubota takes control** — ₹1,873 Cr preferential + open offer → ~44.8%, joint promoter; renamed **Escorts Kubota Ltd (2022)** [primer]. FY22 domestic −6%; exports at a record [K].
8. **FY23: new high 9.45 lakh** [V-snip]; **TREM-IV (>50 HP) from Jan-2023** — buyers shift to 41–50 HP [V CRISIL]. **Sep-2022 board approves KAI/EKI merger** [V AR].
9. **FY24: −7 to −8%** (uneven 2023 monsoon, 94% LPA [K]); Escorts treasury-share cancellation (equity 132 → 110 Cr on screener) [V screener; K].
10. **FY25: +7–8% to 9.40 lakh**; **NCLT approves KAI/EKI merger 21-Aug-2024**; Kubota to 54.07% [V]; **Oct-2024 RED sale agreed** [V].
11. **FY26: record 11.6 lakh (+23.5%)** — monsoon + GST 2.0 + subsidies + TREM-V pre-buying [V]; **RED transferred 1-Jun-2025** [V]; **TAFE–AGCO settlement (Aug/Sep-2025)** [V]; UP greenfield land approved (Q3 FY26) [V].
12. **FY27: the turn.** Q1 +18.6% (record Q1), July +20%, August +6.5%, **September sharply negative** (GST base, festive shift, 87% monsoon) [V]; **wheat MSP +1%** [V]; TREM-V re-phasing draft (Oct-2026 for <25/>75 HP) [V]; Escorts Q2 results 2-Nov-2026 [V].

**Cycle anatomy (rule of thumb)** [E from K/V]: up-cycles last **2–4 years**, down-cycles **1–2 years**; peak-to-trough volume swings **−10% to −22%**; operating margins for Escorts swung **16% (FY21) → 9% (FY23) → 13% (FY26)** [V screener] — roughly **3–4 pp of margin per 10% of volume** [E].

## 11. Technology and disruption risks
1. **Emission tech (TREM-V)**: CRDi + DPF + SCR on sub-50 HP engines adds 15–20% to price [V ICRA]; if the 25–75 HP band is pushed to FY2032 [V CRISIL draft], the shock recedes; if finalised earlier, it favours OEMs with in-house CRDi engines (M&M, Kubota-backed Escorts, Yanmar-backed ITL, Deere) over small players [E].
2. **Electric/CNG/hybrid tractors**: Escorts showed India's first hybrid tractor concept (2019) [V AR timeline]; Sonalika launched an e-tractor (2020 [K]); CNG tractors (government push — Gadkari urged alternative fuels [V-snip autocarpro headline]). Adoption negligible in FY26 [E]; battery cost and duty cycles make farm EVs uneconomic outside orchards/haulage near towns [E].
3. **Precision agriculture / telematics**: GPS guidance, auto-steer and telematics standard on premium export models; minimal in India's ₹6–8 lakh segment [V AR MD&A generic; E].
4. **Custom hiring and "tractor-as-a-service"** (CHCs 75,915; aggregator apps) could reduce per-farm ownership but raise utilisation and replacement frequency [V autocarpro; E]. Net effect so far: **more commercial use, faster replacement** [E].
5. **Small-farm mechanisation substitutes**: power tillers, mini-tractors, combine harvesters (custom hired) — favour VST/Kubota-type compact products [E].
6. **China** in exports (Africa/SE Asia) — price competition [V Escorts call].
7. **Farm-loan stress / NBFC pullback** — a credit event (e.g. rural NPA spike) can cut volumes faster than weather [K/E]; Mahindra Finance GS3 3.45% (Jun-26) shows no stress yet [V].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Monsoon → farm cash → tractor demand, with a one-to-two-season lag.** Every down-year in the series (FY15, FY16, FY20, FY22, FY24) followed a weak or uneven monsoon or an income squeeze [K/E]. The 2026 monsoon at **~87% of LPA (weakest since 2015)** [V] lands on a record base, so FY27 H2 and FY28 are the risk window; ICRA (1–4%) and CRISIL (0–2%) already assume the stall [V].
2. **Terms of trade and MSP.** MSP sets the floor for wheat/paddy incomes in the North (Escorts' heartland). **RMS 2027-28 wheat MSP +₹25 (+1.0%) to ₹2,610 — smallest hike in ~17 years** [V/V-snip] vs +6.6% the year before [V]. Input costs (diesel, fertiliser) and tractor prices (+1–1.5% Apr-26, more planned [V]) rising faster than MSP = **worse affordability in FY27–28** [E].
3. **Policy-driven price shocks: GST and TREM.** The **GST cut (−~6% price, Sep-2025)** pulled demand forward and created a base problem now visible (Sep-26 Escorts −16.7%, M&M −23%) [V]. **TREM-V** would have added **15–20%** to sub-50 HP prices from Apr-2026 [V ICRA]; the **draft re-phasing to FY2032 for 25–75 HP** [V CRISIL] removes that cliff — if finalised (§H). Pre-buying ahead of TREM-V inflated FY26 [V AR].
4. **Tractor finance.** ~95% of tractors are financed [V]; NBFCs (Mahindra Finance GS3 3.45% [V]) and banks set the marginal buyer's access. RBI's 125 bp of cuts in 2025 [V] helped FY26; the **Oct-2026 hike to 5.50%** [V-sib] and any rural NPA rise after a weak monsoon would tighten. OEMs are building captive NBFCs (EKFL penetration 10–15% where live [V]; ITL's Autotrac [V]) to protect share.
5. **Concentration and the cash pile.** M&M at ~44% with ~20% standalone PBIT margins anchors pricing; challengers (TAFE, ITL, Escorts) are all **net cash (₹7,600–10,000 Cr each)** [V], so share wars are funded but rarely suicidal — the industry's OPM band of **13–20%** has held through cycles [V CRISIL 13–13.5% FY27].
6. **Kubota (for Escorts) — the export and technology option.** 54.07% parent [V]; ~60% of exports via Kubota [V]; greenfield COD 2029-30 [V]; US tariff math (India ~22–23% blended vs Japan ~18%) [V]. Still only **~5% of Escorts' volume** and **down 14.8% in H1 FY27** [V].
7. **Regional mix shift (South and West outgrowing North).** FY26–Q1 FY27 growth was led by West/South (South +33% in Q1 FY27) [V], where Escorts (South share ~6% [V]) and ITL (South/West 11–11.5% [V]) are under-indexed and TAFE/M&M are strong. Escorts lost 0.9 pp of share in FY26 largely for this reason [V].
8. **HP mix consolidation into 41–50 HP (~two-thirds)** after TREM-IV made >50 HP dearer [V]; 4WD rising faster than the industry [V]. Players without a full 41–50 HP 4WD range lose share (Powertrac's gap [V]).

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Tractor OEM revenue = [Industry domestic volume × market share + export volume] × tractor realisation + non-tractor revenue (spares, implements, engines) (+ construction equipment for Escorts)**

**EBIT = Revenue × contribution margin − fixed costs**, where contribution margin ≈ 25–30% and fixed costs (staff, R&D, plant overheads, dealer support) are ~13–17% of a normal-year revenue [E] → EBIT margin swings ~3–4 pp per ±10% volume [E].

**PAT = (EBIT + treasury other income) × (1 − ~25% tax)** — for Escorts, treasury income is ~30% of PBT [E].

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed, and primer issues

### 4.5(a) Insights an expert would add
1. **Kubota extracts almost nothing through royalty — value flows to Kubota as dividends and as a supplier.** Escorts paid Kubota ₹5.27 Cr of royalty (0.5% rate, Kubota's global floor) but **bought ~₹1,286 Cr of goods from Kubota group companies** (engines from Kubota Engine Thailand ₹210 Cr; Kubota Corp ₹883 Cr) and paid it **₹218 Cr of dividends**. Meanwhile the Nanda family's Harparshad & Co collects **₹45 Cr/yr** of "Escorts" brand royalty — ~9× Kubota's. This reframes the governance and margin debate: transfer pricing on ₹1.3k Cr of purchases matters more than royalty.
2. **Escorts is still a net importer from Kubota.** Sales to Kubota group ≈ ₹392 Cr (Kubota Europe ₹152 Cr, tripled) vs purchases ≈ ₹1,286 Cr. "India as Kubota's export base" is a FY28+ option, not FY26 reality; FY27 exports guided flat.
3. **Kubota's 2022 equity is mostly unspent.** Only ₹545 Cr (~29%) of the ₹1,855 Cr net preferential proceeds was used by Mar-2026; Escorts holds ~₹9.8k Cr of liquid assets (~33% of m-cap). The greenfield (₹2,000 Cr) and FY27 capex (₹850–900 Cr) absorb less than a third of it — capital return pressure will grow, but a buyback is structurally capped (promoter + IEPF ~70%).
4. **The share story turned in Escorts' favour in H1 FY27.** On wholesale data Escorts was **12.46% in Sep-26 (12.18% Sep-25)** and ≈ **11.3% for H1 FY27 vs 10.9%**; M&M **lost** share YoY in Q1 (44.9% vs 45.2%) and fell to **41.96% in Sep-26**; the big September gainer was **Sonalika (+3.7 pp to 15.95%)** while **TAFE lost 2.4 pp**. The FY26 narrative ("Escorts lost share in the boom") should be paired with "regained some in the first post-boom half".
5. **TREM is now law, and the cliff is in 2032 for the bulk of the market.** Final rules put **<25 HP and >75 HP on Stage V from 1-Oct-2026** (VST: "no big cost increase"), an **interim IIIAA step for 25–50 HP in April 2028**, and **Stage V for 25–75 HP only from April 2032**. VST's CEO puts the full Stage V cost at **~25–30% of today's tractor price** at current technology costs (vs ICRA's 15–20%). Expect a **pre-buy in FY28 (IIIAA) and a much bigger one in FY32**.
6. **Industry volatility is structurally lower because OEMs no longer stuff channels.** M&M (30–40 days) and Escorts (~30 days) both report normal dealer stock; M&M's chart shows core tractor margins stayed **17.4–23.4% through every year from F19 to Q1 F27**, including −10% and −7% industry years — useful for the report's "how bad can the down-cycle get" section.
7. **Mix is moving up, fast.** M&M says **40–50 HP was ~69–70% of industry volume in Q1 F27** (vs ICRA's ~60% in FY24); post-GST, farmers bought more HP for the same rupees. Escorts' >40 HP share is ~66–68%, i.e. still slightly below the industry mix.
8. **Exports peaked in FY22–FY23**, not FY26: CY2022 exports were 1.32 lakh (12.6% of volume) vs 1.06 lakh in FY26 (8.3%). The export-hub thesis has gone backwards for four years; the US tariff regime (Section 301 10% + possible 232 metal tariffs, and VST's "100% tariff bill" risk) and Kubota's own shrinking North American tractor market (Kubota FY26: "tractor market will continue to shrink") argue for caution.
