- FY26 = Apr-2025 to Mar-2026. Q1 FY27 = Apr–Jun 2026. Q2 FY27 = Jul–Sep 2026. H1 FY27 = Apr–Sep 2026. MedPlus, Apollo, Zota, Entero, API Holdings all have March year-ends.

- "MRP" = maximum retail price printed on the pack, inclusive of GST. "PTR" = price to retailer; "PTS" = price to stockist. Trade convention on branded generics: retailer ~20% margin on PTR-to-MRP (ex-GST), stockist ~10% on PTS-to-PTR — an **industry convention, not a regulation**, except where NPPA caps it [V-sib trading-distribution / branded-formulations].
- GST on most medicines **5% from 22-Sep-2025 (was 12%)** [V-sib branded-formulations]. MedPlus revenue is net of GST; MedPlus "GMV" = MRP − GST [V MedPlus glossary].
- **Operating EBITDA (MedPlus)** = pre-Ind AS 116: rent is deducted as an operating cost, ESOP excluded, interest income excluded. **Screener "Operating Profit"** = post-Ind AS 116 (rent capitalised into depreciation + interest). The two differ by roughly rent (~4% of revenue). See trap 1.

## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **MedPlus has three "EBITDA" numbers, and our two products already use two of them.**
   - **Operating EBITDA (company, pre-Ind AS 116, rent expensed): FY26 ₹365.8 Cr = 5.3%** [V Q4 FY26 deck]. This is what the primer quotes ("~₹366 Cr, 5.3%").
   - **EBITDA (company, Ind AS, incl. interest income, ex rent): FY26 ₹678.7 Cr = 9.8%** [V]. FY26 rent ₹272.5 Cr, interest income ₹43.4 Cr, ESOP credit ₹3.1 Cr.
   - **Screener "Operating Profit" (Ind AS, ex other income): FY26 ₹608 Cr = 8.8%** [V screener]. This is what `trading-distribution.html` prints as "OPM 8.8%".
   - Rule for the writer: in the peer table use screener (8.8%) for comparability with other listed companies, but in prose about unit economics use the company's pre-Ind AS operating EBITDA (5.3% FY26; 4.8% Q2 FY27; 4.2% H1 FY27). Always label which.
2. **"Mature-store EBITDA 11.9%" is a STORE-LEVEL number before warehouse, head-office and marketing costs. It is NOT where the blended margin can "converge" to.** Our primer gets this wrong (see Part 3).
   - The company's own bridge (Q4 FY26 deck): 12+-month stores' operating EBITDA *after allocating all non-store costs* = **₹113.3 Cr = 6.7%**; <12-month stores −₹3.6 Cr; pre-operative costs −₹7.6 Cr; pharmacy total ₹102.2 Cr (5.6%) [V].
   - Q1 FY27: store-level 10.4% vs fully allocated **4.3%** (CFO: "On allocating all non-store-related costs, the operating EBITDA of stores greater than 12 months would be INR707 million, which translates to a margin of 4.3%") [V Q1 transcript].
   - Q2 FY27: 12+-month fully allocated ₹94.5 Cr; <12-month stores (incl. all franchisee stores) +₹1.4 Cr; pre-operative −₹7.3 Cr; pharmacy ₹88.5 Cr (4.4%) [V-decode Q2 deck bridge].
   - **So non-store costs are ~5–6% of revenue and the young-store + pre-opening drag is only ~₹30–45 Cr a year (~0.5–0.7% of revenue)** [E from bridges]. The realistic ceiling for the blended margin at today's cost structure is the fully allocated mature number, **~6–7%**, not 11.9%.
3. **Private-label share: falling, not rising, since Q4 FY25 — and the denominator changed in the Q2 FY27 deck.**
   - Old basis (Q4 FY26 deck; total revenue incl. franchisee sales in "Others"): PL pharma + PL non-pharma = **23.3% (Q4 FY25) → 21.4% (Q1 FY26) → 21.7% (Q2 FY26) → 22.2% (Q3 FY26) → 21.9% (Q4 FY26)**; PL pharma alone 13.6% → 12.8% → 12.1% → 11.6% → 11.3% [V-decode Q4 FY26 deck; Q3 22.2% matches primer/IIFL].
   - Q1 FY27 (call): **20.0%** = pharma 10.7% + non-pharma 9.3% [V Q1 transcript].
   - Q2 FY27 deck re-bases (PL shares now on pharmacy revenue with "Others" only 2.5–2.9%): PL pharma **12.2 / 11.9 / 11.6 / 11.0 / 10.7%** and PL others **9.8 / 10.9 / 11.0 / 9.8 / 10.7%** for Q2 FY26…Q2 FY27 → total **22.0% → 21.4%** ("0.6% decrease in private label over Q2FY26") [V-decode Q2 deck; headline V].
   - Do not mix the two bases in one chart. Use the Q2 FY27 deck series for the latest five quarters and say "re-based".
   - The CEO's explanation (Q1 FY27): staff were "heavily incentivized" and "may have gone a little overboard in trying to push it to every single customer … we may have … put up some customers"; the push was dialled back to grow the branded customer base [V]. Target: rebuild by ~0.25–0.5 pp per quarter [V].
4. **FY26 vs FY25 private label "+2.6%" is an annual-average comparison.** The FY26 highlight says "2.6% increase in private label over FY25" [V], and gross margin rose 180 bps to 26.2%. The quarterly trend inside FY26 was flat-to-down. Both are true; the writer must say which.
5. **Store counts: pharmacy stores only, and franchisees are now inside the count.**
   - "Stores" = MedPlus pharmacy stores; excludes optical, clinics, labs, diagnostics and collection centres [V glossary].
   - **Franchisee (FOFO) stores are included** since FY26: ~310 of FY26's 618 net adds were franchisee [V Q4 transcript]; **131 of Q1 FY27's 146 net adds** were franchisee; "600-plus franchisees" at Jul-2026 [V Q1 transcript]. Franchise economics differ (see §6, §9). Closures now include franchisee withdrawals (27 of 52 in Q1 FY27; "franchisee withdrawn" a named closure reason in Q2 FY27) [V].
   - The FY27 guidance of **800 net new stores "including franchisee outlets"** [V Q4 & Q1 transcripts]. H1 FY27 delivered 312 net (441 gross) [V], so H2 needs ~488 net [E].
6. **MedPlus does not track SSSG.** Management: "we do not really track SSSG per se … we continuously add stores close by to our own stores" (cannibalisation) [V Q1 transcript]. The proxy is **"Store-level revenue growth, stores >12 months"**: FY26 +9.4%; Q2 FY27 **+13.2%**; H1 FY27 +14.9% [V]. Do not call it SSSG without the caveat; its base set changes each period.
7. **Apollo HealthCo revenue is wholesale, not retail.** HealthCo's "Offline Pharmacy Distribution" (₹2,884.5 Cr in Q2 FY27) is Apollo Hospitals' **distribution of medicines to the Apollo Pharmacy front-end stores**, which are owned by a separate promoter-linked/FDI-structured retail entity (Apollo Medicals / "Apollo Pharmacies Ltd" — exact entity name in the composite-scheme chart is OCR-garbled) [V-ocr; K]. Apollo Pharmacy's **retail** sales are therefore higher than HealthCo's offline-PD revenue by roughly the retail margin [E: ÷ ~0.8 → ~₹14,000–15,000 Cr annualised retail value]. Never put HealthCo revenue side by side with MedPlus revenue as "pharmacy sales" without this caveat.
8. **"Indian Pharmaceutical Market (IPM)" ≠ "pharmacy retail market".**
   - IPM (AIOCD-AWACS / Pharmarack / IQVIA) is the audited **domestic formulations market**, ~**₹2.46 lakh Cr in FY26 (+8.8%)** [V-snip, Financial Express via search], ₹2.42 lakh Cr in 2023 [V IPA/Pharmarack PDF].
   - Of that, **institutional supply (hospitals) was 31.3%** in 2023 and only ~69% flowed through retail-type channels: standalone chemists 54%, organised chains 11.6%, online 2.5%, Jan Aushadhi <1% [V IPA/Pharmarack].
   - Pharmacy retail also sells non-pharma (FMCG, wellness, baby care: ~20% of MedPlus revenue) that is not in IPM.
   - The valuation basis of IPM audits (MRP vs PTR) is not stated in the fetched sources [FLAG]; assume audited secondary sales and do not add GST/retail margin on top unless a source says to.
9. **Diagnostics is profitable — the "diagnostics losses" story is out of date.** FY26 diagnostics revenue ₹131.0 Cr, operating EBITDA **+₹19.6 Cr (15.0%)**; Q1 FY27 ₹37.1 Cr / +₹6.6 Cr (17.8%); Q2 FY27 ₹41.1 Cr / **+₹9.8 Cr (23.8%)** [V]. But management says it "has not really gone to the level we wanted" (B2C subscription ~2.1–2.2 lakh members vs 2.5–3 lakh hoped), and it will not expand radiology significantly [V Q1 transcript]. "Others" (optical etc.) lost ₹1.4 Cr in FY26 [V].
10. **"Debt-free" vs screener borrowings ₹1,549 Cr.** Screener's borrowings (₹1,424 Cr Mar-26; ₹1,549 Cr Sep-26) are almost entirely **Ind AS 116 lease liabilities** [E: finance cost ₹120.5 Cr FY26 of which ₹120.4 Cr is Ind AS impact — V Ind AS bridge]. Financial debt is ~nil; an analyst on the Q1 call called it "a debt-free balance sheet" uncontested [V]. **Cash + bank + deposits ₹462.2 Cr + investments ₹81.8 Cr = ₹543.9 Cr (30-Sep-2026)**, down from ₹594.2 Cr (Mar-26) [V]. Net cash ex-leases ≈ ₹544 Cr; net debt incl. leases ≈ ₹1,005 Cr [E].

- **A ₹2.5-lakh-crore medicine market still sold mostly by ~10 lakh family chemists; chains are at ~12% and rising ~1 pp every 1–2 years.**
  - IPM ~₹2.46 lakh Cr FY26 (+8.8%) [V-snip]; Sep-2026 month +13.5% value, +3.4% volume (Pharmarack) [V biospectrum]; Equirus MAT Sep-26 +13.2% [V-snip]. FY27 growth is running ahead of FY26 (GST cut to 5% from Sep-2025 flatters volumes in H1 FY27? — not established; FLAG).
  - Channel shares 2023 (IPA/Pharmarack): standalone 54%, institutional 31.3%, **chains 11.6% (₹28,000 Cr)**, online 2.5%, Jan Aushadhi <1%; by 2030: standalone 42%, institutional 34.2%, **chains 17.4% (₹72,832 Cr, 16.2% CAGR)** [V].
  - "40+ retail chain drugstores" in India [V IPA]. AIOCD claims ~**12.4 lakh** chemists, pharmacists and distributors as members [V The Week]; retail chemists are commonly put at **8–12 lakh** [K; sibling branded-formulations says "about 10–12 lakh" V-sib]. Pharmarack's own network: 2.5 lakh chemists, 12,000+ stockists [V IPA PDF].

### 1.2 Value chain (manufacturer → patient)

1. **Manufacturer** sets MRP within DPCO/NPPA rules (ceiling for NLEM drugs; ≤10% annual MRP increase for non-scheduled) [V-sib].
2. **C&F agent** (depot) — handling fee ~2–3% [V-sib trading-distribution, convention].
3. **Stockist/distributor** buys at PTS, sells at PTR; ~10% margin convention; 60,000+ independent distributors, 20+ organised stockists (10% share in 2023 → 25–30% by 2030), 1,000+ institutional suppliers [V IPA]. Listed: **Entero** (FY26 ₹6,591 Cr, OPM 4.0%) [V screener]. Integrated: **Keimed** (Apollo promoter family; to merge into Apollo NewCo; Q1 FY27 revenue ~₹4,115 Cr [V-ocr, legible "41151" ₹m]); **API Holdings B2B** (₹4,082 Cr FY26) [V].
4. **Retailer** buys at PTR, sells at ≤MRP; ~20% margin convention, but chains self-distribute (MedPlus runs its own warehouses; Apollo via HealthCo; Reliance/Netmeds own supply) and so capture part of the stockist slice plus scheme/volume discounts [V-sib; K].
5. **Patient** pays MRP less any discount. MedPlus gives **20% discount on bills >₹1,000** (cut to 19% for non-UPI payments from 7-Jul-2026) and sells a paid membership (₹99 → ₹149; 44–45 lakh active plan holders at Jun-2026 vs ~39–40 lakh a year earlier) [V Q1 transcript].

**Where ₹100 of MRP goes on a branded prescription medicine** [V-sib branded-formulations illustrative, convention-based]: company ~₹68.6, chemist ~₹19.0, stockist ~₹7.6, GST ~₹4.8 (5% rate). For a **MedPlus private-label generic** the "company" slice is largely captured by MedPlus (contract-manufactured by "3 or 4 people who supply … all the top 30, top 40 of the big pharma"; batches tested in MedPlus's own NABL-accredited lab) [V Q1 transcript] — so the retailer's gross margin can be 2–3× a branded line [E].

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **Brand owners** capture most of the value in branded generics (Indian branded-formulation majors earn 20–36% EBITDA [V-sib branded-formulations]).
- **Retail chemists** capture a regulated-by-convention 16–20% gross slice, out of which they pay rent, staff, inventory carrying, expiries and discounts → a typical independent earns low-single-digit net margin [E].
- **Chains** add: (a) the stockist slice via self-distribution (~5–8% of retail value [E]); (b) private label (adds ~10–15 pp gross margin on the PL portion [E]); (c) scale purchase/year-end supplier discounts (MedPlus cited "40 basis points at the year-end … negotiate with our large supplier" [V Q1 transcript]); (d) services (diagnostics, membership fees). They give some back as consumer discounts (MedPlus 19–20% on large bills; online 8–14%).
- **Platforms** (e-pharmacy/quick commerce) attempt to capture the chain's economics without stores, but spend it on delivery and discounts; PharmEasy delivery-associate payments alone ₹111.8 Cr FY26 [V inc42].

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Ageing and chronic disease.** India's chronic disease load (diabetes ~10 crore+ adults, hypertension ~20 crore+) [K, ICMR-INDIAB 2023] makes the monthly refill basket the core of pharmacy demand. Chronic therapies (cardiac, diabetes, CNS) lead IPM growth [V biospectrum Sep-26].
2. **Out-of-pocket spending.** Medicines are the largest component of OOP health spending (~60–70% of OOPE per National Health Accounts) [K]. PM-JAY covers hospitalisation, not outpatient drugs, so pharmacy demand is consumer-paid → price-sensitive, discount-driven [K].
3. **Income and urbanisation.** MedPlus revenue mix is shifting slowly to Tier-2/3 (Tier-2+3 = 39% of pharmacy revenue, Q2 FY27, vs 38% a year earlier) [V-decode]; 226 of H1 FY27's 312 net adds were "beyond Tier-One" [V].
4. **Prescription brand loyalty vs substitution.** 87% of IPM is branded generics [V IPA]; substitution to private label requires counter persuasion (MedPlus's experience: pushing too hard annoyed customers) [V Q1 transcript]. Trade generics (sold on margin to chemists) are growing 15–16% [V IPA].
5. **Doctor consolidation into hospitals** shifts prescriptions to in-hospital pharmacies: institutional share 31.3% → 34.2% by 2030 [V IPA]; "50,000+ hospitals" [V IPA].
6. **Seasonality:** acute (anti-infectives, respiratory) peaks in monsoon Jul–Sep; Q2 is typically the strongest quarter (MedPlus Q2 FY27 revenue +8.4% QoQ) [V; Pharmarack "monsoon lifts IPM demand" V-snip headline].
7. **Wellness/OTC/FMCG adjacency:** ~20% of MedPlus pharmacy revenue is non-pharma (branded 9.2% + PL 10.7%) [V-decode], helping footfall frequency.
8. **Membership/loyalty:** 44–45 lakh MedPlus plan holders; ~10 lakh plans sold per quarter incl. renewals; price raise ₹99 → ₹149 adds ~₹10–11 Cr annual revenue that "will flow through to the bottom line" [V Q1 transcript].

## 4. Supply side and cost structure

### 4.1 Key inputs and their drivers
- **Merchandise (COGS) ~74–76% of revenue** (MedPlus GM 24.5–26.5%) — driven by MRP policy (NLEM/WPI), brand-owner trade schemes, private-label mix and supplier year-end discounts [V].
- **Rent ~4.0% of revenue** (FY26 ₹272.5 Cr; Q2 FY27 ₹80.75 Cr, +19.7% YoY vs revenue +21.3%) [V]. Average store ~**539 sq ft**; network 2.9 million+ sq ft (Jun-26) vs 2.5 million (Jun-25) [V Q1 transcript]. Rent per store ≈ ₹48,000/month [E: ₹80.75 Cr ÷ ~5,559 avg stores ÷ 3].
- **Staff:** 31,000+ employees [V Q2 deck] → ~5.5 per store [E]. Minimum wage hikes from 1-Jun-2026: **Karnataka +60%, Telangana +25%+** [V]; KA + TG ≈ 1,989 of 5,642 stores = 35% of the network [E from V-decode state map]. MedPlus stopped new entries to a staff retention-bonus plan from 1-Mar-2026 and is restructuring PL incentives [V]. Underlying driver: "lower wages, especially when compared to employees in the quick commerce business" [V Q1 transcript].
- **Pharmacist requirement:** every retail drug licence (Form 20/21) requires a registered pharmacist present [K, Drugs & Cosmetics Rules 1945 r.64–65]. Rule 65 governs conditions of sale; licences can be suspended by state Drugs Control officers [V Angel One].
- **Inventory/working capital:** MedPlus inventory ₹1,521 Cr (Sep-26) vs payables ₹364 Cr; NWC **54 days** of revenue; store inventory **86 days for Year-1 stores vs 34 days for stores >12 months**; warehouse inventory ~33 days [V Q2 deck]. Inventory provisions ~₹12 Cr/quarter (~0.8–1.0% of sales); up to ~5% on private label because PL is made-to-order and non-returnable, whereas 90–95% of branded expiries are returned to manufacturers [V Q1 transcript].
- **Technology/logistics:** in-house tech; 735 delivery hubs, 3,441 pincodes served for online (Q2 FY27, ranking of chart series [V-decode]).

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Regulators and licences
- **CDSCO / DCGI** (central) — Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940 & Rules 1945; **state Drugs Control Administrations** licence and inspect retail pharmacies (Form 20/21 retail sale licences; Rule 65 conditions) [K; V Angel One for Rule 65 orders].
- **Pharmacy Council of India** — Pharmacy Practice Regulations 2015 (registered pharmacist must dispense) [K].
- **NPPA** (Dept of Pharmaceuticals) — DPCO 2013 price control; **Para 19** extraordinary powers used for trade-margin caps [V-sib].
- **PMBI** — runs Jan Aushadhi [V PIB].
- **FSSAI** — nutraceuticals/food items sold in pharmacies; reportedly to meet quick-commerce platforms [V-snip].

### 7.2 Price control (DPCO 2013 / NLEM)
- NLEM-listed ("scheduled") formulations get ceiling prices = average of brands with ≥1% share + **16% retail margin** built in [V-sib branded-formulations]. **935 scheduled formulations with ceilings (Jul-2026)** [V-sib]. NLEM 2022 (384 medicines) is the current list [K].
- **WPI-linked annual revision** each April: **+0.65% (Apr-2026)**; near zero since 2024 [V-sib].
- **Non-scheduled**: MRP increase capped at **10% a year** (actual avg 5.6%/yr 2020–25) [V-sib]. ~82% of IPM sits outside the NLEM [V-sib] (so ~17–18% under price control — matches brief's 17–18%).
- **Trade-margin rationalisation (TMR):**
  - Feb-2019: 30% trade-margin cap on **42 non-scheduled anti-cancer drugs**, MRPs of 526 brands cut up to 90% [V-sib].
  - **17-Oct-2026 gazette: 137 non-scheduled anti-cancer drugs, total trade margin (PTS → MRP) ≤30% of MRP from 9-Nov-2026**; MRPs expected to fall up to 70%; patient savings ~₹2,500 Cr/yr; manufacturers to file revised prices within seven days; list "updated periodically" [V-sib].
  - A cap on all non-price-controlled drugs: **press reports only** [V-sib]. A Parliamentary Standing Committee (Aug-2026) noted there is no timeframe for a permanent trade-margin framework [V-sib].
  - **Effect on a pharmacy** [E worked example, mirroring V-sib]: an oncology pack at PTS ₹3,000; old MRP ₹10,000 gave the retailer ~₹4,000 of margin (MRP − PTR ₹6,000) [E]. New MRP ₹4,286 (₹3,000 ÷ 0.7); total trade margin ₹1,286 split stockist/retailer → retailer ~₹850–1,000 [E]. Retailer rupee margin per pack falls ~75–80%; % margin on MRP falls from ~40% to ~20–23%. For MedPlus/Apollo the oncology share is small (not disclosed); the precedent (category-by-category spread) is the risk.
- **Standard chemist margins:** 20% retailer / 10% stockist on branded generics is convention; for NLEM ceilings the formula assumes 16% retail margin [V-sib].

### 7.3 E-pharmacy rules and court cases
- **GSR 817(E), Aug-2018** (dated 28-Aug-2018 in one source, 20-Aug in another [V-snip]): draft Drugs and Cosmetics (Amendment) Rules to regulate sale of drugs by e-pharmacy — **never notified, never withdrawn** [V The Week].
- **GSR 220(E), Mar-2020**: COVID-era notification allowing licensed retailers to home-deliver medicines; still relied upon [V The Week].
- **Courts:** Delhi HC (Dec-2018, Zaheer Ahmed v. Union of India) restrained unlicensed online sale of medicines; Madras HC (Dec-2018/2019) similar order later stayed by a division bench; multiple PILs and contempt petitions since, unresolved [K — not re-fetched; FLAG for writer to verify before quoting].
- **Drugs, Medical Devices and Cosmetics Bill 2025** would make a licence compulsory for online drug sales; enactment status unverified [V-snip].
- **AIOCD strike 20-May-2026** (24 hours; some reports 20–21 May; participation uneven): demands — withdraw GSR 817 and GSR 220, act against deep online discounts and quick-commerce Rx delivery; ~12.4 lakh members claimed (other reports "8 lakh struck", "12.5 lakh") [V The Week; V-snip]. Earlier AIOCD strike 28-Sep-2018 (8.5 lakh chemists) [V-snip headline].
- **Implication:** online/QC players operate in a grey zone; MedPlus and Apollo operate online as **store-fulfilled licensed retail** (each order shipped from a licensed store), the most defensible structure if rules tighten [E].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; timeline in §G)

1. **1980s–2000s: the chemist era.** Medicine retail was ~100% family chemists, supplied by local stockists on credit. Apollo Pharmacy opened in 1987 as an adjunct to Apollo Hospitals [K]. Price control under DPCO 1995 covered far more drugs than today [K].
2. **2006–2015: first chains.** MedPlus (Hyderabad, 2006, founder Gangadi Madhukar Reddy) built dense South Indian clusters; Apollo scaled nationally; Wellness Forever (Mumbai), Guardian, Frank Ross, Noble Plus regionally [V primer; K]. FDI rules (no FDI in multi-brand retail) shaped structures: Apollo split front-end retail (promoter-linked entity) from back-end distribution (Apollo/HealthCo) [K; V-ocr structure].
3. **2015–2021: the online boom.** PharmEasy, 1mg, Netmeds, Medlife, Truemeds raised VC money; discounts of 25–40% [V IPA]. Draft e-pharmacy rules (Aug-2018) and court orders (Dec-2018) froze the legal framework [V; K]. COVID (2020) legalised home delivery (GSR 220) and pulled online share up; conglomerates bought in: **Reliance–Netmeds (Aug-2020), Tata–1mg (Jun-2021), PharmEasy–Medlife merger (2021) and PharmEasy–Thyrocare (₹4,546 Cr, 2021)** [K].
4. **Dec-2021: MedPlus IPO** at ₹796 (band ₹780–796), ~₹1,398 Cr (₹600 Cr fresh + OFS by Warburg Pincus's Lavender Rose and others) [primer; K]. PharmEasy's IPO (DRHP Nov-2021) was withdrawn in 2022 [K].
5. **2022–2024: online bust, chains roll out.** PharmEasy's 2023 rights issue at a ~90% valuation cut (Manipal-led ₹2,400 Cr) [K]; online discounts compressed to 8–14% [V IPA]. MedPlus rolled out ~1,400 net stores in FY23 and squeezed margins (FY23 PAT ₹50 Cr) [V screener; K count]. Apollo raised ₹2,475 Cr from Advent for HealthCo (2024) and announced the HealthCo–Keimed combination and listing [V-sib; K].
6. **FY25: the stall.** MedPlus revenue growth slowed to 9%; older-store revenue growth went negative (−0.9% in Q4 FY25) as the company restructured private label and cut back on the hard PL push [V-decode; V Q4 transcript "restructuring the private label"].
7. **FY26: the rebound.** Revenue +12.3%, GM +180 bps to 26.2%, PAT +46%; mature-store revenue growth re-accelerated to +17.8% by Q4; 618 net adds incl. ~310 franchisee; first diagnostics profits [V].
8. **FY27 so far: cost shock + policy noise.** Wage hikes (Jun-2026), private-label share at a low of 20% (Q1), capex U-turn (Jul), AIOCD strike (May), NPPA cancer-drug TMR (Oct/Nov), Apollo 24|7 breakeven (Q2), MedPlus buyback (Nov) [V].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Formalisation from ~10 lakh chemists to chains (~12% → ~17% by 2030).** Chains grow 16–18% a year vs IPM ~9–10% [V IPA]; the pool they take share from is the 54% standalone channel. Density (cluster economics), reliability (fill rate, 30,000+ SKUs) and discounts are the weapons. The share shift is slow: ~1 pp a year at best [E].
2. **Mix, not markup: private label is the only big lever on a regulated, convention-bound gross margin.** Branded margins are fixed by MRP conventions and NPPA; PL (MedPlus 21–23% of revenue; chains 15–18% → 25–30% by 2030 per IPA [V]) doubles the margin on the substituted basket. But substitution has a ceiling set by customer trust — MedPlus overshot and has been rebuilding since FY25 [V].
3. **Labour and rent inflation against capped prices.** Rent ~4% and staff ~10% of revenue rise with wages/real estate while MRPs rise at ≤WPI (NLEM) or ≤10% (others), actual ~5.6%/yr [V-sib]. The Jun-2026 KA (+60%) / TG (+25%) wage shock shows how quickly 100–150 bps of margin can go [V].
4. **Channel war: online and quick commerce vs store-fulfilled omnichannel.** Online share stuck at ~2.5–3% after a decade [V IPA]; losses narrowing (PharmEasy, Apollo 24|7 breakeven) [V]; QC now attacks the acute/urgent basket. The legal framework (GSR 817 draft, GSR 220 COVID rule) is unresolved and politically contested (AIOCD strike May-2026) [V].
5. **Price policy creeping into the trade margin.** NPPA has now twice used Para 19 to cap trade margins (2019: 42 drugs; Nov-2026: 137 cancer drugs) [V-sib]; Jan Aushadhi's 18,000+ outlets advertise 50–80% cheaper generics [V PIB]. Every expansion compresses branded rupee margins and makes PL more valuable relative to brands.

## B. Bespoke deep-dive themes (own sections in the report)

### B1. "The 11.9% illusion": what MedPlus's mature-store margin does and does not mean
- Store-level EBITDA (11.7% Q2 FY27; 11.9% FY26) excludes warehouse, logistics, HO and marketing (~5–6% of revenue) [V bridge].
- Fully allocated, a mature MedPlus store earns ~4.3–6.7% (Q1 FY27 / Q4 FY26) [V].
- Young stores and pre-opening cost only ~₹30–45 Cr/yr [E from V bridge]; slowing openings would add ~50–70 bps, not 600 bps.
- The real margin levers are therefore PL mix (+10 bps GM per 20–30 bps PL), discount discipline, wage absorption and operating leverage on non-store costs [V; E].
- Teaching device: the company's own bridge chart (Q4 FY26: 1,133 → −36 → −76 → 1,022 → +53 → +1 → 1,076 ₹m) [V].

### B2. "Two ways to own a pharmacy chain in India": MedPlus (listco owns stores) vs Apollo (listco owns distribution + app; stores in a separate retail entity)
- FDI and history explain the Apollo structure; HealthCo reports wholesale revenue (₹2,884.5 Cr offline PD in Q2 FY27) and earns ~7% pre-24|7 EBITDA on distribution [V; V-ocr].
- MedPlus reports retail revenue, keeps the whole retail margin and bears the store costs; earns ~5% [V].
- Apollo NewCo adds Keimed (~₹4,100 Cr/qtr distribution, ~3.2% EBITDA [V-ocr]) → a ~₹25,000 Cr run-rate company targeting 6–7% (reportedly now ~6%) [V-ocr; V-snip].
- Store counts: Apollo 7,662 vs MedPlus 5,642; Apollo's online (24|7: 49m registrations, ~53,000 daily medicine orders) dwarfs MedPlus omni (~₹110 Cr/qtr, ~5.5% of revenue) [V; V-decode].

### B3. "The private-label pendulum": how hard can a chain push its own generics?
- PL share 23.3% (Q4 FY25) → 20.0% (Q1 FY27, old basis) → 21.4% (Q2 FY27, re-based) [V-decode; V].

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Revenue = Stores (avg) × Revenue per store**, where Revenue per store = footfall/day × bills conversion × average bill value × 365, and grows via store maturity (Year-1 → Year-2+) and price/mix.

**Gross profit = Revenue × [ (1 − PL share) × branded margin + PL share × PL margin ] − provisions + supplier discounts − consumer discounts**

**Store EBITDA = Gross profit − (store staff + rent + store running costs)**

**Operating EBITDA (pre-Ind AS) = Σ store EBITDA − warehouse & logistics − HO & marketing − pre-operative costs + franchise income + diagnostics/other EBITDA**

**PAT = Operating EBITDA + interest income − owned-asset D&A − (Ind AS lease interest + ROU depreciation − rent) − tax**

## 3.4 Primer issues (format as primer-issues.md)

The ~11.9% is a **store-level** EBITDA before warehouse, logistics, HO and marketing costs (~5–6% of revenue). The company's own bridge shows 12+-month stores earn **6.7% fully allocated (Q4 FY26: ₹113.3 Cr)** and only **4.3% in Q1 FY27**; young stores (−₹3.6 Cr) and pre-operative costs (−₹7.6 Cr) are a small drag (~₹30–45 Cr a year, ~0.5–0.7% of revenue).
