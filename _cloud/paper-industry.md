- ₹/kg × 1,000 = ₹/t. ₹70/kg = ₹70,000/t.
- FY26 = Apr-2025 to Mar-2026. Q1 FY27 = Apr–Jun 2026. Q2 FY27 = Jul–Sep 2026. All Indian peers use a March year. **International Paper, Suzano, UPM, Stora Enso report on calendar years; Nine Dragons on a June year.**

## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Tonnes vs tpa, and >100% utilisation.** JK Paper's installed capacity is **761,000 tpa** (incl. Sirpur) but it **produced ~8.35 lakh t** and **sold 8.18 lakh t** in FY26 = **~110% utilisation** [V CRISIL]. Indian mills routinely exceed "rated" capacity through debottlenecking. The primer's "~8 lakh TPA" capacity is really closer to production. Always say which.
2. **Volume "8.18 lakh million tonne"** — CRISIL's text has a typo ("lakh million tonne"); it means **8.18 lakh tonnes** (818,000 t). Primer says 8.19 lakh MT. Use "about 8.2 lakh tonnes".
3. **JK Paper is no longer a pure paper company.** FY26 consolidated revenue (₹7,076 Cr, screener) includes **corrugated boxes (ex-Horizon/Securipax, RWPL), mono-cartons (Borkar from Oct-2026) and animal nutrition (Quadragen)** [V CRISIL]. Revenue/tonne and EBITDA/tonne computed on total revenue overstate paper economics. JK reports one "Paper & Board" segment plus packaging; there is no clean grade split [primer].
4. **Four different FY26 numbers for JK Paper.** Revenue: **₹7,076 Cr** (screener/IDBI revenue from operations), **₹7,492 Cr** (CRISIL "company-reported" table — basis unclear, may be total income or include other operating income), **₹7,569 Cr** (primer "consolidated turnover", per an InvestyWise summary). PAT: **₹274 Cr** (screener consolidated), **₹266 Cr** (primer), **₹297 Cr** (IDBI "adj. PAT"), **₹364 Cr** (CRISIL table). EBITDA: **₹924 Cr** (screener OP; IDBI ₹924.1 Cr = 13.1%), **₹984 Cr** (primer), **~₹854 Cr / 11.4%** (CRISIL's adjusted basis). **Use screener for tables; quote the basis in prose.**
5. **Standalone vs consolidated JK Paper.** Q1 FY27 standalone revenue ~₹1,807 Cr vs consolidated ₹1,887 Cr [V-snip quartr]. After the Mar-2026 scheme, the packaging subsidiaries merged into the standalone entity, so standalone now contains most of packaging; Sirpur, RWPL, Quadragen and Borkar remain subsidiaries [V CRISIL entity list].
6. **ITC's paper number is segment PBIT (EBIT), not EBITDA, and is gross of inter-segment sales.** FY26 Paperboards, Paper & Packaging: revenue **₹8,766 Cr**, PBIT **₹797 Cr (9.1%)** [V-sib]. ITC's own cigarette and Classmate businesses buy board from the segment. Do not put ITC's PBIT margin next to JK's EBITDA margin without saying so (segment depreciation is roughly ₹450–550 Cr/yr [E], so segment EBITDA margin ≈ 14–15% [E]).
7. **West Coast consolidated = West Coast + 72.45% of Andhra Paper (fully consolidated) + optic-fibre cables.** WCPM standalone FY26 ≈ ₹4,279 − ₹1,701 ≈ **₹2,580 Cr** revenue [E, ignoring inter-company]. Paper capacity: Dandeli 3.2 LTPA + Andhra 2.56 LTPA = **~5.76 LTPA** combined [V CARE].
8. **TNPL's FY26 PAT (₹248 Cr) is 88% a tax credit.** CARE: PAT ₹247.75 Cr "largely attributable to a **deferred tax credit of ~₹219 Cr**" → underlying PAT ~₹29 Cr [V]. Screener P/E 3.5× is therefore meaningless. Also, TNPL has **two units**: Unit I Karur (W&P, 4 LTPA, bagasse + wood) and Unit II Mondipatti (**board, 2 LTPA**, wood pulp) [V CARE]. TNPL also sells cement (from lime sludge) and power.
9. **Seshasayee "consolidated" vs standalone.** Screener's consolidated page includes associates; CRISIL rates SPBL standalone. The prompt's "IPPL consolidation" could not be identified this session [FLAG]. Seshasayee's known associate is **Ponni Sugars (Erode)** [K]. Capacity: **2.55 LTPA** (Erode + Tirunelveli), with Servalakshmi's 75,000 tpa described as "should add" — so ~3.3 LTPA if included [V CRISIL]. Ask which figure the company now reports.
10. **"Realisation" ≠ list price ≠ distributor price.** Mill net realisation = invoice price − discounts − freight/dealer margins − GST. The Delhi distributor list (e.g. maplitho ₹53–82/kg, SBS ₹86–96/kg, Jun-2026) [V indianprinterpublisher] includes dealer margin and freight, and is GST-exclusive or -inclusive by line. Net mill realisations are typically 10–20% below such lists [E]. Never mix them in one chart.

Other traps worth a line: (a) **"Paper industry" statistics mix organised and unorganised mills** — ~900 mills, only ~526–550 operational [V Infomerics; V IPMA/PTI], and 70–75% of tonnage is recycled-fibre kraft/duplex made by mills that are not in this report's peer set. (b) **Imports of "paper and paperboard"** exclude newsprint in IPMA's series but CMIE's tables split newsprint separately — check scope. (c) **The MIP is ₹67,220/t on CIF value** (before customs duty and freight inland) — not a domestic price floor.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Indian paper is two industries sharing one HS chapter.**
  - **Recycled-fibre (RCF) packaging paper** — kraft/containerboard and duplex — is ~70% of Indian tonnage, made by hundreds of small and mid-size mills in Gujarat (Vapi/Morbi), UP (Muzaffarnagar), Punjab, Tamil Nadu. Its input is waste paper (domestic + imported OCC).
  - **Virgin-fibre (wood/bagasse) W&P and board** — the integrated "majors" — ITC, JK Paper, West Coast (+Andhra), TNPL, Century (now ITC), Seshasayee, Emami (partly), BILT (dormant). They own pulp mills and plantations.
  - CPPRI census production [V via Infomerics Apr-2025]: total **~21.4 mt**, of which **W&P 4.79 mt** (virgin 3.03 / RCF 1.76), **packaging 15.13 mt** (kraft virgin 0.88 / kraft RCF 10.80 / board-duplex virgin 0.87 / board-duplex RCF 2.57), **newsprint 0.67 mt** (RCF), **tissue 0.26 mt**, **specialty 0.52 mt**. Virgin fibre is therefore only ~**4.8 mt of 21.4 mt (~22%)** [E].
- **The member (JK Paper) sits in the virgin minority and in the two grades with the most import pressure: W&P (copier/maplitho/coated) and VAB/FBB board.**
- **FY23 was a once-in-a-generation peak; FY25–FY26 were the trough.**
  - JK Paper OPM: FY23 **31%** → FY24 24% → FY25 14% → FY26 **13%**; PAT ₹1,208 Cr → ₹1,133 → ₹410 → **₹274 Cr** [V screener].
  - West Coast (cons.) OPM: FY23 **33%** → FY26 **10%**; PAT ₹1,087 Cr → **₹156 Cr**. Andhra (standalone) OPM 35% → **4%**; PAT ₹522 Cr → **₹19 Cr**. Seshasayee OPM 26% → 6%; PAT ₹396 → ₹83 Cr. TNPL OPM 19% → 10% [V screener].
  - Industry: BOB Caps puts the sector EBITDA margin at **6.9% in Q3 FY25, the lowest in two decades vs a long-term average of 16.6%** [V-snip coniferous Jul-2025].
- **What broke the spread: (1) imports, (2) wood, (3) domestic competition.**
  - Imports of paper & paperboard hit a **record 2.05 mt in FY25 (≈₹15,000 Cr), vs 1.08 mt in FY21**; China ~27% of the total (+33% YoY), ASEAN ~20% [V IPMA/PTI 30-May-2025]. Q1 FY26 imports were 486 kt (+8%); China 143 kt (+28%) [V-snip].
  - Indonesian VMLP board imports went from **296 t (FY22) to 62,935 t (FY25)** with CIF prices **down 25%** [V IPMA/PTI Aug-2026].
  - Hardwood (eucalyptus/subabul/casuarina) prices "remained elevated during most of FY26"; JK's realisation fell ~3% in FY26 "due to elevated imports from China and Indonesia and increased domestic competition" [V CRISIL].
- **FY27 is the turn, and policy is doing part of the work.**
  - **MIP of ₹67,220/t (CIF) on VMLP** since **22-Aug-2025**, extended four times, now to **31-Mar-2027** (DGFT Notif. 38/2026-27, 30-Sep-2026) [V taxguru].
  - **DGTR final findings of 25-Jun-2026 recommended anti-dumping duty on VMLP from Indonesia**; the Finance Ministry had not notified it as of 12-Aug-2026 (IPMA appeal) [V outlookbusiness]. Status after that: **unverified**.
  - Rupee depreciation (₹94.9/$) and higher freight after the West Asia war lifted import parity: "Realisation started improving from Q4 FY26 due to high import prices as a result of rupee depreciation and increased freight costs" [V CRISIL].
  - Wood prices "started moderating from Q4 FY26 owing to improved availability" [V CRISIL].
  - Q1 FY27: JK Paper OPM **15.4%**, PAT **₹136 Cr (+62%)** [V IDBI/screener]; West Coast OPM **19%**, PAT ₹142 Cr (vs ₹60 Cr); Andhra 12% (vs 9%); Seshasayee 9% (vs 5%); TNPL 10.0% (vs 8.9%); ITC paperboards PBIT +38% [V screener; V-snip ITC].
- **Capacity is consolidating, not exploding — except at ITC.**
  - **ITC bought Century's Lalkuan mill (₹3,498 Cr, 4.8 LTPA)**, taking ITC to ~**1.5 mtpa** of paper and board — about 2× JK Paper [V-sib].
  - JK Paper chose **backward integration** (BCTMP pulp, captive renewable power) and **forward integration** (corrugated, mono-cartons) rather than a new paper machine [V CRISIL].
  - TNPL is adding **34,000 tpa tissue** (₹350 Cr, COD Q3 FY27); Andhra is building a tissue plant and upgrading its pulp mill [V CARE].
- **Valuations price a recovery, unevenly** [screener 23-Oct-26]:
  - JK Paper **₹6,467 Cr m-cap, 19.8× TTM P/E, 1.17× book, ~9.2× FY26 EV/EBITDA** [E].
  - West Coast **₹5,283 Cr, 23.0× P/E, 1.46× book**, net cash ~₹1,147 Cr (screener) → ~10× FY26 EV/EBITDA [E]; stock +74% in a year.
  - Andhra **₹1,237 Cr, 44.8× (trough EPS), 0.64× book**. Seshasayee **₹1,250 Cr, 12.6×, 0.61× book**. TNPL **₹907 Cr, 0.39× book**, P/E n.m. (tax credit).

### 1.2 Value chains (inputs → customer)

1. **Integrated wood-based mill (JK Rayagada/Songadh, Andhra Rajahmundry, West Coast Dandeli, TNPL Unit II, ITC Bhadrachalam).**
   - **Fibre:** clonal eucalyptus/subabul/casuarina grown on farmers' land under farm-forestry schemes (4-year rotation) → bought at mill gate by the tonne (green or debarked) → chipped → **kraft cooking** (white liquor: NaOH + Na₂S) → washing → **bleaching (ECF: ClO₂, H₂O₂, O₂)** → pulp.
   - **Chemical recovery:** black liquor is concentrated and burned in a **recovery boiler** (steam + power, and recovers ~95% of cooking chemicals) → lime kiln (lime sludge) → white liquor. TNPL turns lime sludge + fly ash into **cement** [V CARE].
   - Pulp → **paper machine** (forming → press → dryer → size press/coater → calender → reel) → sheeting/cut-size → dealers/converters.
   - Typical norms [K/E]: **~2.4–2.8 t of debarked wood (bone-dry ~1.2–1.4 t) per tonne of bleached pulp**; ~1.05–1.1 t pulp per tonne of paper incl. fillers; **water 28–60 kl/t** (TNPL **28 kl/t**, "among the lowest") [V CARE]; energy ~1,000–1,400 kWh + 8–12 t steam per tonne of paper [K].
2. **Agro-based mill (TNPL Unit I, Seshasayee Erode partly).** **Bagasse** bartered from sugar mills in exchange for steam/coal (TNPL has long-term barter agreements with six Tamil Nadu sugar mills; bagasse = 45–50% of W&P raw material) [V CARE]. Seasonal (crushing season), needs depithing and storage.
3. **RCF packaging mill (kraft/duplex).** Waste paper (domestic kabadiwala collection at **₹10–18/kg** [V price list, Jun-2026] + imported OCC from US/Europe/Middle East) → pulper → screening/cleaning → (de-inking for W&P/newsprint) → multi-ply board/kraft machine. Low capex per tonne, energy from coal/biomass, small scale (50–500 tpd).
4. **Converting (JK Paper's forward step).** Kraft paper → corrugator → **corrugated boxes** (JK via ex-Horizon/Securipax and RWPL; "market leader in the corrugated box segment" per CRISIL [V]); VAB/duplex → printing → die-cutting → **mono-cartons** (JK via Borkar, Oct-2026 [V CRISIL]).

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **Fibre cost control** is the single biggest determinant of through-cycle returns. A mill with captive/contract wood within 200–300 km and its own pulp line earns the spread between paper price and **its** fibre cost; a non-integrated mill earns the spread between paper price and **world pulp price × ₹/$**.
- **Board > W&P in value per tonne.** Indicative Delhi trade prices (Jun-2026) [V]: SBS ₹86–96/kg; premium woodfree ₹84–98/kg; W&P ₹70–85/kg; maplitho ₹53–82/kg; duplex ₹40–50/kg; kraft liner ₹35–47/kg; newsprint ₹49–67/kg. Coated art up to ₹155/kg (imported high-gloss).
- **Brand and distribution** capture a premium in copier (JK Copier, Century/ITC "Paperkraft", Andhra "Reflection", TNPL "Copier") and in notebooks (ITC Classmate — the reason ITC is vertically integrated from plantation to exercise book).
- **Converters capture value in packaging** — the corrugated and mono-carton businesses earn on service, design and proximity, not on fibre. JK's move into converting is a bet that this layer is steadier than paper.
- **The importer caps the price.** In soft global markets the domestic realisation ≈ landed import price (CIF + BCD (0% under AIFTA for ASEAN) + port/inland freight). This is why trade remedies (MIP/ADD) matter more than any single competitor.

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Education (W&P core).** ~250 m school students, ~43 m in higher education [K, UDISE+/AISHE]. Textbooks (state boards and NCERT tenders), notebooks (exercise books), exam papers. TNPL sells ~25% of its output to the Government of Tamil Nadu for textbooks [V CARE]. **GST 2.0 made exercise books and the uncoated paper for them nil-rated from 22-Sep-2025** (was 12%) [V taxguru] — supportive for notebook demand, but mills report pushback on 0% billing of paper [V-snip reader comment; anecdotal].
   - NEP 2020 emphasis on reduced textbook load and digital content (DIKSHA) is a slow negative; post-COVID return to physical classrooms was a positive in FY22–FY23 [K].
2. **Office / copier.** Tied to white-collar employment, government offices, banking/insurance print; digitisation (e-office, e-courts, UPI) is a structural drag, offset by rising formal employment. Copier is the most branded, highest-margin W&P grade.
3. **Publishing and commercial print.** Coated paper for magazines/brochures declining in developed markets; in India flat-to-low growth; heavily imported at the premium end.
4. **Packaging (the growth engine).**
   - FMCG (personal care, food), **pharma** (India makes ~60 bn+ units of cartons/yr [E]), cigarettes (ITC's own), liquor, QSR/food-service (cup stock, clamshells), e-commerce (corrugated boxes).
   - **Single-use plastic ban (1-Jul-2022)** on 19 SUP items (straws, cutlery, stirrers, plates, cups etc.) under the Plastic Waste Management (Amendment) Rules 2021 [K, MoEFCC] → paper cups, straws and cutlery demand.
   - **GST 2.0 cut cartons/boxes (HSN 4819) from 12% to 5%** while raising kraft/board (4804/4810) from 12% to 18% → an **inverted duty** for corrugators; IPMA said ~90% of kraft/packaging paper used for boxes is affected and MSME working capital is tied up [V taxguru; V-snip IPMA]. Final demand for boxes is helped; converters' cash flow hurt.
   - Packaging grows ~7–8% a year vs ~2–4% for W&P [K/E]; packaging = 57% of demand [V].
5. **Tissue.** Per-capita tissue use in India ~<0.3 kg vs ~5 kg China [K/E]; hotels, QSR and urban households; every new Indian capex (TNPL 34k tpa, Andhra project) targets it [V CARE].
6. **Newsprint.** Declining; India's newspapers rely on imports (~55% of consumption) [V CMIE table]. Emami Paper is the swing domestic producer.

**What moves demand in a quarter:** academic calendar (Q4–Q1 textbook/notebook season — Jan–May), government tender timing, FMCG volumes, festive season (Q2–Q3) packaging, and destocking/restocking by dealers when prices are expected to fall/rise (imports).

### 7.3 Environment, forests and land
- **Forest Conservation Act / National Forest Policy 1988**: industry cannot get forest land for captive plantations; raw material must come from farm forestry or degraded land [K]. Hence the farm-forestry model (JK since 1991; ITC Bhadrachalam since 1990s; TNPL captive + farm forestry on **309,000+ acres** 2004–26 [V CARE]).
- **Transit permits for farm-grown wood**: many states exempted eucalyptus/subabul/casuarina from felling and transit permits — eases supply [K].
- **CPCB charter for pulp & paper (2016)**: water use and effluent norms (ZLD push); state PCBs' consents (e.g. Seshasayee's MDP-IV awaits TNPCB consent) [V CRISIL].
- **Plastic Waste Management Rules**: SUP ban from 1-Jul-2022 [K]; EPR for plastic packaging (2022) [K] — favours paper.
- **EPR for paper packaging**: none in force [K/E] — a potential future risk/opportunity.
- **Carbon**: India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS, 2023) covers pulp & paper among the first sectors with GHG-intensity targets (2025 notification) [K — verify targets].

### 7.4 Labour, state policy
- Mills are large single-site employers; strikes (Andhra FY25: ~3 weeks lost [V CARE]) and state politics (TNPL is GoTN-controlled; CMD Kumar Jayant IAS from 30-Apr-2026) [V CARE].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **Licence Raj to 1990s** — paper capacity reserved/licensed; bamboo and forest leases; small agro mills proliferated after concessions for non-conventional raw material (agro/RCF) [K]. Large mills: Ballarpur (1945), West Coast (1955), Seshasayee (1960), JK (1962), APPM (1964), TNPL (1979), ITC Bhadrachalam (1979) [V CARE/screener/V-sib].
2. **1990s–2000s: forest access ends, farm forestry begins.** JK (1991), ITC (Bhadrachalam clonal programme) and TNPL develop clonal eucalyptus on farmers' land [primer; V CARE].
3. **FY08–FY10 boom and FY11–FY14 capacity wave and glut.** Many mills added capacity (ITC, JK's Rayagada expansion, BILT, Century, TNPL) → oversupply, price cuts; top-10 listed net debt/EBITDA reached **4.6× in FY14** [V-snip coniferous/BOB Caps]. JK Paper made a loss in FY15 (PAT −₹18 Cr) [V screener].
4. **FY16–FY20: consolidation and deleveraging.** Little new capacity; prices firmed; margins 20–28% (JK OPM 27–28% FY19–FY20) [V screener]. **International Paper sells APPM to West Coast (FY19)** [V CARE]; **JK buys Sirpur out of insolvency (2018)** [primer; K]. China's waste-paper import ban (2018–21) reshapes global OCC flows [K].
5. **FY21 COVID shock**: schools/offices shut; W&P demand collapsed; JK OPM 20%, West Coast loss, Andhra/TNPL losses [V screener].
6. **FY22–FY23 super-cycle**: reopening + global pulp spike (BHKP ~$750–800/t, 2021–22 [K]) + European energy crisis cut W&P supply + freight spikes made imports dear → Indian prices surged ~30%; **FY23 margins 31–35%**; top-10 net debt/EBITDA **0.3× by FY24** [V-snip; V screener]. JK bought Horizon/Securipax (2022) [primer].
7. **FY24–FY26 down-leg**: pulp prices fell, China's FBB capacity glut, Indonesian board surged into India, imports a record 2.05 mt (FY25), wood prices peaked → **industry EBITDA 6.9% in Q3 FY25** [V-snip]. Policy response: **MIP Aug-2025**, DGTR ADD findings Jun-2026.
8. **FY27: early recovery** — rupee at ₹94–97/$, West-Asia freight shock, MIP, easing wood; Q1 FY27 margins up across the group [V]. **ITC buys Lalkuan (Aug-2026)** — the largest consolidation deal in Indian paper.

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Digitisation of W&P** — e-office, e-learning, digital exams, e-invoicing. Indian W&P still grows low single digits because of education penetration, but copier demand per office worker falls [K/E].
2. **BCTMP / high-yield pulp** — JK's new hardwood BCTMP (Songadh, from 30-Jun-2026) yields ~85–90% vs ~45–50% for kraft pulp, cutting wood use per tonne, and substitutes imported BCTMP used in board middle plies [V IDBI; K yields].
3. **Board light-weighting and barrier coatings** — PE-free / dispersion-coated cup stock, plastic-free barrier boards for food; regulatory push on PFAS (EU) [K]. Opportunity for virgin-board makers who invest.
4. **Recycled vs virgin** — brands' recycled-content pledges vs food-contact safety (mineral-oil migration) → virgin board keeps food/pharma; recycled takes secondary packaging [K].
5. **Energy transition** — biomass/black-liquor makes integrated mills partly renewable already; JK's hybrid captive RE project (FY28) [V CRISIL]; TNPL wind 35.5 MW [V CARE]; CCTS GHG-intensity targets [K].
6. **Tissue and specialty** as diversification — TNPL, Andhra, Pudumjee [V].
7. **Imports as "technology"** — the real disruptor is world-scale, low-cost fibre in Indonesia/Brazil/China; Indian mills' answer is trade policy, mix and integration.

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert leads with)

1. **The import-parity ceiling (price side).** Indian virgin paper and board are priced at landed import cost. When China/Indonesia have surplus (2023–25 FBB glut, Western W&P decline), Indian realisations fall to meet them regardless of domestic demand. Record **2.05 mt imports in FY25** and Indonesian VMLP up **~200×** in three years [V] broke FY25–FY26 margins; the **₹94.9/$ rupee, West Asia freight and the MIP** are lifting the ceiling in FY27 [V CRISIL]. *Lead with this.*
2. **Domestic wood cost and the farm-forestry model (cost side).** India has no commercial forests for industry; wood comes from smallholder clonal plantations on 4–5-year rotations, so wood supply responds to prices with a lag of half a decade. The FY22–23 price boom pulled demand ahead of plantations → wood peaked FY25–FY26 → plantings since 2021–22 now reaching harvest → easing from Q4 FY26 [V CRISIL]. JK (>85% farm-forestry at Rayagada [primer]), ITC and TNPL (309,000 acres [V]) have the deepest programmes; Andhra's "limited past plantations" cost it in FY25 [V CARE].
3. **Board vs W&P: the mix shift and the board price war.** Board is the growth grade (packaging 57% of demand [V]) and earns more per tonne, so every major has moved there (ITC, JK, TNPL→SBS, West Coast cup stock). That concentrated investment in one grade made board the most import-exposed and price-competitive — "increased domestic competition" is named by CRISIL alongside imports [V]. ITC's Lalkuan purchase adds ~4.8 LTPA under the price leader's control [V-sib].
4. **Trade policy as the swing variable.** MIP (since Aug-2025, now to Mar-2027), DGTR's ADD recommendation on Indonesian VMLP (Jun-2026, pending), AIFTA review (target end-2026), BCD asks (10% → 25%), GST 2.0 inverted duty — a regulator in Delhi sets the floor more than any competitor [V; primer makes the same point].
5. **Capital cycle and integration (backward and forward).** After the FY11–14 glut (net debt/EBITDA 4.6×) the industry deleveraged to 0.3× by FY24 [V-snip] and has largely avoided new greenfield machines. Capital now goes into pulp (JK BCTMP), power (JK hybrid RE, TNPL RSPS), tissue (TNPL, Andhra) and converting (JK corrugated/mono-cartons) [V]. JK is the only one levering up (net debt/EBITDA 2.3×) [V CRISIL].

Secondary forces: energy/coal and freight (West Asia 2026); education policy and GST on notebooks; recycled-fibre availability (waste-paper recovery only 30–43%) [V].

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Revenue = Σ over grades [installed capacity (tpa) × utilisation × net realisation (₹/t)] + converting/other revenue**
**EBITDA = Volume × [realisation − (wood t/t × wood ₹/t) − (purchased pulp t/t × pulp $/t × ₹/$) − chemicals/t − energy/t − freight/t − other conversion/t] − fixed costs + converting EBITDA**

### C.1 JK Paper FY26 top line, rebuilt [E]
- Capacity 7.61 LTPA × utilisation ~107.5% (sales basis) = **8.18 lakh t sold** [V].
- × paper & board net realisation ~**₹74,500/t** [E] = ~₹6,094 Cr.
- + packaging solutions (corrugated, cartons) + animal health + other ~₹980 Cr [E balancing figure].
- = **₹7,076 Cr revenue from operations** [V screener].
