## READ THIS FIRST: nine definitional traps

1. **Avanti's "net profit" ₹657 Cr is before minority interest; attributable profit is ~₹606 Cr.**
   - Thai Union owns **40% of Avanti Frozen Foods (AFFPL)**, the processing/export subsidiary. AFFPL earned PAT ₹130.3 Cr in FY26 [V AR], so ~₹52 Cr belongs to Thai Union.
   - Screener's "Net Profit" FY26 ₹657 Cr is the consolidated figure including NCI; **EPS ₹44.48 × 13.62 crore shares = ~₹606 Cr attributable** [V screener; E arithmetic]. The company's own slide labels ₹656.8 Cr "PAT (after minority interest)" — the label is wrong; EPS proves it.
   - TTM: screener ₹587 Cr pre-NCI; attributable ~₹531 Cr (EPS ₹38.97) [E]. Screener's P/E 16.7 is on attributable EPS.
2. **Godrej Agrovet is the opposite: attributable PAT (~₹472 Cr) is ABOVE consolidated PAT (₹445 Cr)** because minorities share Astec's losses (Astec FY26 PAT −₹81 Cr; ~33% outside holders) [V screener EPS ₹24.58 × 19.22 crore shares; E].
   - Company "underlying" PAT ₹440 Cr excludes a ₹29 Cr land-sale gain and adds back a ₹23 Cr wage-code charge [V FY26 PPT].
   - **Use screener consolidated PAT (₹657 / ₹445 Cr) for sums in Part 3**, and show attributable separately for P/E. Never mix.
3. **Avanti's company "EBITDA" includes other income; screener OP does not.**
   - FY26: company EBITDA ₹947.6 Cr (15.6%) = screener OP ₹750 Cr (12.4%) + other income ₹199–212 Cr [V PPT; V screener]. Other income is mostly treasury yield on a ~₹2,500 Cr cash pile.
   - Segment "EBITDA" for feed (₹737.3 Cr, 16.8%) and processing (₹210.4 Cr, 12.5%) therefore also carry allocated other income. **Segment result** (₹558.5 Cr feed, ₹128.3 Cr processing, consolidated) is the cleaner operating measure [V AR].
4. **Avanti's revenue split is ~72/28 feed/processing in FY26, not ~79/21.** Consolidated segment revenue: feed ₹4,366.8 Cr, processed shrimp ₹1,689.5 Cr [V AR]. Processing jumped 43% in FY26 as US buyers front-loaded before and after tariffs. In Q1 FY27 the mix swung back to 82/18 (feed ₹1,566 Cr, processing ₹334 Cr) [V PPT].
5. **"Feed volume" means different things at each member.**
   - Avanti: **shrimp feed only** — 5,62,060 t sold in FY26 [V]. (Fish feed sales were ₹0.67 Cr — negligible.)
   - Godrej Agrovet "Animal Nutrition": **cattle + broiler + layer + fish + shrimp + (since FY25) pig feed** — 16,46,545 t in FY26 [V]. Mostly cattle feed (+18% in FY26). Godrej does not disclose the species split; its aqua/shrimp feed is a small slice [E: likely <10% of tonnes].
   - **₹/kg is not comparable:** shrimp feed realises ~₹78/kg (Avanti FY26 = ₹4,378 Cr ÷ 5.62 LT) vs ~₹30/kg for Godrej's mostly-cattle mix (₹4,941 Cr ÷ 16.47 LT) [E]. Shrimp feed is fishmeal- and protein-dense; cattle feed is bran, cake and grain.
   - The ACI Godrej Bangladesh JV (1.09 LT in Q1 FY27) is **not** in GAVL's consolidated volume — it is equity-accounted [V PPT].
6. **"Animal Feed" is the sub-sector label, but only ~46% of Godrej Agrovet is feed.**
   - FY26 segment revenue mix: Animal Nutrition 46.43%, Oil Palm 18.77%, Dairy 14.93%, Crop Care (incl. Astec) 11.17%, Poultry & processed food 7.22%, Others 1.48% [V FY26 PPT].
   - **Segment profit (PBIT) mix: Oil Palm 40.12%, Animal Nutrition 36.08%, Crop Care 18.62%, Poultry 3.33%, Dairy 2.05%, Others −0.19%** [V]. Oil palm out-earned feed in FY26.
   - For a "combined feed business" aggregate, use segment rows only (Part 3 §3.2(c)).
7. **Godrej's oil palm segment revenue excludes trading.** FY26 segment revenue ₹1,908 Cr excludes ₹90 Cr trading revenue; segment result ₹384 Cr excludes ₹2.4 Cr trading margin [V]. CRISIL's "edible oil" segment grew "40%" (incl. trading) with "19.3%" margin [V CRISIL] vs company 42.4% / 20.1%. Use the company figure.
8. **US duty on Indian shrimp is a STACK of four instruments with different timings.** Quote each separately (details §7.1):
   - Anti-dumping (AD) cash deposit: **5.53%** for non-examined exporters (incl. Avanti Frozen Foods) from the 2024–25 review final results (scheduled for Federal Register publication 4-Sep-2026); previously 3.76% (2023–24 review, from 9-Feb-2026) and 1.35% before that [V FR; V-snip].
   - Countervailing duty (CVD): **5.77% "all others"** from the 2024 investigation (final determination 28-Oct-2024) [V FR].
   - Tariff: IEEPA reciprocal 25% + 25% "Russia-oil" penalty = **50% from 27-Aug-2025** [K]; cut to **18%** in Feb-2026 under the India–US deal [V Avanti AR]; collection stopped after the **20-Feb-2026** SCOTUS ruling (Avanti: "withdrawal of reciprocal tariffs effective from 24 February, 2026") [V]; then Sec 122 10% to 24-Jul-2026; **Sec 301 10% since 24-Jul-2026** (contested).
   - Today's all-in for Avanti ≈ **21.3%** (5.53 + 5.77 + 10) vs ~**57%** at the Aug-2025 peak (50 + 5.77 + ~1.35) [E].
9. **Market share bases differ.** Avanti's "~51–53% of Indian shrimp feed" (AR FY26) is the company's own estimate [V]. Its call gives a second basis: CY2026 shrimp output 8–9 LT → feed consumption 11–12 LT; Avanti FY26 5.62 LT = **47–51%** [V call; E]. Our primer's "~45–50%" is an older number. Godrej's "leading/dominant organised animal feed player" (CRISIL) has no published share; on Alltech's 57.7 mt India total, GAVL's 1.65 mt ≈ **2.9%** of all compound feed [E] — the market is far more fragmented than shrimp feed.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Two very different companies share one label.**
  - **Avanti Feeds** is a ₹6,066 Cr shrimp-feed monopolist-in-all-but-name (~51–53% share) with a ₹1,689 Cr processing/export arm (60:40 with Thai Union) and ~₹2,500 Cr of treasury [V AR; E].
  - **Godrej Agrovet** is a ₹10,233 Cr Godrej-group agri conglomerate: compound feed (mostly cattle) is 46% of revenue, but oil palm earned 40% of segment profit in FY26 [V].
  - **Combined FY26:** revenue ₹16,299 Cr (+8.8%), OP ₹1,614 Cr (9.9%), consolidated PAT ₹1,102 Cr (+14.8%), m-cap ₹19,618 Cr on 30-Oct-2026 [E: sums of screener].
  - **Combined feed business (segment basis):** 22.09 lakh tonnes, ₹9,308 Cr revenue, ₹906 Cr segment result (9.7%) — 57% of combined revenue [E: Avanti feed segment + GAVL Animal Nutrition].
- **FY26 was a peak-margin year for both; Q1 FY27 broke the pattern for Avanti.**
  - Avanti FY26 feed EBITDA margin **16.8%**, the best since FY18 (23.0%), on cheap fishmeal (higher Peruvian catch) and soymeal [V PPT; V AR].
  - **Q1 FY27: fishmeal consumption cost ₹153/kg vs ₹93 a year earlier (+65%); soymeal ₹58 vs ₹40 (+45%); spot in late August: fishmeal ₹225/kg, soymeal ₹71/kg** [V Avanti call]. Feed EBITDA margin collapsed to **7.5%** (from 18.8%) even as volume rose 17% to 1,93,852 t. Consolidated PAT −37% to ₹116 Cr [V].
  - Avanti raised feed prices ~10% on 19-Jun-2026 and told analysts Q2 would be "again a larger pain point" [V call]. **Stock ₹659, at its 52-week low (H/L ₹1,594/₹655), m-cap ₹8,974 Cr vs ~₹11,700 Cr on 19-Aug and ~₹12,300 Cr in our primer** [V screener; V PPT].

- **Feed is a converter's spread business; who wins is decided by procurement and pricing power.**
  - Avanti priced its way back to share after the ₹4/kg cut of Apr-2025; now it must re-price up against an AP government that is building a **feed-price formula** with a Big-4 consultant [V call].
  - Godrej's Animal Nutrition earned only **₹2,107/t (7.0% margin)** in FY26 on ₹30/kg feed — but grew volume 11.6% (cattle +18%) [V; E].
- **The US is shrinking as India's shrimp outlet; China and the EU are absorbing the difference.**
  - India's frozen shrimp exports FY26: **7,92,647 t, US$5,624 mn** (+6.9% volume, +8.6% $). US share **33%**; US volume **−17.9%**; China **+24%**, EU **+36%**, Japan +5% (MPEDA, as quoted by Avanti) [V call].

- **The duty stack has fallen from ~57% to ~21%, but AD and CVD keep ratcheting up.** Avanti's own AD cash-deposit rate rose 1.35% → 3.76% → 5.53% across two reviews within seven months [V FR; E]. Avanti says it has declared IEEPA refund entries worth **US$15–20 mn**, which CBP will not process while the entries sit under AD/CVD review suspension [V call].

  - Avanti **16.7× TTM P/E (14.8× FY26 attributable), 2.7× book, ~8.7× FY26 EV/OP net of ~₹2,475 Cr cash** [V; E].

### 1.2 Value chains (inputs → customer)

1. **Shrimp: hatchery → pond → processor → importer → retailer.**
   - Broodstock (SPF vannamei, largely imported from the US/Hawaii/Florida via the Chennai quarantine facility [K]) → hatchery (PL10–PL15) → farmer stocks 30–60 PL/m² [K] → **100–150 days of feeding** → harvest at 30–100 count → processor (peeling, grading, freezing, cooking) → reefer container → importer/retailer (US), wholesaler (China, often via Vietnam), retail (EU).
   - **Feed is 50–60% of the farmer's cost of production** [primer; K]. FCR ~1.3–1.5 → a 10 t harvest consumes 13–15 t of feed worth ₹10–12 lakh at ₹78–80/kg [E].
   - Avanti earns at three steps (seed, feed, processing); the farmer carries the biological risk (disease, weather) and the price risk.
2. **Cattle feed: grain/bran/cake → compounder → dealer or dairy co-op → dairy farmer.**
   - Inputs are agri by-products: de-oiled rice bran (DORB), oilseed cakes/meals (soy, cotton, mustard, groundnut), maize, molasses, DDGS from grain ethanol plants, minerals [K].
   - Demand is pulled by milk prices: when procurement prices rise (2026: Amul raised farmer prices from 1-Jun-2026; Tamil Nadu ₹41 → ₹44/litre) farmers feed more compound feed [V-snip].
   - Godrej sells through dealers and through dairy partnerships; its own dairy (Creamline) buys ~milk from the same farmers — a feed–milk loop.
3. **Poultry: maize + soymeal → feed → broiler/layer → live bird or processed meat.**
   - Broiler economics in India are run by integrators (Suguna, Venky's, IB Group, CP, Baramati Agro) who make their own feed and contract farmers [K]. Merchant feed makers like Godrej sell mainly to layer farms and independent broiler growers.
   - Feed = ~65–70% of broiler cost of production [K]. Cost of production ~₹110/kg live weight mid-2026 vs farm-gate ₹120–150 [V-snip].
4. **Oil palm: grower → FFB → GAVL mill → CPO/PKO → refiner.**
   - Under NMEO-OP, the state allots a "factory zone" to a processor, which supplies seedlings, advises growers and **must buy all FFB** in the zone at a formula price linked to international CPO and the OER (~15–17% of the CPO price per tonne of fruit) with a **viability price** backstop [V-sib edible-oil].
   - Palm takes ~4 years to first harvest and ~8 to peak yield; ~50% of GAVL's plantations are juvenile (0–4 yrs) [V GAVL call].
5. **Crop protection: technical (AI) → formulation → dealer → farmer;** Astec makes AIs and intermediates for global majors and GAVL [primer].

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **Shrimp feed is the profit pool in Indian aquaculture; processing is the volume pool.** Avanti FY26: feed ₹4,367 Cr revenue / ₹559 Cr segment result (12.8%); processing ₹1,689 Cr / ₹128 Cr (7.6%) [V AR; E]. Over FY18–FY26 feed earned 81–90% of Avanti's company EBITDA [E from PPT].
- **Branded consumer protein and dairy capture value only with mix:** Creamline EBITDA margin 3.3% (FY26), GFL 6.5%; both are being "rebooted" [V].
- **Oil palm is a policy-protected mill spread:** GAVL's segment margin 20.1% in FY26 (17.0% FY25) with CPO realisation ₹1,12,781/t [V]. The spread moves with the CPO price because the FFB price is a share of it, but OER is the bigger lever (§C.4) [V-sib edible-oil; E].
- **Cattle feed is volume with thin spreads:** GAVL Animal Nutrition 7.0% segment margin, ~₹2,100/t [V; E].
- **Global benchmark:** integrated protein companies (CP Foods, Tyson) and specialist aqua feed (Skretting, BioMar, Haid) earn feed margins of ~3–8% EBIT [K]. Avanti's 13–17% feed EBITDA in FY25–FY26 is exceptional and was flattered by cheap inputs.

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Global shrimp demand and price (Avanti, both engines).**
   - The US consumes ~0.8 mt of imported shrimp a year [V-snip 2025 total]; China imports ~0.9–1.0 mt (mostly Ecuadorian head-on) [K].
   - Indian farm-gate prices follow export realisations with a lag. Oct-2025: 100-count vannamei in Nellore reached **₹245/kg**, described as "relief after prolonged losses" [V-snip New Indian Express]. No Oct-2026 series found (H gap).
   - Avanti (Aug-2026): "At present, the shrimp culture activity is very good… Farm-gate prices have also gone up" [V call].
2. **Indian protein consumption (Godrej).** Milk, eggs and chicken grow ~4–6%/yr [K]. India produces ~240 mt of milk a year (DAHD BAHS: 239.3 mt in 2023-24 [K]); per-capita availability ~470 g/day [K, FLAG — re-verify against BAHS 2025].
3. **Milk procurement prices (cattle feed volume).** 2026: Amul and Mother Dairy raised retail milk ₹2/litre from 14-May-2026 (Amul's first hike since May 2025) and Amul lifted farmer procurement prices from 1-Jun-2026; Tamil Nadu (Aavin) procurement ₹41 → ₹44/litre (Aug-2026) [V-snip]. **High milk prices lift cattle feed demand (Godrej +15% cattle feed volume in Q1 FY27) but squeeze Creamline (EBITDA margin 1.5%)** [V PPT] — the "natural hedge" inside GAVL.
4. **Poultry cycle (Godrej feed, GFL).** Broiler farm-gate in north India crossed ₹150/kg live in late June 2026 before easing to ~₹120 against a cost of ~₹110 [V-snip]. 2026 bird flu (H5N1) outbreaks: Kerala (11 farm outbreaks, Jan-2026, WOAH), Chhattisgarh (Bilaspur, Mar), Maharashtra (Navapur, >1.5 lakh birds culled, May), Bengaluru (Hesaraghatta, Jun), Bihar [V-snip]. Bird flu cuts chicken demand for weeks and raises layer feed risk.
5. **Pet food (Avanti optionality).** India pet food is a ~₹5,000–7,000 Cr market growing ~15–20% [K, FLAG]; Avanti sold 300 t in FY26, targets 800 t in FY27 [V AR].
6. **Edible oil import substitution (Godrej oil palm).** India imports ~56% of its edible oil (2023-24, PIB) [V-sib edible-oil]; NMEO-OP targets 6.5 lakh ha of new oil palm and 11.2 LT of domestic CPO by 2025-26 (actual CPO 3.80 LT in 2024-25) [V-sib].

### 7.2 India: aquaculture and seafood regulation
- **Coastal Aquaculture Authority (CAA) Act 2005, amended 2023** — registration of coastal farms and hatcheries; the 2023 amendment decriminalised offences and brought nucleus breeding centres/broodstock under CAA [K].
- **MPEDA** — export promotion, registration of exporters/processors, residue monitoring (antibiotics) for EU/US; publishes export statistics [K]. EU-approved establishment list and EU residue audits matter for the EU pivot (EU +36% in FY26) [V].
- **Andhra Pradesh** — AP has its own aquaculture statutes (AP State Aquaculture Development Authority Act 2020; seed and feed quality acts [K, FLAG]). In 2026 the **AP government constituted a committee with a Big-4 consultant to design a feed-price mechanism** linked to fishmeal, SBM and wheat prices: "the governments have say in prices because of the various statutes that are there in the Andhra Pradesh" (Avanti JMD) [V call]. **This is the single biggest regulatory risk to Avanti's pricing power.**
- **BIS feed standards:** IS 2052 (compound cattle feed), IS 1374 (poultry feed), IS 16150 (shrimp feed?) — voluntary/partly mandatory; FSSAI does not regulate animal feed [K, FLAG]. A national Feed Act has been discussed for years; not enacted [K].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **1990s–2008: monodon era.** Black tiger shrimp (P. monodon) dominated; white spot (WSSV) outbreaks in 1994–96 wiped out crops; Avanti incorporated 1993, first feed plant at Kovvur 1994 [V AR]. Godrej's feed business dates to the 1970s–90s [primer].
2. **2009–2016: the vannamei boom.** India permitted SPF L. vannamei in 2009; Avanti entered technical collaboration with Thai Union Feed Mill for vannamei in 2009 [V AR]; output and exports multiplied. Avanti's PAT rose from ₹116 Cr (FY15) to ₹466 Cr (FY18) and OPM peaked at 20% in FY18 [V screener]. Thai Union bought 40% of Avanti Frozen Foods (2016) as processing was moved into AFFPL [V AR; V-snip worldfishing].
3. **2017–2019: listing and the first bust.** Godrej Agrovet IPO Oct-2017 [primer]. Shrimp prices fell in 2018–19 on oversupply (India, Ecuador); Avanti PAT halved FY18→FY19 (₹466 → ₹307 Cr) [V].
4. **2020–2023: the input-cost squeeze.** COVID logistics; then soymeal hit records in 2021 (India allowed 12 LT GM soymeal imports in Aug-2021 [K]); Avanti feed EBITDA fell to **7.0% in FY22** and PAT to ₹245 Cr [V]. Godrej's OPM fell to 6% in FY23 (PAT ₹295 Cr) as feed spreads compressed and Astec turned down [V]. Ecuador scaled to >1 mt and undercut India in the US and China [K].
5. **2024: US trade remedies return.** AD/CVD petitions (Oct-2023) on Ecuador, India, Indonesia, Vietnam; CVD on India final 28-Oct-2024 (5.77% all others) [V FR]. Astec's losses (FY24 PAT −₹47 Cr, FY25 −₹135 Cr) [V].
6. **FY25–FY26: the margin peak.** Cheap fishmeal (strong Peruvian catch) and soymeal; Avanti cut feed price ₹4/kg from Apr-2025 to defend share yet earned **feed EBITDA 15.3% (FY25) and 16.8% (FY26)** [V]. Godrej: oil palm +68% result, ROCE 20%, NWC days 39 → 25; bought out Creamline (Apr-2025) [V].
7. **Aug-2025 → Feb-2026: the tariff shock and reversal.** 50% IEEPA tariff from 27-Aug-2025; India's US shrimp volume −57% in Oct-2025 [V-snip]; India diverted to China/EU. Feb-2026: deal cut reciprocal to 18%; 20-Feb-2026 SCOTUS ruling ended IEEPA collection [V AR].
8. **Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026): the protein-cost shock.** El Niño fears, fishmeal ₹100 → ₹230/kg, SBM +45%; Avanti feed margin 7.5%; GAVL strategy reset, shrimp feed under review; Astec relapses in Q2 FY27 (PAT −₹37 Cr) [V].

**The cycle in one line:** Indian shrimp feed margins are set by (fishmeal + SBM cost) minus (what AP farmers can bear, which is set by US/China shrimp prices net of duties). The FY22 trough and the FY26 peak were both input-driven; FY27 combines an input spike with a still-unsettled US duty regime.

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Feed formulation and alternative proteins.** Insect meal (black soldier fly), single-cell protein, krill, poultry by-product meal and fermented soy can cut fishmeal inclusion; global leaders (Skretting, BioMar) run low-fishmeal shrimp diets [K]. Avanti is reformulating "so that we mitigate the usage of the raw material" [V call]. This is both a threat (lower entry barrier) and the main defence against fishmeal spikes.
2. **Functional feeds and disease management.** EHP (Enterocytozoon hepatopenaei), white spot (WSSV), running mortality syndrome and vibriosis are recurring; functional feeds, probiotics and biosecure farming (RAS, lined ponds, nursery systems) shift value toward technical service [K]. Avanti's Q2 FY26 saw "premature harvest and localised disease outbreaks" after heavy rains [V AR].
3. **Genetics.** Ecuador's cost lead partly rests on domesticated, disease-tolerant lines and low-density extensive farming; India relies on imported SPF broodstock (single-source risk) [K]. Avanti's 600 mn PL hatchery made a loss in FY26 [V].
4. **Automated feeding** (acoustic auto-feeders) improves FCR by 10–20% [K, FLAG] — lowers feed tonnes per kg shrimp: a long-run volume headwind for feed makers.
5. **Cattle feed:** precision nutrition, bypass protein, TMR (total mixed ration) for large dairies; sexed semen raising yields → more concentrate demand [K].
6. **Oil palm:** high-yield seed gardens (GAVL's planned seed garden in ~18–24 months), mechanised harvesting, specialty-fat refining (+~200 bps to GAVL EBITDA when scaled) [V call].
7. **Lab-grown/plant-based shrimp and chicken:** negligible in India in the forecast horizon [K].
8. **Climate:** El Niño (fishmeal via Peru, monsoon via India) is the single most important exogenous variable for both companies in FY27 [V AR; V GAVL].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **The protein-cost cycle (fishmeal, soymeal, maize) sets feed margins, and FY27 opened with the sharpest spike on record.** Fishmeal doubled (₹100 → ₹230/kg) and SBM rose ~45% in May–June 2026; Avanti's feed EBITDA/t halved year on year (₹13,987 → ₹6,094) despite +17% volume [V]. Feed makers pass costs on with a lag and only partly, because farmers' affordability is set by shrimp prices they do not control.
2. **The US duty stack on Indian shrimp — AD, CVD and tariffs layered on each other — sets the export leg and, with a lag, farm-gate prices and feed demand.** It fell from ~57% (Aug-2025) to ~21% now, but AD has ratcheted 1.35 → 3.76 → 5.53% and Sec 301 is contested [V FR; E]. India's response has been market diversification: US share down to 33%, China +24%, EU +36% [V].
3. **Pricing power in shrimp feed is now politically contested.** Avanti's 51–53% share is the moat, but the AP government is designing a feed-price formula; feed makers must balance "the affordability and sustainability of the farmer" [V call]. A regulated spread would cap the upside of the FY25–FY26 kind.
4. **Ecuador is the price-setter in global shrimp.** At ~1.39 mt and US$7.47 bn (2025), Ecuador is ~1.75× India's export volume and gained US share during India's tariff spike [V-snip]. Its subsidy-driven cost edge has eroded (Avanti AR: farm-gate prices "converged" for the first time in ~4 years) [V].
5. **For Godrej, portfolio mix — not feed — decides returns.** Oil palm (40% of segment PBIT) is policy-backed but CPO-price-geared (11% crude duty from 24-Sep-2026); the new CEO is pruning (shrimp feed review, live-bird exit, broiler feed reduction) and rebooting dairy and Astec [V].

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Feed maker:** Profit = Tonnes × (Realisation/kg − Raw-material cost/kg − Conversion & freight/kg) − Fixed overheads
- Tonnes = Market tonnes (shrimp output × FCR, or livestock population × penetration) × Share.

**Shrimp processor:** Profit = Export tonnes × (FOB $/kg × ₹/$ × (1 − duties absorbed) − Raw shrimp ₹/kg ÷ yield − Processing & freight ₹/kg)

**Oil palm mill:** Profit = FFB tonnes × [OER × CPO ₹/t + KER × PKO ₹/t − FFB price (≈ 15–17% × CPO ₹/t) − milling cost]

- Duty: if DDP (Avanti as importer of record), AD 5.53% + CVD 5.77% + Sec 301 10% = 21.3% of customs value (~₹840) ≈ ₹179/kg; if FOB, the importer bears it and bids lower [E].
- **Result ≈ ₹185/kg before duty (18.6%); ~₹6/kg after full DDP duty** — which is why exporters sell FOB and the duty is shared through lower prices [E]. Reported FY26 segment result was 7.6%.

### C.4 Worked example 4 — one tonne of FFB at a Godrej mill, FY26 vs Q1 FY27 [E; ILLUSTRATIVE]

| **Margin per t FFB** | **~₹3,467 (15%)** | **~₹4,927 (18%)** |
