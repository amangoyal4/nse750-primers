**Units, conventions and FX**
- ₹ crore (Cr) = 10 million. ₹ lakh crore = ₹1 trillion. 1 lakh tonnes (LMT) = 0.1 million tonnes (MT/MMT). 1 lakh quintals = 10,000 tonnes; 100 lakh quintals = 1 MT. 1 quintal (qtl) = 100 kg. ₹/qtl ÷ 100 = ₹/kg.
- **KLPD** = kilolitres per day of distillery capacity. 100 KLPD × ~330 operating days ≈ 3.3 crore litres/yr [E]. **1 crore litres = 10 million litres = 10,000 KL.** "Bulk litres" (BL) = litres as measured.
- **TCD** = tonnes of cane crushed per day. **Recovery %** = sugar produced ÷ cane crushed (weight). **"Gross recovery"** = recovery before the effect of B-heavy/juice diversion (i.e., the sugar that would have been made); "net/reported recovery" is after diversion.
- **Three different years:** Sugar Season (SS) = **1-Oct to 30-Sep** (e.g., SS 2025-26 = Oct-2025 to Sep-2026). Ethanol Supply Year (ESY) = **1-Nov to 31-Oct** (since ESY 2023-24; before that Dec–Nov). Financial year (FY) = **Apr–Mar** (FY26 = Apr-2025 to Mar-2026). Q1 FY27 = Apr–Jun 2026; Q2 FY27 = Jul–Sep 2026 (sugar's off-season quarter; crushing restarts Oct/Nov).
- **FX: ₹94.9 = US$1** (19-Oct-2026; record low near ₹96.8) — used for all USD conversions. 1 US¢/lb = US$22.05/t; so ICE #11 at 18 ¢/lb ≈ $397/t ≈ ₹37,700/t ≈ ₹37.7/kg raw (before freight/refining) [E].

## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Three calendars.** Policy and industry data come in **sugar seasons (Oct–Sep)**, ethanol in **ESY (Nov–Oct)**, company P&Ls in **FY (Apr–Mar)**. FY26 P&L = the second half of SS 2024-25's inventory sales plus SS 2025-26's crush (Nov-25 to Apr-26). A "2025-26" FRP applies to cane crushed Oct-2025 onwards, i.e., mostly FY26 Q3–Q4 costs, and the sugar it produces is sold through FY27. **Q1 and Q2 are off-season quarters** (no crushing in UP/Maharashtra; sales out of inventory), so their margins tell you about inventory cost vs realisation, not about the coming season.
2. **Gross vs net sugar production.** ISMA/government now report **gross** production (before diversion to ethanol) and **net** (after). SS 2025-26: gross ~31.1–31.2 MT, diversion ~2.9–3.4 MT, **net ~27.8–28.0 MT** [V ICRA; V TEIL IB Q1; V-snip RuralVoice]. Never compare a pre-2022 "production" figure (which was effectively net) with a current gross number.
3. **Recovery % has two bases.** "Gross recovery" adds back the sugar sacrificed to B-heavy/juice diversion; reported (net) recovery does not. Balrampur FY26: seasonal recovery ~11.24% vs Q4 gross ~11.59% [primer]. Triveni reports gross recovery (10.80% SS 2024-25 → 11.06% SS 2025-26) [V TEIL IB Q1]. UP mills recover ~10.5–11.5%; Maharashtra/Karnataka ~10–11.5%; Tamil Nadu ~8.5–9.5% [K/E]. Compare only like with like.
4. **EID Parry: consolidated ≠ EID.** Consolidated FY26 revenue **₹38,534 Cr** includes **Coromandel's ₹31,480 Cr** [V screener both]. Screener's consolidated "Net Profit" **₹1,380 Cr includes minority interest** (~44% of Coromandel); **attributable PAT ≈ ₹569 Cr** [E: EPS ₹32.02 × 17.78 cr shares]. **Standalone EID** (the sugar/distillery company itself) had FY26 revenue **₹3,120 Cr, OP −₹49 Cr, PAT −₹708 Cr** (after ~₹382 Cr negative other income = impairments, largely of PSRIPL) [V screener standalone]. Our primer's "standalone ~₹6,700 Cr, net cash" is wrong on both counts (the ₹6,700 Cr is the **consolidated segment** total for sugar+distillery+nutra, including the PSRIPL refinery and US/Chile nutra subs). The "ex-Coromandel" view used in Part 3 = consolidated minus Coromandel consolidated [E]: FY26 revenue ₹7,054 Cr, OP ₹218 Cr, PAT −₹518 Cr.
5. **Screener's EID P/E (13.8) is unreliable.** ₹11,167 Cr m-cap ÷ attributable FY26 PAT ₹569 Cr = **19.6×**; on TTM EPS ₹26.13 = **24.0×** [E]. Neither matches 13.8. Use the look-through (stake value) framing instead (§B).
6. **Triveni: pre- vs post-demerger, and "gross" vs "net of excise".** (a) See still-listed item 1. (b) Triveni reports **gross revenue** (incl. excise on IMIL/IMFL liquor) and **revenue net of excise**: Q1 FY27 ₹1,950 Cr gross vs **₹1,581 Cr net**; FY26 consolidated gross ₹7,282 Cr; screener shows net (₹6,290 Cr FY26) [V TEIL IB Q1; V screener]. Distillery segment FY26 gross ₹2,883 Cr ≈ ₹1,550 Cr net of excise [V; primer]. Never add Triveni's gross segment revenue to anyone's net revenue.
7. **Renuka: reported vs pre-exceptional PAT; forex.** FY26 consolidated PAT **−₹792 Cr** includes a **~₹295 Cr impairment of the integrated milling division** [primer]; pre-exceptional loss ≈ −₹500 Cr [E]. Q1 FY27 loss ₹252 Cr is driven by **₹182 Cr interest** and **forex losses** on USD debt/raw-sugar payables [V screener; V-snip]. Renuka's EBITDA swings with refinery timing and forex reclassification — use 4-quarter sums.
8. **Lakh tonnes vs million tonnes vs crore litres.** 1 MT = 10 LMT; ISMA quotes LMT, USDA quotes MT (and USDA "production" is centrifugal sugar on a Oct–Sep basis, closer to net). Ethanol: OMC tenders in **crore litres** (1,048 crore litres = 10.48 bn L); companies in KL. Diverting **1 MT of sugar ≈ ~60 crore litres of ethanol** (≈600 L/t sugar equivalent via juice/syrup/B-heavy) [K, industry rule of thumb; E].
9. **Ethanol prices are ex-mill, excluding GST (5%) and transport.** Feedstock prices are per-litre **by route** (C-heavy, B-heavy, juice/syrup, FCI rice, damaged grain, maize). A distillery's realised price is a feedstock-mix average (Triveni Q1 FY27 ₹59.20/L) [V TEIL IB Q1] and also includes ENA/potable alcohol sales at commercial prices.
10. **"Inventory" means stock carried at cost, not market.** Sugar is valued at lower of cost or NRV: Triveni held **3.59 lakh t at ₹38.41/kg** on 30-Jun-2026 (vs 4.45 lakh t at ₹37.41–37.62/kg) [V TEIL IB Q1]. When ex-mill prices rise after the quarter (as in Jul–Oct 2026), Q2/Q3 margins expand on low-cost inventory — a one-off, not a structural margin.

### 1.2 Value chain (one tonne of cane, illustrative UP mill) [E unless tagged]
1. **Farmer → mill gate.** Each UP mill has a reserved command area; growers must supply it and the mill must buy all registered cane (cane reservation under the UP Sugarcane (Regulation of Supply and Purchase) Act) [K; primer]. Price: UP SAP ₹390–400/qtl = **₹3,900–4,000/t** (2025-26) [V-snip]. Harvest & transport is the farmer's cost in UP (mills pay a centre-to-mill transport deduction ~₹9–12/qtl in some states) [K/E]. Statutory payment within 14 days, interest on delay [K].
2. **Crushing.** ~70–72% juice, ~28–32% bagasse (50% moisture) [K]. Juice → clarified → evaporated → crystallised → centrifuged.
3. **Outputs per tonne (UP, sugar-max mode):** ~110–115 kg sugar (11–11.5% recovery); ~45 kg C-heavy molasses (4.5%); ~300 kg bagasse; ~35–40 kg press mud [K/E].
4. **By-product monetisation:** 45 kg C-heavy molasses × ~0.23 L/kg ≈ **10–11 L ethanol** (≈₹600 at ₹57.97/L); bagasse → ~100–130 kWh exportable surplus per tonne cane after captive use in a 87–110 bar plant (≈₹350–550 at ₹3.5–4.5/kWh) [K/E].
5. **Diversion options (the "juice fork"):** **B-heavy** — stop crystallising early: lose ~2–2.5 pp of recovery (~20–25 kg sugar) to gain ~20–25 L ethanol per tonne cane (B-heavy ~300–320 L per t molasses); **juice/syrup** — no sugar at all: ~70–80 L ethanol per tonne cane [K, industry norms; E]. Rule of thumb ~600 L ethanol per tonne of sugar forgone [K/E].
6. **Buyers:** sugar to traders/institutions (released monthly by DFPD quota); ethanol to OMC depots under tender allocations (payment ~21 days) [K]; power to discoms (payment risk varies by state).

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **The farmer captures the cane price** (administered, ratcheting): cane is **~70–80% of a sugar mill's cost of sugar** [primer; K].
- **The government captures the sugar price** (MSP floor, monthly release quotas, export permits/bans, stock limits) and the **ethanol price** (administered).
- **The mill captures:** (i) recovery above the state average (every +0.1 pp recovery at Balrampur's scale ≈ ~10,400 t sugar ≈ ₹42 Cr of revenue with no extra cane cost [E: 1,043 lakh qtl × 0.1% × ₹40.7/kg]); (ii) by-product integration (distillery + cogen per tonne of cane); (iii) mix (refined/pharma sugar, institutional sales premia); (iv) timing (inventory carried into price upswings); (v) for Renuka, the refining spread on imported raws.
- **The OMC/government captures the forex saving** from blending (₹1.72 lakh Cr spent by PSU OMCs on ethanol over three ESYs [V-snip angelone/latestly headline]).

### 2.3 Domestic vs export
- Exports have ranged from **~0 (2023-24 ban; 0.1 MT) to 11.1 MT (2021-22, record)** [K; V TEIL IB Q1 for 0.1/0.8/0.7]. SS 2025-26: quota **1.5 MT (7/14-Nov-2025) + 0.5 MT (13-Feb-2026) = 2.0 MT allowed; only ~0.7–0.8 MT shipped** — world prices were below Indian parity and the Iran/West Asia conflict disrupted trade — then a **full ban to 30-Sep-2026** (May-2026) [V RuralVoice; V ICRA; V-snip GTA/newswav]. A separate 5 LMT pool saw only 87,587 t approved [V-snip].
- SS 2026-27 export stance: **no quota announced; given the ~4 MT opening stock and a deficient monsoon, exports are unlikely and imports are being discussed as a possibility** (RuralVoice warns India "could become a net importer") [V RuralVoice; E].
- India's share of world exports: ~15–17% in 2021-22's peak, now ~1–2% [E].

### 2.4 Growth outlook [E]
- Sugar consumption +1.5% p.a.; production volatile ±10–15% with monsoon/disease; ethanol demand capped at ~1,050–1,100 crore litres until E27/E30 or FFVs scale; grain already supplies ~2/3.
- **The structural growth vector for cane mills has flipped:** 2018–2024 = "divert more sugar to ethanol"; 2025–2027 = "protect sugar for food, buy grain for ethanol". Mill distillery capacity (~1,000+ crore litres of cane-feed capacity nationally [K/E]) is under-utilised.

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Household consumption (~35–40% of sugar)**: population, per-capita ~19–20 kg/yr [K], festivals (Q3 FY = Oct–Dec demand peak), PDS. Price-inelastic; politically sensitive (CPI weight ~1.4% for sugar & confectionery [K]).
2. **Industrial/bulk (~60–65%)** [V TEIL IB Q1]: soft drinks, confectionery, biscuits, ice cream, dairy, pharma. Summer (Q1 FY) beverage demand matters — the West Asia heatwave/heat in 2026 summer [E]. Institutional buyers pay premia for quality (refined/ICUMSA <45) — Balrampur and Triveni emphasise institutional sales; Triveni's realisation is "supported by an increasing share of refined sugar and pharma-grade sugar" [V TEIL IB Q1].
3. **Ethanol blending demand**: E20 → ~1,050–1,100 crore litres/yr. Petrol demand growth ~5–6% p.a. adds ~50–60 crore litres/yr at E20 [E]. Beyond E20 (E27/E30, FFVs, E85, ETJ/SAF) is the only large step-up; no notified mandate beyond E20 as of Oct-2026 [V TEIL IB Q1 lists only "roadmap" work]. Sugar's share of that demand is set by policy (now zero for juice/B-heavy in ESY 2026-27).
4. **Exports**: discretionary, quota-based; profitable only when ICE #11 + white premium exceeds Indian ex-mill parity (~₹40–42/kg ≈ 19–20 ¢/lb raw equivalent after freight) [E]. ICE #11 traded ~14.8 ¢/lb (24-Jul-26) and ~18.2 ¢/lb (27-Aug-26) for the Oct-26 contract [V-snip commodity-board] — below parity most of SS 2025-26, which is why only ~0.7–0.8 MT of a 2 MT quota shipped.
5. **Alcohol (potable)**: ENA demand for IMFL/IMIL grows ~6–8% [K]; excise quota policy in UP affects IMIL (Triveni IMIL −1% in Q1 FY27 "due to revision in UP excise quota allocation policy" [V TEIL IB Q1]).
6. **Power**: discom PPAs, often at regulator-set feed-in tariffs; UPPCL procurement from bagasse cogen.
7. **PLA/bioplastics**: India imports all PLA today [primer]; single-use plastics ban (Jul-2022) and EPR rules are the regulatory pull [K]. Balrampur guides ~₹2,000 Cr annual revenue at full 80 ktpa [V-snip chemindigest] → implied ~₹2.5 lakh/t (~US$2,630/t) [E] — above current global PLA prices (~US$1,800–2,500/t) [K/E; FLAG].

## 5. Industry structure

### 5.1 Concentration
- **Fragmented:** the largest group (Balrampur, ~10 mills, ~1.0–1.1 MT sugar/yr gross) is ~3–4% of national output [E: 1,043 lakh qtl × ~11.2% ≈ 1.17 MT gross; FY26 sugar sold 100.6 lakh qtl = 1.0 MT [primer]]. Top-10 private groups ≈ 25–30% [K/E].
- **By state:** UP ~35–40% of production, Maharashtra ~30–35%, Karnataka ~15%, rest (TN, Gujarat, AP, Bihar, Punjab, Haryana, Uttarakhand) ~15% [K]. A Maharashtra/Karnataka drought therefore moves the national balance; UP's red-rot/jaggery leakage moved it in 2024-26.
- **Ownership:** UP is overwhelmingly private (Balrampur, Triveni, Bajaj Hindusthan, DSCL/DCM Shriram, Dhampur, Dwarikesh, Mawana, Birla/Avadh, Uttam, Dalmia) plus some state/cooperative mills; Maharashtra is ~55–60% cooperative (politically connected) [K]. Cooperatives dominate MSP/FRP lobbying (NFCSF) while ISMA represents private mills.

### 5.4 Imports and China
- India is normally a **net exporter** of sugar; imports are minimal (raw sugar for refining under Advance Authorisation, e.g., Renuka's port refineries) [K]. **2026-27 could flip India toward imports** for the first time since 2016-17 [V RuralVoice warning; E].
- **China** is not a direct competitor in sugar; it is a large importer of raws and a competitor in **PLA** (TotalEnergies-Corbion has Thailand 75 ktpa; NatureWorks US 150 ktpa + Thailand 75 ktpa under construction/started; Chinese PLA capacity ramped in 2022–25) [K; FLAG — capacities from memory].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

- **2014–2016 bust:** global glut, ICE #11 below 11 ¢/lb (2015), FRP rising → **cane arrears peaked ~₹21,000 Cr (Apr-2015)**; soft loans, production subsidy ₹4.50/qtl of cane, mandatory export quotas (MIEQ) [K]. Renuka's Brazil adventure (2010) collapsed into judicial recovery; Wilmar entered (2014) [primer]. Balrampur, Triveni posted FY15 losses (screener: Balrampur FY15 PAT −₹58 Cr; Triveni −₹152 Cr; Renuka −₹1,814 Cr) [V screener].
- **2016-17 shortage:** drought (2015, 2016 monsoons) → production fell to ~20.3 MT [K]; sugar ~₹37–38/kg; FY17 was a record year (Balrampur OPM 26%, PAT ₹593 Cr; Triveni OPM 18%) [V screener].
- **2017–2019 glut:** record 32.5 MT (2017-18) and 33.2 MT (2018-19); ex-mill fell to ~₹25–26/kg (below cost); arrears >₹20,000 Cr again (2018) [K]. Policy response — the modern toolkit: **MSP introduced 7-Jun-2018 at ₹29/kg → ₹31/kg (14-Feb-2019)**; buffer stock 3 MT (2018) and 4 MT (2019) with carrying-cost reimbursement; export assistance (₹10,448/t in 2019-20; ₹6,000/t 2020-21) with MAEQ quotas of 5 MT/6 MT; **ethanol from B-heavy and juice allowed (2018)** with differential pricing; interest subvention for distillery capacity [K]. Renuka FY18 OP −₹2,614 Cr (writedowns) [V screener].
- **2018–2022 ethanol build-out:** blending 5% → 10% (E10 achieved Jun-2022, five months early); National Policy on Biofuels amended May-2022 to bring E20 forward to 2025-26 [K]. Distillery capacity at Triveni 320 → 660 KLPD (FY22→FY23) [primer], Balrampur to ~1,050 KLPD.
- **2021-22 export boom:** record 11.1 MT exports with no subsidy as world prices rose [K]; Triveni sold its 21.85% Triveni Turbine stake (Sep-2022) for **₹1,593 Cr** net [V TEIL IB Q1] (shows in Triveni FY23 other income ₹1,496 Cr [V screener]).
- **2023 tightening:** El Niño; **exports banned from Oct-2023** ("restricted"); **Dec-2023 juice diversion curbs**; stock limits; ICE #11 peaked ~28 ¢/lb (Nov-2023) [K]. Sector P/Es compressed.
- **2024–2025:** red rot in UP; SS 2024-25 net production 26.1 MT with 3.5 MT diversion [V TEIL IB Q1]; FRP ₹340 → ₹355; exports 1 MT allowed (Jan-2025), ~0.8 MT shipped [V-snip]; **E20 achieved in ESY 2024-25**; grain becomes the majority of ethanol; ICE #11 falls to five-year lows by Feb-2026 as Brazil's sugar mix rises to 50.91% [V TEIL IB Q1].
- **2026 (now):** export quota 2.0 MT but ~0.7–0.8 MT shipped then banned; closing stock 10-year low; monsoon −12.6%; **ESY 2026-27 bars cane-juice/B-heavy ethanol**; Triveni demerges gears (TPTL listed Oct-2026) and absorbs SSEL; EID shuts its Kakinada refinery; Renuka's net worth turns negative; Balrampur's PLA plant nears commissioning.
- **Cycle signature:** the industry has moved from a **3–4 year sugar-price cycle** (2010s) to a **policy cycle**: the state now uses diversion and export permissions to manage the price band ~₹36–42/kg ex-mill. The swing variable for listed earnings is no longer the sugar price alone but **(SAP − realisation) × crush, plus the ethanol price/feedstock rules**.

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Grain-ethanol displacement (live):** the ethanol programme no longer needs cane — grain was 72.5% of 2025-26 allocation [V]. Cane-route distillery capacity is stranded unless policy restores diversion. Multi-feed conversion (cane + grain) is the hedge (Triveni's two 200 KLPD multi-feed plants; Balrampur's grain capability) [V; primer].
2. **EVs and flex-fuel:** EV adoption caps long-run petrol (and therefore E20) demand; FFVs (E85) are the counter — the FFV launches of Jun-2026 are the first real step [V TEIL IB Q1].
3. **Sweetener substitution / health policy:** sugar taxes, FSSAI front-of-pack labelling, GLP-1 drugs — long-dated risks to industrial demand [K/E].
4. **Cane varieties and climate:** red rot showed monoculture risk (Co-0238); El Niño risk to 2026-27 [primer; V ICRA].
5. **New products:** **PLA** (Balrampur), **compressed biogas (CBG) from press mud** (SATAT scheme; CBG blending obligation in CGD from FY26 at 1% rising [K]), **SAF via ethanol-to-jet** (mandate 1%/2%/5% for international flights 2027/28/30 [V TEIL IB Q1]), **2G ethanol** from bagasse (IOC Panipat; Praj) [K], **ethanol cookstoves** [V TEIL IB Q1], **ethanol for ED95 buses/marine** [K].
6. **Cogeneration value erosion:** cheap solar (₹2.5/kWh tenders) undercuts bagasse cogen feed-in tariffs; bagasse may be worth more as CBG/2G feedstock [primer; K].
7. **Automation/mechanised harvesting:** labour shortages in Maharashtra cane harvesting; harvesters spreading [K].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert leads with)

1. **The administered price sandwich (cane up, sugar/ethanol frozen).** FRP has risen every season but one since 2018-19 (₹275 → ₹365) and UP SAP ratchets ~₹25–30 every second season (next one likely before the Feb–Mar 2027 UP election), while the sugar MSP has been ₹31/kg since Feb-2019 and B-heavy/juice ethanol prices since ESY 2022-23. Margins therefore depend on *market* sugar prices staying well above MSP — which, in 2026, they are, because supply is short. The sandwich explains why FY26 was a "good operating year, flat profit year" (Balrampur revenue +15.8%, EBITDA +5.3% [primer]).
2. **The food-vs-fuel switch has flipped to "food first".** From 2018 to 2025 policy pushed sugar into ethanol (diversion peaked ~4 MT); in 2026 policy is pulling it back: export ban (May-2026), and **zero diversion in the ESY 2026-27 tender** (16-Oct-2026). Mills' cane-feed distilleries (built on that 2018–22 promise) must now run on C-heavy molasses or grain; the ethanol programme itself (E20) no longer needs cane (grain 72.5% of allocation).
3. **A genuine supply shock: 10-year-low stocks + failed monsoon + El Niño.** Closing stock ~4.1 MT (Sep-2026) vs a "comfortable" ~7 MT; monsoon −12.6% (Karnataka −35%); USDA −14% for 2026-27 to 29.5 MT. This is bullish sugar prices and inventory gains in FY27 H1, but bearish crush volumes, recoveries and fixed-cost absorption in SS 2026-27 — and it raises the odds of stock limits, MSP-irrelevance and possibly imports.
4. **Distillery overcapacity and the grain pivot.** Offers of 1,776 crore litres vs ~1,050 needed (ESY 2025-26); dedicated grain plants with LTOAs get preference; maize price cut to ₹68.86 incl. incentive. Integrated mills' distillery returns now depend on feedstock flexibility (Triveni 61% grain ethanol, targeting 3:1) and on "Beyond E20" (E27/E30, FFV/E85, ETJ/SAF) arriving.
5. **Business-model divergence explains valuation dispersion.** Balrampur (quality + PLA option) is 40% of the group's m-cap on 10% of revenue; Triveni has unbundled (gears → TPTL, 29.88% retained) into a sugar–alcohol–water company; EID is a holdco trading at ~39% of its Coromandel stake's value with a loss-making own business after shutting its refinery; Renuka is a Wilmar-guaranteed, negative-equity refiner whose interest bill (₹736 Cr) exceeds its EBITDA ~33×.

Supporting forces (for the writer's list of 6–10):
6. **Working capital and inventory accounting** — sugar mills are "warehouses with a crushing plant attached" (primer): inventory days ~240 at Balrampur/Triveni [V screener]; inventory at cost (₹38.41/kg Triveni) vs rising market → inventory gains; interest ~1.2–1.6% of revenue; Renuka's interest >8% of revenue.
7. **Trade policy and world prices** — exports profitable only above ~19–20 ¢/lb raw equivalent; SS 2025-26 quota 2.0 MT but only ~0.7–0.8 MT shipped; ICE #11 five-year lows (Feb-2026) then war-volatility; Brazil sugar mix 50.91%.
8. **By-product optionality (cogen, PLA, CBG, SAF)** — cogen tariffs regulated and eroding vs solar; PLA is the only non-administered-price product among members; SAF mandate 2027–30 opens ethanol-to-jet.
9. **Cane agronomy and state economics** — UP (SAP, private mills, high recovery ~11%, reservation areas) vs Maharashtra/Karnataka (FRP, cooperatives, monsoon-dependent, arrears ₹4,898 Cr) vs Tamil Nadu (cane-short, low recovery; EID's base).

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Integrated mill (Balrampur/Triveni):**
EBITDA = Cane crushed (t) × [ Recovery% × Sugar realisation (₹/kg) × 1,000 × (1 − diversion share) + Ethanol L per t × Ethanol price (₹/L) + Surplus kWh per t × Tariff ] − Cane crushed × SAP − Conversion & overheads ± Inventory valuation effect + Grain-ethanol margin × grain KL.

Short form: **EBITDA/t of cane = (Revenue/t − SAP − conversion cost/t)**; group EBITDA = EBITDA/t × crush + grain-distillery margin + other businesses.

**Worked example — Balrampur FY26 [E, label ILLUSTRATIVE]**
- Cane crushed 1,043 lakh qtl = 10.43 MT [primer].
- Revenue ₹6,271 Cr ⇒ **₹6,013 per tonne** of cane [V screener; E].
- Cane cost: 10.43 MT × ~₹3,950/t (SAP ₹390/400; FY26 mixes SS 2024-25 ₹360/370 and SS 2025-26 ₹390/400 seasons — use blended ~₹3,800/t) ≈ **₹3,960–4,120 Cr** [E].
- Conversion, grain/other materials, employees, other expenses ≈ ₹1,400–1,560 Cr (balance) [E].
- **EBITDA ₹747 Cr = ₹716 per tonne** [V screener OP; E].
- Sensitivities [E]: +₹10/qtl SAP → −₹104 Cr (−14% EBITDA); +₹1/kg sugar realisation on ~1.0 MT sold → +₹100 Cr; +0.1 pp recovery → +~₹42 Cr; +₹3/L on ~30 crore litres of ethanol (1,050 KLPD × ~290 days) → +₹90 Cr.

**Port refiner (Renuka refinery):** Refining EBITDA = Throughput (t) × [White price − Raw price (landed: ICE #11 × 22.05 × FX + freight + duty if any) − refining cost (~$40–60/t)] [K/E].

**Holdco (EID):** Value = 56% × Coromandel m-cap + EID own businesses (EV) − EID standalone net debt − holdco discount.

**Distillery (standalone grain):** Margin/L = Ethanol price − (grain kg/L × grain ₹/kg) + DDGS credit − conversion. Maize: ~2.5–2.6 kg/L; at ₹22–24/kg maize → ₹57–62/L feedstock; DDGS credit ~₹6–8/L; price ₹68.86 → spread ~₹13–18/L before conversion (~₹8–10/L) [E/K]. FCI rice at ₹23.20/kg, ~2.2 kg/L → ₹51/L feedstock vs ₹60.32 price [E].
