**Units, conventions and FX**
- ₹ crore (Cr) = 10 million. ₹ lakh crore = ₹1 trillion = ₹1,00,000 Cr.
- **MMT / MT** = million (metric) tonnes; **MTPA** = million tonnes per annum (capacity). Companies use "MMT" (APSEZ) and "MT"/"mtpa" (JSW) for the same thing.
- **TEU** = twenty-foot equivalent unit (one standard container). Rule of thumb for converting TEU to tonnes in Indian port statistics: ~13–15 t per TEU [E, derived: MoPSW FY26 major ports 213.0 Mt container tonnes ÷ 14.92m TEU = **14.3 t/TEU**; non-major 161.6 Mt ÷ 10.77m = **15.0 t/TEU**] [V arithmetic on MoPSW data].
- **RoRo** = roll-on/roll-off (cars driven onto car-carrier ships), counted in units (vehicles).
- **POL** = petroleum, oil and lubricants (crude + products); **EXIM** = export–import; **coastal** = domestic sea cargo between Indian ports.
- **"Major ports"** = the 12 port authorities owned by the Union (MoPSW): Deendayal (Kandla), Mumbai, JNPA, Mormugao, New Mangalore, Cochin, Chennai, Kamarajar (Ennore), V.O. Private *terminals inside major ports* (PPP) count as major-port cargo.

## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **"Cargo" for APSEZ is two numbers.** FY26 **501 MMT is the group total including international ports** (Haifa, Colombo, Dar es Salaam, and NQXT from Q4) [V deck]. All-India market share (27.6% in Q1 FY27) is computed on **domestic** volume only (115.3 MMT in Q1 FY27; 112.9 a year earlier, **+2%**) [V]. The group total grew **+15%** in Q1 FY27 (138.1 vs 120.6 MMT) mostly because NQXT (10 MMT) and Colombo (6.9 MMT) were added [V]. Never compare APSEZ's group tonnes with India's port total.
2. **"Market share" — whose denominator?** APSEZ's ~27–28% is of **all-India cargo (major + non-major)**; its ~45% container share is of all-India TEU [V]. JSW's ~7% and GPPL's <1% must be computed by us [E]. All-India FY26 = **914.73 MMT major (MoPSW PDMP, +7.00%) + 753.07 MMT non-major (+1.44%) ≈ 1,668 MMT** [V both MoPSW PDFs; E sum]. Note the ministry press statement gives major ports **915.17 MMT (+7.06%)** — a revision/rounding difference vs the PDMP table (914.73) [V-snip IANS vs V PDF]. Use 914.73 with the table; 915 in prose.
3. **Major vs non-major is not public vs private.** Private operators run PPP terminals inside major ports (JSW at Paradip, Ennore, Mangalore, Tuticorin, JNPA, Kolkata; APSEZ at Kandla-Tuna, Mormugao, Ennore, and now Paradip) — that tonnage is in the **major-port** count. Almost all private *ports* (Mundra, Jaigarh, Dharamtar, Pipavav, Krishnapatnam, Gangavaram, Dhamra, Hazira) are **non-major**. So "major ports grew 7% while non-major grew 1.4% in FY26" does **not** mean "government beat private".
4. **Revenue/EBITDA per tonne is not comparable across companies.** APSEZ domestic ports Q1 FY27: revenue **₹604/t**, EBITDA **₹447/t** [E: ₹6,964 Cr ÷ 115.3 MMT; ₹5,152 Cr ÷ 115.3]. JSW ports Q1 FY27: revenue **₹390/t**, EBITDA **₹194/t** [E: ₹1,208 Cr ÷ 31.0 MT; ₹601 Cr ÷ 31.0]. The gap is mostly **mix** (APSEZ ~42% container by tonnes vs JSW mostly dry bulk) and **harbour/marine revenue** inside APSEZ domestic ports (APSEZ's "Harbour" cluster alone made ₹1,295 Cr revenue at 89% margin in Q1 FY27 [V]). GPPL reports per TEU/tonne/unit, not a blended figure.
5. **APSEZ "Ports" EBITDA margin (~74%) ≠ group margin (~60%).** Domestic ports 74% (Q1 FY27), International ports 41.8%, Marine 44.8%, Logistics 18.7% (trucking 5.9%), SEZ/port development ~100% but episodic [V]. Group FY26 EBITDA margin 59% [V].
6. **JSW Infra: "operating EBITDA" excludes other income; "EBITDA" includes it.** Q1 FY27 operating EBITDA ₹674 Cr (46.6%) vs EBITDA ₹731 Cr (48.7%) [V deck]. Screener OP = operating EBITDA (₹674 Cr) [V]. Guidance (FY27E ₹3,000 Cr, FY28E ₹5,000 Cr) is on the **operating** basis [V].
7. **JSW Infra "captive" means JSW Group (mostly JSW Steel + JSW Energy) — ~52% of tonnes.** Third-party share is ~48% (FY26 and Q1 FY27); it **peaked at ~49% in FY25** and has been flat since, because group cargo grows faster when JSW Steel's Dolvi expands (Q1 FY27: group cargo +13.7%, third-party −1.9%) [V deck]. The narrative "third-party share keeps rising" is no longer true on the latest data.
8. **JSW Infra capacity "186 MTPA" excludes the UAE O&M terminals** (Fujairah 24 + Dibba 17 = 41 MTPA, operated not owned) but its **cargo "122 MT" includes ~6.6 MT of UAE volumes** (Fujairah liquid 5.71 + Fujairah port 0.84) [V deck]. Indian cargo was **115 MT** in FY26. Utilisation computed as 122/183 overstates Indian utilisation slightly.
9. **GPPL's FY26 dividend payout is ~98–100% of PAT, not "80%+".** Screener payout FY24 103%, FY25 100%, FY26 98% [V]; trailing yield **6.62%** at ₹157 [V]. Q1 FY27 revenue (+33%) is **flattered by duty-benefit scrips** (export-incentive scrips booked as revenue and expense); ex-scrips revenue +20%, EBITDA +25% [V deck].
10. **Concession clocks differ by an order of magnitude — and Mundra's is not 30+ years.** GPPL ends **29-Sep-2028**; **Mundra (APSEZ's flagship, ~44% of its domestic tonnes) ends 16-Feb-2031** under the original GMB concession, with extension requests filed in 2015 and 2021 and **no Gujarat extension framework yet** [V CargoConnect 10-Aug-26]. APSEZ's own deck claims "average remaining port concession tenure of 30+ years" [V] — that average is across 15 ports and evidently assumes extensions or counts newer concessions; the writer must not let the Mundra date disappear. JSW's Jaigarh is a 50-year BOOST concession [primer].

### 1.2 Value chain (ship → shelf)

1. **Origin hinterland** (factory, mine, refinery) → road/rail to port (APSEZ and JSW now own pieces of this leg).
2. **Port gate / CFS / ICD** — customs, stuffing, storage.
3. **Terminal** — yard → quay crane → ship. The terminal operator earns handling charges; the **port owner** (major port authority or state maritime board) earns **royalty / revenue share** and **vessel-related charges** (port dues, pilotage, berth hire) where it provides them.
4. **Marine side** — dredged channel, breakwater, pilots, tugs. At private non-major ports the developer provides all of it and keeps the vessel-related revenue (this is why APSEZ's "Harbour" division runs at ~89–91% margin [V]).
5. **Ocean leg** — shipping line (Maersk, MSC, CMA CGM…) earns freight; at Indian container terminals the lines often co-invest (Mundra CT-3 with MSC 50:50; CT-4 with CMA CGM 50:50; Ennore with MSC; Vizhinjam pending 49% TiL/MSC) [V deck; K]. Pipavav is controlled by APM Terminals (Maersk) [V].
6. **Destination port** abroad — APSEZ now owns some (Colombo WCT/CWIT 51%? [K], Haifa 70% with Gadot [K], Dar es Salaam CT2 via EAGL [V: "East Africa Gateway Ltd"], NQXT Australia 100% [K]).

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **The port developer at a non-major port captures the most:** it owns the channel, the land and the berths under a 30–50-year concession, sets its own tariffs (no TAMP-style regulation; under the Indian Ports Act 2025 tariff-fixing at non-major ports sits with State Maritime Boards [V-snip Trilegal/PRS]) and pays a low royalty to the state (GPPL: **revenue share "under 5%"**, Jefferies [V BusinessToday]).
- **A PPP terminal operator inside a major port captures less:** it bid away a revenue share to the port authority (often 30–50%+ in recent container/bulk terminal bids [K; e.g. JNPA's BMCT/PSA bid ~35.8% K]) and historically faced TAMP-regulated ceilings (until 2021).
- **Shipping lines capture network rent** and decide which terminal is a hub. Hence the line–terminal JVs.
- **Land and logistics** capture the "door" end at lower margin (APSEZ logistics 18.7% EBITDA margin [V]; trucking 5.9%).

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Energy imports (POL + coal ≈ 52% of all-India tonnes)** [E from §2.2]. India imports ~85–88% of its crude [K, PPAC]; coal imports ~240–270 Mt/yr (thermal + coking) [K, FLAG]. Thermal coal at major ports +14% and coking coal +12% in Apr–Jul FY27 [V]. Coking coal is steel-linked (JSW Steel: 33.4 MTPA India capacity → 50.3 MTPA by FY30 [V JSW deck]).
2. **Steel and iron ore** (JSW's anchor): JSW Steel Dolvi expansion (+5 MTPA steel) underpins Dharamtar (+21 MTPA) and Jaigarh (+15 MTPA) expansions [V deck]. Iron ore at major ports +12.8% Apr–Jul FY27 [V].
3. **Containerised EXIM (manufactured goods)** — linked to merchandise trade and the China+1 shift; APSEZ cites "cargo boost via China+1 manufacturing capex" [V deck]. **Headwind: US 10% Sec-301 tariff on top of MFN since 24-Jul-2026** [V-sib]. Major-port container tonnes in **July 2026 were only +0.35%** YoY [V] — the first month after the US tariff change and a visible slowdown from +10% in FY26.
4. **Auto exports (RoRo):** GPPL RoRo +40% in FY26 (229k units) and +53% in Q1 FY27; Mundra loaded a record 6,548 cars on one vessel [V].
5. **Fertiliser & agri** — fertiliser +23.8% at majors Apr–Jul FY27 [V]; non-major fertiliser +51.9% in FY26 [V].
6. **Coastal shipping** — coastal cargo at majors +13.87% Apr–Jul FY27 (24.15% of total) and at non-majors +17.0% Apr–May [V]: the government's coastal push (Coastal Shipping Act 2025 [K]) and coal movement by sea (rail-sea-rail) are lifting it.
7. **Transhipment** (§2.3) — a new demand pool created by Vizhinjam, Vadhavan (later) and Great Nicobar (later).
8. **Logistics cost policy** — PM Gati Shakti, DFC (Western DFC to JNPA/Mundra/Pipavav), GPWIS/LSFTO rail schemes, Gati Shakti cargo terminals (GCT) [V JSW deck]; JSW deck cites rail freight 1.7 bn t in FY26, targeting 45% rail modal share by 2030, and port capacity "to quadruple to 10,000 MTPA by 2047" (Maritime Amrit Kaal Vision) [V].

How the economics differ:
- **Developer vs terminal operator:** a non-major-port developer keeps vessel-related charges (pilotage/towage/port dues) and pays a low royalty → domestic port margins of 70%+; a PPP terminal operator at a major port pays a revenue share and typically earns 30–55% [E/K].
- **Captive vs third-party:** captive take-or-pay cargo gives volume certainty at a negotiated (arm's-length-tested) tariff; third-party cargo is more contestable but usually priced at market and diversifies risk. JSW's deck: Q1 FY27 group cargo +13.7%, third-party −1.9% [V].
- **Container vs bulk:** containers earn more revenue per tonne (and per berth-metre), require cranes/yard/rail, and are line-dependent; bulk needs conveyors and storage and is commodity-cycle dependent.

### 7.2 Tariffs

- **Major ports:** TAMP set ceilings (cost-plus 2005 guidelines; then 2008/2013 normative tariffs; **2018 guidelines** introduced market-linked tariffs for new PPP projects with a reference tariff and annual indexation to WPI) [K, FLAG]. Since the MPA Act 2021, **Port Authority boards** set scale of rates; PPP concessionaires on post-2021 concessions set market tariffs [K]. Legacy PPP terminals remain on indexed reference tariffs [K].
- **Non-major ports:** tariffs set by the developer under the concession, subject to the State Maritime Board — now codified by the Indian Ports Act 2025 [V-snip]. This **pricing freedom is a structural advantage of APSEZ, JSW (Jaigarh/Dharamtar) and GPPL**.
- **Royalty/revenue share:** GPPL's current revenue share **"under 5%"** (Jefferies) [V BusinessToday]; the Pipavav and Mundra renewals will set the template for whether Gujarat raises it.

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **1990s — Gujarat opens the coast.** GMB (est. 1982) pioneered private ports: Pipavav (conceived 1992, concession 1998, first private port), Mundra (Gujarat Adani Port, 1998; concession 2001) [V/K].
2. **2000s — containers arrive.** Maersk/APMT acquires control of Pipavav (2005) [primer]; Mundra builds CT-1/CT-2 (DP World CT-2) [K]; **Mundra Port & SEZ IPO Nov-2007 (~116× subscribed)** [primer]; Pipavav IPO Sep-2010 [primer/K].
3. **2010–2020 — consolidation and the east coast.** APSEZ buys Dhamra (2014), Kattupalli (2015), Krishnapatnam (2020–21), Dighi (2021), Gangavaram (2021–22), Karaikal (2023), Gopalpur (2024) [K]. JSW develops Jaigarh and Dharamtar and wins major-port terminals (Paradip, Ennore, Mangalore) [primer]. TAMP tariff regime under strain; landlord-model PPPs at majors.
4. **2021 — regulatory reset.** MPA Act 2021 (TAMP abolished); MIV 2030 [K].
5. **2023 — shocks and listings.** Hindenburg report (24-Jan-2023) — APSEZ falls with the group; Haifa acquisition completes (Jan-2023) [K/primer]; **JSW Infra IPO (₹2,800 Cr at ₹119; listed 3-Oct-2023)** [primer].
6. **2024–25 — transhipment and internationalisation.** Vadhavan approved (₹76,220 Cr, Jun-2024) [V-snip/K]; US indictment (Nov-2024) [primer]; Vizhinjam commercial ops (Dec-2024) and formal commissioning (May-2025) [K]; Colombo CWIT live (2025); Dar es Salaam CT2 (2024); Gemini Cooperation (Maersk + Hapag-Lloyd) launches Feb-2025 [primer]; NQXT acquisition announced (Apr-2025) [K]; Indian Ports Act 2025 passed (Aug-2025) [V-snip].
7. **FY26 — records.** Majors 915 MMT (+7%); APSEZ crosses 500 MMT (incl. international) [V]; JSW 122 MT; GPPL record PAT ₹515 Cr [V].
8. **2026 — war, tariffs, resolution.** West Asia war (28-Feb), ceasefire (7-Apr); OFAC settlement (18-May) [library]; JSW QIP (Jun quarter) [V]; MSC–APSEZ Vizhinjam deal [V]; US Sec-301 10% (24-Jul) [V-sib]; Adani criminal counts dismissed (Aug) [library]; GMB comfort letter to Pipavav (Aug) [V]; APSEZ Oman subsidiary (21-Oct) [V].

Cycle character: Indian port volumes are **not very cyclical** in aggregate (one down-year, FY21 Covid) but **commodity-mix shocks** (coal, POL, LPG) move individual ports hard (e.g., APSEZ Krishnapatnam −19% in Q1 FY27 [V]). Valuation cycles are driven more by **group events (Hindenburg 2023, indictment 2024)** than by volumes.

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Automation and digital twins** — APSEZ: Vizhinjam "India's first automated port"; Kaleris AI across 15 container terminals; 91 MMT efficiency unlock targeted by 2030; Port Community System upgrade cut pre-berthing time 24% [V Q4 deck].
2. **Energy transition** — coal is ~24% of all-India tonnes and ~33% of APSEZ domestic volume (38.4 of 115.3 MMT in Q1 FY27) [V; E]; NQXT is a coal export terminal [primer]. Long-run thermal-coal decline is the main structural volume risk; offset by LNG/LPG, green-ammonia/hydrogen export terminals (future) [K].
3. **Shipping-line network design** — alliance re-shuffles (Gemini, Feb-2025) decide hub vs spoke; Pipavav's volumes depend on Maersk's routing [primer; V].
4. **Mega-ship economics** — 24,000 TEU ships need ~16–18 m draft; only Vizhinjam, Mundra and (later) Vadhavan/Great Nicobar can take them on the west/south coasts [K]; Vizhinjam handled 399m+ LOA vessels [V].
5. **New government mega-ports** — Vadhavan (Phase 1 ~6.85m TEU by ~2030, 23.2m TEU ultimate) [V-snip] will compete directly with Mundra and Pipavav for the north-west hinterland via DFC; Great Nicobar will compete with Vizhinjam and Colombo for transhipment. Both are landlord-model — terminals will be bid out to private operators (APSEZ/JSW/DP World/PSA could win them) [K].
6. **Rail modal shift / DFC** — cuts inland cost and can re-allocate hinterland between ports; APSEZ and JSW own rakes/ICDs to capture it [V].
7. **Cyber and sanctions screening** — sanctioned-vessel/LPG cargo scrutiny (relevant to the OFAC matter) [library; K].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Scale begets share: APSEZ's network effect.** Deep-draft, all-weather ports + line JVs + rail/ICDs + land bank = a self-reinforcing share gain (all-India share ~24% FY22 [K] → 27.6% now [V]; container ~45%). The flip side is concentration risk and political scrutiny (Congress allegations on Gujarat policy [V-snip]).
2. **Concession tenure is the asset life — and Gujarat's pioneer concessions are expiring.** Pipavav 2028, Mundra 2031 [V]. The terms (tenure, royalty) of the first renewals will reset valuations for GPPL directly and for APSEZ's single largest asset. GMB's Comfort Letter (Aug-2026) is the first step [V].
3. **Mix beats volume.** Revenue grew 2–10× faster than tonnes in Q1 FY27 at all three (§2.4) because containers, RoRo, liquids, marine and logistics earn more per tonne than coal. Watch container share and RoRo, not just MMT.
4. **Energy-import exposure is a double-edged sword.** POL + coal ≈ 52% of all-India tonnes [E]; the West Asia war (Feb–Apr 2026) and LPG disruption hit liquids and Gujarat volumes while lifting coal and transhipment [V].
5. **The capex super-cycle and its funding.** APSEZ ₹90–100k Cr (FY27–31) at ≤2.5× ND/EBITDA; JSW ~₹39k Cr funded by a ₹7,503 Cr QIP and low leverage; government Vadhavan ₹76,220 Cr. Returns depend on filling capacity in a market growing ~4–5% a year in tonnes [V; E].
6. **Captive cargo vs third-party (JSW) and parent-as-customer (GPPL).** Both are related-party structures whose pricing and routing determine minority value [V; primer].
7. **Internationalisation (APSEZ only).** Intl ports EBITDA ₹730 Cr in Q1 FY27 (+256%) at 41.8% margin — now ~11% of group EBITDA [E: 730 ÷ 6,541] — with Israel, Sri Lanka, Tanzania, Australia (and Oman?) risk [V].

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Port revenue = Σ over cargo types (volume × realisation per unit) + vessel-related charges + storage/ancillary; EBITDA = revenue − (royalty/revenue-share + O&M + power + staff); fixed costs dominate, so incremental margins exceed average margins.**

Equivalently for a developer: **EBITDA ≈ capacity × utilisation × (realisation − variable cost per tonne) − fixed costs.**

### C.1 Worked example 1 — APSEZ domestic ports, Q1 FY27 annualised [E from V]
- Volume 115.3 MMT × 4 = **461 MMT/yr**.
- Revenue/t ₹604 → revenue ₹27,856 Cr/yr (Q1 × 4 = 6,964 × 4).
- EBITDA/t ₹447 → EBITDA ₹20,608 Cr/yr (74%).
- Sensitivity: +1% volume at the **incremental** margin of ~67% ≈ +₹187 Cr EBITDA/yr [E: 1% × 27,856 × 0.67]; +₹10/t realisation with no cost ≈ +₹461 Cr/yr.
- Capacity 653 MMT → utilisation ~71% [E].

### C.2 Worked example 2 — one new JSW greenfield bulk port (Jatadhar-type) [E composite]
- Capex ₹3,050 Cr for 30 MTPA (₹102 Cr/MTPA) [V].
- Year-3 utilisation 70% → 21 MT × EBITDA/t ₹200 (JSW average, [E]) = **₹420 Cr EBITDA** → pre-tax return on capex ~13.8% EBITDA/capex, ~10% EBIT/capex after ~₹100 Cr depreciation (30-yr) [E].
- With an anchor JSW Steel take-or-pay, utilisation risk is low; at third-party-only 40% utilisation, EBITDA ~₹240 Cr → ~8% EBITDA/capex [E].

### C.3 Worked example 3 — GPPL, Q1 FY27 [V figures; E arithmetic]
