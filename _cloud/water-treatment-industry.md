## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **"Order book" means three different things across the three members.**
   - **Wabag:** total backlog **including O&M** (₹19,394 Cr Jun-26 = EPC ₹12,728 + O&M ₹6,666 Cr). O&M backlog is multi-year (up to 20 years at Kodungaiyur/Perur) and burns slowly. **Excludes framework contracts** from Q1 FY27 [V deck]. "EPC" in Wabag's deck includes design-build (DB) and EP.
   - **Ion Exchange:** **"Engineering order book" only** — Treatment Solutions + Industrial Products + Lifecycle Services (₹2,473 Cr Jun-26; ₹2,643 Cr Mar-26). It **excludes chemicals and consumer (no backlog concept)**, and it apparently **excludes the 20-year Oman PDO DBOOT (OMR 73.46m ≈ ₹1,730 Cr)** and excluded the **Hyundai $52.83m (~₹503 Cr)** order won after the quarter [V decks; Oman inclusion not stated — FLAG]. Its backlog includes **₹295.5 Cr (Mar-26) of a UP SWSM (Jal Jeevan Mission) project**, ~11% of backlog in Jun-26 [V].
   - **EIEL:** total order book **including O&M, HAM and renewables/IPP** (₹6,721 Cr Jun-26 = water ₹3,694 + renewables ₹3,027 Cr). Water = WWTP-EPC ₹2,184 + WWTP-HAM ₹403 + WSSP ₹109 + O&M ₹998 Cr [V deck].
   - **Never add the three order books as if they were the same thing.** Part 3 shows the sum (₹28,588 Cr) only with this caveat; the like-for-like "execution" backlog is ≈ Wabag EPC ₹12,728 + Ion ~₹2,473 + EIEL execution (₹2,696 water + ₹1,948 renewables) ≈ **₹19,845 Cr** [E].
2. **Wabag's EBITDA includes forex — and it mattered in Q1 FY27.**
   - Company EBITDA is struck **after forex gain/loss**. FY26: EBITDA ₹524.1 Cr (13.3%) includes a **₹46.7 Cr forex gain** (FY25 ₹7.9 Cr) → ex-forex ₹477.4 Cr (12.1%), which is ≈ screener's operating profit ₹478 Cr (12%) [V deck; V screener].
   - **Q1 FY27: company EBITDA ₹116.3 Cr (13.1%) includes a ₹36.8 Cr forex gain** (vs a ₹13.6 Cr loss in Q1 FY26). **Ex-forex EBITDA ₹79.5 Cr = 9.0%** vs 14.9% a year earlier; screener OP ₹80 Cr (9%) and screener "other income" ₹58 Cr carry the gain [V deck; V screener; E arithmetic]. An analyst on the call put the ex-forex margin at "15.8% → 8.9%"; the CFO refused the framing ("forex is completely operational… we don't have forex loans") [V transcript].
   - **Use screener OP in peer tables; show the company figure with the forex note in prose.** Wabag's medium-term guide (13–15% EBITDA) is on the company basis.
3. **Wabag's net cash excludes HAM project debt.** "Net cash ₹965 Cr (Jun-26), excl. HAM" vs **₹856.6 Cr including HAM** SPVs; Mar-26 ₹950 Cr excl. / ₹833.7 Cr incl. [V decks]. Screener: borrowings ₹228 Cr; cash & bank (company BS) ₹1,059 Cr → ~₹831 Cr [E]. Use the company "excl. HAM" figure in prose but state the basis; the HAM SPVs are equity-accounted (share of profit ₹5.5 Cr in Q1 FY27) [V call].
4. **EIEL's "debtor days 53" is an illusion — look at contract assets.**
   - Trade receivables Mar-26 only **₹165.3 Cr** (53 days of revenue on screener), but **"other current financial assets" ₹691.3 Cr** (unbilled/contract assets, retention) plus **non-current other financial assets ₹296.9 Cr** (largely HAM service-concession receivables) [V FY26 balance sheet in Q1 deck].
   - All-in receivable-type assets ≈ **₹1,153 Cr = ~367 days of FY26 revenue** [E]. Screener **working-capital days rose 34 → 89 → 137** (FY24–FY26) and **CFO was negative three years running** (−₹102 / −₹59 / −₹63 Cr) [V screener].
   - Management on 12-Aug-2026: the working-capital cycle "still remains bloated… a slight slowdown in the cash flow receiving from the government clients" [V transcript].
5. **HAM accounting changes the cash-flow statement.** Under Ind AS 115 / Appendix D (service concession, financial-asset model), HAM construction is booked as revenue with a **financial asset (annuity receivable)**; the annuity's interest unwinds as "annuity" income (EIEL FY26 ₹27.4 Cr; Q1 FY27 ₹8.7 Cr "UP annuity interest") [V deck]. EIEL says its operating cash flow "goes negative by somewhere around ₹85 Cr" because concession receivables sit in operating activities [V call]. So **CFO understates the cash quality of HAM-heavy firms**; adjust before comparing with Wabag (asset-light) [E].
6. **Ion Exchange: consolidated vs standalone, and trust shares.**
   - Use **consolidated**: FY26 operating income ₹2,914.8 Cr, operating EBITDA ₹210.2 Cr (7.21%), PAT ₹143.2 Cr [V deck]. Screener OP ₹214 Cr (7%) ≈ same.
   - **EPS includes ~2.37 crore shares held by staff-welfare trusts (IEI & HMIL) and 43.8 lakh shares held by subsidiaries** [V deck footnote]. Screener shows these as "Others 16.18%" in shareholding. The "promoter" 25.08% is separate. Market cap on screener uses all issued shares (~14.67 crore) [E] — treasury-like holdings are ~19% of shares, so **free-float-adjusted m-cap is materially lower**; per-share metrics differ slightly by source.
   - Segment data is now on a new five-segment basis (Q1 FY27); FY22–FY26 restated history exists only as charts in the Aug-2026 deck (values read off charts — [V, chart-read]).
7. **"EBITDA margin" for EIEL is pre-other-income; PAT now carries IPP depreciation and interest.** FY26 EBITDA ₹276.8 Cr (24.16%) excludes other income ₹42.4 Cr [V]. Q1 FY27 finance cost doubled to ₹14.5 Cr (₹7.0 Cr) and depreciation to ₹8.7 Cr (₹3.2 Cr) because of renewables/IPP assets (PPE ₹70 → ₹253 Cr in FY26) [V deck]. PAT growth (+6.5%) therefore lags revenue growth (+49%).
8. **Mixed-basis segment splits.** Wabag reports **EPC/O&M, municipal/industrial and India/overseas** for revenue, intake and backlog (all [V]). Ion reports business segments + "exports 26% / domestic 74%" (FY26). EIEL reports **by project type (WWTP/WSSP/O&M/renewables/annuity), by model (EPC/HAM/O&M/IPP/annuity) and by state**. There is **no common "municipal vs industrial" split** across all three; Part 3 gives a best-effort [E] split.
9. **Contract values: gross vs share, LoA vs signed, INR at which rate.** Wabag's Kuwait Doha SWRO II is **KD114.28m ($372m ≈ ₹3,530 Cr) for the HEISCO–Wabag JV** (MEED, from a Reg 30 filing dated 25-Aug-2026), not Wabag's share; at the June LoA Wabag only said "Mega" (its threshold is >₹1,000 Cr) [V-snip]. The **Yanbu 300 MLD** figure appears as **$317m (Sep-2024)**, **$272m / ₹2,332 Cr (Jul-2025)** and **₹2,038 Cr (our primer)** [V-snip; primer] — see §H. **Perur 400 MLD** is a **Wabag–Metito JV** worth ~₹4,276–4,400 Cr in total [V-snip]. Always say "JV value" or "Wabag share".
10. **"Net worth" and ROE bases differ.** Wabag ROE 15.7% (company FY26), 15.9% (screener); Ion 12.0% (screener); EIEL 15.3–17.8% (company chart) vs 16.8% (screener). EIEL's equity jumped from ₹292 Cr (FY24) to ₹995 Cr (FY25) on the **Nov-2024 IPO**, so FY25 ROE/ROCE are distorted by mid-year capital [V screener; K IPO].

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Three listed companies, three different businesses, one theme: India (and the Gulf) paying to clean water.**
  - **Va Tech Wabag** = asset-light, technology-led **water EPC + O&M**, 52% overseas, desalination specialist. FY26 revenue ₹3,944 Cr, PAT ₹370 Cr, backlog ₹19,394 Cr (Jun-26), net cash ₹965 Cr [V].
  - **Ion Exchange** = integrated **chemicals (resins) + engineering + products + services + consumer**, 26% exports; FY26 revenue ₹2,915 Cr, PAT ₹143 Cr (−31%), and **Q1 FY27 PAT ₹3 Cr** [V].
  - **Enviro Infra Engineers** = young **government STP/WTP EPC + HAM** contractor that has pivoted fast into **solar/wind/BESS**; FY26 revenue ₹1,146 Cr, PAT ₹188 Cr, order book ₹6,721 Cr (45% renewables) [V].
  - **All three: FY26 revenue ₹8,005 Cr (+12.8%), operating profit ₹969 Cr (12.1%), PAT ₹701 Cr; m-cap ₹19,171 Cr on 22-Oct-2026 → 27.3× FY26 PAT, 28.1× TTM** [E: sums of screener 22-Oct-26]. Wabag alone is 49% of revenue, 53% of PAT, 58% of m-cap.
- **The domestic funding tap was turned off in FY26 and is being turned back on in FY27.**
  - Jal Jeevan Mission: **BE ₹67,000 Cr → RE ₹17,000 Cr (−75%) in FY26**, and **zero central allocation or release to states in FY26** (only ~₹232 Cr of old unspent funds used). FY27 BE **₹67,670 Cr** (+298% vs FY26 RE) [V PRS]. Ministry of Jal Shakti total: FY26 BE ₹99,503 Cr → RE ₹41,437 Cr (−58%); FY27 BE ₹94,808 Cr [V PRS].
  - **JJM extended to Dec-2028** ("JJM 2.0"; Cabinet Mar-2026) with total outlay **₹8.69 lakh Cr** and central assistance **₹3.59 lakh Cr** [V-snip]. Tap coverage **82.19% (15.90 crore of 19.36 crore rural households) on 4-Aug-2026** vs 3.23 crore (~17%) at launch in Aug-2019 [V-snip, Lok Sabha].
  - The consequence was a **contractor payment crisis**: Assocham (Oct-2025) complained of certified bills unpaid for **10–12 months** in UP, MP, Jharkhand, Odisha, WB, Maharashtra, Assam and J&K [V-snip]. Listed proof: **Ion Exchange "calibrates execution in proportion to funding received" on its ₹295 Cr UP SWSM (JJM) job** and its Treatment Solutions segment lost ₹17 Cr EBIT in Q1 FY27; **EIEL's working capital "still remains bloated"** [V transcripts].
- **Sewage, not drinking water, is the bigger long-run engine — and the gap is enormous.**
  - CPCB (Mar-2021): India generates **72,368 MLD** of sewage vs **31,841 MLD installed** STP capacity, **26,869 MLD operational** and only **20,235 MLD actually treated (~28%)** [V-snip, Parliament answers / Down To Earth].
  - Namami Gange's STP target is **7,000 MLD by Dec-2026**; by Dec-2025 **3,806 MLD (58%)** was complete across 138 sewage projects [V PRS]. AMRUT 2.0: **8,777 projects worth ₹1.97 lakh Cr approved, 7,687 (₹1.37 lakh Cr) awarded by 9-Jul-2026; 605 sewerage/septage projects** [V-snip, Lok Sabha via KashmirLife].
  - **HAM** (40% of capex during construction, 60% as performance-linked annuities over 15 years, plus O&M) has made sewage plants bankable; EIEL now has **five HAM projects** [V-snip IFC/NMCG; V EIEL].
- **Desalination is where the big tickets and the best references are — and the Gulf is both the prize and the risk.**
  - Wabag's backlog is **~49% overseas, of which Middle East is ~35–40% of the total**; its top projects: **Yanbu 300 MLD (Saudi), Doha-Kuwait 272 MLD, Perur 400 MLD (Chennai)**, Al Haer 200 MLD ISTP (Saudi) [V deck + call].
  - The West Asia war has **directly hit Gulf desal** (Kuwait plants struck; desal is ~90% of Kuwait's drinking water) [V-snip], delayed Ion Exchange's GCC dispatches and March chemical exports [V Ion FY26 deck], and pushed Wabag to send force-majeure letters [V call]. Yet Saudi has declared **2027 the "Year of Water"** and Wabag says the bid pipeline has not slowed [V call].
- **Margins and cash are where the three diverge.**
  - Wabag: steady 12–13% EBITDA, **net cash for 14 straight quarters**, NWC 104–108 days, but **receivables ₹2,524 Cr (234 days) + ₹648 Cr non-current receivables** [V].
  - Ion: EBITDA margin halved (10.7% FY25 → 7.2% FY26 → 4.5% Q1 FY27) on the Roha resin start-up, legacy EPC losses and input costs; turned from **net cash ~₹451 Cr (Mar-24) to net debt ~₹135 Cr (Mar-26)** [V; E].
  - EIEL: highest margin (24% FY26; guided 19–22% FY27) but cash-hungry; from **net cash ~₹330 Cr (Mar-25, IPO money) to ~₹21 Cr net debt (Mar-26)** [E from V BS].

### 1.2 Value chain (inputs → customer)

1. **Funding source** (the true customer): Union missions (JJM, AMRUT 2.0, Namami Gange, SBM-U 2.0) + state budgets; multilaterals (JICA for Perur; ADB for Chennai ring main; World Bank; EXIM India lines for Africa); Gulf sovereign budgets (SWA, MEWRE); industrial capex (RIL, BPCL, IOCL, GAIL, solar/semiconductor fabs).
2. **Owner / tendering authority**: ULBs, state Jal Nigams/PHEDs (UPJN, BUIDCO, CMWSSB, BWSSB, DJB), SWSMs for JJM, NMCG for HAM, Gulf authorities; industrial owners via EPCM consultants.
3. **Process technology & design** (Wabag, Ion, IDE, Veolia, Acciona, Metito) — the IP layer: process design, membrane/biological selection, guarantees.
4. **Equipment & consumables**: membranes (DuPont/FilmTec, Toray, Hydranautics–Nitto, LG Chem; Ion's own), high-pressure pumps (Flowserve, KSB, Danfoss), energy-recovery devices (ERI), blowers, UV, instruments; resins (DuPont, Lanxess, Purolite–Ecolab, Mitsubishi, Ion Exchange, Thermax, Chinese makers such as Sunresin); chemicals.
5. **Civil construction** — typically 35–60% of a municipal STP/WTP contract value [E]; Wabag subcontracts it ("construction for us is a pass-through" — CFO, Aug-2026 [V]); EIEL self-performs much of it.
6. **Commissioning, performance testing, then O&M** (5–20 years).
7. **End users**: city residents (drinking water, sanitation), rivers (Ganga/Yamuna), industry (process water, compliance).

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **Process IP + references** earn the highest margins: Wabag's design-build (DB/EP) jobs "will generally be better in margin because there is no construction" [V call]. Wabag holds **125+ IPRs** and claims a GWI "top-3 private desalination plant supplier" ranking (GWI 2024) [V deck].
- **Consumables (resins, chemicals, membranes)** earn recurring margin off the whole installed base: Ion's Specialty Chemicals EBIT margin **25.2% (FY25) / 21.0% (FY26)** vs Engineering **6.4% / 5.1%** [V deck chart, E arithmetic].
- **O&M** earns modestly higher margins than EPC with near-zero capital: EIEL "O&M in itself gives a better EBITDA margin" but is only 3–5% of execution value per year [V call]; Ion's O&M "10–12% margin" [V call].
- **Civil works** earn the least and absorb most working capital.
- **HAM/BOT** converts construction margin into a 15-year annuity (financing return) — capital-intensive but sticky (§6).

## 4. Supply side and cost structure

### 4.1 Key inputs and price drivers
- **Civil materials** (cement, steel, pipes — DI/MS/HDPE) and labour: the bulk of municipal EPC cost; fixed-price contracts carry price-variation clauses only sometimes [K]. EIEL cited input-cost increases for cutting its FY27 margin guide from 22–24% to **21–22%** (water) [V call].
- **Imported process equipment** priced in USD/EUR/JPY: membranes, HP pumps, ERDs, instruments — rupee at ~₹95/$ raises costs for INR-priced Indian contracts; Wabag runs a **natural hedge** (export revenue vs imported equipment) plus PCFC for the gap [V call].
- **Petrochemicals for resins**: styrene, divinylbenzene, oleum/sulphuric acid, amines; crude-linked. Ion: "input cost increases… in the past two quarters" with a **time lag in pass-through**; Roha drag ~**6 pp** on chemicals segment margin and missing forex gain ~**3–4 pp** in Q1 FY27 [V call].
- **Energy** for desal O&M (3–4 kWh/m³ SWRO) — usually a pass-through in O&M/BOOT tariffs [K].
- **Labour/skills**: EIEL's headcount rose from ~900 at IPO to **~2,300–2,340**; employee cost **7% of revenue in Q1 FY27 vs 3–3.5% historically** [V call/deck].
- **Financing cost**: bank guarantees and LC charges are a real cost for EPC players — Wabag's **bank charges ₹49.8 Cr in FY26 (1.3% of revenue)** vs net interest *income* ₹23.2 Cr [V deck]. EIEL finance cost ~4% of revenue in Q1 FY27 [V call].

**HAM economics (NMCG model, approved Jan-2016)** [V-snip IFC/NMCG docs; K details]:
- Concessionaire builds the STP; **40% of quoted capex is paid during/at construction milestones**; the **remaining 60% is paid as quarterly annuities over 15 years**, with **interest on the outstanding annuity balance** (benchmarked to a bank rate plus a margin [K — verify exact formula]) and a **separate O&M payment** for 15 years; **both annuity and O&M are performance-linked**; **NMCG is the payer** (central money), states monitor [V-snip].
- First HAM STPs: Varanasi Ramana 50 MLD, Haridwar 82 MLD, Mathura 67.5 MLD; then 11 more (UP: Naini, Jhusi, Phaphamau 72 MLD etc.) [V-snip].
- **Why HAM is "safer" than state EPC:** payer is NMCG (Union budget line ₹3,100 Cr FY27 BE [V PRS]) and the contract is annuity-based with escrow; **why it is capital-hungry:** the developer finances 60% of capex for up to 15 years (equity ~10–15% of project cost + debt) [E].
- **EIEL Varanasi wins (Q1 FY27):** Lohta 60 MLD ₹130.14 Cr + DDU Nagar 45 MLD ₹126.78 Cr = ₹256.92 Cr; **18-month construction + 15-year O&M**; biogas-to-power + solar on site [V deck/call; V-snip]. Implied capex ≈ **₹2.2–2.8 Cr per MLD** [E].

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Funding missions (the demand regulators)
- **Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM)** — launched 15-Aug-2019 to give every rural household a functional tap (FHTC) by 2024 [K]; Centre:State **50:50** (90:10 for NE/Himalayan states; 100% for UTs) [K]. Launch outlay ₹3.06 lakh Cr (Centre ₹2.09 lakh Cr) [V PRS]. **Extended to Dec-2028** (announced Budget Feb-2025; Cabinet approval Mar-2026 for "JJM 2.0" with **₹8.69 lakh Cr total, ₹3.59 lakh Cr central**) [V-snip]. PRS (Feb-2026): **"no extension guidelines have been published"**; the conditions for allocation have been made more stringent (Himachal report) [V PRS; V-snip]. Irregularities: 17,000+ complaints; six states' penalties ₹129 Cr (₹12 Cr recovered); UP recovered ₹340 Cr in liquidated damages [V PRS].
- **AMRUT 2.0** (launched 1-Oct-2021; 2021-22 to 2025-26; outlay ₹2.99 lakh Cr incl. ₹76,760 Cr central [K]) — universal water supply in all ~4,900 ULBs and sewerage/septage in 500 AMRUT cities [K]. Approvals ₹1.97 lakh Cr (8,777 projects), awards ₹1.37 lakh Cr (7,687 projects) by Jul-2026 [V-snip]. **Whether AMRUT 2.0 has been formally extended beyond Mar-2026 was not verified** [FLAG §H].
- **Namami Gange / NMCG** — approved May-2015 (₹20,000 Cr, 2014–21), extended 2021–26 (₹22,500 Cr); allocated ₹30,096 Cr FY15–FY26; spent ~₹18,842 Cr to Mar-2025; disbursed ₹20,430 Cr vs ₹26,825 Cr estimate (76%) [V PRS]. FY27 BE ₹3,100 Cr (FY26 RE ₹2,687 Cr; FY25 actual ₹2,976 Cr) [V PRS]. PAC (2024) flagged low utilisation and delayed UCs [V PRS].
- **SBM-U 2.0 / SBM-G** — used-water management and faecal sludge; SBM-G FY27 BE ₹7,192 Cr [V PRS].
- **Multilateral funding**: JICA (Perur), ADB (Chennai ring-main grid), World Bank (state water/sewerage programmes; EIEL cites "execution experience in World Bank-funded projects") [V decks].

### 7.2 Environmental standards (the compliance regulators)
- **CPCB/MoEFCC STP discharge norms** (notified Oct-2017: BOD ≤30/20 mg/l, TSS ≤100/50 mg/l depending on city class) and the **NGT's stricter Apr-2019 order** (BOD ≤10, COD ≤50, TSS ≤20, N-total ≤10, P ≤1 mg/l for new STPs) [K — verify]. Stricter norms push **tertiary and MBR/SBR technology** — EIEL uses SBR "to meet stringent effluent quality standards set by the NGT" [V deck].
- **ZLD**: CPCB/SPCB mandates for highly polluting industries (textiles, distilleries, pharma, tanneries) in water-stressed clusters; MIDC CETP upgrades (EIEL Kolhapur CETP with ZLD ₹395.5 Cr) [V deck; K].
- **National Framework on Safe Reuse of Treated Water (2022)** and state reuse policies (Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu); thermal power plants within 50 km of an STP must use treated sewage (MoP tariff policy 2016) [K].
- **Water quality / BIS** standards for drinking water (IS 10500) and purifiers [K].

### 7.3 Trade and tax
- GST: works contracts for government water supply/sewerage at concessional rates (12% → 18% changes in GST 2.0, Sep-2025?) — **not verified** [FLAG].
- No safeguard/ADD protecting resins or membranes found [V-snip negative].
- Export incentives: RoDTEP for resins/chemicals [K].

### 7.4 Gulf regulation
- Saudi Water Authority (SWA, ex-SWCC) procures EPC desal directly (Yanbu, Aljouf) and IWPs via SWPC; **2027 declared "Year of Water"** [V Wabag call]; Kuwait MEWRE procures EPC + O&M (Doha SWRO II); local-content partners required (HEISCO) [V call].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

- **1924–2005: European technology roots.** WABAG founded in Breslau (Wrocław) in 1924; via Deutsche Babcock (1986), VA Tech (1999) and Siemens (2005), it ended with Indian management: **2005 management buyout of WABAG India backed by ICICI Venture**, and in **2007 WABAG India took over WABAG Austria from Siemens** ("reunification"); **IPO 2010** [V Wabag deck timeline].
- **1964–1985: Ion Exchange** formed as a subsidiary of Permutit (UK) in 1964; became a wholly Indian company in 1985 [V Ion deck]; resin manufacturing at Ankleshwar from 1979 [primer].
- **2009–2024: EIEL** incorporated 2009; first major order an 18 MLD CETP at Balotra (₹16 Cr); first HAM (3 STPs, 63 MLD at Bareilly, UPJN/NMCG) 2021; **IPO Nov-2024** at ₹148 (₹140–148 band), heavily oversubscribed (~90×) [V deck; primer; K].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Who pays, and when — not demand — is the binding constraint.** Demand is non-discretionary (72,368 MLD of sewage, 18% of rural homes untapped), but FY26 proved that central funding can go to zero in a year (JJM central release FY26: ₹0) and that state payments lag 10–12 months. Every number in this sub-sector — order book, margins, ROCE — must be read through the payer's quality. Wabag's net cash and multilateral/Gulf mix is a direct response to its FY23 write-off.
2. **Order book ≠ revenue ≠ cash.** Wabag's backlog is 4.7× TTM revenue but burns at ~29% a year; EIEL's is 5.3× after a 242% jump while revenue grew 7% in FY26. Conversion depends on land, clearances, funding and (for O&M) plant completion. Book-to-bill, burn rate and NWC days together are the native gauges.
3. **Technology + references gate the high-margin work.** Mega SWRO, UPW, ZLD and reuse plants go to a few qualified technology houses (Veolia, IDE, Acciona, Metito, Wabag); civil-heavy JJM went to infra contractors at lower margins. Wabag's DB/EP shift ("construction for us is a pass-through") and Ion's resin IP are the moats.
4. **The annuity layer (O&M, HAM, consumables) is what makes the sector investable.** Wabag targets O&M at 20% of revenue (18% now; O&M backlog ₹6,666 Cr); Ion's resins/chemicals carry ~70% of segment EBIT on ~29% of revenue (FY26: ₹182 Cr of ~₹257 Cr old-basis segment EBIT); EIEL's HAM/O&M (~₹1,400 Cr of backlog) adds 15-year visibility.
5. **Geography and FX: the Gulf is the growth engine and the tail risk.** Wabag 52% overseas, Ion 26% exports; Saudi's 2027 "Year of Water" and Kuwait's need for redundancy after strikes argue for more awards; the same war delays dispatches and raises force-majeure claims.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Water EPC/O&M (Wabag/EIEL):**
Revenue_t ≈ Σ (opening EPC backlog × burn rate) + (O&M plants in operation × annual O&M fee) [+ annuity interest for HAM]
EBITDA = Revenue × (DB/EP mix × high margin + EPC-with-civil mix × lower margin + O&M margin) ± FX
Cash = EBITDA − Δ(receivables + contract assets + retention − advances − payables) − tax
Value ≈ Backlog × execution margin × collection probability + PV(O&M/annuity tail)

**Integrated (Ion Exchange):**
EBIT = Σ segments: Chemicals (capacity × utilisation × realisation − feedstock) + Treatment Solutions (backlog burn × margin) + Products + Services − Consumer losses − unallocable

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed

1. **Wabag's receivable tail is ageing even as headline debtor days look stable.**
   - Billed receivables **>3 years overdue rose ₹46 Cr → ₹225 Cr in FY26**; >1 year = ₹546 Cr (21% of billed).
   - The ECL allowance nearly doubled (₹57 → ₹103 Cr), and management introduced a "more stringent" provisioning policy in Q1 FY27 (other expenses up).
   - Add **₹648 Cr of non-current retention**, **₹228 Cr of indirect-tax contingents (2.3× in a year)** and the unresolved ~₹140 Cr TSGENCO award.
   - The "net cash ₹965 Cr" story therefore co-exists with ~₹1,000 Cr of slow or contested assets. The FY23 write-off (₹289 Cr) is the precedent. This is the single most important caveat for a bull case [E].
2. **EIEL's cash is stuck in *unbilled* work, not unpaid bills.**
   - Unbilled revenue jumped ₹266 → ₹608 Cr (53% of FY26 revenue), while trade receivables *fell*.
   - The bottleneck is certification and measurement by state agencies before invoicing. That is invisible in debtor days (53) and only partly visible in screener WC days (137).
   - Bank guarantees doubled to ₹513 Cr, and the IPO cash cushion shrank to ₹58 Cr.
   - Together with a ₹2,000 Cr revenue guide (+75%) and a 2.5× workforce, FY27 is a balance-sheet stress test. Watch H1 unbilled and CFO in the Nov-2026 filing [E].
3. **India is a net exporter of ion-exchange resins.** HS 3914: exports $87.6m vs imports $66.8m in 2024.
   - The Roha story is therefore an **export/global-share** story, not import substitution. Management points to North America (WQA certification) and 10% of global volume.
   - However, Roha's first live block is only **3,696 m³ of a 42,600 m³ plan**, utilisation is "softer than expected", and **loan repayments start 30-Sep-2026**.
   - Chinese resin imports into India rose ~58% in 2024: price competition is arriving at home [E].
4. **Ion's long-duration backlog shrank 22% in FY26.** Ind AS 115 RPO >1 year fell ₹1,870 → ₹1,467 Cr.
   - Unbilled revenue tripled (₹74 → ₹233 Cr), receivables >1 year overdue rose 49%, and MSME payables doubled.
   - The Hyundai (~₹500 Cr) and Oman PDO wins matter more than they look, because they refill a thinning engineering book. Management also disclosed a **second large legacy project** beyond UP JJM [V call].
5. **The Yanbu saga is a template for Gulf award risk.** A publicly announced $317m Saudi award was cancelled three months later (stock −19%), then re-awarded ~14% cheaper ($272m) after re-tendering.
   - The writer should treat any "Mega" Gulf LoA as provisional until contract signing and effectiveness. Wabag now behaves this way: it removed two framework orders, and the Ind AS 115 backlog excludes framework contracts.
   - Kuwait was announced at LoA in June and **signed only on 25-Aug-2026** [E from V sources].
6. **AMRUT 2.0's sewage build-out has barely started.** Only **658 of 6,815 MLD (≈10%)** is operational five years in; central release is 34%; the scheme is extended only to Mar-2027.
   - Contrast AMRUT-1 (86% of STP MLD operational) and JJM's tap-connection push.
   - For STP specialists (EIEL, Wabag India) the **execution and payment window is FY27–FY29**, and its size depends on whether an "AMRUT 3.0" or further extension follows. That is a key watch item [E].
7. **JJM 2.0 rewards O&M competence.** Gram panchayats can certify "Har Ghar Jal" only once O&M arrangements exist, and central money rises by just ₹1.51 lakh Cr.
   - This tilts JJM 2.0 spend toward service delivery, water-quality treatment and O&M contracts rather than new pipe networks.
   - Wabag (O&M backlog 34%) and Ion (Lifecycle Services) fit that better than civil-led contractors [E].
8. **Ownership signals differ sharply.**
   - Wabag: the founder holds 15.6% and a single non-institutional investor (Rekha Jhunjhunwala) holds 8.0%, larger than all DIIs (5.9%). The next generation is formalised (Rohan Mittal KMP; new Deputy MD May-2026).
   - EIEL: promoters at 70% absorbed a ₹11 Cr cyber fraud personally (a good-faith signal), but the fraud itself and the auditor's s.143(12) report are governance flags.
   - Ion: the only >5% holder is the Executive Chairman (5.8%), with ~16% in staff trusts and subsidiaries [V ARs].
