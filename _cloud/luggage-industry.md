## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **"India's No. 1 luggage" — three different claims, all "true".**
   - **VIP** calls itself "Asia's leading and the world's second-largest manufacturer and retailer of luggage" with "~38% share of the organised market" [V screener profile; VIP AR].
   - **Safari** says it is the **"No. 1 Luggage Brand in India" in revenue terms for the third successive year** (Euromonitor, Personal Accessories 2026 ed., GBN, all retail channels, value RSP, 2025 data) [V Safari AR FY26]. That is a **brand** claim: "Safari" alone vs "VIP", "Skybags" and "American Tourister" separately.
   - **By company revenue, Safari overtook VIP in FY26: ₹2,047 Cr vs ₹1,858 Cr consolidated** [V screener]. The lead held in Q1 FY27: ₹589 Cr vs ₹578 Cr.
   - **Both of our primers say Safari is "#2 behind VIP". On revenue that is now false.** On organised-market "share" claims it depends on the source (see §5).
2. **Market size is quoted at retail value (RSP), company revenue is net of trade margin and GST.**
   - The ₹17,000 Cr CY2024 market (MOSL; VIP AR) is a **consumer-price** figure. Safari + VIP + Samsonite India company revenues (~₹5,700 Cr [E]) are ~33% of it, which matches MOSL's "VIP, Samsonite and Safari together control about 33 per cent of the market" [V BS/MOSL].
   - Never divide company revenue by RSP market size and call it "share" without saying so. The share of "organised" (~45–52% of the market) is ~2× that.
3. **Safari: standalone vs consolidated.**
   - The plants (Halol highway unit + Jaipur) sit in a wholly owned subsidiary, **Safari Manufacturing Ltd (SML)**. One Halol GIDC unit is in the listed parent [V Safari AR BRSR: "3 manufacturing units, comprising 1 unit under SIIL and 2 units under SML"]. There is a second WOS, Safari Lifestyles Ltd.
   - Standalone revenue ≈ consolidated (FY26 ₹2,042.5 Cr vs ₹2,047.0 Cr) because the parent sells everything. But **profit splits:** Q1 FY27 standalone PAT **₹28.87 Cr** vs consolidated **₹47.8 Cr** [V-snip scanx; V PL].
   - Always use consolidated.
4. **VIP: standalone vs consolidated, and the Bangladesh subsidiaries.**
   - VIP has **4 overseas WOS in Bangladesh**: VIP Industries Bangladesh, **VIP Industries BD Manufacturing** (material subsidiary), **VIP Luggage BD** (material subsidiary) and VIP Accessories BD. There is 1 Indian WOS, Blow Plast Retail [V AR].
   - **7 of VIP's 9 plants are in Bangladesh; 2 are in India** (Nashik-Satpur and Sinnar) [V AR BRSR §18]. The MD&A says "10 in-house manufacturing facilities across India and Bangladesh" — a 9-vs-10 inconsistency inside the AR. Use "9–10".
   - Bangladesh mostly sells to the Indian parent, so consolidated revenue ≈ standalone (FY26 ₹1,858 Cr vs ₹1,849 Cr). Profit differs: Bangladesh "returned to profitability" in FY26 [V AR].
   - Exports are only **2.73% of standalone turnover** [V AR BRSR].
5. **VIP FY26 loss: what is one-off.** FY26 consolidated (company) [V AR]:
   - Revenue ₹1,858.13 Cr (−14.7%); **EBITDA −₹218.57 Cr**; PBT pre-exceptional **−₹416.20 Cr**; **exceptional +₹78.18 Cr** (standalone ₹63.03 Cr = ₹63.53 Cr gain on non-core asset sales − ₹0.50 Cr Guwahati warehouse fire settlement); **PAT −₹338.01 Cr**.
   - **Pre-exceptional PAT ≈ −₹416 Cr** (tax ~nil).
   - Management also calls these **non-recurring**: **₹123 Cr one-time inventory provisions** and **~₹40–50 Cr channel liquidation support**. Adding them back gives an "adjusted" loss of ~₹240–250 Cr pre-exceptional [E], still deeply loss-making.
   - The standalone balance sheet carries a **cumulative inventory write-down provision of ₹106.36 Cr** [V note 10].
   - VSS: none found in the AR. The cost reset was mainly inventory and channel. Employee costs were "managed in line with revenue" [V AR].
6. **Screener "operating profit" ≠ company EBITDA (VIP).**
   - FY26 screener OP is **−₹241 Cr** vs company EBITDA **−₹218.6 Cr**.
   - Quarterly: screener OP Q2/Q3/Q4 FY26 = −106/−77/−82; deck EBITDA = −96/−73/−78. **Q1 FY27: −₹11 Cr screener vs −₹7 Cr company** [V both].
   - The gap is mostly other income/forex that the company includes. Screener also books **₹101 Cr of FY26 "other income"**, which includes the ₹78 Cr exceptional gain.
   - Use the company EBITDA in prose; use screener in the standardised grid and state the basis.
7. **Gross margin: company-reported vs screener proxy.**
   - Safari company GM: **FY25 45.7%, FY26 47.1%**, Q1 FY27 **44.6%** [V PL, "company data"]. Screener's material cost FY26 51% gives a proxy of ~49%, which overstates.
   - VIP company GM (deck): **Q1 FY26 45% → Q2 31% → Q3 29% → Q4 37% → Q1 FY27 41%** [V deck]. PL's "adjusted GM" for Q1 FY27 is **39.3%** [V PL page]. A press summary says "41.4%, −360 bp YoY" [V-snip].
   - VIP screener material cost: FY24 46% / FY25 53% / **FY26 64%** → proxy GM 54% / 47% / 36%. Note: screener's VIP FY26 "manufacturing cost" is 0%, a reclassification, so the FY26 material cost includes conversion costs and the proxy is not comparable to earlier years.
8. **Net debt / cash: screener misses Safari's cash; VIP's screener debt includes leases.**
   - **Safari:** screener shows borrowings ₹117 Cr and investments ₹13 Cr. But the company/PL balance sheet has **cash & bank ₹270 Cr + investments ₹76 Cr (₹63 Cr non-current + ₹13 Cr current) vs debt ₹116.6 Cr → net cash ≈ ₹230 Cr (Mar-26)** [V PL from company]. Use net cash ₹230 Cr.
   - **VIP:** screener borrowings ₹738 Cr. The company reports **net debt ₹309 Cr (Mar-26), down from ₹377 Cr**, which excludes lease liabilities [V AR]. Standalone lease liabilities are ₹317.9 Cr (₹256.83 Cr non-current + ₹61.05 Cr current) and standalone borrowings ₹382.6 Cr [V AR].
   - Use ₹309 Cr ex-leases in prose and say "plus ~₹320 Cr leases".
9. **Revenue growth in FY26 means different things.**
   - VIP's −14.7% was partly **deliberate**: "correct channel inventory overstocking, reset pricing guardrails, reduce sell-in of legacy SKUs" [V AR]. Sell-out fell less than sell-in.
   - Safari's +15.5% includes price cuts at the value end and volume. **Q1 FY27 volume +16–17% vs value +11.5%** [V PL], so realisation per piece fell ~4–5% [E] despite a 4–6% price hike in May-26.
   - Compare volumes where possible.
10. **"Capacity" units differ.**
    - PL's Safari note says both "**6.5 lac pieces per month**" (front page) and "**5mn units per month to 6.5mn units per month**" (body) for the Jaipur expansion [V PL].
    - **6.5 lakh/month (7.8 m pieces/yr) is the plausible figure.** 6.5 m/month (78 m/yr) is impossible against Safari's revenue: ₹2,047 Cr ÷ ₹1,500–2,500 per piece ≈ 8–14 m pieces/yr across hard, soft and backpacks [E].
    - Halol capacity is not disclosed in anything fetched.
    - VIP does not disclose capacity at all.

### 1.2 Value chain (input → consumer)

1. **Resins and components.** PP copolymer (Reliance, IOCL, HPCL-Mittal, Haldia; plus imports), PC (imported or Indian distributors), ABS. Wheels, trolley tubes (aluminium), zips (YKK etc.), locks, fabric (nylon/polyester from China/India). Resin is a crude-linked commodity (naphtha → propylene → PP; BPA → PC).
2. **Manufacture.**
   - **Hard:** injection moulding (PP) or sheet extrusion + thermoforming (PC), then assembly. It is capital- and mould-intensive and automatable: Safari is "investments in additional machinery and focus on increased efficiencies via automation" [V AR].
   - **Soft:** cutting, stitching and assembly. Labour-intensive, so it sits in low-wage Bangladesh/China.
   - Safari uses heavy **contract labour** (₹116.9 Cr FY26, 5.7% of revenue) and **job work** (₹40.1 Cr, 2.0%) [V Safari AR note 32]. Its PL Q1 note flags a **manpower shortage constraining Jaipur ramp-up** [V].
3. **Brand owner.** Design, sourcing, quality, warranty (3–10 years), marketing. A&P is ~8% of revenue for both listed players (Safari ₹159.7 Cr = 7.8%; VIP standalone ₹153.1 Cr = 8.3%) [V ARs]. D2C brands spend ~20% (Mokobara ₹46 Cr on ₹230 Cr = 20%) [V Entrackr].
4. **Distribution.** Safari reaches 8,500+ customer touchpoints; VIP ~14,000 points of sale in ~1,400 towns [V ARs]. Channels:
   - **MT** (DMart, Reliance Trends/Smart, Shoppers Stop, Lifestyle, Central — hypermarkets are big for Safari's value range).
   - **EBOs** (Safari Select / Urban Jungle; VIP / Skybags stores).
   - **GT dealers.**
   - **CSD** (Safari has an SVP–Canteen Sales Dept [V AR]).
   - **E-commerce marketplaces** and **q-commerce** (Safari: "building specialised supply chain and front-end capability" for q-com [V AR]).
   - **D2C websites**, **institutional/corporate gifting** and exports (tiny: Safari 0.23%, VIP 2.73% of standalone turnover [V BRSR]).
5. **Consumer.** Purchase triggers: trip (air/rail), wedding/trousseau/gifting, student migration, replacement. Replacement cycles are shortening as luggage becomes "an 'outfit' for … social media travel content" [V Safari AR].

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **At the brand + channel interface, not the factory.** A ₹6,000-MRP trolley may sell at ₹3,000–3,600 after the routine 40–50% "discount" [E]. After GST (18%) and the trade/platform margin (~25–35% of ex-GST price) [E], the brand books ~₹1,900–2,300. In-house COGS is ~₹1,000–1,200 [E, worked example §C].
- **The brand keeps a ~45–50% gross margin** (Safari 47.1% FY26 [V]; Samsonite global 59.6% [V]). It then spends ~33–35 points on A&P, freight, staff, rent and other costs, leaving **12–15% EBITDA for a healthy mass brand** [V Safari] and **~17% for a global premium brand** [V Samsonite adj. EBITDA 17.3%].
- **The factory adds ~5–8 points of GM** vs importing a finished bag [E; Safari's management repeatedly cites "backward integration benefit"; PL]. Captive components (wheels, trolley handles, locks) add another ~1–2 points [E].
- **Retail/platform margin is the largest single slice of consumer spend** [E]. That is why D2C brands (own website + marketplaces) and EBOs (own retail) chase it.

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **1968–2010: the VIP era.** VIP (incorporated 1968 per its CIN L25200MH1968PLC013914 [V]; "Established in 1968" [V AR]) built moulded hard luggage at Nashik and made "VIP" synonymous with the suitcase. Brands were added: Aristocrat, Alfa, Carlton (UK; acquired 2004 [K]), Skybags (2012 [K]) and Caprese (2013 [primer]). Safari (CIN 1980 [V]; brand from 1974 [screener]) was a small moulded-luggage maker.
2. **2011–12: the Jatia pivot.** Sudhir Jatia (described in both primers as ex-VIP MD) took control of loss-making Safari (a 41.34% block [primer]; MarketScreener). He started the share-grab against VIP with value pricing, MT/hypermarket and online distribution, and later in-house PP moulding at Halol.
3. **2014–2019: soft-goods offshoring.** VIP shifted soft-goods manufacturing to Bangladesh (own plants) for cost. Both brands imported soft luggage from China. Organised luggage grew at mid-teens rates.
4. **FY21 Covid collapse.** Pair revenue fell 60% (Safari ₹686 → ₹328 Cr; VIP ₹1,714 → ₹619 Cr) [V screener]. Both made losses (Safari −₹21 Cr, VIP −₹97 Cr).
5. **FY23: revenge travel and peak margins.** VIP OPM 15% and ROCE 28%; Safari OPM 16% and ROCE 37% [V]. Safari's revenue went ₹705 → ₹1,212 Cr (+72%).
6. **FY24–FY25: the price war.**

## 11. Technology and disruption risks
- **D2C and design velocity.** Brands that launch 100+ SKUs a year in trending colours compress replacement cycles and force incumbents to match. VIP's answer: 80+ launches giving ~50% of revenue [V deck]. D2C brands outspend on digital A&P (20% of revenue).
- **Marketplaces and quick commerce.** Online price transparency drives the "permanent 50% off MRP" culture. Q-commerce (cabin trolleys, backpacks in 10 minutes) favours SKU-light, fast-moving ranges. Safari is building a q-com supply chain [V AR].
- **Materials.** PC vs PP vs recycled/bio-based shells (Uppercase's recycled positioning); aluminium shells at the top end. Lighter-weight shells are the main product innovation (Samsonite Proxis/C-lite type).
- **Smart luggage.** GPS/BLE trackers (AirTag pockets), USB ports, TSA locks. Low margin impact so far [K].
- **Automation.** Moulding automation lowers labour intensity. Safari's Jaipur ramp is limited by manpower [V PL], an argument for further automation.
- **Counterfeits / unorganised.** Lookalike PC/PP trolleys at ₹1,500–2,500 online squeeze the value tier [E].
- **Airline baggage policies.** Low-cost carriers' cabin-only fares and checked-baggage fees push demand toward cabin and medium sizes. Safari observed this size-mix shift [V AR].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Branded conversion vs a crowded branded field.**
   - Organised/branded luggage is ~45–52% of a ₹17,000 Cr (CY24) market growing ~10–12% [V MOSL; V-snip CRISIL]. The runway is real.
   - But since FY24 the incremental share has gone to D2C brands, Samsonite's value brands (Kamiliant/American Tourister) and private labels, not to the listed pair.
   - The listed pair's combined revenue was **flat FY25→FY26 (₹3,950 → ₹3,905 Cr)** while the market grew ~10% [E]. The pair is losing share collectively.
2. **The make-vs-buy margin lever (hard luggage in-house; soft luggage abroad).**
   - Gross margin is set by how much of the basket is moulded in your own plant and how many components you make yourself. Safari has been adding Jaipur capacity, then captive wheels/trolleys/locks [V PL]. VIP has Bangladesh for soft goods.
   - FY26 GM: Safari 47.1% vs VIP ~36%. Even "normal" VIP (FY23) had a ~45–47% GM proxy.
3. **Price war + input-cost shock = the margin cycle.**
   - Safari EBITDA margin 18.5% → 10.5% (Q1 FY24 → Q2 FY25) [V]. VIP from +15% (FY23) to −13% (FY26) [V].
   - The 2026 resin spike (PP/PC +40% at peak [V-sib]) arrived just as the price war was easing. Price hikes of 4–6% were taken in Apr/May-26 [V].
   - **Margins are cyclical**: the drivers are discount depth, resin and FX, not a structural moat.
4. **Channel inventory discipline.**
   - Luggage is durable, seasonal and SKU-heavy. Sell-in vs sell-out mismatches turn into discounting and write-downs.
   - VIP's inventory days went 217 (FY23) → **325 (FY24)** → 222 → 150 (FY26), and net inventory ₹698 → ₹472 Cr, at a cost of ₹123 Cr provisions + ₹40–50 Cr channel liquidation support [V].
   - Safari 144 → 124 → 138 → 116 days [V].
5. **Ownership and brand rights.**
   - VIP: Multiples (Renuka Ramnath) took control in Sep/Dec-2025 at ₹388/share; there is a new MD (Atul Jain); a ₹500 Cr raise has been approved.
   - VIP lost "Carlton" in court; Safari has licensed it.
   - Safari is founder-run (Sudhir Jatia 36.7% + LLP 8.0%) with DII ownership rising to 28.5% [V screener].

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Revenue** = Σ over categories (pieces sold × net realisation per piece), where net realisation = MRP × (1 − consumer discount) ÷ (1 + GST 18%) × (1 − trade/platform margin).

**EBITDA** = Revenue × gross margin − (A&P + freight + employees + rent/other) and

**Gross margin** = 1 − [in-house share × (resin + components + conversion) + bought-in share × landed import cost] ÷ net realisation.

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed

1. **The listed pair imports from itself.**
   - Bangladesh is India's No. 2 source of HS 4202 goods (US$63–101 m a year, 2022–24) and ships **8.3–8.5 m suitcases a year**, 3× the 2019 level.
   - That flow is substantially VIP's own seven-plant base plus Safari's "captive third-party" vendor.
   - Bangladesh's LDC graduation, its politics and VIP's own under-utilisation ("Bangladesh was a key issue") are therefore an **Indian luggage cost-base risk**, not a sideshow.
   - The 2024 drop in BD import value (−37% with flat pieces) is VIP's destocking showing up in customs data.
2. **The price floor is set at the border.**
   - Chinese suitcases landed at **US$5.90 a piece CIF in 2024 (−24% YoY), with volume +28%**. With BCD of only 15%, a finished import costs ~₹580 before IGST.
   - This, not just D2C marketing, is the economics behind 2024–25's value-tier price war and Safari's push into in-house components.
3. **Safari is quietly re-shoring its own bought-in range.**
   - Purchases of finished goods fell 10% in Q1 FY27 while in-house raw-material consumption rose 33%. The in-house value share went ~61% → ~70%.
   - With wheels made in-house and robotic arms on the moulding lines, this is why Safari's GM fell only ~120 bp through a 40–60% PP spike.
   - Watch whether Carlton (imported from China) reverses this mix.
4. **The organised market is a three-way tie, and Samsonite is the under-discussed third leg.**
   - Organised company revenue: Safari ~₹2,050 Cr ≈ VIP ~₹1,860 Cr ≈ Samsonite India ~₹1,800 Cr.
   - Samsonite is **expanding Indian manufacturing** and winning with entry-level American Tourister/Kamiliant products "particularly in India" (Q3-25).
   - Its group trades at **~5× EV/EBITDA vs Safari ~21×**. Any re-rating argument for Safari has to explain that 4× gap.
5. **VIP's ₹500 Cr raise is a working-capital necessity, not a growth option.**
   - Unencumbered cash fell from ₹116 Cr to ~₹91 Cr (Mar → Jul-26). Fund-based limits are "near-full" on low drawing power, and additional bank lines are "yet to be tied up" ahead of the festive season.
   - CRISIL issued an "awaiting adequate information" advisory (6-Aug).
   - CRISIL also says Multiples does **not** envisage an immediate infusion, so expect a **QIP/rights at a discount to ₹264**: ~13–16% dilution [E] vs the ₹388 control price.
   - CRISIL's upgrade trigger is a 7–9% sustained operating margin.
6. **VIP's real free float is ~28%.** The 8.44% Samvibhag co-investor stake sits in "public" while the PE sponsor is "promoter", so index free-float and liquidity are overstated.
