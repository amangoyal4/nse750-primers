## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **GTV is not revenue.** Gross Transaction Value (GTV, also "gross bookings") is the total ticket/room value booked through a platform. Revenue is what the company keeps (or, for principal-model businesses, what it books gross). FY26: **TBO GTV ₹36,809 Cr vs revenue ₹2,677 Cr; ixigo GTV ₹18,692.7 Cr vs revenue ₹1,228 Cr** [V company releases/screener]. **IRCTC does not publish a "GTV"** — the railway fare it collects on e-tickets (~₹35–40k Cr a year [E: ~13.3 crore tickets/qtr × ~₹700 avg fare — FLAG, not sourced]) passes straight to Indian Railways and is **not** in IRCTC revenue; only the convenience fee and other platform income are. Never add GTVs to revenues, and never compare IRCTC "revenue" to an OTA's GTV.
2. **Take rate = revenue ÷ GTV, and it is NOT a margin.** It mixes in how revenue is recognised (gross vs net). TBO's consolidated take rate jumped from **6.3% (Q1 FY26) to 8.3% (Q1 FY27)** almost entirely because **Classic Vacations** (consolidated from 1-Oct-2025) books revenue at a **25.4% "take rate"** — that figure **includes ~12.4 pp of commission passed on to US travel advisors** — while TBO's **organic take rate fell to 6.0%** [V TBO shareholder letter Q1 FY27; V-snip for the 12.4% advisor-commission component]. The cleaner economic measure is **gross profit ÷ GTV**: organic 3.9% vs Classic 12.9%, consolidated 4.95% in Q1 FY27 [V letter].
3. **Gross vs net recognition across the four:**
   - **Thomas Cook** books most **holiday/MICE packages gross** (the full package price is revenue; cost of services is an expense), so its revenue (₹8,398 Cr FY26) looks huge vs its EBITDA (₹428 Cr, 5.1%) [V screener]. **Forex is booked net** (only the spread/fee is revenue). Hence the primer's segment picture "travel ₹6,703 Cr vs financial services ₹485 Cr" understates forex's economic weight; forex earns a ~45% EBIT margin [V investywise Q1 FY27: Financial Services EBIT margin 45.3%].
   - **TBO** books **air net** (commission/markup; take rate 2.3%) and **hotels largely net of supplier cost but gross of its markup** (take 10.6% consolidated incl. Classic) [V letter].
   - **ixigo** books mostly net (convenience fees, commissions, add-on margins, advertising).
   - **IRCTC** catering, Rail Neer and tourism are booked gross (food/water/tour sales); internet ticketing is net (fees only).
4. **IRCTC's segments and what each means.** Reported segments: **Catering** (mobile/on-board + static/station catering + e-catering), **Rail Neer** (packaged drinking water), **Internet Ticketing** (convenience fee + non-convenience-fee income such as payment-gateway income, advertising, loyalty, co-branded cards), **Tourism** (Bharat Gaurav trains, packages, Maharaja's Express, Tejas operations, air/hotel bookings) and **State Teertha** (pilgrimage trains run for state governments; often reported as a separate small segment) [K; State Teertha figures not sourced this session]. Segment "results" in IRCTC's notes are **segment profit before unallocable items and tax** — not EBITDA. Q1 FY27 segment results (₹ Cr): Ticketing 289.6, Catering 68.0, Tourism 19.3, Rail Neer 11.2 [V-snip filing summary]. Ticketing = ~74% of segment profit on ~26% of segment revenue.
5. **IRCTC: "convenience fee revenue" ≠ "internet ticketing revenue".** Ticketing segment revenue (Q1 FY27 ₹360.99 Cr; FY26 ~₹1,536 Cr [V-snip]) = convenience fee (~₹247–252 Cr a quarter, ~₹1,000 Cr a year [V-snip Q2/Q4 FY26]) + non-convenience-fee income (~₹110 Cr a quarter [E residual]). Convenience fee per ticket averaged ~**₹18.4** in Q4 FY26 (₹247 Cr ÷ 13.39 crore tickets) [E from V-snip].
6. **Forex turnover is not forex revenue.** Thomas Cook's forex business handles turnover of several tens of thousands of crore a year (retail turnover +8% YoY in Q1 FY27 [V investywise]) but books only the margin; the primer's "~₹485 Cr financial-services revenue" is a **residual of total income** (it includes other income) — not a reported segment figure [primer says so]. Do not compute "forex margin %" on revenue; compute it on turnover if turnover is disclosed (it was not found this session — §H).
7. **Revenue vs "total income" (Thomas Cook).** The primer's cover "₹8,558 Cr total income (FY26)" = revenue from operations **₹8,398 Cr** + other income ₹156 Cr (screener) [V screener; E]. Use **₹8,398 Cr** as revenue in all tables. Q1 FY27 "total income ₹2,153 Cr (−12%)" similarly vs screener revenue ₹2,092 Cr (−13%).
8. **EBITDA bases differ by company.**
   - **TBO "Adjusted EBITDA"** = EBITDA + ESOP cost (+ M&A costs); FY26 ₹414 Cr vs screener operating profit ₹378 Cr [primer; V screener].
   - **ixigo "Adjusted EBITDA" = EBITDA + ESOP expense − other income**; FY26 ₹120.9 Cr vs screener OP ₹62 Cr (screener deducts ESOP; ixigo's FY26 ESOP charge was large — Q2 FY26 alone ₹26.9 Cr) [V ixigo releases; V screener]. ixigo's "Contribution Margin" (FY26 ₹474.3 Cr) = revenue − direct marketing/payment/customer costs [V].
   - **IRCTC** EBITDA ≈ screener OP (Q1 FY27 ₹386–387 Cr) [V quartr/screener].
   - **Thomas Cook** reports segment EBIT, not EBITDA; screener OP ₹428 Cr FY26.
   - In Part 3, **screener OP is the one common basis**; company-adjusted figures are shown alongside.
9. **PAT basis.** Part 3 uses **screener consolidated "Net Profit"** (FY26: IRCTC 1,393; TBO 244; ixigo 71; Thomas Cook 220). For IRCTC, TBO and ixigo minorities are nil or negligible [K]. **Thomas Cook has a minority interest (DEI is 51%-owned [V screener About])**, and screener's consolidated net profit row may include minorities — **owners' share is not separately verified** (§H). **Pre-exceptional:** FY26 had no material exceptional items at IRCTC/TBO/Thomas Cook [V screener: no large other-income swings]; ixigo's FY26 statutory PAT (₹71.5 Cr) is after associate losses (Freshbus) — its "profit before share of loss of associates and exceptional items" was ₹102.6 Cr [V]. **Q2 FY27 ixigo PAT ₹63.42 Cr includes the ~₹40.66 Cr Freshbus exceptional gain** [V-snip] → pre-exceptional ~₹23 Cr after tax [E].
10. **ixigo and IRCTC are partners, not peers, in trains.** ixigo/ConfirmTkt is an **IRCTC authorised agent (B2C principal service provider)**; every ixigo train ticket is issued by IRCTC and pays IRCTC its fee/charges [K; primer]. "Train GTV" at ixigo is a subset of IRCTC's ticket flow. Do not sum "rail market share" across the two.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Four very different businesses share one label.**
  - **IRCTC** = a state-granted toll on Indian Railways (e-ticketing monopoly, catering mandate, Rail Neer, rail tourism). FY26 revenue ₹5,215 Cr, OP ₹1,666 Cr (32%), PAT ₹1,393 Cr, ROCE 46% [V screener].
  - **TBO Tek** = a global **B2B** travel-distribution marketplace (hotels + air for ~33,700 monthly transacting travel agents in 100+ countries), now owning US luxury wholesaler **Classic Vacations**. FY26 GTV ₹36,809 Cr, revenue ₹2,677 Cr, PAT ₹244 Cr [V screener; primer].
  - **ixigo** = a **B2C** OTA built bottom-up from trains (ConfirmTkt) and buses (AbhiBus) into flights and now hotels. FY26 GTV ₹18,692.7 Cr, revenue ₹1,228 Cr, PAT ₹71.5 Cr [V].
  - **Thomas Cook India** = an integrated outbound-travel + **forex** + resorts (Sterling) + global attractions-imaging (DEI) group, 64.8% owned by Fairfax. FY26 revenue ₹8,398 Cr, OP ₹428 Cr (5%), PAT ₹220 Cr [V screener].
  - **All four, FY26:** revenue **₹17,518 Cr** (+13.3% YoY), OP **₹2,534 Cr (14.5%)**, PAT **₹1,928 Cr** (+3.5%); m-cap **₹63,897 Cr** on 21-Oct-2026 close → **33.1× FY26 PAT** [E: sums of screener 22-Oct-26]. **IRCTC alone is 72% of the PAT and 57% of the m-cap.**
- **2026 has been a shock year for travel, and the stocks show it.**
  - **West Asia war from 28-Feb-2026:** on day one Indian carriers cancelled ~1,221 flights; by 4-Mar >1,200 Indian and ~3,880 foreign-carrier flights had been cancelled; DGCA told operators to avoid 11 flight-information regions incl. Dubai, Doha, Kuwait, Bahrain, Muscat; Air India Express had ~25% of its fleet idle at one point; Europe fares spiked and demand shifted to South-East Asia [V-snip moneylife/thecore/superkalam].
  - **Domestic air traffic fell 6.34% YoY in Aug-2026** (12.126 m vs 12.947 m); Jan–Aug 2026 flat at 110.53 m (−0.18%) [V angelone/DGCA].
  - **Rupee ~₹94.9/$ (record near ₹96.8)** makes outbound trips ~8–10% dearer in rupees than a year ago [E; V-sib], while TBO's international revenue translates up (TBO's Q1 FY27 H+A GTV grew 49.8% reported vs 38.9% constant currency) [V letter].
  - **52-week ranges (screener 22-Oct-26):** IRCTC ₹453 (H/L 736/446 — at its 52-week low); TBO ₹1,554 (1,772/1,004 — near its high); ixigo ₹141 (329/136 — down 57% from high); Thomas Cook ₹97.4 (169/86.2) [V screener].
- **The policy levers are unusually direct.**
  - Railways sets IRCTC's fee, catering and Rail Neer terms (and owns 62.40%).
  - **Budget 1-Feb-2026 cut TCS on overseas tour packages to a flat 2% (from 5% up to ₹10 lakh / 20% above)** and on LRS education/medical remittances to 2% (from 5%) — a direct tailwind for Thomas Cook/SOTC packages from 1-Apr-2026 [V outlookbusiness; effective date V-snip].
  - **GST 2.0 (22-Sep-2025):** hotel rooms ≤₹7,500/night cut from 12% (with ITC) to **5% without ITC** [V-snip theweek/deccanherald].
  - **Tatkal Aadhaar authentication** (online from 1-Jul-2025; OTP incl. counters/agents from 15-Jul-2025; agents barred from first 30 minutes) [V-snip business-standard] — reshapes agent (ixigo) vs direct (IRCTC) booking.
- **Valuations price four very different stories (screener 22-Oct-26):** IRCTC **26.2× P/E**, 2.0% yield, the cheapest it has been in years; TBO **62.8×**; ixigo **52.5× TTM** (TTM PAT inflated by other income and the Freshbus gain; ~88× FY26 PAT [E]); Thomas Cook **20.4×**, ~5.8× EV/OP on company cash [E].

### 1.2 Value chains (supplier → intermediary → traveller)

1. **Rail ticket (IRCTC):** Indian Railways (capacity, fare, quota rules) → CRIS's PRS back-end → IRCTC e-ticketing platform (fee ₹15/₹30 + GST per ticket non-AC/AC; lower for UPI per primer — conflict, see §H) → **direct users (app/web) or authorised agents** (ixigo, MakeMyTrip, Paytm, etc. that add their own fee) → passenger. The fare goes to Railways; IRCTC keeps the fee + payment-gateway and other income.
2. **Air ticket (TBO B2B / ixigo B2C):** Airline (fares via NDC/direct connect or GDS: Amadeus, Sabre, Travelport) → consolidator/OTA → agent or traveller. Commissions are tiny (TBO air take rate **2.3%**, gross profit **1.25% of GTV** [V letter]; ixigo flight gross take ~**8.6%** incl. convenience fees and add-ons [V-snip broker note]).
3. **Hotel room (TBO / ixigo / Thomas Cook):** Hotel → channel manager / bedbank (Hotelbeds, WebBeds, Expedia EPS, TBO) → B2B agent or OTA → traveller. Net-rate contracts give the intermediary a 10–20% markup; TBO's hotel take rate **10.6%**, gross profit **6.2% of GTV** [V letter]; ixigo hotels gross take **>12%** [V].
4. **Package holiday (Thomas Cook/SOTC; Classic Vacations):** Airline seats + hotel + transfers + guide + visa + insurance bundled by the operator; sold through stores, franchisees, digital, and (Classic) US advisors. Operator takes inventory/price risk; revenue booked gross.
5. **Forex (Thomas Cook):** Banks/wholesale market → AD-II licensee → retail customer (notes, card load, remittance). Revenue = buy-sell spread + fees; float on unspent card balances.

### 1.3 Where value is captured (and by whom)

- **Choke points with regulatory protection earn the most.** IRCTC's ticketing segment converts ~80% of revenue into segment profit (Q1 FY27: ₹289.6 Cr on ₹360.99 Cr) [V-snip; V psuconnect for revenue].
- **Fragmentation earns a markup.** Hotels (fragmented, opaque pricing) pay intermediaries 5–6× more per rupee of GTV than airlines (TBO gross profit 6.2% vs 1.25% of GTV) [V letter].
- **Customer ownership vs supplier ownership.** B2C OTAs (ixigo) spend on performance marketing and discounts (contribution margin ₹474 Cr on ₹1,228 Cr revenue = 39% [V]); B2B platforms (TBO) spend on sales teams and credit to agents (TBO debtor days 724 on screener [V]).
- **Money flows are a second toll.** Forex (Thomas Cook Financial Services EBIT margin 45.3% [V investywise]) and float (IRCTC's payables to Railways; Thomas Cook's card float) earn interest income — IRCTC other income ₹277 Cr in FY26 [V screener].

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Rail is the mass base.** Indian Railways carries the largest reserved-passenger flow in the world; IRCTC sells **~14.6 lakh tickets/day** [V]. Online share of reserved tickets ~**86–89%** (89.24% "digital penetration" quoted Dec-2025) [V-snip dcf-analysis]; primer says ~80% (stale). New trains (Vande Bharat chair/sleeper, Amrit Bharat) lift catering spend more than ticket counts.
2. **Air: capacity-constrained, fare-led.** Domestic traffic was flat Jan–Aug 2026 and **−6.3% in Aug-2026** [V]. Drivers: IndiGo's Dec-2025 FDTL meltdown (2,507 flights cancelled 3–5 Dec 2025; DGCA penalty ₹22.20 Cr + ₹50 Cr bank guarantee, 17-Jan-2026) [V-snip], engine-related groundings (Pratt & Whitney GTF issues, 2024–26 [K]), Air India post-AI171 crash safety review (Jun-2025 [K]), then the West Asia war and higher ATF. Higher fares lift OTA GTV/booking but cut volumes; ixigo explicitly cites "higher airfares and limited availability of confirmed train tickets" as driving bus demand [V].
3. **Outbound boom — paused, not reversed.** Departures +6.3% in CY2025 to 3.28 crore [V-snip]; LRS travel remittances ~flat-to-down in FY26 (−2 to −3%) [V-snip]. 2026 headwinds: war (Gulf is India's largest outbound corridor [K]), rupee at record lows, US/UK/Schengen visa friction. Tailwinds: **TCS cut to 2%** (from 1-Apr-2026), rising passport issuance, new routes to SE Asia/Central Asia. TBO saw India H+A GTV **−2%** in Q1 FY27 as Indians rerouted east to lower-ADR destinations [V letter].
4. **Hotel ADR inflation.** Branded ARR +10–12% YoY in Jun-2026 [V-snip HVS]; GST cut on ≤₹7,500 rooms (22-Sep-2025) lowers consumer price but removes ITC (hotels complain) [V-snip]. For OTAs, ADR inflation lifts GTV and absolute commission.
5. **Students and remittances.** Education LRS fell ~21% in FY26 (~$2.3 bn) [V-snip] — US/Canada/UK/Australia visa tightening [K]. Negative for Thomas Cook's student-forex franchise.
6. **Corporate travel & MICE.** Steady with GDP; Thomas Cook's MICE business is India's largest outbound MICE operator [primer/K].
7. **Pilgrimage/spiritual tourism.** Maha Kumbh (Jan–Feb 2025) created a high base for ixigo Q4 FY25/IRCTC [V ixigo release mentions "high base-effect of MahaKumbh"]; Bharat Gaurav trains are an IRCTC growth line.
8. **Global B2B demand (TBO).** 64% of TBO GTV is now from non-India source markets (Q1 FY27) [V]; APAC H+A +56%, Europe +37%, LATAM +20%, MEA flat, North America 24% of H+A GTV in its seasonal-peak quarter [V].

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Railways ↔ IRCTC (the defining relationship)
- **Convenience fee:** ₹15 (non-AC) / ₹30 (AC) + GST per e-ticket (per Yourstory Jul-2026 [V-snip]); primer says ₹10/₹20 for UPI and ₹15/₹30 for card — **conflict (FLAG §H)**. History: fee withdrawn Nov-2016 (demonetisation push), restored Sep-2019; **28-Oct-2021 order to share 50% of the fee with Railways withdrawn within 24 hours** [K; primer].
- **Fee sharing on non-fee income:** IRCTC shares some ticketing-related income with Railways under MoUs [K, FLAG].
- **Catering policy:** Catering Policy 2017 (mobile catering back to IRCTC; base kitchens; menu tariffs fixed by Railways); new trains (Vande Bharat) increase catering via pre-paid meals [K].
- **Tatkal & ARP rules:** **Aadhaar authentication mandatory for Tatkal online from 1-Jul-2025; Aadhaar-OTP from 15-Jul-2025 including counter and agent bookings; agents barred from the first 30 minutes of Tatkal windows (10:00–10:30 AC, 11:00–11:30 non-AC)**; from ~Feb-2026 only Aadhaar-verified users may book Tatkal and first-day general (ARP-open) tickets [V-snip business-standard/mobileidworld]. **Advance Reservation Period (ARP) was cut from 120 to 60 days from 1-Nov-2024** [K]. Anti-tout drive: 13,580 touts and 65 railway employees arrested 2023–Jun-2026 (Lok Sabha, Aug-2026) [V-snip].
  - **Implication:** agent channels (incl. ixigo) lose the Tatkal opening window; direct IRCTC users gain. Ticket counts are roughly flat (ticketing revenue +0.6% in Q1 FY27) [V].
- **Rail Neer pricing:** set by Railways (₹15/1-litre bottle reduced to ₹14 in Sep-2025 after GST 2.0? [K — FLAG, not verified]).
- **GST disputes:** GSTAT order (15-Oct-2026) — ₹50.41 Cr + 18% interest [V screener].
- **Navratna** status (Mar-2025) and Schedule 'A' CPSE (Jul-2024) [V screener About; primer].

### 7.2 Aviation
- DGCA FDTL (pilot rest) norms phased in 2025; IndiGo's Dec-2025 failure → **₹22.20 Cr penalty (₹1.80 Cr systemic + ₹20.40 Cr for 68 days at ₹30 lakh/day), ₹50 Cr bank guarantee, CEO cautioned (17-Jan-2026)** [V-snip outlookbusiness/moneylife].
- Fare monitoring: civil aviation ministry "closely monitoring" fares during the war [V-snip]; earlier (Dec-2025) the government capped fares during the IndiGo disruption [K, FLAG].
- West Asia: DGCA advisories to avoid 11 FIRs (later 9; Oman and Saudi usable with conditions) [V-snip]; Pakistan airspace closed to Indian carriers since Apr-2025 [K] — together adding 3–4 hours to some westbound flights [V-snip].

### 7.3 Outbound money: LRS, TCS, forex licensing
- **LRS limit US$250,000 per person per year** [K, RBI].
- **TCS (Income-tax Act s.206C(1G)):**
  - From 1-Oct-2023: 20% above ₹7 lakh for most LRS and on overseas tour packages (5% up to ₹7 lakh for packages) [K].
  - **From 1-Apr-2025 (Budget 2025): LRS threshold raised to ₹10 lakh** [K]; tour packages 5% up to ₹10 lakh, 20% above [V-snip outlookmoney].
  - **Budget 1-Feb-2026: overseas tour packages → flat 2% with no threshold; education & medical LRS → 2% (from 5%); other LRS 20% above ₹10 lakh unchanged** [V outlookbusiness; V-snip for "other LRS" and the 1-Apr-2026 effective date]. EaseMyTrip's co-founder called it "one of the most significant measures" [V].
  - TCS is creditable against income tax — a cash-flow friction, not a final tax.
- **Forex dealers:** RBI Authorised Dealer Category II / FFMC licensing; Thomas Cook is an AD-II [K].

### 7.4 GST (indirect tax)
- **GST 2.0 effective 22-Sep-2025:** two main slabs 5% and 18% (+40% demerit). **Hotel rooms ≤₹7,500/night: 12% with ITC → 5% without ITC**; rooms >₹7,500 remain 18% [V-snip theweek/deccanherald; K for the >₹7,500 slab]. FHRAI says the loss of ITC hurts small hotels [V-snip tribune].
- **Tour operators:** 5% without ITC on package value (or 18% with ITC on commission) — unchanged by GST 2.0 [K, FLAG — not verified this session].
- **Air tickets:** economy 5%; business 12% → **18%** under GST 2.0 [K, FLAG — search did not confirm].
- **OTAs/e-commerce operators:** GST on commissions/fees at 18%; ECO liability for hotel accommodation (s.9(5)) for unregistered hotels [K].

### 7.5 Data, consumer protection, competition
- CCI 2022 order against MakeMyTrip-Goibibo (OYO) and Treebo/FabHotels issues [K]; DPDP Act 2023 rules (2025) on traveller data [K]; consumer-protection "dark patterns" guidelines (Nov-2023) apply to drip pricing of convenience fees [K].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; timeline in §G)

- **Pre-2010: offline agents and IRCTC's birth.** IRCTC incorporated 1999; e-ticketing from 2002 [K]. Thomas Cook India dates to 1881 [V screener].
- **2010s: OTAs and consolidation.** MakeMyTrip listed on NASDAQ (2010), merged Goibibo (2016–17) [K]. Fairfax bought 77% of Thomas Cook India in 2012 [V screener], then Sterling (2014), Kuoni India incl. SOTC (2015), Tata Capital forex (2017), 51% of DEI (2019) [V screener About]. Cox & Kings collapsed in 2019 [K].
- **2019: IRCTC IPO (Oct)** — 112× oversubscribed [K]; OFS Dec-2020 and Dec-2022 took GoI to 62.40% [primer].
- **2020–21: COVID.** Thomas Cook revenue fell from ₹6,833 Cr (FY20) to ₹795 Cr (FY21), PAT −₹295 Cr; TBO revenue ₹142 Cr FY21 with a loss [V screener]. ixigo bought ConfirmTkt (Feb-2021) and AbhiBus (Aug-2021) [primer].
- **Oct-2021: the fee-sharing shock** at IRCTC — reversed within a day [primer].
- **2022–24: revenge travel.** Thomas Cook revenue back to ₹7,299 Cr (FY24); IRCTC PAT ₹1,111 Cr (FY24) [V screener]. **TBO IPO (May-2024; listed 15-May)** and **ixigo IPO (Jun-2024; ₹740 Cr issue)** [primer; K].
- **2025: policy and shocks.** Tatkal Aadhaar (Jul-2025); GST 2.0 (22-Sep-2025); **TBO closes Classic Vacations ($125 m, effective 1-Oct-2025)** [V-snip]; **Prosus → ixigo ₹1,296 Cr (Oct-2025)** [V-snip]; IndiGo meltdown (3–5 Dec 2025) [V-snip].
- **2026: war, rupee, TCS relief.** Budget TCS cut (1-Feb); **West Asia war (28-Feb)** → airspace closures, fare spikes, GCC demand collapse; ceasefire 7-Apr [V-sib]; Thomas Cook demerger scheme (20-Mar); rupee record lows (May → Oct); DGCA traffic −6% (Aug); RBI hike to 5.50% (7-Oct) [V-sib]; ixigo record PAT (Q2 FY27, 17-Oct) on a one-off gain; ED penalty on TBO (21-Oct).
- **Cyclicality:** travel volumes fall fast in shocks (COVID, war) but recover; IRCTC's ticketing is the least cyclical (rail is a necessity); Thomas Cook and TBO are the most geared to discretionary international travel.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

- **IRCTC ticketing:** Revenue = tickets/day × 365 × (fee per ticket + non-fee income per ticket). Profit ≈ revenue × ~80%.
- **IRCTC catering:** Revenue = meals × price (Railways-fixed tariff); profit = revenue − food cost − licence fee − labour (≈9–14% margin).
- **B2B platform (TBO):** Revenue = GTV × take rate; GP = GTV × GP margin (air ~1.25%, hotels ~6.2%); adj EBITDA = GP − SG&A.
- **B2C OTA (ixigo):** Revenue = transactions × avg ticket value × take rate + ads; CM = revenue − marketing − PG − support; adj EBITDA = CM − indirect costs.
- **Tour operator + forex (Thomas Cook):** EBIT = (package pax × package price × package margin) + (forex turnover × spread) + (Sterling room-nights × ARR − costs) + DEI.
