## READ THIS FIRST: nine definitional traps

1. **"Industrial Gases" here is really one-and-a-half companies.**
   - Only **Linde India** and **Ellenbarrie** are gas companies.
   - **Refex Industries** is classified under "Industrial Gases" because it began as Refex Refrigerants (2002). In FY26 refrigerant gas was **~1% of revenue** [V screener key points] and is now shut.
   - Refex's FY26 revenue split [V screener key points]: **ash & coal handling 58%**, wind (Venwind Refex: wind-turbine manufacturing and EPC) 17%, green mobility 7%, refrigerant 1%, other (solar generation and module trading) 17%.
   - Refex's FY26 revenue of ₹2,277 Cr is about **44% of the "sector" revenue but carries almost no gas economics.**
   - **The writer must lead with the "pure-play gases" subgroup (Linde + Ellenbarrie)** and treat Refex as an adjacent profile: an HFC/refrigerant-regulation case study plus a governance case study.
2. **Linde India: segment revenue is gross of inter-segment sales, and Project Engineering (PED) is lumpy.**
   - FY26 segment revenue: Gases & related ~₹2,128 Cr; PED ~₹981 Cr gross. These do not sum to the ₹2,531 Cr consolidated revenue because PED builds Linde's own plants (inter-segment elimination ≈ ₹578 Cr) [primer; E: 2,128 + 981 − 2,531].
   - **PED share of revenue swings:** 13% in Q1 FY26 vs **22% in Q1 FY27** [V screener key points].
   - That swing is why Q1 FY27 revenue rose 21.6% while PAT fell 2.4% and OPM dropped from 34% to 29%: PED earns a lower margin, and "materials and goods bought" rose to 27.1% of revenue from 19.6% [V-snip].
   - Revenue FY24 ₹2,769 Cr → FY25 ₹2,485 Cr → FY26 ₹2,531 Cr while PAT rose ₹434 → ₹455 → ₹549 Cr [V screener]. **Judge Linde on gases revenue, operating profit and PAT, not total revenue.**
3. **Linde India: standalone vs consolidated is nearly identical; company PAT vs screener PAT differs slightly.**
   - FY26 PAT: ₹551 Cr (company/press, "₹5,508.74 mn") [primer] vs **₹549 Cr on screener consolidated** [V]. Use screener in tables.
   - Q1 FY27: consolidated ₹104.6 Cr vs standalone ₹103.0 Cr [V-snip]. The consolidated scope adds only small JVs/associates [E].
4. **"Linde in India" ≠ Linde India.**
   - **Linde plc owns two Indian companies:** the listed **Linde India Ltd** (75%, via The BOC Group) and the unlisted, 100%-owned **Praxair India Pvt Ltd**.
   - Post-2018 carve-up [primer, from CCI/gasworld]:
     - Linde India got the **North, East and West-2** regions and **exclusivity in Project Engineering**.
     - Praxair India got **South, Central and West-1** and **exclusivity in HyCO** (hydrogen, CO, CO₂, incl. carbon capture).
   - Market-share figures such as "Linde ~26%" usually **combine both** [primer, gasworld].
   - **Hydrogen gas supply in India is Praxair India's franchise, not Linde India's.** Linde India's hydrogen exposure is engineering (building plants).
   - Several press items say "Linde is building an ASU at Dholera for Tata Electronics' fab" (H2-2027) [V-snip]. **Which Linde entity owns that ASU is unconfirmed.** Do not credit it to Linde India without a filing.
5. **TPD vs Nm³/h vs "capacity".**

   - Conversion: **1,000 TPD O₂ ≈ 29,200 Nm³/h** [E: 1,000,000 kg ÷ 1.429 kg/Nm³ ÷ 24 h].

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Indian industrial gases is an oligopoly of unlisted or foreign-held giants, with a thin listed slice.**
  - The two largest suppliers are **INOX Air Products** (unlisted; 49.74% Jain family / 49.74% Air Products [V CRISIL Jan-2024]) and the **Linde group** (listed Linde India + unlisted Praxair India).
  - Each is credited with roughly a quarter of the market. The top players hold ~80% [primer, gasworld; V-snip].
  - Next come **Air Liquide India** (unlisted), **Taiyo Nippon Sanso K-Air India / Nippon Gases** (unlisted), **Air Water India**, **Ellenbarrie** (listed), and regionals such as Bhuruka/Bhoruka, Goyal MG, SICGIL, NovaAir and Southern Gas. Ken Research counts ~310 players [V Ken Research].
  - **Listed pure-play gas revenue in India is only ~₹2,500 Cr** (Linde gases ~₹2,128 Cr + Ellenbarrie ₹342 Cr) [E]. The market is ~₹13,400–36,000 Cr depending on scope (§2).
- **Market size:** **US$1.41 bn (≈₹13,400 Cr) in 2025** for outsourced industrial gases (Ken Research; 6.7% CAGR 2020–25; 7.5% forecast to $2.18 bn by 2031) [V]. Broader definitions that include captive, hydrogen and specialty give **$3.06–3.81 bn** (TechSci, IMARC) [V-snip]. On-site's share of value is **48% (2025) → 52% (2031)** [V Ken].
- **The listed pair earns spectacular margins and trades at spectacular multiples.**

### 1.2 Value chain (air → customer)

1. **Feedstock:** atmospheric air is free (78.08% N₂, 20.95% O₂, 0.93% Ar, 0.04% CO₂ plus traces of Ne/Kr/Xe/He) [K]. **The real "raw material" is electricity.** Compressors drive the process, and cryogenic distillation is energy-intensive.
2. **Production:**
   - **Cryogenic ASU:** filter → compress (main air compressor, ~5–6 bar) → pre-purify (molecular sieve removes H₂O/CO₂) → heat exchange → expansion turbine → double distillation column → argon side-column → products as gas (pipeline) or liquid (via liquefier) [K].
   - **Liquid production costs 2–3× the power of gaseous production per tonne** [K].
   - Non-cryogenic **PSA/VPSA/membrane** generators serve small on-site needs (hospitals, small fabricators). These were the COVID-era "PSA plants" [K].
3. **Distribution modes:**
   - **(a) On-site/tonnage:** pipeline over the fence from a dedicated ASU on a 15–20-year take-or-pay contract.
   - **(b) Merchant/bulk liquid:** cryogenic road tankers (typically 15–20 t) to customer storage tanks (VIE tanks).
   - **(c) Packaged/cylinders:** high-pressure cylinders (~7 m³ O₂ each), cylinder quads and dewars.
   - The same ASU usually feeds all three: anchor pipeline customer + liquid surplus + cylinder filling [primer; K].
4. **End-markets:** steel/metals (largest), chemicals/refining, healthcare, glass, electronics/solar, food and beverage, fabrication/welding, pharma, auto, paper, water treatment [V Ken: steel ~23.3%, healthcare ~9.8% of application demand, 2024].

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **Density, not scale, is the moat.**
  - Liquid O₂/N₂ is worth ~₹8–15/kg, and a cryogenic tanker costs roughly ₹50–80 per km to run [E]. Bulk liquid is uneconomic beyond ~200–300 km [primer Ellenbarrie: "molecule-mile" ~200 km; K industry norm].
  - Each ASU therefore commands a local catchment. Regional density lets a ₹342 Cr regional (Ellenbarrie) earn 34–39% EBITDA, matching Linde's 36%.
- **On-site tonnage captures the most durable value.**
  - Revenue = fixed facility fee (capital recovery) + variable charge with **electricity passed through** + escalators. The supplier earns a utility-like return on capital (~12–15% IRR [E]) for 15–20 years.
  - Customers outsource because gas is essential but small in their cost base, and because self-supply ties up capital and know-how [primer].
- **Merchant liquid captures the highest margin per tonne but is cyclical.**
  - Prices move with industrial demand, power cost and spare capacity.
  - **Argon** and **medical** O₂ are the richest products. The 2021 COVID medical-oxygen shock briefly sent LOX prices multiples higher, until NPPA capped LMO prices in Sep-2021 [K].
- **Packaged/cylinders:** highest price per unit, highest logistics cost, most fragmented. Value comes from brand, safety record and distribution.
- **Engineering (ASU EPC):** lumpy, lower-margin. Strategic value: self-build capability lowers own capex and wins third-party orders (Linde India PED; Ellenbarrie's BHEL order).
- **What does NOT capture value:** feedstock (free), and trading of imported commodity gases such as HFC refrigerants (Refex's old business, with thin margins and high working capital).

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Steel (the anchor; ~23% of demand by value, the majority of tonnage O₂).**
   - India crude steel **168.4 Mt FY26 (+10.7%)**; **Q1 FY27 42.1 Mt (+3%)**; capacity **221.9 Mtpa (Jun-2026)**; National Steel Policy 2017 target **300 Mtpa by 2030–31** [V-snip JPC via BigMint/SteelRadar; K NSP-2017].
   - Oxygen intensity [K/E]: BF-BOF integrated **~90–120 Nm³ O₂ per tonne of crude steel** (BOF blowing ~50–60 Nm³/t plus BF enrichment and casting/cutting). EAF **~30–40 Nm³/t**. Stainless (AOD) adds **argon (~10–15 Nm³/t)** and nitrogen.
   - **Rule of thumb: 1 Mtpa BF-BOF ≈ 350–450 TPD O₂** [E: 100 Nm³ × 1.429 kg ÷ 365 days].
   - Pipeline: JSW (Vijayanagar, Dolvi), Tata Steel (Kalinganagar phase 2, now Linde-supplied), JSPL Angul, AM/NS India Hazira (Hazira complex ~7,900 TPD O₂/N₂ capacity [V Ken]), SAIL modernisation, NMDC Nagarnar, Jindal Stainless.
   - **Captive vs outsourced:** SAIL and older integrated plants run captive ASUs. New greenfields increasingly outsource on BOO contracts (Tata–Linde; JSW–INOXAP/Linde/Air Liquide historically) [K]. The outsourcing ratio is a key swing factor (H list).
2. **Chemicals, refining, fertiliser and coal gasification.**
   - N₂ for inerting; O₂ for oxidation and gasification; H₂ for refining.
   - **Coal gasification** is a new O₂-hungry segment: BHEL–Coal India's Odisha coal-to-ammonium-nitrate project needs a **1,200 TPD ASU** (Ellenbarrie EPC, ₹481 Cr) [V screener ann.]. The government targets **100 Mt of coal gasification by 2030** [K, Ministry of Coal].
3. **Healthcare (≈9.8% of demand).**
   - Medical O₂ (LMO + cylinders + PSA), N₂O, medical air.
   - COVID 2021: peak medical demand ~**8,900–9,000 TPD** in May-2021 vs ~700–1,000 TPD pre-COVID; industrial O₂ was diverted [K, MoHFW/DPIIT statements].
   - Legacy: PM-CARES funded **~1,200+ PSA plants** in public hospitals; hospitals installed LMO tanks; NPPA capped LMO/cylinder prices (Sep-2021) [K].
   - Many PSA plants are now under-utilised. LMO demand normalised to a structurally higher base (more ICU beds) [E].
4. **Electronics, semiconductors and solar.**
   - **10 ISM-approved semiconductor projects across 6 states, ~$18 bn** (2025), with up to 50% fiscal support [V Ken]. Tata Electronics–PSMC Dholera fab **₹91,000 Cr** [V-snip].
   - Fabs need huge volumes of **UHP N₂** (the biggest by volume; a mid-size fab ~10,000–30,000 Nm³/h [E/K]) plus UHP O₂/Ar/H₂/He and ESGs.
   - Suppliers: INOXAP ₹500 Cr Dholera ESG hub (land bought Oct-2025; ops in 12–18 months) and a long-term UHP N₂ contract with ReNew's Dholera TOPCon solar-cell plant (Nov-2025). Linde ASU at Dholera for the Tata fab, ops H2-2027 [V-snip].
   - Solar cell/wafer plants (PLI) and display/OSAT plants (Micron Sanand, CG Power, Kaynes) add N₂/Ar demand [K].
5. **Glass, F&B, pharma, auto, fabrication.** Oxy-fuel glass furnaces (O₂), CO₂ for beverages, N₂ for food packaging and freezing, Ar/CO₂ welding mixes, N₂ blanketing for pharma API reactors [K].
6. **Hydrogen and energy transition (optionality).**
   - National Green Hydrogen Mission (Jan-2023): **₹19,744 Cr outlay to FY2029-30; 5 MMT/yr green H₂ by 2030** [K/V-snip]. SIGHT component **₹17,490 Cr** [V-snip PTI].
   - Allocated: **412 ktpa green H₂ production + 1.5 GW/yr electrolyser manufacturing**; 21 firms bid for 3.4 GW against 1.5 GW offered [V-snip].
   - SECI green-ammonia auctions: **₹55.75/kg (Aug-2025, 75 ktpa) → ₹50.75/kg (Jakson, 85 ktpa)**; NTPC REL 70 ktpa at ₹51.80/kg signed Mar-2026 [V-snip].
   - **By-product O₂:** ~8 t O₂ per t H₂ electrolysed [V Ken]. 5 MMT of H₂ would release ~40 Mt of O₂, many times India's merchant market [E]. This is a potential long-run threat to merchant O₂ prices near electrolyser clusters. Watch.

**Demand elasticity:** low. Gas is typically **<2–3% of a steel mill's cost** [E/K]. Take-or-pay floors protect on-site volumes. Merchant volumes swing with IIP/manufacturing.

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Safety and licensing
- **PESO (Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation)** licences cryogenic storage, gas cylinders and filling plants under the **Gas Cylinders Rules 2016** and the **Static and Mobile Pressure Vessels (Unfired) Rules 2016** [K]. Each new plant/tank needs PESO approval, which is a modest entry barrier.
- **Medical oxygen** is a drug under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act. Manufacturing licences come from the State FDA/CDSCO. Since 2021, medical O₂ has been on the **NLEM** and its price is regulated by **NPPA**: in Sep-2021 LMO was capped at ₹15.22/m³ and cylinder O₂ at ₹25.71/m³ (ex-GST), with extensions thereafter (current status unverified) [K; FLAG].
- **Environment:** ASUs are low-polluting (CPCB "white/green" category for most) [K].

### 7.2 Listed-company governance: SEBI LODR related-party rules (central to Linde India)
- Reg. 23 SEBI LODR: material RPTs (above ₹1,000 Cr or **10% of annual consolidated turnover**, whichever is lower, aggregated across the year) need **prior approval of shareholders, with related parties not voting** [K, LODR as amended 2021–22].
- **Linde India case chronology** [V-snip unless stated]:
  1. **Oct-2018:** Praxair–Linde merger. CCI clears it with an India carve-up; some Linde India assets are transferred to Praxair India [primer].
  2. **2020:** JV/business arrangements between Linde India and Praxair India (later characterised by SAT as an RPT requiring approval) [V-snip company filing 10-Dec-2025].
  3. **24-Jun-2021 AGM:** non-promoter shareholders reject the Praxair RPTs by an "almost 100%" majority. The company continues transacting, arguing no single contract crossed materiality [V-snip moneylife/IIM-K case].
  4. **Apr-2024:** SEBI interim order directs NSE to appoint an independent valuer for the transactions and says prima facie material RPTs were executed without shareholder approval [V-snip BS 30-Apr-2024].
  5. **22-May-2024:** SAT sets aside the SEBI interim order and asks the company to appear for document inspection [V-snip BS].
  6. **2025:** SEBI (WTM Ashwani Bhatia) passes a fresh order criticising the company for proceeding with transactions shareholders had voted down; valuation needed to decide materiality [V-snip].
  7. **5-Dec-2025:** SAT dismisses Linde's appeal. **Aggregation of RPTs is mandatory**; the 2020 JV agreement is an RPT needing minority approval. Company "assessing financial impact" (filing 10-Dec-2025) [V-snip; NSE filing title].
  8. **5-Mar-2026 EGM:** resolution for **₹417.7 Cr of FY2025-26 RPTs with Praxair India** is **defeated: 11% for, 89% against**; BOC Group (promoter) did not vote [V Motilal Oswal 6-Mar-2026; V-snip askfuzz/BS]. Vote-count figures in press reports are garbled. Use the percentages only.
- **What it means:** Linde India cannot lawfully continue material RPTs with Praxair India without minority approval. Possible outcomes [E]:
  - (a) Restructure or stop the inter-company flows (gas swaps, plant sales, engineering services).
  - (b) Compensate Linde India for past transfers following an independent valuation (the "bull catalyst" in our primer).
  - (c) A merger of Praxair India into Linde India, or a delisting/open offer by Linde plc (speculative; nothing filed). **Treat (c) as speculation only.**
  - Further SEBI action or penalties.
- Writer: present this as "an unusual and important precedent in Indian RPT enforcement", not as a resolved matter.

### 7.3 Refrigerants: the Kigali Amendment and India's HFC phase-down (relevant to Refex)
- **Montreal Protocol, Kigali Amendment (Oct-2016)** [K]. India ratified it in Aug/Sep-2021 and is in **Article 5 Group 2**:
  - Baseline = average HFC consumption **2024–2026** + 65% of the HCFC baseline.
  - **Freeze in 2028**.
  - Reductions: **10% by 2032, 20% by 2037, 30% by 2042, 85% by 2047** [K, UNEP/MoEFCC].
- India's HCFC phase-out (HPMP Stage III) runs to 2030, with a servicing tail [K]. India Cooling Action Plan (2019) [K].
- **Implication for Refex's exit:** HFC quotas from 2028 are likely to be allocated on 2024–26 baseline consumption/imports [E]. Refex quit refrigerant trading during the baseline window. That forfeits any future quota "rights", but also removes regulatory and working-capital risk. Fluorochemical producers (SRF, Navin, GFL; not in this sub-sector) are the structural winners from a quota regime [E].
- Refrigerant gas was ~1% of Refex's FY26 revenue, sourced from China and other markets, with OEM collaborations (LG, Voltas, myTVS, KI Mobility, Cars24) [V screener key points].

- **India Semiconductor Mission:** ₹76,000 Cr scheme (2021); up to 50% fiscal support for fabs; 10 projects approved by 2025 (~$18 bn) [K; V Ken]. ISM 2.0 under discussion [K/E].
- **National Green Hydrogen Mission:** ₹19,744 Cr (SIGHT ₹17,490 Cr; pilots ₹1,466 Cr; R&D ₹400 Cr) [K/V-snip]. Green Hydrogen Hubs; ISTS waiver for green H₂ projects commissioned before 2030 [K].

- **1935–1990s, the BOC era:** Indian Oxygen & Acetylene Co (BOC, UK) dominates. Steel PSUs build captive ASUs. Small regionals emerge (Ellenbarrie 1973, Kolkata) [primer].
- **1990s–2000s, MNC entry and JV wave:** Praxair buys into India (Praxair India; ~$120 m sales by 2005, then "India's largest industrial gas producer" [V-snip]). INOX and Air Products form INOXAP (1999 [K]). Air Liquide enters. Tonnage outsourcing by private steel (JSW, Essar, Jindal) begins.
- **2006–2013:** Linde AG buys BOC globally (2006). BOC India is renamed **Linde India (Feb-2013)** [primer].
- **2014–2018, steel downturn and overcapacity:** merchant LOX prices weak. Linde India earns 15–16% OPM, high debt (borrowings ₹1,445 Cr in Dec-2016) and near-zero PAT (₹16–26 Cr in 2016–18) [V screener].
- **Oct-2018:** Praxair–Linde merger and the CCI carve-up. Linde India gets one-off gains: other income **₹862 Cr in CY2019** and ₹335 Cr in CY2021 [V screener]. These are most likely gains on post-merger business/asset transfers [E — verify in annual reports]. Debt is repaid (borrowings ₹1,199 Cr → ₹108 Cr in 2019) [V screener].
- **2020–2021, COVID:** merchant O₂ is diverted to hospitals. The **April–May 2021 second wave** brings LMO shortages, Oxygen Express trains, PSA plant drives, NPPA price caps (Sep-2021) and import of ISO containers [K]. Gas companies are recognised as critical infrastructure.
- **2021–2023, margin reset upward:** steel boom and merchant price discipline. INOXAP EBITDA 48–50% (FY21–22) [V CRISIL]. Linde India OPM rises from 24% (CY19) to 31% (FY25) and 36% (FY26) [V screener].
- **2023–2026, the new tonnage wave and governance fights:**
  - Linde wins Tata Steel Kalinganagar (Sep-2023), commissions Jindal Stainless 1,450 TPD (FY26) and completes the Tata ASUs (Sep-2026).
  - SEBI/SAT RPT saga (2024–2025); minority rejection (Mar-2026).
  - **Ellenbarrie IPO (Jun-2025; listed 1-Jul-2025, ₹852.5 Cr at ₹400, 22× subscribed)** [V-snip].
  - Refex exits refrigerants (Jan-2026) and pivots to ash logistics, WTG and EV.
- **Listed stock cycle:** Linde India peaked near ₹8,000+ (52-week high ₹8,049) and now trades at ₹5,724, a −6% 1-yr / −1% 3-yr CAGR [V screener]. Ellenbarrie listed at ~₹486–492 (NSE/BSE), fell to ₹175 (52-week low) and is now ₹303 (−24% vs the ₹400 issue price) [primer for listing price; V screener]. Refex is −38% over 1 yr [V screener].

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Non-cryogenic generation (PSA/VPSA/membrane):** cheaper for small, low-purity needs. It erodes cylinder and small-bulk demand (hospitals post-COVID, small fabricators). Mitigant: the majors sell and service PSA units too [K].
2. **Green hydrogen by-product oxygen:** large electrolyser clusters (Kandla, Paradip, Tuticorin) could flood local merchant O₂ markets at near-zero marginal cost. Low probability before 2030 given the slow green-H₂ ramp [E].
3. **Steel decarbonisation:**
   - **H₂-DRI + EAF** reduces BOF oxygen demand per tonne (EAF ~30–40 Nm³/t vs BF-BOF ~100) but raises hydrogen/nitrogen demand.
   - **Oxygen-enriched BF / top-gas recycling** and **CCUS** raise O₂ demand.
   - The net effect for ASU owners is uncertain; tonnage contracts protect the existing book [E].
4. **Power-market change:** time-of-day tariffs and RE open access favour ASUs that can liquefy at off-peak hours (energy storage via LIN/LOX). A competitive edge for operators with flexible plants [K/E].
5. **Digital/remote operations:** centralised remote operating centres (Linde, Air Liquide) cut opex. Telemetry on customer tanks optimises logistics [K].
6. **Semiconductor-grade purity:** UHP gases and ESG need new capability. Global majors and INOXAP hold the edge; Ellenbarrie is unproven in UHP [E].
7. **Refrigerant transition (Refex-specific, now moot):** HFC → HFO and natural refrigerants (R-290, CO₂) [K].
8. **Coal-power decline (Refex's ash business):** long-dated. India's thermal fleet is still growing (~80 GW new coal planned by 2032 [K]) and the 100% fly-ash utilisation mandate (MoEFCC Dec-2021 notification) underpins demand [K].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Steel capex is the order book.**
   - Steel accounts for ~23% of value demand and most tonnage oxygen.
   - India must add ~80 Mtpa (221.9 → 300 Mtpa) by 2030. At ~350–450 TPD O₂ per Mtpa (BF-BOF), that is 20,000–30,000 TPD of new ASUs, worth ~₹9,000–14,000 Cr of capex [E].
   - The winners are whoever signs the 15–20-year BOO contracts. Linde's Tata Steel (3,600 TPD) and Jindal (1,450 TPD) wins show the listed arm is getting its share in the East.
2. **Physics makes the market local, and local makes it an oligopoly.**
   - Bulk liquid travels ~200–300 km economically, so each ASU is a regional franchise [primer; K].
   - That is why a ₹342 Cr company (Ellenbarrie) earns the same ~35% margin as a Linde subsidiary, and why INOXAP has earned 40–50% EBITDA [V CRISIL].
   - Competition happens at bid time for tonnage contracts, not as a running price war.
3. **Power is the input; contract design decides who bears it.**
   - Electricity is ~50–70% of production cash cost [K/E] (~24% of revenue for one major [V Ken]).
   - On-site contracts pass it through. Merchant and packaged prices reset with a lag.
   - Margin swings in merchant-heavy players follow tariffs and spare capacity. RE open access is the cost lever.
4. **The listed sector is governance-defined.**
   - **Linde India:** a 75% parent with a 100%-owned sister in the same market. Minorities rejected RPTs in 2021 and again in Mar-2026 (89% against ₹417.7 Cr); SAT confirmed in Dec-2025 that RPTs must be aggregated.
   - **Ellenbarrie:** promoter holding above 75% (MPS sell-down due by ~2028).
   - **Refex:** IT search (Dec-2025), CMD insider-trading penalty, 47% promoter pledge.
   - For the investor, governance explains a large share of valuation dispersion.
5. **Scarcity premium.**
   - The two largest Indian gas businesses (INOXAP, Praxair India) are unlisted.
   - Linde India's 25% float and Ellenbarrie's 22.85% float are the only listed pure-play exposure, so they trade at 89× and 35× P/E.
   - The premium compresses when growth disappoints. Both stocks are near 52-week lows in Oct-2026.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**On-site/tonnage:**
Revenue = Facility fee (₹/month, fixed; recovers capex + return) + Variable charge × volume (power cost per t × t drawn, pass-through) ± escalation (WPI/CPI). Take-or-pay minimum = typically 70–90% of contracted volume [K/E].

**Merchant liquid:**
Revenue = Capacity (TPD) × utilisation × operating days × product mix × realisation (₹/t delivered).
EBITDA = Revenue − kWh/t × tariff × t − freight (₹/t-km × km × t) − plant O&M − SG&A.

**Packaged:**
Revenue = cylinders filled × gas per cylinder × price + cylinder rental + hardgoods.

**Engineering:**
Revenue = Order book × % completion in the period. EBITDA margin 8–20%.

### C.1 Worked example 1 — a 1,800 TPD on-site ASU for a steel mill (Tata-type) [E composite]
- **Capex** ₹800 Cr (₹0.44 Cr/TPD; in the ₹0.40–0.55 Cr/TPD range implied by Ellenbarrie's ₹104.5 Cr/220 TPD and the BHEL ₹481 Cr/1,200 TPD EPC price).
- **Power:**
  - 1,800 t/d × 300 kWh/t = 540 MWh/day.
  - × 365 days = 197 GWh/yr.
  - × ₹6.5/kWh = **₹128 Cr/yr pass-through**.
- **Facility fee:**
  - Target ~15% pre-tax IRR over 20 years needs ~₹128 Cr/yr of cash (annuity factor 6.26).
  - Plus O&M ₹16 Cr.
  - Total ≈ **₹144 Cr/yr**.
- **Revenue** ₹272 Cr (power ₹128 + fee ₹144).
- **EBITDA** ₹128 Cr. **Margin 47% on revenue incl. power; 89% on the fee alone.**
- **Below EBITDA:**
  - Depreciation ₹40 Cr (20 yr) → EBIT ₹88 Cr.
  - Year-1 pre-tax ROCE 11% on gross capex; ~22% on average net book over the life.
- **Two of these (Linde–Tata 2 × 1,800 TPD) ≈ ₹540 Cr revenue and ~₹255 Cr EBITDA a year.** That is ~21% of Linde India's FY26 revenue and **~28% of its FY26 OP** [E]. Linde may have paid less than ₹1,600 Cr and set a lower fee. Treat this as the upper end and flag it.
- **Linde-specific caveat:** a plant purchase (vs build) may come with a lower facility fee if Tata Steel funded part of the cost. **Contract terms are undisclosed** [FLAG].

### C.2 Worked example 2 — a 220 TPD merchant liquid plant (Ellenbarrie Uluberia-II type) [E composite]
- **Capex** ₹104.5 Cr (IPO object) [V-snip].
- **Volume:**

  - Power: 61,600 t × 750 kWh/t × ₹6.5 = ₹30.0 Cr (34% of revenue).

## 4.5 New insights and new primer issues

### 4.5.1 New insights the first pass missed
1. **INOXAP is listing, and it is bigger and more profitable than Linde India.**
   - The DRHP (filed ~30-Sep / 5-Oct-2026) shows FY26 revenue ₹3,034 Cr, EBITDA 51%, PAT ₹914 Cr, net cash ₹850 Cr and 21,180 TPD.
   - On revenue it is #1 (22.4%, CRISIL). Its PAT is **1.7× Linde India's FY26 PAT**.
   - Air Products (Prodair Corporation) is selling ~38.6 m shares. INOX entities sell the rest.
   - **The "only listed pure-plays" scarcity premium that props up Linde India (89×) and Ellenbarrie (35×) faces a direct listed comparable in 2027.** INOXAP's IPO valuation will reset sector multiples. This is the single biggest new thing for the report.
2. **Linde India's FY26 is flattered.**
   - A ₹90 Cr liability reversal sits inside opex. A ₹55.5 Cr JV share (Bellary Oxygen reclassification) sits in "other income".
   - Strip both and FY26 PBT (~₹588 Cr) is **below FY25 (₹614 Cr)**. Q4 FY26 gases-segment PBIT margin fell to 25% (from ~30% for the year).
   - The stock's de-rating since its ₹8,049 high is more about earnings quality than about the EGM.
3. **The Praxair saga has moved to the Supreme Court and to money.**
   - NSE's valuer has put numbers on business "allegedly foregone/gained" (report 16-Mar-2026, not public).
   - The SC admitted Linde India's appeal **without a stay**, but asked to be informed before SEBI acts on the valuation.
   - Since Mar-2026, **Linde India has stopped transacting with Praxair India.** That flow was ₹265 Cr of revenue and ₹171 Cr of purchases in FY25. Expect some revenue/cost dislocation in FY27 and an auditor qualification each year until it is resolved.
4. **Ellenbarrie's 34–39% margin comes with a treasury-heavy, partly opaque balance sheet.**
   - ₹198 Cr is in AIFs (₹155 Cr Level-3), ₹56 Cr is lent against pledged listed shares, and ₹56 Cr more is committed to AIFs, all while carrying ₹180 Cr of borrowings.
   - Other income was 37% of Q1 FY27 PBT. Core P/E ≈ 48×.
   - **A gas-only valuation must strip this out and assess its risk.**
5. **On-site TPD ≠ revenue.** Ellenbarrie is 53% on-site by TPD but only ~15–20% on-site by revenue. INOXAP earns ~₹14 lakh per TPD-year vs Ellenbarrie's ~₹18 lakh. On-site contracts (lease-cum-O&M, customer-paid power) bill a fee, not the gas value.
   - **Never multiply TPD by a merchant price.**
   - Power-cost ratios are mix-dependent too: Linde 16.5% vs Ellenbarrie 21.6%. Linde's BRSR energy includes power its on-site customers pay for directly.
6. **Imported argon has collapsed (₹53 Cr → ₹9 Cr, −83% in FY26)** as Indian ASU capacity has grown (INOXAP merchant +15% in two years; Ellenbarrie +40%). That matches the argon price trough Ellenbarrie described.
   - Argon is the margin kicker for merchant ASUs. A domestic argon surplus is the main near-term margin risk for merchant-heavy players.
   - Helium (~₹394 Cr, Qatar-led) is the only material gas import.
7. **Refex's "industrial gases" tag rested on ₹34.6 Cr of refrigerant revenue (1.4%) that is now zero.**
   - Its economics are a thermal-power services franchise: NTPC 28% + IL&FS TN Power 12% of revenue, ash EBIT 17.6%.
   - On top sits a WTG EPC ramp funded by ₹509 Cr of parent guarantees.
   - The PAT "conflict" was a reporting-basis issue (continuing vs total), not an error.
8. **Steel demand for O₂ is decelerating in the near term.** Hot metal +1.4% and crude steel +3.0% in Q1 FY27 vs +10.7% in FY26 [V PIB].
   - Tonnage additions (Tata 3,600 TPD at Linde; Jai Balaji 325 TPD at Ellenbarrie) are contract-driven and still add revenue.
   - Merchant volumes face a slower industrial backdrop alongside the diesel/logistics shock Refex reported in Q1.
