**Units, conventions and FX**

## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **NMDC's FY26 revenue (₹32,071 Cr consolidated / ₹31,554 Cr "turnover" standalone) is inflated by one-off HR-coil trading for NMDC Steel.**
   - Q4 FY26 standalone: iron ore sales ₹7,280 Cr, **"other sales" ₹3,893 Cr**, "purchase of stock in trade" ₹2,837 Cr [V NMDC Q1 FY27 PPT, Q4 column].
   - Motilal Oswal: "third-party value-added sales of INR30b in 4QFY26… a temporary HR coil trading arrangement to support the working capital requirements of NMDC Steel… a one-off" [V MOSL 1-Jun-26]. CMD on the call: "we stepped in and we were doing trading business" [V transcript].
   - **Ex-trading revenue FY26 ≈ ₹28,420 Cr (+19% on ₹23,910 Cr)** [V MOSL 284.2b vs 239.1b], not "+33–34%". Our primer's "+33%" headline is technically correct but misleading.
   - Margin consequence: screener FY26 OPM 29% (Q4 23%) vs **company iron-ore EBITDA margin ~41–42%** in a clean quarter (Q1 FY27 41%) [V]. Use EBITDA/t (₹1,797–1,842/t FY26) rather than margin % for FY26.
2. **NMDC "price" ≠ NMDC "realisation" ≠ revenue/t. And the price basis itself is disputed.**
   - **List price** (FOR, Bailadila): lump 65.5% Fe, 10–40 mm; fines 64% Fe, −10 mm. Latest found: **w.e.f. 9-Sep-2026 lump ₹5,400/t, fines ₹4,500/t** [V-snip AngelOne/PSUConnect/Investywise]; 8-Aug-26 ₹5,250/₹4,500 [V Sahi]; Jul-26 ₹5,450/₹4,700 [V Sahi]; 5-Apr-26 ₹5,300/₹4,500 [primer]; 22-Oct-25 ₹5,550/₹4,750 [V-snip ETV Bharat].
   - **Basis conflict (flag):** 2026 reports say the base prices **exclude** royalty, DMF, NMET/NMEDT, cess, forest permit fee, transit fee, environmental cess and GST [V Sahi Aug-26; V-snip Sep-26 reports]. The Oct-2025 report says prices **include** royalty, DMF and NMET and exclude the rest [V-snip ETV Bharat]. **Do not compare 2025 and 2026 list prices until the writer checks NMDC's own filing wording.** If the basis changed, the 2026 "cuts" are smaller net increases than they look.
   - **Average domestic realisation** (company KPI, ₹/t of ore sold, all mines, incl. Karnataka): Q1 FY26 5,353 → Q2 4,973 → Q3 4,681 → Q4 4,759 → **Q1 FY27 5,548** (lump 6,540; fines 4,991) [V NMDC PPT]. This is *above* the Bailadila list price because it is gross of royalty and the 150% "additional amount" (which NMDC books as revenue and then as expense — see trap 3).
   - **Broker "NSR"/blended ASP:** FY26 ₹5,657/t (MOSL) or ₹5,701/t (JM); JM's "realisation" ₹6,378/t is just revenue ÷ tonnes and includes HR-coil trading [V]. Name the basis every time.
3. **Royalty accounting at NMDC: the "additional amount" is the single biggest cost line.**
   - NMDC's leases were extended under the 2021 MMDR amendment, which made government companies pay an **"additional amount" equal to 150% of royalty** for extension [K; V NMDC PPT line "Additional Amount (150% of Royalty)"].
   - Q1 FY27: royalty & other levies ₹1,644 Cr + additional amount ₹1,851 Cr = **₹3,495 Cr on ₹6,508 Cr of iron-ore sales (54%)** [V NMDC PPT]. JM: royalty & cess **₹2,204/t in FY26 (34.6% of sales incl. trading; ~40.6% in FY25)** vs operating "other costs" of only ₹786/t and staff ₹386/t [V JM].
   - So NMDC's *mining* cost is ~₹800–1,200/t, but its *statutory* cost is ~₹2,200/t. Any comparison of NMDC margins with a private captive miner or with auctioned-mine operators must specify whether premiums/additional amounts are in cost.
4. **NMDC net cash: three numbers.**
   - Screener: borrowings ₹6,407 Cr (Mar-26) − investments ₹1,372 Cr = "net debt ₹5,035 Cr". **Wrong** — it ignores cash.
   - Screener schedule: cash equivalents **₹11,387 Cr** (Mar-26) [V screener schedule]. Cash − borrowings = **net cash ~₹4,980 Cr** [E]. JM: net cash ₹5,243 Cr (used in its SOTP) [V JM]. MOSL: net debt/equity −0.2 [V].
   - NMDC carried **₹6,407 Cr of borrowings** while holding ₹11,387 Cr cash — consistent with receivables ballooning to **₹9,206 Cr (Mar-26) from ₹3,509 Cr (Mar-24)**, much of it NMDC Steel and RINL dues [V screener schedule; V MOSL: NSL dues to be recovered over 16–18 months; RINL ~7–8 mtpa paid via bill discounting with ~45-day lag]. **Use net cash ~₹5,000 Cr for prose, and flag the receivables.**
5. **NMDC's "contingent liability ₹24,037 Cr" (screener) includes the Karnataka mineral-tax claim of ₹15,785.72 Cr** that NMDC disclosed on 13-Aug-2026 and did not provide for [V Outlook Business 18-Aug-26; V screener]. Whether it crystallises depends on the MMDR Amendment Act 2026 (§7.3). It equals **~26% of NMDC's m-cap** [E].
6. **MOIL: use standalone; the consolidated screener page is stale.** Screener's MOIL "consolidated" view shows only data to FY20 plus a broken TTM (sales ₹976 Cr, PAT ₹74 Cr, P/E 43.1) [V screener]. The standalone page (P/E 14.8, FY26 sales ₹1,470 Cr, PAT ₹267 Cr) is the right basis [V screener]. The new 51% JV (MOIL MPSMCL Mining) is pre-revenue.
7. **Sandur: consolidated ≠ mining.** Consolidated FY26 revenue ₹5,088 Cr (screener) / total income ₹5,163 Cr (company) includes **Arjas Steel's SBQ steel (~₹3,141 Cr FY26 revenue [V-snip])** at a thin margin. Standalone (mines + ferroalloys + coke/power) FY26 ≈ revenue ₹2,011 Cr, PAT ₹543 Cr [primer]; Q1 FY27 standalone total income ₹554 Cr, **EBITDA ₹237 Cr (43%)**, PAT ₹161 Cr vs consolidated ₹1,390 / ₹358 (26%) / ₹228 Cr [V Sandur PPT]. The FY25 consolidated base includes Arjas only from 11-Nov-2024, so **FY26 growth (+62%) is mostly an acquisition effect**, not organic.
   - **Sandur "net-debt-free" is standalone.** Consolidated: gross debt/equity 0.27 at Jun-26 on shareholders' funds ₹3,481 Cr (≈ ₹940 Cr gross debt [E]) with consolidated cash & equivalents ₹565 Cr → **consolidated net debt ~₹375 Cr (Jun-26)** [E]. Screener Mar-26: borrowings ₹999 Cr, cash ₹88 Cr, investments ₹281 Cr [V].
8. **Ashapura: "revenue" is ~84% Guinea bauxite trading-style export revenue.** Q1 FY27 Guinea turnover ~₹1,360 Cr of ₹1,616 Cr (84%), Guinea EBITDA ₹163 Cr; India business EBITDA therefore only ~₹26 Cr [V call; E]. Guinea EBITDA is quoted **per tonne in US$ ($6.3/t Q1 FY27; $5.9/t Q4 FY26)** [V]. **PAT basis:** screener Q1 FY27 PAT ₹108 Cr vs company ₹115.34 Cr (EPS ₹12.07) — screener likely shows profit after minorities differently; use the company figure in prose and screener in tables.
9. **"Manganese ore" volumes are not comparable across MOIL and Sandur.** MOIL reports in lakh tonnes of all grades (FY26 production ~19.07 LT, of which ~1.3 mt "prime"/average grade and ~0.5 mt low grades) [V MOIL meet; V-snip FY26]; Sandur's manganese ore is low-phosphorus, semi-mechanised, ~0.6 mtpa cap, and an increasing share is consumed captively in its own ferroalloy furnaces [V PPT]. Realisation: MOIL Q1 FY27 NSR ₹9,749/t vs Sandur ₹7,954/t — different grade mixes, not efficiency.
10. **India "iron ore production" has three bases.** IBM/Ministry of Mines (official, lagged), BigMint estimates (FY26 **313 mt**, merchant 188 + captive 125) [V BigMint 15-Jun-26], and company sums. Steel-making iron-ore *requirement* ≈ 1.5–1.6 t ore per t of hot metal [primer; K]. Never mix "EC capacity" (521 mt) with production.

- **Four members, three unrelated businesses, one listed giant.**

  - **Ashapura** is not an iron/manganese story at all: Indian bentonite/specialty clays (~16% of revenue) plus a **Guinea bauxite export** business (~84%) shipping to China.

### 1.2 Value chains

1. **Iron ore (NMDC model; merchant, PSU, Chhattisgarh + Karnataka)**
   - Open-cast mechanised mining on the Bailadila range (Kirandul complex: Deposits 14, 11, 14NMZ; Bacheli: Deposits 5, 10, 11A; new Deposit 4 and 13 via NMDC-CMDC JV) and in Karnataka (Donimalai; Kumaraswamy) [V MOSL; K].
   - Crush → screen into lump/fines → rail from the pithead on the **Kirandul–Kottavalasa (KK) line** to Visakhapatnam/steel plants (the binding constraint), or now via **slurry to Nagarnar** (concentrate → 2 mtpa pellet plant) [V-snip].
   - Customers: integrated mills (RINL ~7–8 mtpa, NMDC Steel, JSW, AM/NS India, SAIL in deficit), DRI/sponge-iron and pellet makers; Karnataka ore historically had to be sold through **e-auctions** under the Supreme Court's Karnataka regime and the 50 mt/yr state cap [V BigMint; K].
2. **Captive iron ore (context)** — Tata Steel (42 mt FY26), SAIL (36 mt), JSW (18 mt), Jindal (6 mt) mine for their own furnaces; captive production was flat at 125 mt in FY26 while merchant rose 13% to 188 mt [V BigMint]. The 2026 MMDR Act **removes the 50% cap** on captive mines' open-market sales [V FPJ] — more potential merchant supply.
3. **Manganese ore → ferro-alloy → steel** — MOIL's ~10 mines (underground Balaghat, Kandri, Munsar, Chikla…; opencast Dongri Buzurg) [primer; V meet] sell graded ore monthly-priced to ~central-India SiMn/FeMn smelters; imported 44% ore lands at Visakhapatnam/Paradip/Kakinada and must pay **~₹1,500/t extra logistics** to reach central India [V MOIL meet]. Smelters blend high-grade import with Indian medium/low grade to the 33–37% Mn furnace feed [V].
4. **Integrated Mn/Fe → ferroalloy → coke → steel (Sandur model)** — captive ore at cost, own coke and WHRB power, own SBQ bar mill. One tonne of ore climbs from ~₹3,000–8,000/t (ore) to ~₹71,000/t (alloy) or ~₹74,000/t (SBQ steel) [V realisations]; the margin migrates upwards only if conversion is efficient.
5. **Bentonite (Ashapura India)** — Kutch (Gujarat) mines → drying/activation/grinding → domestic foundries, pellet plants, drillers; exports to the Middle East/Europe hit by freight in 2026 [V call].
6. **Guinea bauxite (Ashapura)** — open-cast mines near Boffa → river barging → transhipment at Boffa port (5 → **8 mtpa**) and GSM jetty (6 → **10 mtpa** by Q4 FY27) → Capesize to China → Shandong refineries [V call]. Pricing CIF China (index ~**$72/t**, up from ~$61 on freight) [V call].

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **At the lease, not the plant.** NMDC's mining cash cost at Bailadila is **~₹800–810/t** (down from ~₹1,000) [V call; V MOSL] against an average realisation of ₹4,700–5,550/t. The gap is resource rent, split between **the state (~₹2,200/t of royalty, 150% additional amount, DMF, NMET, cess)** [V JM] and the shareholder (EBITDA ~₹1,800/t) [V JM/MOSL].
- **Statutory take ≥ shareholder take.** In FY26 NMDC paid ~₹11,082 Cr of royalty & cess vs EBITDA ₹9,260 Cr (screener OP; JM ₹92,595 mn, identical) [V JM: royalty & cess ₹110,823 mn]. Include dividends to the Government (60.79%) and taxes and the state's share of NMDC's rent is ~65–70% [E].
- **Manganese:** value accrues to whoever owns medium-grade ore close to central-India smelters (logistics moat ~₹1,500/t) [V], but the ceiling is the landed import price.
- **Ashapura Guinea:** value is captured in logistics (port capacity, barging, freight) not geology — EBITDA only ~$6/t on a ~$60–72/t product [V].

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets
- **Steel is ~98% of iron-ore demand and ~90% of manganese demand** [primer; K].
  - FY26 crude steel 168.4 mt (+10.7%); finished steel consumption 163.7 mt (+7.6%); India a net *exporter* of finished steel (6.60 mt exports vs 6.52 mt imports; imports −31.7%) [V BigMint/JPC]. The steel safeguard duty on flat products (12% provisional Apr-2025, later definitive) [K] helped domestic mills.
  - Q1 FY27: crude steel 42.06 mt (**+3.0%**) — a sharp slowdown — while consumption grew 8.3% [V-snip Ministry of Steel]. April-2026 crude 14.09 mt (+5.8%) [V-snip].
  - Installed crude steel capacity ~221.9 mtpa (Jun-26) → NSP target 300 mtpa by 2030-31 [V-snip].
  - Iron-ore intensity: ~1.5–1.6 t ore per t hot metal [primer]; FY27 ore requirement ~300 mt [V BigMint].
  - Mn intensity: ~11 mt Mn ore for 300 mt steel → ~37 kg ore/t steel [V MOIL meet; E].
- **Iron-ore demand by route:** BF-BOF (~45%) and DRI/induction (~55% of output by route) [K]. DRI needs lump/high-grade pellets; NMDC is developing **67% Fe DR-grade pellets** (66.5% achieved; premium **$20–30/t**) [V call].
- **Seaborne/China:** India's export window opens only when Chinese demand and the 62% Fe price are high enough to cover the **30% export duty on ≥58% Fe lump/fines** (nil on <58% and pellets) [V CMD: "With a 30% export duty, it doesn't make too much of a sense"; K for the slabs]. 2026 exports are at multi-year lows.
- **Manganese:** central-India SiMn/FeMn smelters (Nagpur–Raipur–Vizag belt); demand follows steel but ferro-alloy margins swing with power cost and Chinese SiMn exports [primer; K].
- **Bentonite:** foundries (auto castings), pelletising (iron-ore pellets need ~0.5–1% bentonite [K]), drilling, civil, pet litter [primer].
- **Bauxite:** Chinese alumina refinery feed; four new Chinese refineries → +20–30 mt bauxite demand [V Ashapura call]; alumina prices "under pressure" [V].

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 The MMDR framework (pre-2026)
- **MMDR Act 1957**, amended **2015** (12-Jan-2015 ordinance; Act Mar-2015) [K]:
  - Grant of major-mineral leases only by **auction**; 50-year lease terms; **DMF** (30% of royalty for old leases, 10% for auctioned) and **NMET** (2% of royalty) created [K].
  - **Merchant (non-captive) leases granted before 2015 expired on 31-Mar-2020**; captive leases run to **31-Mar-2030** [K]. The 2020 lapse put ~300+ leases (incl. ~24 working iron-ore mines in Odisha) up for auction; winners bid premiums of ~90–150% of ASP [K; BigMint "above 100%" V].
- **2021 amendment:** allowed captive mines to sell up to 50% of output after meeting end-use; **government companies' leases extended on payment of an "additional amount" = 150% of royalty** (iron ore) [K; V NMDC P&L line]; transfer of statutory clearances to new lessees [K].
- **Royalty:** iron ore 15% of IBM ASP (state-wise monthly) [V BigMint]; bauxite metallurgical grade linked to LME aluminium [K]; manganese ~5% [K-verify].
- **Export duty on iron ore** [K except where marked]:
  - Pre-May-2022: 30% on lump/fines ≥58% Fe; nil on <58%; pellets nil.
  - **22-May-2022:** raised to **50% on all lump/fines grades and 45% on pellets** (inflation-control package with steel inputs).
  - **19-Nov-2022:** withdrawn — back to **nil below 58% Fe and on pellets; 30% on ≥58% Fe** lump/fines.
  - Status Jun-2026: CMD confirms "With a 30% export duty, it doesn't make too much of a sense" for NMDC's high-grade ore [V call].
- **Karnataka regime:** Supreme Court mining ban (2011) → Category A/B/C mines; production ceiling (now **50 mt/yr**) and sale through e-auction under a Monitoring Committee; downhill pipe conveyor directions for Ballari (Sandur became the first private miner in Ballari/Vijayanagara to set one up, Apr-2026) [V BigMint; V Sandur PPT; K].

### 7.2 The Supreme Court's 8:1 ruling and state taxes (2024)
- **25-Jul-2024, *Mineral Area Development Authority v. Steel Authority of India*:** nine-judge bench, 8:1 (Justice B.V. Nagarathna dissenting) [K]: royalty is **not** a tax; Entry 50 List II lets states tax mineral rights and Entry 49 lets them tax mineral-bearing land; MMDR does not limit that power [V-snip; K].
- **14-Aug-2024:** demands allowed for transactions from **1-Apr-2005**; payable in **12 years of instalments from 1-Apr-2026**; interest and penalty for demands before 25-Jul-2024 waived [V-snip EY/LiveLaw]. Review petitions dismissed (Oct-2024) [V-snip The Week]; Centre filed a curative petition (date unverified) [V-snip Outlook].
- **State responses** [mostly K; flag]:

### 7.3 The MMDR Amendment Act, 2026 (Act 20 of 2026)
- Passed by Lok Sabha ~12-Aug and Rajya Sabha **13-Aug-2026**; **President's assent 17-Aug-2026** [V SCC Online; V-snip FPJ/simpliance].
- **Section 9D (new):** "State Governments cannot impose any tax, cess or other levy on mineral rights or mineral-bearing lands" except subject to conditions the Centre prescribes; any such levy **not deposited/recovered before commencement is "deemed invalid at all material times"**, notwithstanding any court order; amounts already recovered are **not refundable** [V SCC Online]. Section 13 clause (ta) lets the Centre make rules on conditions [V].
- Other provisions [V FPJ, secondary]: **removes the 50% cap on captive-mine sales**; one-time lease-area expansion for deep-seated minerals (>200 m) — up to 30% (composite licence) / 10% (mining lease); critical/strategic minerals (lithium, graphite, nickel, cobalt, gold, silver) can be added to a lease **without extra payment**; other added minerals pay an amount equal to royalty (+ premium for auctioned mines); sale of old **dumps** permitted; **NMET → National Mineral Exploration and Development Trust**; an authority to regulate **mineral exchanges**.
- **Commencement:** by notification; **none found by 22-Oct-2026** (flag). Mines Minister G. Kishan Reddy said no fiscal-impact assessment was made [V Outlook].
- **Sequencing risk:** If the Act commences before Karnataka's bill receives assent, Karnataka's retrospective tax is likely void; if Karnataka's bill gets assent first, NMDC could owe the full ~₹15,786 Cr in 12 instalments (~₹1,316 Cr/yr ≈ 18% of FY26 PAT) [V Outlook citing ET legal analysis; E arithmetic]. Expect constitutional litigation by states either way [E].
- **Why it matters for each member:** NMDC (Karnataka now; Chhattisgarh if it legislates), Sandur (Karnataka), MOIL (Maharashtra/MP — no state tax found), Ashapura (Gujarat bentonite/bauxite — no Gujarat levy found; the Guinea business is outside Indian law). Note the Act does not touch states' powers over *minor* minerals [V-snip].
- **Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act 2026** — decriminalisation provisions relating to MMDR commenced **1-Aug-2026** [V-snip simpliance]. Detail not read.

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)
1. **Statutory take, not geology, sets Indian mining margins.** NMDC mines at ~₹810/t but pays ~₹2,200/t to the state (royalty 15% + 150% additional amount + DMF + NMET + cesses) [V]; auctioned Odisha mines pay premiums >100% of ASP [V BigMint]. Any change — the 2026 Act's Section 9D, Karnataka's retrospective tax, royalty revisions — moves profit more than a year of cost-cutting.
2. **India is now long iron ore, so price follows China and the export duty.** Production (313 mt) exceeds requirement (~300 mt) and EC capacity (521 mt) is far higher [V]. With a 30% duty on ≥58% Fe exports and China cutting steel, surplus must clear domestically — NMDC's 2026 price cuts and its inventory build (H1 sales flat) are the symptoms [V].
3. **Evacuation is the volume constraint.** NMDC has ore for decades; it is limited by the KK rail line (~28–30 mtpa from Bailadila), now relieved by the 15 mtpa slurry pipeline (Sep-26) and the Kirandul–Jagdalpur doubling (to ~40, later 60 mtpa) [V]. The 100 mtpa target needs ₹7,000–10,000 Cr/yr of capex — NMDC is turning from a cash cow into a capex story [V].
4. **Manganese is import-priced.** India imports ~5.5 mt of Mn ore a year vs MOIL's ~1.9 mt [V]. MOIL's price = landed import parity + ~₹1,500/t inland freight advantage [V]; its profit swings 30–70% on 5–10% price moves [V screener].
5. **Government ownership defines capital allocation for 87% of m-cap.** NMDC (60.79%) and MOIL (53%+ GoI; 64.68% incl. states) [V] pay ~41% of profit out [V], carry policy mandates (NMDC Steel trading support, RINL supply on bill discounting) [V], and trade at a PSU discount.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Miner:** EBITDA = Sales tonnes × (Realisation/t − Statutory levies/t − Cash cost/t) ± inventory change ± other businesses.
- Statutory levies/t (iron ore, extended government lease) ≈ ASP × 15% × (1 + 1.5 + 0.30 + 0.02) ≈ **42% of ASP** [K formula; V NMDC P&L lines; E].

### C.1 Worked example 1 — one tonne of NMDC ore, Q1 FY27 [V PPT; E per-tonne]
| Per tonne sold (11.73 mt) | ₹/t |
|---|---|
| Average domestic realisation | 5,548 |
| Royalty & other levies | −1,402 |
| Additional amount (150% of royalty) | −1,578 |
| Operational expenses (incl. stock-build tonnes) | −1,535 |
| Pellet job-work | −211 |
| Inventory credit (stock adjustment) | +933 |
| Other sales, net (₹287 Cr) | +245 |
| **EBITDA** | **≈ 2,400** (₹2,817 Cr) |
- Strip out the inventory credit and EBITDA ≈ ₹1,470/t — closer to the FY26 run-rate of ₹1,800/t [E]. Statutory levies (₹2,980/t) > operating cost (₹1,535/t) > EBITDA.

### C.2 Worked example 2 — MOIL, Q1 FY27 [V; E]
- 3.69 LT × ₹9,749/t = ₹360 Cr ore revenue (+ ~₹11 Cr EMD/FeMn/power) = ₹370.9 Cr revenue from operations [V].
