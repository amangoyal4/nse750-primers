## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Oil year vs fiscal year.**
   - SEA import data run Nov–Oct; company volumes run Apr–Mar. OY2024-25 (Nov-24 to Oct-25) mostly overlaps FY26 (Apr-25 to Mar-26) but is not the same.
   - The SEA headline "vegetable oil" imports include **non-edible oils** (~0.3 mt/yr). "Edible oil" imports are slightly lower. Example: Nov-25 to Sep-26, **edible 151.64 LT vs vegetable 154.64 LT** [V FPJ/SEA].
2. **Crude vs refined duty, BCD vs effective duty.**
   - The headline number in news is the **basic customs duty (BCD)**. The effective rate adds **AIDC (Agriculture Infrastructure & Development Cess, 5% on crude oils)** and **SWS (Social Welfare Surcharge, 10% of BCD+AIDC)**.
   - Example after 24-Sep-2026: crude palm BCD 5% → **effective 11%** (5 + 5 + 1.0) [V BT]. Refined palm BCD 27.5% → **effective ~30.25%** (27.5 + 10% SWS) [E, arithmetic; refined oils carry no AIDC].
   - **The crude–refined differential is what matters for domestic refiners**, not the level. The Sep-2026 cut kept the effective differential at **~19.25 points** for palm and soy (11% vs 30.25%) and also for sunflower (5.5% vs ~24.75%) [E arithmetic].
3. **AWL "revenue" includes commodity trading and pass-through; volume is the real denominator.**
   - FY26 revenue ₹74,731 Cr on **6.85 MMT** total volume [V CARE] = ~₹1.09 lakh/t average.
   - Since Q1 FY27 AWL reports **Food & FMCG on a margin basis** and **Edible Oil and Industry Essentials on a ₹/tonne basis** [V AWL PPT].
   - AWL also **"normalises" EBITDA** by regrouping commodity-derivative gains/losses from other income/other expenses into EBITDA. Q1 FY26 reported EBITDA ₹366 Cr becomes **normalised ₹519 Cr**; Q4 FY26 goes from ₹524 → ₹628 Cr [V AWL PPT].
   - **Screener's OP = AWL's reported (un-normalised) EBITDA**, so screener understates AWL's EBITDA in quarters with derivative gains booked in other income. FY26: screener OP ₹2,131 Cr vs **company segment-EBITDA total ₹2,343 Cr** [V].
4. **Patanjali FY26 PAT (₹1,815 Cr) is flattered by tax credits.**
   - Screener tax rate: Q2 FY26 −2%, Q3 −63%, Q4 −122%; FY26 −34% [V screener]. FY26 PBT was ₹1,354 Cr, *lower* than FY25's ₹1,726 Cr. At a normal 25% tax, FY26 PAT ≈ **₹1,016 Cr** [E].
   - Screener's headline **P/E 17.9×** therefore flatters the stock. On normalised TTM PAT (TTM PBT ₹1,559 Cr × 0.75 ≈ ₹1,169 Cr) the P/E is **~33×** [E]. **Use both, and say which.**
5. **Patanjali's segment definitions changed twice.**
   - The "Food & Other FMCG" segment was reclassified into **"FMCG" in Q2 FY26**, now including **HPC (home & personal care)**, which was integrated w.e.f. **1-Nov-2024** via a ₹1,100 Cr slump sale from Patanjali Ayurved [V Q2 FY26 PPT; V transcript].
   - The edible-oil segment now has sub-lines: **edible oils 93.21%, oil palm plantation 6.11%, oleochemicals 0.68% of segment revenue (FY26)** [V AR].
   - Old disclosures had "Edible Oils", "Food & FMCG", "Wind power" and "Others". Pre-FY26 segment series are not like-for-like.
6. **Patanjali's "EBITDA" has three numbers.**
   - Company "operating EBITDA" FY26 ~₹1,932 Cr (primer, "ex-exceptional"; margin ~4.8%) vs **screener OP ₹1,762 Cr (4.4%)** [V screener; primer].
   - Segment EBITDA: **Edible Oils ₹755.05 Cr (2.58%; 38.15% of total)**, FMCG **61.13% of total** [V AR]. That implies a segment total near ₹1,980 Cr before unallocables [E].
   - Use screener in peer tables and company figures in prose, and label each.
7. **"Branded share" is a company definition, not an audited metric.**
   - Patanjali says "branded ~75% of edible-oil segment sales" (FY26 AR) and ~76% (Q2 FY26) [V]; the primer says ~85% (from a Q3 FY26 press summary).
   - AWL does not publish a branded-share number but describes Fortune's "single-largest market share in the organised domestic edible oil industry" [V CARE].
   - Gokul's B2C share is ~10% (FY26; read from a chart, label uncertain) [V-chart, E].
8. **Market share bases differ.**
   - AWL's e-com/q-com market shares (edible oil ~29%, nuggets ~46%, besan ~28%) are **channel shares on e-commerce/quick-commerce platforms only** [V AWL PPT], not national shares.
   - Nutrela's "~40% share" is of **branded soya chunks** [V transcript/AR].
   - No primary source gives a national edible-oil market share for any player. Say "single-largest" for Fortune; do not invent a %.
9. **Patanjali oil-palm "allocated area" ≠ planted area.**
   - **6.63 lakh ha allocated** (factory zones assigned by 12 states) vs **1,10,722 ha under cultivation (Mar-26)** / **1,15,861 ha (30-Jun-26)** [V AR; V transcript].
   - India's national oil-palm coverage was **6.20 lakh ha** (Nov-2025, PIB) [V]. Patanjali's planted area is therefore ~18% of the national total [E], and its allocated zone is larger than the whole country's current planted area.
10. **Gokul's EBITDA basis.**
    - Company "EBITDA (including other income)" FY26 = **₹676.49 Cr (2.81%)** on its quarterly P&L slide, but **₹716.20 Cr** on its annual P&L slide (revenue − RM − employee − other expenses, which seems to include other income) [V PPT].

### 1.2 Value chain (inputs → customer)

1. **Import-refine-pack (port-based) — the dominant model for all three.**
   - Crude palm/soy/sunflower is bought in USD on CIF India terms, often from Singapore trading desks. Gokul's Maurigo/Riya Pte and Wilmar for AWL play this role [V CRISIL; V CARE].
   - The oil is shipped to Kandla/Mundra (Gujarat), Haldia (WB), Kakinada/Krishnapatnam (AP), Mangalore and Chennai, and stored in port tank farms. Gokul storage: Gandhidham 96,500 t, Haldia 42,500 t, Krishnapatnam 45,000 t, Mangalore 13,500 t [V PPT; units shown as "MTPD" on the slide but are tonnes].
   - Refining (degumming, neutralisation, bleaching, deodorisation; physical refining for palm) and fractionation (palm → olein + stearin) follow. Then packing into pouches, PET, jars and tins (the 9 standard sizes since Sep-2026), and distribution.
   - AWL's **Mundra refinery, 5,000 TPD, is "India's largest single-location refinery"** [V AWL Q2 update]. **AWL group refining 18,510 TPD, crushing 7,775 TPD (Mar-26)** [V CARE]. **Gokul refining 6,250 TPD, seed crushing 3,200 TPD** [V CRISIL]. Patanjali: **26 facilities incl. 7 port-based refineries, 7 integrated plants, 3 seed-crushing, 3 palm-crushing, 6 oil-palm mills** [V AR/Q2 FY26 PPT]. Patanjali's TPD capacity was not found in the documents fetched.
2. **Domestic seed crush (inland).** Soybean (MP/Maharashtra), mustard (Rajasthan), groundnut/cotton (Gujarat). Seed → expeller/solvent extraction → crude oil + de-oiled cake (DOC, ~80% of soybean by weight, exported or sold to feed). Crush margins depend on the MSP floor for seed and the meal export price. Patanjali (ex-Ruchi) and AWL are big soy crushers; Gokul has a 500 TPD mustard plant [primer] within 3,200 TPD of seed processing.
3. **Oil-palm plantation → FFB → mill → CPO (domestic, nascent).**
   - The farmer grows on own land under a state-allotted **factory zone**. The processor must buy FFB at a government-set price linked to CPO (the **viability price** under NMEO-OP protects the farmer) [V PIB].
   - Patanjali FY26: **6,37,919 t FFB processed → 1,17,881 t CPO (OER 18.48%)**; 6 mills with 1.15 mt FFB capacity; plantation revenue ₹1,792.58 Cr incl. stock transfer [V AR].
   - Gokul: **550 ha planted FY25–26; +900 ha in FY27** (AP) [V PPT].
4. **Downstream adjacencies.** Food staples (atta, rice, pulses, sugar, besan, soya chunks) ride the same distribution network: AWL Food & FMCG; Patanjali FMCG incl. biscuits, ghee and HPC. Oleochemicals/specialty fats (AWL Industry Essentials, Patanjali oleochem 0.68% of segment); biodiesel (Gokul).

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

- **Population × per-capita × substitution from loose to packed.** Per-capita intake roughly doubled in rural India in 18 years (5.76 → 10.58 kg) [V PIB].
- **Price elasticity is low in aggregate but high across oils.** Buyers switch rapidly between palm, soy and sunflower on spreads.
  - Example: palm became pricier than soft oils in 2025 → palm's import share fell to a record-low 47% (OY24-25); soy imports +59% [V-snip].
  - Apr-2026: sunflower 434 kt (vs 211 kt) while palm hit a 12-month low of 514 kt [V UkrAgroConsult].
  - Sep-2026: crude sunflower 1.05 LT vs 2.72 LT a year ago [V FPJ].
- **Festive season (Sep–Nov)** lifts demand — the explicit rationale for the 23-Sep-2026 duty cut [V BT].
- **HoReCa / institutional** is ~36% of consumption (100 − 64% household, IMARC) [V AR; E]. AWL HoReCa volume +27% (Q1 FY27), +21% (Q2) [V PPT/update].
- **Channel shift:**
  - **Alternate channels** (e-com, q-com, modern trade): AWL alternate channels revenue +21–33%; q-com value +64% (Q1 FY27); AWL edible-oil share on e-com/q-com ~29% [V PPT].
  - Patanjali MT + e-com + q-com ≈ **15% of revenue, target 20% in 18 months** [V transcript].
- **Pack standardisation (Legal Metrology SoP amendment, PIB 6-Jun-2026):**
  - Nine standard sizes — 200 ml/g, 500 ml/g, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 15, 20 L/kg; weight must be shown on volume packs; 3-month transition → **effective ~Sep-2026** [V-snip PIB; V AWL "effective September 2026"].
  - It ends "grammage play" (e.g. 810 g "1-litre" packs) — AWL calls it "a positive structural development … level playing field" [V AWL Q2 update]. **Branded leaders benefit at the expense of regional packers that relied on odd sizes** [E].
- **Exports:** branded exports (AWL +86% Q1 FY27 volume, +59% Q2 [V]); Gokul exports 18% of Q1 FY27 revenue, driven by biodiesel to the EU [V PPT]; Patanjali exports ₹187.77 Cr FY26, 37 countries [V AR].

### 4.1 Key inputs and their price drivers
- **CPO** (≈ half of import tonnage). Driven by:
  - Indonesian/Malaysian output (El Niño risk);
  - **Indonesia's biodiesel mandate (B35 → B40 in 2025 → B50 transition from 1-Jul-2026, full rollout slated 1-Oct-2026)** — B50 absorbs ~1.5 mt of extra CPO, cutting Indonesian export availability by ~16.7% per one analysis [V-snip];
  - Indonesian export levies;
  - crude oil (POGO spread — palm-oil minus gasoil; when gasoil is high, biodiesel blending pulls palm out of food);
  - the ringgit.

- **Why the September 2026 cut matters:**
  - The cut lowers landed cost by ~5.5 points for palm/soy and ~11 points for sunflower. That supports volumes, but it triggers **inventory losses on stock bought at the old duty** — Q3 FY27 risk [E].

- **Agri cess (AIDC)** — introduced in Budget 2021 to fund farm infrastructure. On crude edible oils it is currently 5%, which is why "nil" BCD still means 5.5% effective [V BT].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **1986–2000s: Ruchi Soya's rise.** Ruchi (Indore) became India's largest soya crusher and edible-oil company (Nutrela, Mahakosh, Ruchi Gold) [K]. Adani–Wilmar JV formed 1999; Fortune launched 2000 [primer].
2. **1990s "Yellow Revolution"** (Technology Mission on Oilseeds) → near self-sufficiency, then WTO-era tariff cuts → import surge [V PIB].
3. **2008–2016: commodity super-cycle and over-expansion.** Ruchi's FY12 revenue ₹30,270 Cr on a 2.4% margin [V screener 2010–19 series]. Ruchi's FY16 loss ₹1,274 Cr; FY18 operating loss −₹5,107 Cr (−42% OPM, write-offs) [V screener]. Debt ~₹12,000 Cr [primer].
4. **2017–2019: insolvency.** NCLT admitted Ruchi on 15-Dec-2017; Adani Wilmar and Patanjali bid; Adani withdrew (2019); Patanjali's ₹4,350 Cr plan was approved 24-Jul-2019 [primer; K]. FY20 "other income" ₹7,501 Cr = debt write-back → FY20 PAT ₹7,672 Cr [V screener standalone].
5. **2020–2022: price spike.** CPO from ~$600/t (mid-2020) to ~$1,900+/t (Mar-2022) [K]. India cut duty to near-zero; Indonesia banned exports 28-Apr to 23-May-2022 [K]. Patanjali FPO in Mar-2022 at ₹650 (₹4,300 Cr) [primer; K price]. **Adani Wilmar IPO 8-Feb-2022 (₹3,600 Cr, ₹230/share)** [primer; K price]; the stock spiked to ~₹878 in Apr-2022 [K].
6. **2023: deflation.** Prices fell ~40–50%; inventory losses → **AWL Q1–Q2 FY24 net losses (−₹79 Cr, −₹131 Cr)**; FY24 PAT only ₹148 Cr [V screener]. Patanjali Q1 FY24 OPM 2.2% [V screener].
7. **2024: duty hike (Sep) → inventory gains.** AWL FY25 PAT ₹1,226 Cr; Patanjali FY25 OPM 6% [V screener]. Adani announced its exit on 30-Dec-2024 [K/primer].
8. **2025: duty cut (May) + the Adani unwind.** Jan-2025 OFS (~13.5%, ~₹4,855 Cr), Jul-2025 20% to Wilmar's Lence at ₹275 (~₹7,150 Cr), Nov-2025 final 13% + 7% → Adani fully out; realisation ~₹15,707 Cr [V-snip]. Patanjali's 2:1 bonus (Sep-2025). Patanjali's FMCG reclassification (Q2 FY26).
9. **2026: war-inflation windfall + policy whiplash.** West Asia war (Feb–Apr), B50, monsoon deficit, rupee record low → Q1 FY27 margin peaks. Standard packs (Jun → Sep-2026). **Duty cut 24-Sep-2026.** Record Malaysian stocks (3.51 mt, Sep-2026) [V].

**Cycle signature:** profits peak when prices *rise* (inventory gains), not when they are high. AWL PAT: FY23 ₹582 Cr → FY24 ₹148 Cr → FY25 ₹1,226 Cr → FY26 ₹1,045 Cr [V screener]. Gokul is the exception — steady compounding: FY21 ₹45 Cr → FY26 ₹369 Cr [V screener].

- **The domestic refiner's protection is ≈ ₹19,000/t** — far above its ~₹3,000–5,000/t EBITDA. The differential, not efficiency, is what keeps imported refined oil out. **A cut in the refined duty to the crude level would expose the industry's idle capacity** [E].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **The duty differential, not the duty level, decides who refines.**
   - Since May-2025 the crude–refined effective gap has been ~19.25 points (it survived the Sep-2026 cut). It is worth ≈ ₹19,000/t of protection [E].
   - Result: refined imports collapsed to 5% of the mix [V], and AWL's edible-oil volume grew 6% in FY26 [V CARE].
   - **The single policy variable a writer must track.** The leak is Nepal (SAFTA, 736 kt) [V].
2. **Inventory and hedging swing the P&L more than demand does.**
   - Volumes grow ~2–5%/yr. EBITDA/t swings ±30–50% (AWL edible oil ₹5,030/t in Q1 FY27, +30%; company PBILDT/t ₹3,425 FY26 vs ₹3,779 FY25) [V].
   - AWL PAT went ₹148 → ₹1,226 → ₹1,045 Cr over FY24–26 [V].
   - Rising prices = gains (Q1 FY27); falling prices or duty cuts = losses (FY24; potentially Q3 FY27).
3. **Import dependence (~56%) ties Indian margins to Jakarta, Kuala Lumpur, Buenos Aires, the Black Sea, crude oil and the rupee.**
   - India buys ~20% of world trade in the three main oils [V UkrAgroConsult].
   - 2026 combined war-driven freight (+100% at the peak), B50, a record-low rupee and a weak monsoon [V].
4. **The value is migrating to adjacencies: FMCG (Patanjali), staples (AWL), plantation (Patanjali), biodiesel/export (Gokul).**
   - Patanjali's FMCG = 27.6% of revenue but 61.13% of FY26 EBITDA [V AR].
   - AWL's Food & FMCG is still at 6% margins and only 9% of revenue [V].
   - The market's multiple follows the mix: EV/revenue Patanjali ~1.0× vs AWL ~0.3× [E].
5. **Working-capital financing is the hidden balance sheet.**
   - LC/acceptance-funded imports make screener "borrowings" misleading. AWL screener ₹1,109 Cr vs CARE net debt ₹4,425 Cr; Gokul non-fund-based LC limits ₹3,125 Cr vs ₹589 Cr borrowings [V].
   - ROCE comparisons need a common basis.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Edible-oil refiner:**
EBITDA = Σ_oils [ Volume (t) × ( Realisation/t − Landed crude cost/t − Conversion & packing/t − Selling & distribution/t ) ] ± Inventory revaluation ± Hedge P&L

where Landed crude cost/t = CIF $/t × ₹/$ × (1 + effective duty) + port & storage.

**Group (AWL / Patanjali):** EBITDA = Edible oil (tonnes × EBITDA/t) + Food/FMCG (revenue × margin) + Industry Essentials/plantation (tonnes × EBITDA/t) − Unallocable.

### C.1 Worked example 1 — one tonne of refined palmolein, AWL-type, Q1 FY27 vs "normal" [E composite]
| Line | Normal (₹/t) | Q1 FY27-type (₹/t) |
|---|---|---|
| CIF CPO $1,050 × ₹94.9 | 99,645 | 99,645 |
| Duty 16.5% (pre-Sep-2026) | 16,441 | 16,441 |
| Port/storage/losses | 1,500 | 1,500 |
| **Landed crude** | **1,17,586** | **1,17,586** |
| Refining + packing | 3,000 | 3,000 |
| Freight-out, A&P, trade margin to distributor | 6,500 | 6,500 |
| Realisation (ex-factory, net of GST) | 1,30,600 | 1,32,100 (prices rose after stock bought) |
| **EBITDA/t** | **~3,500** | **~5,000** |
- The ₹1,500/t difference is essentially inventory gain. AWL edible oil went from ₹3,870 → ₹5,030/t [V; E]. On 0.98 MMT per quarter that is ≈ ₹115 Cr of extra EBITDA [E].

### C.2 Worked example 2 — AWL FY26 group reconciliation [V CARE/PPT; E]
- Volume 6.85 MMT × PBILDT/t ₹3,425 = **₹2,346 Cr** ≈ company normalised EBITDA ₹2,343 Cr [V].
- Segments: edible oil ₹1,884 Cr + F&FMCG ₹315 Cr + IE ₹428 Cr − unallocable ₹284 Cr = ₹2,343 Cr [V PPT].
- Edible oil ~4.5 mt [E] → ~₹4,190/t; IE ~1.6 mt → ~₹2,700/t; F&FMCG ₹315 Cr on ~₹6,700 Cr revenue (9% of ₹74,731 Cr) → ~4.7% [E].

### C.3 Worked example 3 — Patanjali FY26 segment build [V AR; E]
- Edible oils: ₹29,314 Cr × 2.58% = ₹755 Cr (38.15% of total) → total ≈ ₹1,979 Cr.

## 4.5 New insights and new primer issues

### 4.5.1 What an expert would add (not in Parts 1–3)
1. **Q1 FY27's ₹5,030/t is a cyclical high, not a step-change.**
   - AWL's FY26 average edible-oil EBITDA was already **~₹4,420/t** [E: AR volume 4.26 MMT ÷ segment EBITDA ₹1,884 Cr]. Management's "normal" band is ₹4,000–4,500 [V call].
   - Q1's +30% compares with a weak Q1 FY26 (~₹3,870). Against the FY26 average it is only **+14%**.
   - CARE's ₹3,500–3,700 is *company-wide* PBILDT/t (incl. Foods and IE) — a different metric. **Do not compare it with the edible-oil-only ₹5,030.**
2. **The rupee, not the dollar price, drove the 2026 windfall.**
   - CPO in rupees before duty averaged ₹1.12 lakh/t in OY2025-26, **above the 2021-22 crisis average (₹1.09 lakh/t)**, while the dollar price was 15% lower [V SEA; E].
   - In Q1 FY27 the landed rupee cost of CPO was ~30% above Q1 FY26. Long-inventory holders (explicitly Patanjali) booked that as margin.
   - **A rupee reversal is now the main downside risk to H2 FY27 margins**, alongside the 24-Sep duty cut.
3. **Nepal is now the whole refined-oil story.**
   - Nepal supplied **682 kt of the 740 kt (92%) of India's refined imports in Nov-25 to Sep-26**, mostly refined soy (606 kt ≈ 12% of India's soy-oil imports) [V SEA; E].
   - SEA itself began counting Nepal in its statistics in Oct-2025.
   - AWL's AR carries a **customs case on denial of SAFTA preferential duty** (under appeal) — evidence that customs is contesting SAFTA origin claims [V AR].
   - The policy lever is rules of origin, not the duty rate.
4. **Soft oils have displaced palm, and Argentina is now the #1 supplier.**
   - Palm's share was 59% (OY22-23) → 47–48% (OY24-25/25-26). Soy imports hit a record 5.47 mt.
   - **Argentina (3.44 mt in 11 months) out-supplies Malaysia (2.98 mt) and Indonesia (2.91 mt)** [V SEA].
   - With B50 live in Indonesia since Jul-2026 [V], palm is unlikely to regain its discount, so the mix shift looks structural [E].
5. **Patanjali's related-party perimeter is the real valuation discount.** The FY26 AR shows:
   - ~₹1,257 Cr of purchases from promoter-group entities, ₹366 Cr of freight via Patanjali Parivahan, ₹236 Cr of security/manufacturing charges and a ₹123 Cr royalty to Patanjali Ayurved — **~₹2,000 Cr/yr of related-party flows**;
   - a **₹600 Cr capital advance to Patanjali Ayurved** for a biscuit plant (refundable if YEIDA approval fails within 6 months);
   - the **Ashav award (~5% of equity)**.
   - Together with the ₹788 Cr earlier-year tax credit behind FY26 PAT, these explain why FIIs cut their stake from 14.4% to 7.2% and the stock fell 40% while operations improved [V screener/AR; E interpretation].
6. **Overcapacity is quantified at last.**
   - AWL's refineries run at **60–61%** [V call]. Patanjali is ~55% [E: ~2 mt on ~3.6 mt (2021 capacity)]. CRISIL puts the industry at 70–80% [V].
   - **Gokul runs at ~83%** [V AR]. That high utilisation plus supplier credit (54% of purchases via trading houses) explains its 37% ROCE better than "asset-light" does.
   - It also explains why the 19.25-point duty differential is worth more to domestic refiners than any efficiency gain.
7. **The government is squeezing domestic crushers from both sides.**
   - The mustard MSP rose 6.7% (₹6,613, RMS 2027-28) and soybean 7.1% (₹5,708).
   - In the same weeks, the crude-oil import duty was cut to 5% (palm/soy) and nil (sunflower).
   - Imported crude rapeseed oil stays at 38.5% effective duty, so **mustard oil (AWL #1; Patanjali's Q1 growth driver) remains protected from imports but not from cheaper substitute oils** [V SEA/CCEA; E].
8. **Bonded port stock softens the duty-cut "inventory loss".**
   - On 1-Oct-2026, 1,236 kt sat in port tanks [V SEA]. Oil held in bonded warehouses pays duty only at ex-bond clearance, at the rate then in force [K, customs warehousing rules].
   - So part of the stock benefits from the 24-Sep cut rather than suffering from it. **The Q3 FY27 markdown risk is mainly on duty-paid inventory in pipeline/factory stock (~836 kt nationally)** [E].
   - The writer should temper Part 1 §9.3's ₹250–275 Cr AWL markdown estimate accordingly.
