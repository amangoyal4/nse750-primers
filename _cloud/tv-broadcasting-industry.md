## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Network18 consolidated is now a news + digital company. The FY24 and FY25 numbers include Viacom18 entertainment.**
   - Screener consolidated sales: FY23 ₹6,223 Cr, **FY24 ₹9,297 Cr** (Viacom18 + JioCinema IPL year), **FY25 ₹6,888 Cr** (Viacom18 until 14-Nov-2024), **FY26 ₹2,121 Cr** (news + digital + fintech only) [V screener].
   - **Never compute a growth rate across FY25→FY26 for Network18.** The like-for-like FY26 growth of the operated business is not disclosed as one number. Q1 FY27 grew +10.3% YoY on a comparable basis (News18 Marathi consolidated only from 29-Oct-2025, which flatters growth slightly) [V].
   - FY25 PAT −₹1,777 Cr included a ~₹1,225 Cr Dec-2024 hit (screener "other income" −₹1,225 Cr in Q3 FY25 = loss on deconsolidation/fair-value of Viacom18) [V screener; E interpretation].
2. **Network18 FY26 "₹155 Cr profit" is not operating profit.**
   - Operating EBITDA: **₹43 Cr (2.0%)** [V screener; company op EBITDA ~same].
   - Pre-tax before associates and exceptionals: **−₹266.0 Cr**. Share of associates/JVs: **+₹303.4 Cr**. Exceptional items: **+₹117.8 Cr** (Eenadu TV fair-value gain ₹150.6 Cr less labour-code gratuity charge ₹32.8 Cr). Tax ~nil. **PAT ₹155.2 Cr; attributable to owners ₹152.2 Cr** [V Network18 release].
   - **Pre-exceptional PAT ≈ ₹37 Cr** (owners ≈ ₹34 Cr [E]). Q1 FY27 PAT: **−₹38.4 Cr (owners −₹38.7 Cr)**, with associates' share down to ₹37.7 Cr from ₹70.3 Cr [V].
3. **Zee has four EBITDA numbers for FY26. Quote the basis every time.**
   - Company "adjusted EBITDA" **₹754.7 Cr (9.3%)** [primer, from the Q4 FY26 release].
   - Screener operating profit **₹399 Cr (4.9%)** [V].
   - PL Capital's reproduction of company line items: **EBITDA ₹346.3 Cr (4.3%)** after a Q4 FY26 EBITDA of **−₹268.6 Cr** (Q4 content cost 74.3% of sales, presumably inventory/content write-downs) [V PL].
   - Pre-ZEE5 "legacy" EBITDA ≈ adjusted EBITDA − digital EBITDA [E].
   - **Use screener OP in peer tables; use "adjusted EBITDA ₹755 Cr" only when quoting management.** The gap (~₹350–410 Cr) is mostly Q4 one-offs the company excludes.
4. **Zee reported vs adjusted PAT.**
   - FY26 reported PAT **₹271 Cr** (PL: ₹271.3 Cr; one exceptional of −₹9.4 Cr). PL's "adjusted PAT" of ₹636.5 Cr adds back the Q4 write-downs (Q4 adjusted PAT ₹290.7 Cr vs reported −₹103.7 Cr) [V PL].
   - Q1 FY27: reported PAT **₹74.3 Cr** (tax was a −₹0.2 Cr credit); PL adjusted **₹53.6 Cr (−59.6%)** after removing a ₹20.7 Cr fair-value gain [V PL]. Press reports of "₹76.3 Cr" are a different line (likely before OCI/rounding) [V-snip].
5. **ZEE5 "breakeven" is a quarterly, not an annual, fact.**
   - ZEE5 quarterly EBITDA (₹ Cr): Q1 FY26 −65.8, Q2 −31.2, **Q3 +56.4**, Q4 −8.4, **Q1 FY27 +4.4** [V PL Exhibit 4].
   - **FY26 sum −₹49 Cr** on revenue ₹1,488.8 Cr. FY25 −₹548 Cr on ₹976 Cr [E: sums of PL quarters]. Say "near-breakeven, first positive quarters".
6. **Sun TV: cricket and film income make quarters lumpy; Q1 is always the IPL quarter.**
   - IPL runs late-Mar to early-Jun, so **Sun's Q1 carries most of the year's cricket-franchise income**: Q1 FY27 **₹629.8 Cr** cricket income vs ₹473.0 Cr in Q1 FY26 (FY26 full year ₹662.1 Cr) [V indiantelevision; primer].
   - Q2 FY26 consolidated sales of **₹1,300 Cr (+39% YoY)** with depreciation/amortisation of ₹404 Cr reflect Sun Pictures' **Coolie** (released 14-Aug-2025): theatrical/rights revenue plus film-cost amortisation [V screener; E attribution].
   - **Compare Sun's quarters only YoY, and strip cricket and film when talking about "the TV business".**
   - Sun's standalone and consolidated numbers differ (Q1 FY27 revenue ₹1,423 Cr standalone vs ₹1,458 Cr consolidated) [V]. This dossier uses consolidated unless labelled.
7. **"Subscription revenue" means different things.**
   - **Sun** discloses **domestic subscription** (₹1,891.7 Cr FY26; ₹485.5 Cr Q1 FY27): linear pay-TV from DPOs. International subscription and Sun NXT are not cleanly broken out [V-snip/primer].
   - **Zee's** "subscription" (₹4,079.6 Cr FY26; ₹1,136.9 Cr Q1 FY27) **includes ZEE5 B2C/telco subscriptions and international** (domestic ₹1,033.4 Cr + international ₹103.5 Cr in Q1) [V PL].
   - **So Zee's +15.8% subscription growth in Q1 FY27 is not comparable with Sun's +3.3%.** Much of Zee's is digital (FIFA-driven ZEE5 B2C adds) plus a linear price increase.
8. **Viewership share numbers have stopped being published.**
   - MIB told BARC to **pause news ratings from March 2026** (West Asia coverage), extended repeatedly. In **July 2026 MIB told BARC to stop publishing all TV ratings** until it is licensed under the new **Television Ratings Policy, 2026** [V Network18 release].
   - Still suspended as of 21-Sep-2026; broadcasters set a **November deadline** before seeking other remedies [V-snip e4m]. A 31-Jul report of "provisional registration valid to a Jan-2027 review" has not been confirmed as implemented [V-snip].
   - **Any "network share" quoted after Week 26 of 2026 does not exist.** Zee's Q1 FY27 17.9% and the week-24 peak of 20% are the last prints [V PL]. Network18's shares are for Weeks 1–8 of 2026 [V].
9. **JioStar is not consolidated by any member. Do not add JioStar to "sector" aggregates.**
   - JioStar FY26: revenue from operations **₹31,048 Cr**, EBITDA ₹4,885 Cr, PAT ₹3,210 Cr [V-snip e4m]. Q1 FY27: ₹10,946 Cr / PAT ₹665 Cr [V indiantelevision].
   - Reliance's media segment (JioStar + JioHotstar + Jio Studios + Network18) FY26: revenue ₹40,682 Cr, EBITDA ₹5,842 Cr, PAT ₹3,434 Cr per one report; the AGM said ₹34,917 Cr [V-snip; V indiantelevision for ₹34,917 Cr]. **Perimeters differ** (gross vs operating revenue, intra-group). Quote "about ₹31,000–35,000 Cr" and name the basis.
   - **JioStar alone is ~2.1× the combined revenue of all three members (₹14,555 Cr)** [E].
10. **Cash: screener under-counts Sun and Zee and over-counts Network18.**
    - **Sun:** screener investments **₹7,821 Cr** (Mar-26) excludes bank deposits sitting in "other assets". Borrowings ₹106 Cr. **Net cash ≥ ₹7,715 Cr** [V screener]; the true figure is higher (bank balances unverified).
    - **Zee:** screener investments ₹1,275 Cr understates the treasury. Company-basis cash & bank **₹1,493.5 Cr + current investments ₹1,230.3 Cr ≈ ₹2,724 Cr** vs borrowings ₹194–265 Cr → **net cash ≈ ₹2,459–2,530 Cr** [V PL balance sheet; primer ~₹2,760 Cr]. **Use ~₹2,500 Cr.**
    - **Network18:** screener investments **₹6,123 Cr are strategic** (Viacom18/JioStar look-through, BookMyShow, Eenadu TV at fair value), not cash. Borrowings **₹3,288 Cr**; FY26 finance cost ₹204.8 Cr [V]. **Treat Network18 as net-debt ~₹3,000–3,300 Cr on an operating basis** [E], with the stakes as a separate SOTP item.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **A shrinking pie, one giant, three very different listed members.**
  - **Indian TV revenue fell to ₹61,700 Cr in CY2025**: TV advertising **−10%+**, subscription **−8%**, about **11 million pay-TV homes lost in one year** (FICCI-EY 2026) [V storyboard18].
  - WPP Media's mid-2026 forecast has **TV ad spend falling another 6.8% in CY2026**, while India's total ad market grows 8.8% to ~₹2 lakh Cr [V indiantelevision].
  - Digital took **₹94,700 Cr of a ₹1,50,000 Cr ad market in CY2025 (~63%)**. TV's share is ~18% [E: ~₹27,600 Cr TV ad ÷ ₹1.5 lakh Cr; see §2].
  - **JioStar** (Reliance-controlled since 14-Nov-2024) has a **34% TV viewership share (Q1 FY27), IPL + ICC + Star + Colors**, and FY26 operating revenue of **₹31,048 Cr** — more than double the three listed members combined [V/V-snip].
- **The three members are three different businesses:**
  - **Sun TV — the regional cash machine.** FY26 revenue ₹4,335 Cr, OP ₹2,183 Cr (**50.4%**), PAT ₹1,441 Cr, ROCE 16%, net cash ≥₹7.7k Cr, 75% promoter, 15× P/E [V screener]. Ad −10–12% YoY through FY26 but **−2.6% in Q1 FY27**. Domestic subscription **+9.7% in FY26 but only +3.3% in Q1 FY27**. The IPL team (Sunrisers Hyderabad) earned **₹630 Cr in Q1 FY27 alone** [V].
  - **Zee — the national entertainment network in margin crisis.** FY26 revenue ₹8,099 Cr (−2.4%), OP **₹399 Cr (4.9%)**, PAT ₹271 Cr. Ad −10.2% in FY26 and **−11.5% in Q1 FY27**. EBITDA margin **4.1%** in Q1 FY27 after it bought FIFA WC 2026, Bundesliga and Serie A rights and launched sports channels (A&P +62%). ZEE5 is now around breakeven on ~₹1,500–1,800 Cr revenue [V PL/screener]. Market cap **₹6,605 Cr = 0.56× book**, about 2.6× its net cash [E].
  - **Network18 — the news house plus a JioStar sliver.** FY26 revenue ₹2,121 Cr, op EBITDA 2%, pre-exceptional PAT ~₹37 Cr (all from associates). Holds **~6.3% look-through of JioStar** [E], worth roughly **₹4,400 Cr at the Nov-2024 deal value** vs a market cap of **₹3,742 Cr**, with ₹3,288 Cr of borrowings [E/V].
- **FY26 aggregate (straight sums, screener consolidated):** revenue **₹14,555 Cr**, OP **₹2,625 Cr (18.0%)**, reported PAT **₹1,867 Cr** (owners ₹1,864 Cr), m-cap **₹34,000 Cr** (22-Oct-26) → **18.2× aggregate P/E** [E]. Sun is **30% of revenue but 70% of market cap**. Zee is **56% of revenue but 19% of market cap** (§3.2).
- **What decides the next two years (the forces, detail in §A):**
  1. **Cord-cutting pace.** Pay DTH fell **13.4% in a year to 48.56m (Jun-2026)**; large MSO+HITS cable fell **15%, from 38.3m to 32.5m, in nine months to Mar-2026**. Airtel IPTV is the one growing pipe (3.6m) [V/V-snip TRAI via trade press].
  2. **The ad cycle plus structural migration.** FMCG is shifting to digital/quick-commerce media; non-government TV ad inventory **fell >10% YoY in Apr–Jun 2026** (Network18) [V]. The West Asia war hit corporate budgets.
  3. **Pricing power on subscription under TRAI's NTO.** Annual price hikes of ~7–8% (Sun FY26) can offset falling homes only so long [V-snip Elara].
  4. **JioStar's scale** in content, sports, distribution and CTV, plus free DD FreeDish (where Star Utsav joined in Apr-2025) [V].
  5. **Ratings blackout and governance/regulation shocks:** the BARC suspension, the SEBI order on Zee, and the Star v Zee ICC arbitration (outcome "expected Q3 FY27" per PL) — a **$940m (~₹8,000 Cr) claim against a ₹6,605 Cr company** [V-snip/V PL].

### 1.2 Value chain (content → home → cash)

1. **Content creation:** in-house production houses and commissioned producers (daily soaps at ~₹10–40 lakh per episode for regional GECs, ₹40 lakh–₹1 Cr+ for Hindi prime-time [K/E ranges]). Film rights acquisition (Zee's inventory is ~₹4,400 Cr of movie rights out of ~₹6,760 Cr total content inventory incl. advances, Q1 FY27 [V PL Exhibit 5, ₹71.8 bn total]). Sports rights (IPL/ICC at JioStar; FIFA, Bundesliga, Serie A at Zee in 2026).
2. **Packaging:** channels per language (GEC + movies + music + kids + news) and bouquets. Teleport/uplink, satellite transponders (ISRO/NSIL or foreign), MIB uplink/downlink permission. **917 permitted private satellite channels, 342 pay (238 SD/104 HD), 566 FTA (Mar-2026)** [V medianews4u, TRAI data].
3. **Distribution:**
   - **Pay DTH:** 4 private operators. **48.56m active (Jun-2026)**, Tata Play 14.84m (30.6%) [V-snip TRAI PR 135/2026]; Airtel Digital TV, Dish TV/d2h, Sun Direct (Sun Direct is owned by the Maran family, not by Sun TV Network [K]).
   - **Cable:** 752 registered MSOs (Mar-2026; 842 a year earlier); large MSO + 2 HITS operators **32.53m subs** [V]. Large MSOs include Hathway and Den (both Reliance/Jio-controlled), GTPL, SITI, Asianet.
   - **IPTV:** Airtel IPTV **3.6m** (Mar-2026; 1.57m in Sep-2025), AP Fibernet 0.41m [V].
   - **Free DTH:** **DD FreeDish** (Prasar Bharati), about **45–50m homes** [K: industry estimates; not in TRAI's paid figures]. Broadcasters buy slots via e-auction. No subscription revenue, but huge rural ad reach.
   - **Digital:** OTT apps, YouTube, CTV (**~40m weekly active CTV homes in CY2025, from 30m** [V FICCI-EY via storyboard18]).
4. **Measurement:** BARC India (broadcaster–advertiser–agency JV). The panel was ~55,000 meters [K]. Ratings are **suspended since Jul-2026 pending licensing under the Television Ratings Policy 2026** [V].
5. **Monetisation:** advertisers (via agencies, with volume rebates), DPOs (monthly payments, often with long receivable cycles: Sun debtor days 121, Zee 78 [V screener]), consumers (OTT).

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **Broadcaster with a must-have regional bouquet** captures the most: Sun's **50% OP margin** comes from owning the whole Tamil evening (GEC + movies + news + music + kids) with low incremental content cost, plus distribution leverage [V screener; primer].
- **The DPO layer is squeezed:** "DPOs control 80% of TV revenue" (headline of FICCI-EY 2026 coverage, i.e. the consumer rupee flows through them) [V-snip storyboard18 headline]. DTH operators are losing subscribers at double-digit rates and most are loss-making or barely profitable (Dish TV's losses, Tata Play's IPO shelved) [K].
- **Sports rights holders** capture the ad premium but pay most of it to the rights owner (BCCI/ICC). JioStar's EBITDA margin is only **8.5% operating / 9.6% reported** in the IPL quarter, Q1 FY27 [V].
- **News** captures little: Network18 has India's #1 news network by share (13.8%) yet earns a **1.5–2% EBITDA margin** [V].
- **Content producers** (independent studios, stars) capture rising fees: Rajinikanth-scale films budgeted at ~₹375 Cr (Coolie) [primer, K].

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Households with TV and pay-TV.**
   - TV homes ~**190–210m** (BARC Broadcast India Survey 2023: ~210m TV homes of ~313m homes [K, approx.]).
   - Pay-TV (cable+DTH+HITS+IPTV): **~91m in CY2025 [E: back-solved from FICCI-EY's −3.1% CAGR to 83m in 2028]**, after losing ~11m in 2025 [V]. The industry peak was ~120m+ around 2018–19 [K, approx.].
   - **Free TV (DD FreeDish + terrestrial):** ~45–50m homes and growing in rural markets [K; FICCI-EY: "Free TV continues to expand in rural markets" V].
   - **Pay-DTH trajectory (TRAI):** Mar-25 56.92m → Jun-25 ~56.1m → Sep-25 52.78m → Dec-25 50.99m → Mar-26 49.05m → **Jun-26 48.56m** [V medianews4u; V-snip others].
   - Cable (large MSO+HITS): Jun-25 38.32m → Dec-25 35.14m → **Mar-26 32.53m** [V].
2. **Advertiser categories.**
   - TV ad money is dominated by **FMCG (~45–50% of TV ad volumes by BARC/TAM AdEx [K, approx.])**: HUL, Reckitt, P&G, Nestle, Dabur, Godrej Consumer, Colgate, Marico, ITC. Then e-commerce/quick-commerce, autos, telecom, BFSI, education/gaming (real-money gaming ads ended after the Aug-2025 online gaming ban [K]), and **government/political** (elections).
   - **FMCG was the largest digital category in 2025: 32% of digital spends, ₹23,243 Cr** (dentsu-e4m) [V-snip]. The channel shift is visible in the advertiser's own mix.
   - Q1 FY27: **government and political demand rose sharply (state elections in Tamil Nadu, West Bengal, Kerala, Assam, Puducherry), while non-government TV ad inventory fell >10% YoY; overall industry ad inventory +3%** (Network18) [V].
   - **West Asia war and inflation** cut corporate budgets in Q4 FY26–Q1 FY27. Zee blamed the "Middle East crisis" for its −11.5% ad decline [V PL]. JioStar cited "challenges in the advertising market" [V].
   - **GST 2.0** (22-Sep-2025) cut GST on many FMCG items, and early-2026 trade press expected FMCG to raise H2 ad spends ~10% YoY "post-GST stabilisation" — before the war [V-snip e4m]. Evidence it reached TV: none yet. Sun's Q4 FY26 ad was −10.1% and Zee's Q3 FY26 −9.5% [V-snip].
3. **Content-led viewership spikes:** cricket (IPL Mar–Jun; ICC events), FIFA (Zee, Jun–Jul 2026, "400m+ reach across platforms" [V PL]), big film premieres, festivals (Diwali Q3), elections (news).
4. **Regional language mix:** regional channels' share of TV ad spend is "roughly half and rising" [primer Zee, industry estimates; unsourced exact figure]. Southern-language TV is the most resilient to cord-cutting [primer, K].
5. **Subscription pricing:** broadcasters revise MRPs annually (Reference Interconnect Offers). Sun took a **~7–8% tariff hike in FY26** [V-snip Elara]. Zee's Q1 FY27 subscription growth was driven by "higher linear subscription pricing", digital subscribers and ARPU [V PL].

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 TRAI tariff regime (NTO)

- **NTO 1.0 (effective 1-Feb/31-Mar-2019):** à-la-carte MRP for every pay channel; bouquets allowed; **channels above ₹19 MRP cannot be in bouquets**; DPO network capacity fee (NCF) capped at **₹130 + GST for 100 SD channels**; DPO distribution fee of up to 20% of MRP; carriage fee caps of ₹0.20/sub/month per SD channel [K].
- **NTO 2.0 (Jan-2020 amendment):** cut the bouquet MRP cap to **₹12**, NCF ₹130 for **200 channels**, twin conditions on bouquet discounts. Challenged; upheld by the Bombay HC (Jul-2021) [K; V-snip moneylife/BS].
- **NTO 3.0 (Nov-2022 amendment, effective Feb-2023):** **₹19 cap restored** for bouquet inclusion; bouquet discount capped at **45%** of the sum of à-la-carte prices. Broadcasters raised prices ~10–15% (Feb-2023), sparking the cable operators' blackout of Star/Zee/Sony feeds [K].
- **2024 amendments (Jul-2024, TRAI Interconnection & QoS):** **NCF ceiling removed (forbearance)**; DPOs may offer NCF discounts (regional, long-term); carriage-fee framework relaxed (no cap for HD; reduced fees as subscribers rise) [K; FLAG — verify exact clauses against the TRAI notification].
- **2025–26:** no new broadcasting tariff amendment found in this session [V-snip search, negative]. Annual MRP increases continue: Sun ~7–8% (FY26) [V-snip Elara]; Zee "higher linear subscription pricing" (Q1 FY27) [V PL].
- **What it means:** regulation caps price discrimination and bundling power, so **subscription ARPU rises only with annual MRP hikes, while homes fall 10%+ a year in DTH**. Broadcasters' domestic subscription still grew in FY26 (Sun +9.7%; Zee linear roughly flat), but Sun's slowed to **+3.3% in Q1 FY27** [V]. The squeeze is arriving.

### 7.2 Ratings: BARC and the Television Ratings Policy 2026

- BARC is the sole ratings agency (since 2015). After the 2020 "TRP scam" (Republic/others), MIB revised the 2014 policy guidelines in 2025–26 [K].
- **2026 timeline:** Mar-2026 MIB pauses news ratings for 4 weeks (West Asia war coverage), extended repeatedly. **Jul-2026: MIB directs BARC to stop publishing all ratings until licence renewal under the Television Ratings Policy 2026** — bigger sample, board governance, audits, data-protection compliance, methodology disclosure, periodic inspections [V Network18 release]. BARC applied for renewal in May-2026 [V-snip]. A 31-Jul report said provisional registration would come, valid to Jan-2027 [V-snip]. **Still suspended at 21-Sep; broadcasters set a November deadline** [V-snip].
- **Impact:** advertisers buy on old data/reach. This hurts challengers trying to prove share gains (Zee's 17.9% → 20% momentum is now invisible) and favours scale/reach players and digital (measurable) media [E].

### 7.3 Content, uplink and FDI

- Uplinking/downlinking guidelines 2022: annual permission fees and mandatory public-service content of 30 minutes a day [K].
- **FDI:** news broadcasting 26% (government route); non-news 100% automatic [K]. (Relevant to Disney's 36.84% in JioStar and Sony's ownership.)
- **Broadcasting Services (Regulation) Bill (draft 2023/2024)** would extend regulation to OTT/digital news. Withdrawn for re-drafting in 2024; status in 2026 unknown [K; unsourced].
- **CCI approval of Reliance–Disney (Aug-2024)** came with voluntary modifications (e.g. on cricket ad pricing/bundling) [K].

### 7.4 Sports-rights rules

- **Sports Broadcasting Signals (Mandatory Sharing with Prasar Bharati) Act 2007:** national-importance events (India cricket matches, ICC events) must be shared with DD free [K]. This dilutes pay-TV exclusivity of cricket and boosts free TV.
- **IPL media rights 2023–27 (Jun-2022 e-auction): ₹48,390 Cr total.** TV (India) Star ₹23,575 Cr (₹57.5 Cr/match); digital (India) Viacom18 ₹20,500 Cr (₹50 Cr/match); non-exclusive package C Viacom18 ₹3,258 Cr; overseas Viacom18/Times ~₹1,057 Cr. All now sit inside JioStar [K].
- **ICC media rights 2024–27:** Star (Disney) won India TV + digital, ~$3 bn [K]. Star sub-licensed TV to Zee (Aug-2022, ~$1.4 bn); Zee walked away after missing the first ~$200m payment (2024); Star filed a $940m LCIA claim (16-Sep-2024) [V-snip; §B3].

### 7.5 Governance and securities regulation (Zee)

- SEBI interim order (Jun-2023) barring Subhash Chandra/Punit Goenka from board roles over alleged fund diversion; set aside by SAT (Oct-2023); probe continued [K/primer].
- **SEBI final order 31-Jul-2026** (unauthorised pledge of ZEEL property): ZEEL 2-month market restraint; Goenka and Chandra 12 months; penalties ₹1.48 Cr total. **SAT 14-Aug-2026: partial stay** for the warrant issue and dividend [V-snip].
- **MCA inspection notice (5-Oct-2026)** under the Companies Act [V screener].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

- **1991–2000: the satellite boom.** Star TV (1991), Zee TV (1992, India's first private Hindi satellite channel), Sun TV (1993, Tamil). Cable operators proliferate unregulated.
- **2000s: regional bouquets and analogue cable.** Sun builds four Southern clusters. IPOs: Zee (listed 1990s), Sun TV (Apr-2006), TV18/Network18 (2006–07). DTH arrives: Dish TV (2003), Tata Sky (2006), Sun Direct (2007), Airtel (2008).
- **2008–2011: IPL and news proliferation.** IPL starts in 2008 (Sony rights ₹8,200 Cr/10 yrs). Sun buys Sunrisers Hyderabad (Dec-2012, replacing Deccan Chargers).
- **2012–2017: digitisation (DAS phases I–IV).** Mandatory set-top boxes made subscription transparent, and broadcasters' subscription revenue doubled. Reliance takes control of Network18/TV18 (2014). Star buys IPL rights 2018–22 for ₹16,348 Cr (Sep-2017).
- **2019–2020: NTO 1.0/2.0.** À-la-carte pricing. Pay-TV homes peak and begin to fall. Essel group debt crisis: Zee promoter stake falls from ~41% to under 5% via pledge invocations [primer].
- **2021–2024: consolidation attempts.** Invesco v Zee (Sep-2021). Zee–Sony merger agreement (Dec-2021), NCLT approval (Aug-2023), **Sony termination (22-Jan-2024)**, **settlement with all claims withdrawn (27-Aug-2024)** [K]. IPL 2023–27 rights ₹48,390 Cr (Jun-2022). Viacom18/JioCinema streams IPL free (2023). Reliance–Disney deal announced 28-Feb-2024; CCI approval Aug-2024; **completed 14-Nov-2024 (JioStar)**. TV18/E18 merge into Network18 (record date 16-Oct-2024).
- **2025: free-fall of pay TV.** ~11m pay-TV homes lost; TV ad −10%+ [V]. JioHotstar launches (Feb-2025). Star Utsav moves to DD FreeDish (Apr-2025) [V]. Sun buys Northern Superchargers (The Hundred), renamed Sunrisers Leeds [K]. GST 2.0 (Sep-2025). Online real-money gaming ban (Aug-2025) removes an ad category [K].
- **2026: shocks.** West Asia war (from 28-Feb) hits ad budgets. BARC ratings suspended (news Mar; all Jul). FIFA WC (Zee). SEBI order on Zee (31-Jul). JioHotstar 530m MAU (Q1 FY27).

**Cycle pattern:** broadcaster margins peaked in FY18–FY19 (Zee OPM 31–32%; Sun 68–69%) [V screener] and have compressed since: Zee to 5%, Sun to 50%. The cycle is **structural decline punctuated by event spikes** (IPL, elections, films), not mean-reverting.

### 9.3 Subscription arithmetic (why NTO + cord-cutting matters) [E]

- Domestic subscription revenue = **paying homes carrying the channel × average monthly realisation per home × 12**.
- Sun FY26: ₹1,892 Cr ÷ 12 = ₹158 Cr/month. If Sun reaches ~40–45m paying homes (Southern pay-TV, incl. cable), realisation is **~₹35–40 per home per month** (after the DPO's 20% share) [E; FLAG — homes not disclosed].
- **Breakeven math:** with homes falling ~8–10%/yr, Sun needs ~9–11% annual price/mix gains just to hold revenue flat. FY26's +9.7% came with a 7–8% tariff hike [V-snip], implying mix/volume was roughly flat-to-positive in the South. Q1 FY27's +3.3% implies volume erosion of ~5% offset by price [E].
- Zee domestic subscription Q1 FY27 ₹1,033 Cr (+15.7%) includes ZEE5's FIFA-led B2C surge. Linear is likely low-single-digit [E; not split].

### 9.4 Ad-yield arithmetic (operating de-leverage) [E]

- For a broadcaster whose content cost is fixed in the short run, **incremental ad revenue drops ~80–90% to EBITDA** [E].
- Zee: Q1 FY27 ad −₹87 Cr YoY plus A&P +₹172 Cr (FIFA/sports launch) plus content +₹61 Cr, against subscription +₹155 Cr and other +₹14 Cr → EBITDA −₹149 Cr (₹228 → ₹79 Cr) [V PL; E bridge].
- Sun: Q1 FY27 ad −₹7 Cr and subscription +₹15 Cr, with cricket +₹157 Cr income and +₹65 Cr cost → EBITDA +₹116 Cr (standalone ₹617 → ₹733 Cr) [V; E bridge]. **Cricket, not TV, drove Sun's Q1 profit growth.**

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Cord-cutting to broadband/OTT and CTV.** The pay-DTH base lost 7.5m in a year; Airtel's IPTV (TV over fibre) doubled in two quarters, so some "cord-cutting" is pipe-switching and the broadcaster may still be paid via IPTV [V]. FICCI-EY's 83m pay-TV forecast for 2028 implies a further ~8m lost [V/E].
2. **Free TV (DD FreeDish).** Expanding rural free reach. Broadcasters put FTA versions there (Star Utsav from Apr-2025 [V]; Zee Anmol and others [primer]), which cannibalises pay homes in the Hindi belt. Less relevant in the South, where FreeDish carries fewer regional pay channels [K].
3. **Short video and micro-dramas** (YouTube Shorts, Reels, JioHotstar Tadka) compete for daytime and late-evening GEC viewing [V Tadka].
4. **AI content production** lowers the cost of dubbing and localisation. It helps library monetisation (Zee, Sun) but also lowers barriers for new regional OTT entrants [E].
5. **Measurement shift:** the BARC suspension plus the growth of CTV and digital attribution moves budgets to measurable media [E].
6. **Sports streaming free/at low price** (JioCinema IPL 2023 free; JioHotstar tiers) devalues linear sports and pulls ad money to digital during IPL windows; Zee cited "cricket programming" as a Q1 drag [V-snip].
7. **News:** platform dependence (YouTube, Google, Meta) and government ad/ratings interventions [V Network18].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Linear-TV subscriber decline is now double-digit in DTH, and it is the subscription annuity that is at risk.**
   - **Data:** pay DTH 56.9m (Mar-25) → 48.6m (Jun-26), −13.4% YoY. Large MSO+HITS cable 38.3m → 32.5m in nine months. ~11m pay-TV homes lost in CY2025. FICCI-EY sees pay TV at ~83m by 2028 [V].
   - **Offsets:** annual MRP hikes (Sun ~7–8%), IPTV over fibre (Airtel 3.6m), and the Southern markets' stickiness [V-snip/V].
   - **Signal:** Sun's domestic subscription growth fell from +9.7% (FY26) to **+3.3% (Q1 FY27)** — the first evidence that price is no longer outrunning volume in the South [V].

2. **The ad market has split: digital and government up, linear corporate down.**
   - **Data:** CY2025 TV ad −10%+ while total ad +13.5% and digital +26% (63% share). WPP: TV ad −6.8% in CY2026 [V].
   - **Q1 FY27:** non-government TV ad inventory −10%+; industry inventory +3% only because of state elections (Network18) [V].
   - **Members:** Zee ad −11.5%, Sun ad −2.6% (after −10 to −12% in FY26) [V].
   - **Causes:** FMCG's structural shift to digital/quick-commerce (32% of digital spend is FMCG), the West Asia war, a weak monsoon forecast and the gaming-ad ban [V-snip/K].

3. **JioStar's scale sets the market's terms.**
   - **Data:** 34% TV viewership, 44% Hindi pay-TV share, IPL (1.2bn reach in 2026) and ICC rights, JioHotstar 530m MAU, FY26 revenue ₹31,048 Cr, PAT ₹3,210 Cr [V/V-snip].
   - **Effects:** JioStar can bundle cricket and GEC inventory for advertisers and price aggressively, and its DPO negotiating leverage dwarfs Zee's. Star Vijay is Sun's main Tamil rival [primer].
   - **Pricing power:** Zee and Sun cannot match it on sports or digital reach. Their defence is regional depth (Sun) and multi-language GEC breadth plus niche sports (Zee).

4. **Operating leverage plus content-cost rigidity make broadcaster margins asymmetric.**
   - **Zee:** OPM 32% (FY19) → 15% (FY25) → **4.9% (FY26)** → 4.1% (Q1 FY27). Content cost reached 60% of revenue in FY26 [V].
   - **Sun:** 69% → 50%. Rising film amortisation and cricket costs, while ads fall [V].
   - **Read:** a 10% ad decline can halve a national network's EBITDA. Sun's 50% margin buys it room that Zee does not have.

5. **Governance, regulation and legal tail-risks are unusually large relative to equity values.**
   - **Zee:** SEBI 31-Jul-2026 order; the 3.99% promoter's ₹126 warrants (stock ₹68.5); **Star's $940m ICC claim (outcome expected Q3 FY27)** against a **₹6,605 Cr** market cap [V/V-snip].
   - **Industry:** ratings blackout (BARC licence) [V].
   - **Sun:** Maran family politics/DMK cycle; the May-2026 Tamil Nadu election result is relevant [primer; K]. Sun denied a sports-demerger report on 30-Sep-2026 [V].
   - **Network18:** a Reliance-controlled holdco; minorities do not control the JioStar stake [primer].

6. **Sports is the swing factor both ways.**
   - **Sun:** owns franchises that capture value from rising media-rights pools (IPL central rights 2023–27 ₹48,390 Cr) — **cricket income ₹630 Cr in Q1 FY27** [V].
   - **Zee:** buying rights (FIFA WC 2026, Bundesliga, Serie A) raised A&P 62% and cut Q1 margin to 4.1% [V PL]. Its previous sports bet (ICC sub-licence) produced the $940m claim.
   - **JioStar:** carries the IPL/ICC cost risk.

7. **OTT economics: profitable only at JioStar scale, or at breakeven by restraint.**
   - **ZEE5:** loss of ₹548 Cr (FY25) → −₹49 Cr (FY26) → +₹4.4 Cr (Q1 FY27), on revenue +53–58% [V/E].
   - **Sun NXT:** no disclosure.

   - **Read:** OTT now **stabilises** these groups (an offset, not an engine). It does not replace linear profit pools (Zee legacy EBITDA ~₹800 Cr in FY26 vs ~₹1,760 Cr in FY25 on the primer's adjusted basis [primer, E]).

8. **Capital allocation: the cash piles are the valuation floor.**
   - **Sun:** net cash ≥₹7.7k Cr (33% of m-cap); 34% payout; FY26 FCF −₹409 Cr after a ~₹2.2k Cr capex/acquisition year (fixed assets +₹959 Cr, CWIP +₹544 Cr — consistent with buying the Leeds franchise and new facilities) [V screener; E attribution].

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Broadcaster revenue = Ad revenue + Subscription revenue + Content/other (+ Cricket for Sun)**

- **Ad revenue** = Σ channels [ad minutes sold per hour (≤12) × hours × fill rate × effective rate per 10-second slot], where the rate ≈ f(ratings share × total TV ad pool).
- **Subscription revenue** = paying homes carrying the channels × realisation per home per month (MRP × (1 − DPO share ~20%) × bouquet discount effects) × 12, + OTT subscribers × ARPU.
- **EBITDA** = Revenue − content/programming (amortised; largely fixed in the short term) − A&P/distribution (carriage/placement) − employees − other.
- **PAT** = EBITDA + treasury income − D&A (film amortisation) − interest − tax (+ associates for Network18).

### C.1 Worked example 1 — Sun TV, FY26 consolidated (₹ Cr) [V screener; E splits]
- Domestic subscription ₹1,892 [primer, V-snip] = e.g. ~42m paying homes × ~₹37.5/month × 12 [E: homes not disclosed].
- Advertising ~₹1,150–1,200 [E] (Q1 290 + Q2 ~300 [E] + Q3 292 + Q4 276).
- Cricket ₹662; movies, international, other ~₹580–630 [E] (incl. Coolie theatrical and rights in Q2).
