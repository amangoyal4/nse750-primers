- "Label" = record company that owns or controls **sound recordings (masters)**. "Publisher" = owner/administrator of the **underlying musical and literary works** (composition, lyrics). ## READ THIS FIRST: eight definitional traps

1. **"EBITDA" means three different things across the two companies — the single biggest trap.**
   - **Tips expenses 100% of content cost in the quarter of release.** Its "operating EBITDA" (FY26 ~73%) is **after** all content cost. Tips calls itself the "only listed player to expense off 100% of content cost in the quarter of release" [V Q2 FY27 PPT].
   - **Saregama capitalises new-music cost and charges it through the P&L as a "content charge"** (amortisation of outright purchases and royalty advances, plus marketing of new content). Its headline **"Adjusted EBITDA" = EBITDA BEFORE the content charge** (FY26 ₹404.7 Cr, 41%) [V PPT definition: "Adjusted EBITDA: EBITDA excluding music Content Charge"]. Management says new music "charges through the P&L in ~10 years" [V Q1 FY27 PPT].
   - Saregama FY26 content charge **₹139.9 Cr** → EBITDA **after** content charge **₹264.8 Cr (26.9%)** [E arithmetic from V].
   - **Screener's "operating profit" for Saregama (FY26 ₹337 Cr, 34%) is a third basis**: screener books ~₹72 Cr of the content amortisation in "depreciation" (screener FY26 D&A ₹82 Cr vs company depreciation ₹9.7 Cr) and the rest (~₹68 Cr, marketing/royalty-type) in expenses [E reconciliation].
   - **Rule for the writer:** compare Tips 73% only with Saregama's "after content charge" figure (~27% group; music net margin 46%) or with screener OPM (34%). **Never put Tips 73% next to Saregama 41% adjusted EBITDA** — the primers do exactly this (Part 3.4).
2. **Saregama's "Music" segment changed definition.** From FY26 reporting, "Music" = **Licensing + Artiste Management + Retail (Carvaan, vinyl)** [V PPT]. Music revenue FY26 **₹814.4 Cr (+17%)**; music EBITDA **₹516.7 Cr (63%)**; **music net margin (EBITDA − content charge) ₹376.8 Cr (46%, +28%)** [V]. Our primer's "Q4 FY26 music revenue ₹200.4 Cr" is a narrower licensing-type number from a press article; on the company's segment basis Q4 FY26 music was **₹242.9 Cr** [V PPT]. Use the company series.
3. **Saregama's consolidated revenue fell in FY26 because of one concert tour.** FY25 revenue ₹1,171.3 Cr included **₹285.2 Cr of live-events revenue, mostly Diljit Dosanjh's India tour**; FY26 live events were **₹61.8 Cr (−78%)** [V Q4 PR]. Video also fell (₹192.0 Cr → ₹108.4 Cr). Music grew 17%. "Revenue −16%" is a mix effect, not a demand problem. Q1 FY27 shows the same pattern in reverse: revenue +27%, video −52%, music +39%, live +214% [V].
4. **Content "spend" has four numbers at Saregama — always name which.**
   - **Investment in content** (company KPI = cost of content + marketing): FY26 **₹340.1 Cr** = ₹235.4 Cr new music (organic) + ₹104.7 Cr catalogue purchases (inorganic, e.g. NAV Records Haryanvi catalogue) [V Q4 PR/PPT]. FY27E ₹300–350 Cr [V].
   - **Cash "spent on new content"** in the cash-flow statement: FY26 **₹265.3 Cr**; Q1 FY27 ₹44.4 Cr [V Q1 PPT]. An analyst on the Q1 call pointed out the Q4 FY26 deck showed ₹186 Cr for the same line; management "will take this offline" [V transcript] — **unreconciled; flag**.
   - **Content charge** through the P&L: FY26 **₹139.9 Cr**; Q1 FY27 ₹40.2 Cr (+53%) [V].
   - **Acquisition consideration** (Bhansali CCPS etc.): FY26 cash outflow **₹336.1 Cr** [V cash flow] — not "content spend", but it buys future content.
   - At Tips there is one number: **content cost** expensed (FY26 ₹59.2 Cr; H1 FY27 ₹70.8 Cr; FY27 guide ₹90–100 Cr) [V].
5. **Industry size: "music" has at least three definitions.**
   - **FICCI-EY 2026: music segment 2025 = ₹5,900 Cr (₹59,000 Mn), +10%; next-3-year CAGR 8%** [V Saregama Q4 PPT citing FICCI-EY; V EY press release for +10%].
   - **Tips' deck, citing the same EY report "net of tax, live performance and artist management":** CY23 ₹3,979 Cr, CY24 ₹3,906 Cr, **CY25 ₹4,348 Cr**, CY26E ₹4,716 Cr, CY28E ₹5,527 Cr (8.3% CAGR) [V PPT; ordering of the CY23/CY24 bars inferred from layout — see §H].
   - **IFPI "recorded music" (trade value, labels' wholesale revenue)**: India ~**15th** largest market (H1-2025 ranking), and grew **<3% in 2024** [V-snip MBW/IFPI CEO]. IFPI does not publish India's absolute number in free material.
   - Market-share maths changes with the denominator: Tips FY26 ₹376 Cr = **6.4%** of ₹5,900 Cr but **8.6%** of ₹4,348 Cr; management says "8% to 10% of the industry" [V transcript].
6. **Calendar-year industry vs fiscal-year companies.** EY/IFPI 2025 = Jan–Dec 2025; company FY26 = Apr-2025 to Mar-2026. Shares computed by dividing FY26 company revenue by CY25 industry revenue are approximate [E].
7. **Saregama's ROE/ROCE are depressed by the ₹750 Cr QIP cash (FY22) and acquisitions; Tips' ROE ~88–92% is flattered by expensing.** Tips' balance sheet carries **no content asset** (other intangibles ₹0.1 Cr [V]), so equity is tiny (₹324 Cr Sep-26) relative to profit. Saregama carries intangibles ₹504.9 Cr + goodwill ₹293.9 Cr + content inventory within ₹251.5 Cr inventories [V Jun-26 BS]. The 118% vs 17% ROCE gap is partly accounting, not only economics. A like-for-like comparison would capitalise Tips' last ~5 years of content (~₹280 Cr) [E] — still >60% ROCE.
8. **Platform "revenue share" vs "per-stream rate" vs "fixed fee".**
   - DSP subscription/ad pools are split pro-rata by streams (Spotify, JioSaavn, Apple, Amazon); YouTube pays a share of ad revenue; short-video apps (Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts in India) have mostly paid **fixed fees / MGs**, not ad-share, so Shorts/Reels volume growth does not lift label revenue until contracts move to ad-share [V both transcripts].
   - Per-stream illustrations: Tips' deck says **global labels earn 50–90 paise per stream vs 4–10 paise in India** [V PPT]; the Tips MD used "₹0.05 per stream" as an illustrative YouTube rate [V transcript]. These are management illustrations, not audited data — label "indicative".
   - Tips' Instagram/Meta and some international DSP income flows **through Warner Music** ("we are under Warner umbrella; they negotiate the entire world deal") — about **25–30% of Tips' revenue** [V transcript]. Renewal of the Warner deal is in **FY28** [V].

## 1. What the sub-sector does: products, value chain, where value is captured

### 1.1 Products / rights (what a label owns and sells)

| Right / product | What it is | Who pays | Members' exposure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sound recording (master)** — s.13(1)(c), Copyright Act 1957 | The fixed recording; term **60 years** from the year after publication (s.27) [K] | DSPs, YouTube, broadcasters, sync users, venues | Both: the core asset |
| **Underlying musical & literary works (publishing)** | Composition and lyrics; term life of author + 60 years [K] | Same users, often via IPRS | Both own publishing on most film music bought outright (pre-2012 contracts); Tips' international publishing administered by Warner Chappell (2023 deal) [V-snip] |
| **Audio streaming licence** | Pro-rata share of subscription + ad revenue, often with an MG | Spotify, JioSaavn, Apple, Amazon, Gaana, YouTube Music | Largest line for both |
| **Video / YouTube** | Official channels + Content ID on UGC; ad-revenue share | Google | Saregama 211 mn YouTube subscribers (Q1 FY27); Tips 164.5 mn (Q2 FY27) [V] |
| **Short-form video (Shorts, Reels)** | Mostly fixed-fee/MG deals today; moving to ad-share | Meta, Google, Moj/Josh, ShareChat | Tips: Meta via Warner; Shorts deal "under negotiation" (Q2 FY27) [V] |
| **Sync** | One-off licence for film, OTT series, ad, game | Producers, brands, OTTs | Saregama: 15+ theatrical, 10+ digital, 10+ brand sync deals in Q1 FY27 [V]; Tips: part of non-digital |
| **Public performance / broadcast** | Radio, TV, venues, events, weddings; statutory (s.31D) for radio/TV | Broadcasters, hotels, malls, event organisers | Via PPL/IPRS/own licensing; untapped (§7) |
| **Artiste management & brand** | Share of artistes' bookings, endorsements | Brands, event organisers | Saregama only (309 artistes, 440 mn reach, Q1 FY27) [V] |
| **Retail** | Carvaan players, vinyl | Consumers | Saregama only |
| **Live events** | Concerts, festivals, devotional, comedy | Ticket buyers, sponsors | Saregama only (FY26 ₹61.8 Cr) |
| **Video content** | Films (Yoodlee — being wound down), TV serials (Sun/Udaya), digital series, short-form (Pocket Aces) | OTTs, broadcasters, brands | Saregama only (FY26 ₹108.4 Cr) |

### 1.2 Value chain (song → listener → money)

1. **Creation:** film producer commissions songs (composer, lyricist, singers, musicians); or the label commissions non-film/independent music. Film music is the core of Indian listening (Hindi + Tamil/Telugu/Punjabi etc.).
2. **Rights acquisition:** label buys the **music rights of a film** (master + usually publishing, perpetual, worldwide) for an outright fee or MG + royalty, often **before the film releases**. Prices for big Hindi films are reported in the ₹15–40 Cr range per album [K/E — press reports; no primary]. Saregama's Bhansali deal converts this auction into a pre-agreed formula.
3. **Marketing:** promotion to make the album a hit (YouTube premieres, influencer reels, playlists). Tips counts promotion inside content cost [V transcript]; Saregama inside "investment in content" [V].
4. **Distribution:** direct to DSPs/YouTube (Saregama) or through **Warner Music** for parts of Tips' repertoire internationally and on Meta [V].
5. **Consumption:** ~192 mn music-streaming users (IFPI CEO), YouTube 18+ adults ~72 min/day (Dentsu via Tips deck) [V-snip; V PPT].
6. **Collection:** DSP statements (monthly/quarterly), YouTube Content ID, collecting societies (IPRS for authors/publishers; PPL/Novex/RMPL-type for labels — §7).
7. **Split:** platform keeps ~30–50% of its revenue; the rest goes to rights owners pro-rata [K, global norms]. Labels then pay artist/author royalties where contracts require (post-2012 author royalty; artist royalties on newer deals — Saregama's "royalty expense ₹34 Cr in Q4 FY26" [primer; V-snip storyboard18]).

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **The owner of the master captures the most margin.** Tips earns 70–76% EBITDA after all content cost; T-Series 35.5% operating margin (with film production and higher costs) and 35.8% net margin (helped by other income) [V Tofler]; UMG 22.5% adjusted EBITDA (artist royalties are a bigger cost line in Western contracts) [V-snip].
- **Platforms struggle.** Three Indian DSPs shut in 15 months; Spotify **cut** India Standard pricing from ₹199 to ₹139 in May-2026 [V-snip]. Platforms "rent the customers"; labels own the inventory.
- **Old catalogue is nearly pure profit; new content earns less.** Tips: **85% of revenue from content acquired more than 3 years ago, 15% from the last 3 years** [V transcript]. Saregama: **60% of FY26 music revenue came from music released after 2000** [V transcript] — Saregama is more "new-content" weighted than its heritage image suggests.

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 The Copyright Act, 1957 and the 2012 amendment
- Sound recordings are protected for **60 years** from the beginning of the calendar year following publication (s.27); literary/musical works for **life + 60 years** (s.22) [K]. Saregama's earliest shellac-era masters have already entered the public domain [primer; K].
- **Copyright (Amendment) Act, 2012** [K]:
  - Authors (lyricists, composers) get an **unwaivable right to receive royalties equally with the assignee** for utilisation of their works other than in a cinematograph film shown in a cinema hall (s.18(1) provisos, s.19(9)–(10)) — a permanent cost on post-2012 deals that legacy catalogue largely avoids.
  - Created **s.31D statutory licensing for radio and TV broadcasting** of literary/musical works and sound recordings, at rates fixed by the Copyright Board (later IPAB, now the High Courts).
  - Copyright societies must be registered; authors must be members of the society (IPRS).
- **Tribunals Reforms Act 2021** abolished the IPAB; its copyright functions moved to the **High Courts** (Delhi HC has an IP Division) [K].

### 7.2 Statutory licensing for internet streaming (s.31D) — settled for labels
- **Tips Industries v. Wynk (Bombay HC, 2019):** s.31D statutory licence **does not extend to internet broadcasting/streaming** — DSPs must negotiate voluntary licences [V-snip livelaw/scconline]. Wynk's appeals were **disposed of in 2023** (Patel J) [V-snip].
- DPIIT's **2016 Office Memorandum** (which tried to extend s.31D to internet broadcasters) was **rescinded on 21-Aug-2024** [V-snip ssrana/foxmandal].
- A **2019 draft Copyright Amendment Rules** proposal to include internet broadcasting in 31D did not become law [K]. No 2026 legislative change found [V-snip search; H].
- **Implication:** labels keep pricing power vs DSPs; a reversal would cap streaming rates at a regulator-set level — the single biggest regulatory tail risk [E].

### 7.3 Public performance and collecting societies
- **IPRS** (Indian Performing Right Society; authors/composers/publishers; registered copyright society): FY25 collections ~**₹741.6 Cr (+42%)**, distributions ~₹608.8 Cr (record), public performance ₹101.7 Cr [V-snip loudest/CISAC coverage]. CISAC: India collections up 357% since 2020 [V-snip].
- **PPL (Phonographic Performance Ltd)** — labels' body for sound-recording public performance; its registration as a copyright society lapsed years ago and it operates as an **assignee/agent** of member labels; PPL collected ~₹164 Cr in 2022 [V-snip MBW]. Novex Communications operates similarly for some labels [K]. **Recorded Music Performance Ltd (RMPL)** was formed by labels as a registered society for sound recordings [K — verify status; H].
- **Calcutta High Court, Division Bench, 8-May-2026 (IPRS v. Vodafone Idea):** a **separate IPRS licence is required** whenever underlying musical/literary works are commercially communicated, even when embedded in sound recordings; **labels cannot issue licences that conflict with IPRS-administered rights** [V-snip; single report — verify judgment]. Relevant to caller-tune/telco and potentially DSP licensing; it raises total licence cost for users and shifts value from labels to authors on post-2012 works [E].
- Delhi/Bombay HC litigation on whether PPL/Novex can license without registration (restaurants, events) has gone both ways over 2022–2025 [K — verify latest; H].

### 7.4 AI and copyright
- **Global:** UMG settled with Udio (Oct-2025) and will launch a licensed AI platform in 2026; Warner settled with Udio (19-Nov-2025) and with Suno (Nov-2025); Sony still litigating [V-snip MBW/Decrypt/Hollywood Reporter]. UMG–Nvidia deal cited by Tips [V PPT]. IFPI: "build an ecosystem where AI and human artistry thrive together" [V MBW].
- **India:** no AI-specific copyright amendment; DPIIT set up an expert committee on AI and copyright (2025) [K — verify]; Indian labels (including T-Series and Saregama via the music industry body) sought to intervene in **ANI v. OpenAI** in the Delhi HC (2025) [K — verify]. Saregama: "support licensed innovation, but will continue our fight against unlicensed exploitation"; new licensing agreements include anti-dilution protections; two internal AI teams [V transcript]. Saregama licensed music to **OpenAI** for a brand campaign (Q4 FY26) [V PR].

### 7.5 Other policy
- **GST** 18% on licensing of copyrights [K]. Industry numbers in the Tips deck are "net of tax" [V].
- **Broadcast royalty rates** (radio) were last set by the Copyright Board/IPAB for FM radio (2010; revised 2020–21 by IPAB) [K — verify]; radio is a shrinking share.
- **Live events:** central and state governments are promoting concerts (single-window clearances, "concert economy") [V Saregama transcript, general]; relevant only to Saregama.

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **1902–1985: The Gramophone Company monopoly.** First Indian commercial recordings (1902); Dum Dum pressing plant; HMV dominates film music [primer; K].
2. **1983–2000: Cassette revolution.** T-Series (founded 1983 by Gulshan Kumar) and Tips (1988 company founding per Tips deck; label older) break HMV's dominance with cheap cassettes and cover versions; RPG buys HMV from EMI (1985–86); renamed Saregama (2000) [V Tips PPT for 1988; K].
3. **2000–2015: Piracy and the lost decade.** MP3 piracy; ringback tones a big but unstable stream (Tips MD: telcos made "around ₹5,000 crores" from caller-tune subscriptions with 8% penetration) [V transcript]; labels' revenues stagnate [K].
4. **2016–2019: Jio data shock and the streaming land-grab.** Cheap data (Sep-2016) explodes listening; Gaana, JioSaavn (merger 2018), Wynk, Hungama, Spotify (2019), Resso (2020) — MG-heavy licensing [K]. **Tips v. Wynk (2019)** blocks statutory-licence route [V-snip].
5. **2020–2022: Re-rating of catalogue owners.** Tips' film business demerged (2021–22); Saregama QIP **₹750 Cr** (FY22) [V PPT] and begins buying new music (FY22 content ₹109.5 Cr vs ₹19.5 Cr FY21) [V]. Tips' P/E rises from 8.6× (Apr-2020) to ~30× [V chart].
6. **2023–2024: Diversification and peak multiples.** Saregama buys Pocket Aces (Sep-2023), runs the Diljit Dosanjh tour (FY25 live revenue ₹285 Cr); Tips promoter sells ~11% (Mar-23 75.0% → Mar-24 63.86%) [V screener]; Warner–Tips global deal (Mar-2024); Tips P/E peaks at **77.7× (14-Nov-2024)** [V].
7. **2024–2025: Platform shake-out.** Resso (Jan-24), Wynk (Aug-24), Hungama (Apr-25) shut; India growth <3% in 2024 (IFPI) [V-snip]; Tips Q4 FY25 PAT dips to ₹30.6 Cr on heavy content (₹29.9 Cr) [V]; both stocks de-rate (Tips P/E 38.9× Oct-25; Saregama 34.2× Apr-26) [V].
8. **2025–2026: Paid-subscription push and recovery.** Paid subs +37% to 14.4 mn (2025) [V]; Saregama: Bhansali CCPS (Dec-25), *Dhurandhar* blockbuster album, film production wound down (2026); Tips buyback (₹44.44 Cr), Q2 FY27 revenue +35%; Jio Platforms lists (28-Oct-2026) [V-snip].

### 9.2 Content economics, worked [E]
- **Saregama's stated model:** pay X; charge it through P&L over ~10 years; recover cash in ~5 years; earn for 60–80 years; IRR 26% [V PPT]. A ₹100 album returning ₹20+/yr in years 1–5 and ~₹10–15/yr thereafter fits a 26% IRR only if early years are front-loaded (hit songs earn most in years 1–3) [E].
- **Tips' stated model:** expense in the quarter; target 4–5 year payback; budget ~20–22% of revenue [V]. Because Tips writes everything off immediately, its reported margin **understates** steady-state economics in a heavy-release quarter (Q1 FY27 50%) and **overstates** it in a light one (Q3 FY26 79%) [V screener quarterly OPM].
- **Revenue attribution test (Tips):** if 15% of FY26 revenue (~₹56 Cr) comes from content bought in FY24–FY26 (₹55.6 + ₹71.0 + ₹59.2 = ₹185.8 Cr), the run-rate yield on 3-year content is ~30% per year → ~3.3-year simple payback [E]. Consistent with the 4–5 year target once later-year decay and promotion are included.
- **Saregama equivalent:** music revenue grew ₹350 Cr FY22→FY26 (₹464.1 → ₹814.4 Cr) against cumulative content investment of ₹1,080 Cr (FY22–FY26: 109.5 + 119.2 + 194.9 + 316.0 + 340.1 = 1,079.7) → incremental revenue/investment ~32% [E; ignores catalogue growth, so it overstates new-content yield].

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **AI-generated music.** Floods DSPs with cheap content and dilutes royalty pools; streaming fraud (IFPI's CEO focus) [V MBW]. Mitigants: majors' licensing settlements with Udio/Suno (2025) create a paid-training model; Indian labels insert anti-dilution clauses [V]. Opportunity: AI music videos for old songs (~₹70,000 per video vs much higher conventional cost — Saregama) [V transcript]; licensing to AI firms (Saregama–OpenAI brand licence) [V].
2. **Platform concentration and rate cuts.** Spotify's May-2026 India price cut (−30%) lowers ARPU per paid user even as it may lift volumes [V-snip]. YouTube's revenue share "stable", no increase expected (Tips) [V].
3. **Short-form fixed-fee trap.** Shorts/Reels are where consumption is growing, but payment is fixed-fee until ad-share deals land [V]. Delay risk visible: Tips' YouTube Shorts deal 3–4 months late [V].
4. **Metering changes.** YouTube changed Shorts view-counting from 31-Mar-2025 — Saregama's quarterly views jumped from 129 bn to 284 bn [V PPT]. Views are not revenue.
5. **Copyright regime risk.** Any extension of s.31D to internet streaming; author-royalty claims (IPRS v. Vodafone Idea, May-2026) shifting value to authors [V-snip].
6. **Producers retaining music rights.** Big studios (YRF Music, Jio Studios, Dharma) can keep or auction rights, raising prices; Saregama's Bhansali stake is the defensive response [K/E].
7. **Format risk is low historically.** Saregama's masters survived shellac → vinyl → cassette → CD → download → streaming [primer]. The risk is the **pipe owner**, not the format.

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Free-to-paid conversion is the whole upside.** India has ~190 mn music-streaming users but ~14–20 mn payers (3% of internet users vs 57% in the US) [V; V-snip]. Every 10 mn new payers at ~₹100–140/month adds ~₹1,200–1,700 Cr of gross subscription spend, of which labels get roughly half [E] — that is 20–30% of today's industry. The pace depends on platforms curtailing free supply (Saregama's thesis) and on price points (Spotify cut to ₹139 in May-2026). Labels do not control this lever.
2. **Content cost inflation vs catalogue yield — the capital-allocation test.** Both labels now spend far more on new music than five years ago (Saregama ₹19.5 Cr FY21 → ₹340.1 Cr FY26; Tips ₹32.7 Cr FY22 → ₹90–100 Cr FY27E) [V], into an auction where T-Series, Sony, Zee, Universal and producers compete. The old catalogue's free carry hides poor returns for years. Saregama claims a 26% IRR and 5-year payback; Tips targets 4–5 years and writes everything off at once [V]. The market pays 44–46× for the assumption that this spend compounds.
3. **Platform power and consolidation.** Three DSP exits in 15 months (2024–25) cut MG income — FICCI-EY's digital licensing grew only 2% in 2025 [V]. Remaining buyers (Google, Spotify, Jio, Meta, Apple, Amazon) are trillion-dollar or Reliance-scale counterparties; YouTube's rate is "stable", Shorts/Reels still fixed-fee [V]. Labels' counterweight: irreplaceable repertoire and the 2019 Bombay HC ruling that streaming is not statutory-licensable [V-snip].
4. **Accounting shapes perception.** Tips' expensing vs Saregama's capitalisation explains much of the 73% vs 41%/27% margin gap and the 118% vs 17% ROCE gap. Quarter-to-quarter Tips margins swing 48–82% with release timing [V screener]. Analysts who do not normalise mis-read both.
5. **Diversification vs purity.** Saregama is building an "entertainment flywheel" (artiste management, live, short video, Carvaan/vinyl, Bhansali films) while winding down own films; Tips refuses artiste management and live ("a lot of trouble") [V]. FY26 shows the cost: Saregama's non-music businesses consumed ~₹112 Cr of EBITDA (music EBITDA ₹516.7 Cr vs group adjusted EBITDA ₹404.7 Cr) [V; E].

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Label revenue = Σ over platforms [ (consumption units) × (monetisation per unit) × (label's share of platform pool) ] + sync + public performance + other**

- For DSPs: **paid subscribers × ARPU × payout ratio (~50–55% to rights holders) × label's stream share** + **ad-funded streams × ad revenue per stream × payout share**.
- For YouTube: **views × CPM/1,000 × rights-holder share (~50–55% of net ad revenue) × label's claimed share**.
- **Profit = Revenue − content cost (expensed or amortised) − royalties to authors/artists − people/overhead.** For a catalogue owner, catalogue revenue has ~no variable cost; new content carries its acquisition cost plus royalties.

### C.1 Worked example 1 — one paid subscriber's money [E, ILLUSTRATIVE]
- Spotify Standard India ₹139/month → ₹1,668/yr; less GST 18% (₹254) → ₹1,414 net [E].
- Payout to rights holders ~52% (recorded ~45% + publishing ~7–10%, global norm) [K] → ~₹735/yr.
- If a label owns 10% of that user's streams → **₹73.5/yr per paid subscriber** to that label.
- 10 mn incremental paid users industry-wide → ~₹735 Cr to all rights holders; a 10%-share label (Tips' claimed 8–10%) gets **~₹70 Cr ≈ 19% of Tips' FY26 revenue** [E].

### C.2 Worked example 2 — one ad-funded stream / YouTube view [E, ILLUSTRATIVE]
- Tips' deck: Indian labels earn **4–10 paise per stream** vs global 50–90 paise [V PPT]. At 5 paise, 1 bn streams = **₹5 Cr**.
- Tips' FY26 YouTube views **228.3 bn**; at an assumed ~1–2 paise per view of label revenue, YouTube would be ₹23–46 Cr [E] — a plausible but unverified order of magnitude (Tips does not disclose YouTube revenue) [H].

### C.3 Worked example 3 — Tips Music FY26 P&L from the equation [E]
- Revenue ₹375.5 Cr = digital ~₹270 Cr (72%) + non-digital ~₹105 Cr [E from 70–75% range].
- Of digital, paid-subscription-linked ~₹27–40 Cr (10–15%) [E from V].
- Warner-routed (Meta/international) ~₹94–113 Cr (25–30%) [E from V].
- Content cost ₹59.2 Cr; other costs ~₹40 Cr → OP ₹276 Cr; + OI ₹19 Cr − D&A ₹2 Cr → PBT ₹292 Cr; tax 26% → **PAT ₹216.6 Cr** [V].

### C.4 Worked example 4 — a Saregama film-album investment [E, ILLUSTRATIVE, using management's model]
- Pay ₹30 Cr for a big Hindi album (incl. marketing) [E].
- Year 1–3 revenue ~₹10 Cr/yr, years 4–5 ~₹5 Cr/yr, thereafter ~₹2–3 Cr/yr for decades [E] → cumulative ₹40 Cr by year 5 (≈ 5-year cash payback incl. royalties/costs), IRR ~20–26% if the tail persists [E].
- P&L: content charge ~₹3 Cr/yr over 10 years (straight-line equivalent) → album looks profitable from year 1 in "adjusted EBITDA" and after content charge; at Tips the same album would show a ₹30 Cr hit in quarter 1 [E].

## 4.2 Company deep-dives

### 4.2.1 Saregama India (SAREGAMA · 532163)

#### (a) Management-guidance digest — Q1 FY27 call (4-Aug-2026) and Q4 FY26 call (14-May-2026), paraphrased [V SAR-Q1T, SAR-Q4T]

**Guidance (numbers management committed to):**
- **Music vertical revenue (licensing + artiste management + retail): +20–23% a year, medium term**, repeated on both calls. Management says the platform-shutdown effect "is completely over", which is why it is confident; expect "a quarter here and a quarter there" to miss, but the full year to land in range (Q4 FY26 call).
- **Music EBITDA margin (before content charge): 60–65% a year.** This guidance covers the music vertical only, "nothing else" — no group-margin guidance (Q4 call).
- **New-content investment FY27: ₹300–350 Cr, "most of that is already committed"** (Q1 call). ₹1,000 Cr committed over FY25–FY27 (AR: "INR 10,000 Mn between FY 2024-25 and FY 2026-27"). From FY28 the investment should rise only "closer to the rate of inflation", linearly not in steps, once Saregama holds a 25–30% share of new content. Management expects EBITDA and then PBT growth to converge on revenue growth after that (Q4 call).
- **Unit economics (management model, repeated in AR):** ~5-year cash payback, P&L charge over ~10 years, then 55–75 years (Q4 call) or 60–80 years (AR) of earnings at ~90% steady-state margin; implied IRR ~26%.
- **Video:** own film production (Yoodlee) discontinued; films still on the balance sheet will be released over 3–4 quarters; video "not expected to go up substantially"; focus on TV serials, digital series and short-form (Pocket Aces). All Hindi film-music ambitions now run through Bhansali Productions (BP), which "secures our A and B+ Hindi film music album requirement for the next 24 to 30 months" (Q4 call).
- **Pocket Aces:** broke even in FY26; "some amount of profit" expected in FY27 (Q1 call).
- **FY27 album slate:** *Love and War* (Bhansali; January 2027), Rajinikanth film (Kamal Haasan's company), *Paradise* (Telugu, Nani), *Naagzilla* (Dharma, Kartik Aaryan), a second BP film (Tiger Shroff). Several Q4 FY26 releases slipped into FY27, which the CEO said depressed Q4 investment (Q4 call).

**The 6–8 most important analyst Q&A points:**
1. **Royalty mechanics (Q4 call):** on newly bought content, **royalties are payable only on Hindi film music**; South Indian and Hindi non-film deals are one-time payments with no royalty overflow. Royalty provisions are made on estimated consumption and trued up when platform logs arrive — this is why the royalty line moves independently of revenue.
2. **Subscription is the growth engine (Q4 call):** revenue from free tiers was "flat" in FY26; the growth came from paid subscription. Saregama refuses to split subscription vs free ("too competitor-sensitive"). Paid penetration of internet users per Goldman Sachs: Sweden 67%, US 57%, UK 50%, Brazil/China ~19%, India <3% (Q4 call).
3. **Content-cash reconciliation (Q1 call):** an analyst noted FY26 "spent on new content" was ₹186 Cr in the Q4 deck but ₹265 Cr in the Q1 deck; management took it offline. **Resolved in 4.2.1(f) below from the audited cash-flow statement.**
4. **Other income collapse (Q1 call):** other income fell (₹14.2 Cr → ₹4.2 Cr YoY) because treasury was redeployed into "specific investments … namely Bhansali Productions".
5. **AI (Q4 call):** "AI slop" content is flooding platforms but "seeing no traction"; no market-share loss; Saregama wants pool rules that pay "only genuine IP"; it will license to AI platforms "at the right time"; new licence agreements carry protections.
6. **Dhurandhar (Q4 call):** five months after release the album still generated ~6 mn audio streams and ~11 mn YouTube views a day; Pocket Aces' footprint helped extend its life — management's proof that the marketing flywheel works.
7. **Live and superfans (Q1 call):** Carvaan Live runs at least 4 shows a month per city (Mumbai ~48 a year); devotional and diaspora tours (Arjan Dhillon US tour, Sep-2026); Luminate data cited — superfans spend ~100–105% more than average listeners.
8. **Catalogue scale:** 1,80,000+ songs growing at **~5,500 new releases a year** (Q4 call).

### 4.3.4 Collecting societies and public performance

- **IPRS FY2024-25 [V-snip loudest/filminformation quoting the IPRS annual report]:** collections **₹741.6 Cr (+42%)**; distributions **₹608.8 Cr (+21%)** — "highest in its history"; streaming income alone >₹600 Cr (+59%) per one report; CISAC: India collections +357% since 2020. **IPRS' total collections (₹742 Cr) now exceed Saregama's entire FY26 music-vertical revenue less retail (~₹745 Cr) [E]** — authors' and publishers' money is a business the size of the larger listed label.
- **PPL / Novex / RMPL (status Apr-2026) [V SPICY-PPL]:**
  - PPL surrendered its copyright-society registration in **2014**; RMPL is the registered society for sound recordings (registered Jun-2021; a Delhi HC single judge set that aside in Mar-2022, but a Division Bench stayed the order in Apr-2022, so RMPL continues to function) [V; 2021–22 dates V-snip].
  - **Delhi HC Division Bench, Azure Hospitality v PPL (Apr-2025):** PPL cannot license without being or joining a registered society; the user pays at **RMPL's tariff** pending trial. **Supreme Court stayed that direction inter partes**; SLP pending (Sagar Kumar SLP 13597/2025 tagged).
  - **Bombay HC follows Novex (Jan-2024):** an owner/assignee that is not a society **can** license; ad-interim injunctions for PPL/Novex in *Star Health* (15-Dec-2025), *Karigari* (16-Dec-2025), *Trinetra* (late Dec-2025; "the contrary decision taken by the Delhi High Court would not bind this Court"), *Yashraj Satwara* (Feb-2026), *Absolute Legends League* (Mar-2026). Appeals clubbed with the Novex appeal.
  - **So in 2026 India has a live Delhi–Bombay split** on public-performance licensing by label agents; the Supreme Court will decide. Labels collect public-performance money mainly through PPL (Saregama, Tips and others are PPL members [K]) — a final loss in the Supreme Court would push them into RMPL tariffs.
- **Calcutta HC, Vodafone Idea v IPRS (2026:CHC-OS:170-DB; Basak & Rashidi JJ; reported 8-May, uploaded 9-May-2026) [V CHC-VIL]:** appeals dismissed. A telco offering caller tunes/ringtones needs an **IPRS licence for the underlying musical and literary works**; a licence from the sound-recording owner is not enough. The Court called the 2012 Amendment a "paradigm shift": authors cannot waive their right to equal royalties for exploitation other than cinema-hall exhibition, and author licences other than to heirs or a copyright society are void. **On Saregama specifically: "Saregama does not have any legal authority or competence to grant licence"** for those underlying works; deposits made by Vodafone Idea are released to IPRS on an undertaking to refund if IPRS loses the pending suits. Saregama told the court its own dispute with Vodafone Idea had been settled and it had no further liability to IPRS. **This upgrades Part 1 §7.3 from [V-snip] to [V] and adds a fact the first pass missed: Saregama was a litigant and lost the licensing-competence point.** The economic reading: on post-2012 (and, per this reasoning, any) works where authors retain the royalty right, a user pays two licences — label and IPRS — which caps the label's share of the user's budget.

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed, and new primer issues

### 4.5.1 New insights (what an expert would add)

1. **Saregama's "licensing" grew only ~12% in FY26 — the +17% music headline leans on artiste management.** CARE's rationale splits it: licensing ~+12% (FY25 ~+13%), artiste management "nearly doubled". Pure licensing is growing about as fast as FICCI-EY's industry (+10%), not twice as fast. The 20–23% music guidance therefore depends on artiste management, brand partnerships and retail as much as on catalogue streams. Tips (pure licensing, +21% FY26, +35% Q2 FY27) is the cleaner read on the streaming market.
2. **The industry's 2025 growth was non-digital.** FICCI-EY: music +10% but digital licensing only +2%; "other licensing" (performance and publishing shares, sync, physical, exports) +23%; other income (live, artiste management, branded content) is 15% of the segment. Streaming (58% of the pie) is the slowest-growing line after the 2024–25 platform exits. The listed labels' outperformance came from share gains, short-video/social deals and non-digital lines — not from a streaming boom yet. The paywall thesis (14.4 mn → 28–30 mn subs by 2028) is still a forecast.
3. **Saregama's free cash flow after content is about −₹110 Cr on every basis, and the restated deck moved ~₹79 Cr between lines.** The FY26 deficit was funded by redeeming ₹350 Cr of FDs (much of it QIP money; ~₹101 Cr of QIP proceeds is still unspent). With the Bhansali payment (₹325 Cr) and FY27 content of ₹300–350 Cr, the QIP cushion is nearly gone. Saregama will either need FY27 CFO of ₹300 Cr+ or more debt (it has added a ₹130 Cr short-term line rated A1+). Watch Q2 FY27 cash flow.
4. **Saregama lengthened amortisation in FY23, shifting ~₹37 Cr of charges into FY28+.** The change of estimate (to 10 years) lowered amortisation by ₹5–11 Cr a year in FY23–FY27. It is disclosed and defensible, but it widens the capitalise-vs-expense gap with Tips, and FY28+ will carry a catch-up of ~₹37 Cr just as management says content spend will "flatten".
5. **The two labels' royalty burdens are an order of magnitude apart.** Tips paid ₹5.9 Cr of royalty (1.6% of revenue); Saregama ₹63.4 Cr (7.8% of music revenue) plus a ₹15 Cr brand royalty to a promoter-group entity (RPSG Resources). New Hindi film deals carry royalty, while South/non-film deals are one-time (Saregama). So Saregama's mix shift towards new Hindi film music structurally raises its royalty line.
6. **Related-party channels differ in kind.** At Saregama money flows **out** to the promoter group (brand royalty ₹15 Cr; MD pay ₹20.3 Cr, +39%). At Tips the exposure is a promoter-owned, loss-making film company (Tips Films; ₹158 Cr turnover, −₹15.8 Cr PAT) that can sell film music to the listed label under a **₹60 Cr-a-year omnibus approval** at valuer-set prices. That is equal to ~60% of Tips' FY27 content budget. Analysts should ask each quarter how much of Tips' content cost went to TFL.
7. **Labels are buying the film studios.** Saregama–Bhansali (₹325 Cr; Dec-2025/Jan-2026) and Universal Music India–Excel Entertainment (30% at ~₹2,400 Cr; Jan-2026) replace the album-by-album auction with equity-linked, formula-priced supply. This caps content inflation for the buyers and raises it for everyone else (T-Series, Tips, Sony, Zee) bidding for the remaining A-list films. Tips' FY27 content budget (+50–70%) and its "very high due to intense competition" comment fit this.
8. **Authors are taking a bigger slice — and Saregama lost the point in court.** The Calcutta HC (May-2026) held that Saregama had "no legal authority or competence" to license the underlying works it does not own. IPRS (authors and publishers) collected ₹742 Cr in FY25 (+42%), about the size of Saregama's music-licensing revenue. With dual licensing entrenched (label + IPRS), a user's music budget is split two ways. Separately, India now has a Delhi–Bombay split over whether PPL and Novex can license public performance without being a registered society; the Supreme Court will decide.
9. **AI policy could become the next s.31D.** DPIIT's December 2025 working paper favours a mandatory blanket licence for AI training with government-set royalties paid through a new collective (CRCAT). That would cap what labels can charge AI firms, just as statutory licensing would have capped streaming rates before Tips v Wynk (2019) and the August 2024 rescission. Saregama's AI-licensing optionality (it has already licensed to OpenAI for a brand use) depends on this staying voluntary.
