## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **"DFI" is a policy idea; "AIFI" is an RBI legal category; IFCI is neither any more in law.** IFCI is a **government company registered as an NBFC (NBFC-ND-SI since FY08; now an NBFC under scale-based regulation)** [V CARE]. The five RBI-regulated AIFIs are EXIM Bank, NABARD, NHB, SIDBI and NaBFID [K; NaBFID's AIFI status V NaBFID PPT]. IDBI and ICICI were DFIs that became banks (2004 and 2002) [K]. PFC/REC/IRFC/IREDA/HUDCO are government NBFCs (most are NBFC-IFC/infrastructure finance companies) — "sovereign lenders", not AIFIs. Never call IFCI "an AIFI" and never call NaBFID "an NBFC".
2. **IFCI's two GNPA numbers are both right.** RBI-basis **GNPA 95.79% (Mar-26), 95.68% (Jun-26)** [V AR, CARE]; Ind AS **gross Stage 3 98.28% (Mar-26), 98.23% (Jun-26)** [V ICRA]. Stage 3 includes reclassified security receipts and accrued income on Stage 3, so it is wider. Use one basis per chart; the primer uses 95.79% (correct, RBI basis).
3. **"Loan book" for IFCI ≠ performing loans.** Gross loans ₹4,169 Cr (Mar-26) [V AR note: gross carrying amount 4,168.99]; **Stage 1 = ₹0**; Stage 2 ₹71.6 Cr; the **standard net loan book is ~₹70 Cr (Jun-26)** [V ICRA]. CARE's "AUM" (₹4,367 → ₹4,169 → ₹3,981 Cr) is the gross book, ~96% of it bad [V CARE].
4. **Two net worths for IFCI standalone; one is negative in regulatory terms.** Reported standalone net worth ₹1,785.5 Cr (Mar-26) / ₹1,797 Cr (Jun-26) [V AR / ICRA]; **tangible net worth ₹809 Cr** (CARE's adjusted figure) [V CARE]; but **CRAR −18.78% (Mar-26), −17.58% (Jun-26)** because regulatory capital deducts investments in subsidiaries and net DTA ("high group exposures") [V CARE, ICRA]. CRAR has been negative **every quarter since Dec-2020** [V ICRA]. Government-owned NBFC minimums: **Tier 1 10%, CRAR 15%** [V ICRA].
5. **IFCI consolidated profit is mostly NOT IFCI's.** FY26 consolidated PAT **₹434.71 Cr = ₹180.87 Cr to owners + ₹253.84 Cr to non-controlling interests** [V AR]. The primer's "~₹435 Cr net profit" is the total; IFCI shareholders' share is ₹181 Cr. Screener's EPS (₹0.67) and P/E (88.9) are on the owners' share.
6. **Screener's IFCI consolidated "book value ₹33.2" excludes NCI.** Equity attributable to parent **₹8,944 Cr**; NCI ₹6,560 Cr; total equity ₹15,505 Cr [V AR]. P/B therefore = **1.78×** on parent equity (₹15,945 Cr ÷ ₹8,944 Cr) — not the primer's 2.3–2.5×.
7. **SHCIL's profit is largely the NSE dividend.** IFCI consolidated dividend income ₹389.94 Cr vs standalone ₹186 Cr [V AR]; SHCIL standalone "turnover" ₹1,137 Cr vs SHCIL consolidated revenue from operations ₹749 Cr [V AR]. The ~₹385–390 Cr gap matches NSE's FY25 dividend (₹35/share [K]) × ~10.99 crore shares [E]. SHCIL's FY26 PAT ₹526 Cr (standalone) / ₹538 Cr (consolidated) therefore contains roughly **₹300+ Cr of after-tax NSE dividend** [E]; the custody/DP/e-stamping franchise earns perhaps **₹150–250 Cr** after tax [E]. Do not value SHCIL's "core" business on its headline PAT and then add the NSE stake again — that double-counts.
8. **IFCI's FY25 "₹7,011 Cr total comprehensive income" was an NSE mark-up, not a profit.** OCI ₹6,662 Cr in FY25 = fair-value gain on SHCIL's NSE shares (FVTOCI ₹7,156.75 Cr gross) [V AR]. It is why consolidated reserves jumped from ₹2,045 Cr (Mar-24) to ₹5,996 Cr (Mar-25) [V screener]. Never present it as earnings.
9. **IFCI's standalone "revenue" is not interest on live loans.** FY26 standalone revenue ₹897.7 Cr = interest ₹341.3 Cr (largely income recognised on Stage 3/recoveries and treasury) + dividends ₹186 Cr + rent ₹53.6 Cr + fees ₹79.1 Cr + fair-value gains ₹237.7 Cr [V AR]. **NII is negative on a cash basis** (interest-bearing assets far below ₹4,185 Cr of debt incl. accrued interest) [V ICRA]. Lender KPIs like NIM or credit cost/AUM are meaningless for IFCI; use recoveries, liquidity vs debt service, and SOTP.
10. **NaBFID's "sanctions" are three different numbers.** FY26 annual sanctions ₹1,33,020 Cr; **cumulative** sanctions ₹3,36,445 Cr (Mar-26) / ₹3,59,357 Cr (Jun-26); cumulative "in-principle" sanctions much higher (~₹5.7–6.8 lakh Cr, [V-chart]) [V NaBFID]. Loan book outstanding ₹1,16,950 Cr (Mar-26, incl. NCDs and non-fund-based) vs "credit outstanding" ₹1,15,424 Cr (Mar-26) vs total credit exposure outstanding ₹1,28,185 Cr (Jun-26) [V]. The primer's "over ₹2.3 lakh crore sanctioned" was the Jul-2025 cumulative figure; it is stale.

# PART 1 — INDUSTRY DOSSIER: India's development financial institutions, with IFCI as the lens

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **India now runs two DFI systems at once.** One is the **legacy system**: IFCI (1948), ICICI (1955, became a bank 2002), IDBI (1964, became a bank 2004), plus the state financial corporations. It failed after 1991 when cheap statutory funding was withdrawn [K]. The other is the **sovereign-refinance and new-DFI system**: NABARD (1982), EXIM (1982), NHB (1988), SIDBI (1990), and **NaBFID (Act of 2021)** [K; NaBFID V]. The first group is being wound down or sold (IFCI consolidation; IDBI to Fairfax on 14-Oct-2026). The second is growing at 15–95% a year (SIDBI loans +16.8%, NaBFID loan book +95% in FY26) [V].
- **IFCI is a holding company wearing a lender's licence.** No new loan sanctioned or disbursed in FY22–FY26 or Q1 FY27 [V CARE, AR]; standard net loan book ~₹70 Cr [V ICRA]; ~₹4,185 Cr of debt incl. accrued interest [V ICRA]. Its value is **52.86% of StockHolding (SHCIL)**, which owned **~4.44% of NSE** going into NSE's Sep-2026 IPO [V AR, ICRA], plus a **government-scheme advisory franchise** (Project Management Agency for 10 of 14 PLI schemes; nodal agency for the Sugar Development Fund; India Semiconductor Mission and PM E-DRIVE roles) [V AR].
- **The market prices IFCI on a sum-of-the-parts and on policy news, not on earnings.** M-cap ₹15,945 Cr (23-Oct-2026) vs FY26 owners' PAT ₹181 Cr (P/E ~88×) and standalone PAT ₹52 Cr (P/E ~301×) [V screener, AR]. My rough SOTP at NSE's ₹1,670 is **~₹8,000–13,000 Cr before any holding-company discount** [E, §B1] — i.e. even after a ~45% fall from the Sep-2026 peak, IFCI (₹15,945 Cr) trades above its look-through value.
- **The state decided to build a new DFI rather than fix the old one.** NaBFID: 100% GoI, ₹20,000 Cr paid-up equity, ₹5,000 Cr grant to lower its cost of funds, sovereign guarantee on foreign-currency borrowing, 10-year tax exemption, AAA domestic rating, **CRAR 45.13%, nil NPA, ROE ~11%** (Jun-26) [V NaBFID PPT]. IFCI: CRAR −17.58%, GNPA 95.68%, CARE BB / ICRA B+ [V]. Same owner, opposite outcomes. The difference is funding design and capital, not mission.
- **Funding is the whole game for a non-deposit lender.** NaBFID raised a debut **US$750 m 10-year bond at 6.122% (UST + 105 bp)** on 30-Sep-2026 [V]; it raised ₹59,849 Cr of rupee bonds in FY24–Q1 FY27 [V-chart]. IFCI has **not mobilised fresh resources** since FY22; it services debt from recoveries, asset sales and past GoI equity (₹2,000 Cr over FY20–FY25) [V AR, ICRA]. The same sovereign owner gets AAA pricing for one entity and sub-investment-grade for the other.
- **The PSU lenders that worked (PFC, REC, IRFC, HUDCO, IREDA) are captive-sector financiers, not general DFIs.** They lend to one sovereign-linked sector (power, rail, housing/urban, renewables), carry GNPA of 0.7–3.8% (PFC 0.66%, IREDA 3.75%) [V screener], and trade at P/E 4–14× and P/B 0.7–2.0× [V screener]. IFCI's general-industrial project-finance book had no such anchor; that is why it failed.
- **Policy direction is consolidation and fewer, larger sovereign financiers.** Budget 2026-27: restructure PFC/REC "to achieve scale and improve efficiency in public sector NBFCs" [V-snip outlookmoney]. IFCI group consolidation (pending GoI approval) [V ICRA]. IDBI privatised [V-ann]. SIDBI recapitalised [V]. This is the frame for every IFCI scenario.

## 1. What the sub-sector does: products, value chain, where value is captured

### 1.1 What a DFI does (the textbook function, and what Indian DFIs actually do in 2026)
- **Core job:** provide **long-tenor, often fixed-rate, project and term finance** that banks (funded by short-term deposits) will not provide, or provide only with severe asset-liability mismatch. Also: refinance to banks for priority sectors (NABARD, SIDBI, NHB), export and overseas-investment credit (EXIM), credit enhancement and take-out finance (NaBFID's Partial Credit Enhancement launched Jul-2025 [V-chart timeline]), equity/venture funds (SIDBI Fund of Funds, IFCI Venture), and **government-scheme administration** (IFCI as PLI Project Management Agency; NABARD for RIDF; SIDBI for MSME schemes) [K; IFCI roles V AR].
- **Funding sources:** equity and grants from the state; tax-free/infrastructure bonds; market bonds and CP; bank lines; ECBs/global bonds (sometimes with sovereign guarantee); for NABARD/SIDBI, **deposits placed by banks that miss priority-sector-lending targets** (RIDF and MSME Refinance Fund deposits) — a quasi-statutory cheap funding source [K]. SIDBI's balance sheet carries ₹2,05,037 Cr of "deposits" (Mar-26) [V], which are largely these PSL-shortfall deposits [K].

### 1.3 IFCI's value chain today (where its rupees actually come from)
1. **Legacy loan book → recoveries.** NPA/SR/project-equity recoveries ₹580 Cr (FY25), ₹536 Cr (FY26), ₹199 Cr (Q1 FY27) on ICRA's definition; CARE counts ₹576 Cr (FY26) and ₹202 Cr (Q1 FY27); the AR splits FY26 into ₹397 Cr from NPAs + SRs and ₹140 Cr from unquoted investments [V]. These recoveries are the main cash source for debt service (₹548 Cr serviced in FY26: ₹225 Cr principal + ₹323 Cr interest) [V AR].
2. **Holding-company income.** Dividends ₹186 Cr standalone (FY26) [V AR]; consolidated earnings of SHCIL (₹538 Cr PAT FY26, of which IFCI's economic share is 52.86%) [V AR].
3. **Government-scheme advisory / nodal fees.** Fees & commission ₹79.1 Cr standalone FY26 (₹73.2 Cr FY25) [V AR]; consolidated fees & commission ₹632 Cr (mostly SHCIL custody/DP/e-stamping) [V AR].
4. **Real estate.** Rental income ₹53.6 Cr standalone (tenants include SBI, SBI Life, Grid Controller, BPCL, NPS Trust in IFCI Tower) [V AR]; IIDL (100%) is a real-estate subsidiary.
5. **Treasury.** Average deployment ₹732 Cr in FY26 (₹1,145 Cr FY25) at 7.28%; income ₹53.3 Cr [V AR].
- **Where value is captured:** almost entirely in SHCIL (market infrastructure: custody, depository participant, e-stamping CRA) and SHCIL's NSE stake. The lending licence currently destroys value (negative carry on ₹4,185 Cr of debt; finance cost ₹416 Cr FY26 vs interest income ₹341 Cr) [V AR].

### 2.2 India's infrastructure-finance need (the demand side for the new DFI model)

## 6. Business-model archetypes and their economics

| Archetype | Example(s) | Funding edge | Asset edge | ROA / ROE (FY26) | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Statutory refinance AIFI** | NABARD, SIDBI, NHB | PSL-shortfall deposits, AAA, govt equity | Lends to banks/NBFCs (low credit risk) | SIDBI ROA 0.92%, ROE ~15.4% [V ROA; ROE E = 5,493 ÷ avg NW 35,652] | Thin margins; capital calls as book grows |
| **2. New infra DFI** | NaBFID | ₹25k Cr free capital, sovereign guarantee on FX debt, tax holiday | Infra project loans (long tenor) | ROA ~3.3%, ROE ~11.5% [V-chart] | Concentration, greenfield execution risk, ALM; nil NPA so far |
| **3. Captive-sector sovereign NBFC** | PFC, REC, IRFC, HUDCO, IREDA | AAA quasi-sovereign bonds | Lends to state utilities / Railways / urban bodies | PFC ROE 20.7%, REC 20.1%, IRFC 12.8%, HUDCO 20.0%, IREDA 15.6% [V screener] | State-utility payment risk, concentration, rate cycle |
| **4. Export credit agency** | EXIM | Govt equity + bonds; GoI-backed LoCs | Export credit, sovereign lines of credit | ROA ~2% [E: 4,273 ÷ ~₹2.2 lakh Cr book] | Country risk |
| **5. Legacy general-industrial DFI (failed)** | IFCI (and pre-2004 IDBI) | **None after 1991** — market-rate funding, no deposits | Corporate/project loans to private industry | IFCI standalone ROA ~0.5%, ROE ~2.9% (RONW incl. OCI) [V AR] | Adverse selection, high cost of funds, NPA spiral |
| **6. DFI converted to bank** | ICICI Bank (2002), IDBI Bank (2004) | Retail deposits | Universal banking | IDBI ROE 14.1% [V screener] | Legacy NPAs (IDBI's FY16–FY20 losses) |

- **The key economic insight:** a non-deposit lender's spread is set by its rating. AAA sovereign lenders fund at ~7% and lend to captive sovereign-linked borrowers at ~9–10%, earning ~2–3% spreads with ~0.1–1% credit cost. IFCI, funded at ~10–11% without sovereign comfort, had to lend to riskier private projects at 12–14% — and adverse selection did the rest [E/K].
- **IDBI is the counterfactual that worked (eventually):** it converted to a bank in 2004, suffered huge losses FY16–FY20 (cumulative screener net losses ≈ ₹44,476 Cr FY16–FY20 [E, sum of screener rows]), was recapitalised by LIC (2019) and GoI, and is now being sold for ₹52,878 Cr for 60.72% [V-ann]. IFCI never got a deposit franchise.

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Who regulates whom
- **IFCI:** RBI as an NBFC (scale-based regulation, SBR; government NBFCs were brought under the full NBFC prudential framework in stages from 2022 [K]). Required: **Tier 1 ≥10%, CRAR ≥15%** for government-owned NBFCs [V ICRA]. IFCI has been in breach since Dec-2020 [V ICRA]. It is also a "government company" u/s 2(45) Companies Act since **April 2015**, when GoI acquired ₹60 Cr of preference shares from other holders [V CARE]. Subject to C&AG audit and RTI (116 RTI applications in FY26) [V AR]. Listed equity → SEBI LODR; listed bonds → SEBI.
- **AIFIs (NABARD, SIDBI, EXIM, NHB, NaBFID):** regulated and supervised by RBI under their own Acts plus RBI's AIFI prudential directions (capital adequacy under Basel III-type norms — NaBFID's regulatory minimum CRAR is **9%** [V NaBFID PPT]; leverage ratio minimum 4% [V]; IRACP norms; ICAAP; investment classification). RBI consolidated its directions by entity class during 2025–26 [K; verify the exact title of the AIFI directions — see §H].
- **NaBFID Act, 2021** (passed by Parliament Mar-2021, institution founded Apr-2021) [V-chart timeline; K]: authorised capital ₹1 lakh Cr; GoI initial ownership 100% (Act allows GoI to dilute to 26% [K]); ₹20,000 Cr paid-up; ₹5,000 Cr grant; **sovereign guarantee on foreign-currency liabilities at a concessional fee; reimbursement of FX hedging cost; tax exemption for the first 10 years** [V NaBFID PPT]. RBI granted **AIFI status** in 2022 [V-chart timeline; K: Mar-2022].
- **ECL transition:** RBI's final expected-credit-loss directions were issued **27-Apr-2026, effective 1-Apr-2027** for commercial banks (excl. SFBs, payments banks, LABs) [V-snip KPMG]. The Oct-2025 draft and the Governor's 1-Oct-2025 policy statement covered **AIFIs too**, with a glide path to 31-Mar-2031 for existing books [V-snip Outlook Business]; whether the final text covers AIFIs is **not confirmed** [§H]. IFCI and the PSU NBFCs already report under **Ind AS 109 ECL** (NBFCs moved to Ind AS from FY19–FY20) [K], so ECL is not a new shock for them; it is relevant for SIDBI/NABARD/EXIM/NaBFID (which report under Indian GAAP/RBI formats) [K].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **1948–1990: the nation-building era.** IFCI (1-Jul-1948, IFC Act) [V CARE], ICICI (1955), IDBI (1964), state financial corporations; funded through government loans, RBI's National Industrial Credit (Long Term Operations) Fund and bonds subscribed by banks under statutory liquidity rules [K]. DFIs supplied the bulk of long-term project finance [K].
2. **1991–2004: the funding rug is pulled.** Narasimham Committee reforms withdrew concessional funding; SLR eligibility of DFI bonds was cut; DFIs had to borrow at market rates [K]. **Narasimham-II (1998) and the Khan Working Group (1998)** recommended DFIs convert into banks or NBFCs [K]. **ICICI reverse-merged into ICICI Bank (2002); IDBI became IDBI Bank (2004)** [K]. IFCI corporatised in 1993 [V CARE], renamed IFCI Ltd Oct-1999 [V CARE]; NPAs mounted.
3. **2002–2008: IFCI's first rescue.** GoI-led restructuring of liabilities in 2002-03 [V CARE]; IFCI **resumed lending in 2008** (corporate and project loans) [V CARE] — into the infrastructure/real-estate boom.
4. **2008–2018: the second boom and bust.** Revenue peaked ~₹4,465 Cr consolidated in FY16 [V screener]; loans to infrastructure, real estate, telecom, power went bad in the twin-balance-sheet crisis (IBC 2016) [K]. IFCI losses FY17 (−₹308 Cr), FY19–FY23 [V screener].
5. **2015: IFCI becomes a government company** [V CARE]; GoI stake 56.42% by Mar-2020 [V ICRA].
6. **2020–2022: collapse into run-off.** CRAR negative from Dec-2020 [V ICRA]; FY21 loss −₹1,912 Cr, FY22 −₹1,761 Cr consolidated [V screener]; **lending stopped in FY22** "due to capital and liquidity constraints" [V ICRA]. GoI infusions ₹200/200/100 Cr [V screener].
7. **2021: the state builds a new DFI.** NaBFID Act (Mar-2021) [K/V-chart]; Budget 2021-22 also announced a DFI for infra — IFCI was not chosen [K].
8. **2023–2025: stabilisation.** Recoveries and write-backs return IFCI to small profits (FY24 consolidated PAT ₹241 Cr; FY25 ₹349 Cr) [V screener]; GoI ₹500 Cr/yr infusions FY23–FY25 [V]; GoI-appointed independent directors (May-2023) [V CARE]; Nov-2024 consolidation in-principle; Rahul Bhave appointed MD (Mar-2025) [V-snip The Week 21-Mar-2025; AR signatory V].
9. **2025–2026: the market-infrastructure re-rating.** FY25 NSE fair-value gain (₹6,662 Cr OCI) [V AR]; 14-Jul-2025 board recommendation; NSE DRHP (17-Jun-2026), SEBI nod (4-Sep-2026), listing (24-Sep-2026) [V-snip]; IFCI stock 74% rally in 13 sessions (May-2025) [V-snip BS headline], >₹100 (Sep-2026) [V-snip], ₹59 (Oct-2026) [V].

**Cycle shape for IFCI's stock:** policy/NSE-headline driven spikes and retracements. The primer notes "~100%+ swings within a year" [primer]; the Sep–Oct 2026 move (₹108 high → ₹59.2) is a ~45% drawdown in ~7 weeks [V screener H/L; E].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert leads with)

1. **IFCI's legacy bad book is a run-off, not a turnaround.** GNPA 95.68%, standard net loans ₹70 Cr, no sanction/disbursement for five years [V]. Recoveries (~₹550 Cr/yr) barely cover interest; GNPA stock fell only ₹104 Cr in FY26 [V]. PCR ~72–73% means the residual net NPA of ₹574 Cr is the remaining recovery upside, and Q1 FY27 already brought ~₹200 Cr [V]. The lending business has negative value on a standalone basis.
2. **Sovereign capital is the lifeline — and the government has stopped writing cheques.** ₹2,000 Cr infused FY20–FY25; **nil in FY26, nil earmarked for FY27** [V ICRA]; GoI stake 56.42% → 72.57% [V]. CARE's rating "derive[s] strength from majority ownership" and its positive triggers are "substantial equity infusion by GoI" or merger completion [V CARE]. The state's revealed preference is consolidation, not recapitalisation.
3. **The value is in the subsidiaries — above all SHCIL and its NSE stake — and the consolidation is the only path to reach it.** SHCIL FY26: revenue from operations ₹749 Cr, PAT ₹538 Cr, net assets ₹13,760 Cr [V AR]; 4.44% of a ₹4.13 lakh Cr exchange = ~₹18,350 Cr pre-sale [E]. IFCI's 52.86% look-through ≈ ₹9,700 Cr pre-tax [E]. Until GoI approves the merger, that value sits behind a 47% minority and a holding-company structure, while IFCI's own ₹4,185 Cr debt sits at the parent [V].
4. **The DFI model was revived — but in a new vehicle with a different funding design.** NaBFID's free capital (₹25,000 Cr), sovereign guarantee on FX debt, tax holiday and AAA rating give it ROE ~11.5% at CRAR 45% and nil NPAs, with a loan book doubling every year [V]. IFCI is the cautionary tale that justified building NaBFID from scratch [primer; E].
5. **Funding cost decides who survives among non-deposit sovereign lenders.** AAA captive-sector lenders (PFC/REC/IRFC/HUDCO) earn 13–21% ROE at GNPA <1–4% [V screener]; IFCI's sub-investment-grade legacy debt (~11% cost [E]) against zero earning assets produces negative cash NII [V ICRA]. The sub-sector's history is the post-1991 loss of cheap funding.
6. **Policy is consolidating the state's financial-company estate.** PFC–REC merger (RBI NOC 15-Oct-2026) [V-ann]; IDBI sold to Fairfax (14-Oct-2026) [V-ann]; SIDBI recapitalised [V]; IFCI group consolidation pending [V]. Fewer, bigger, better-rated sovereign financiers is the direction.
7. **IFCI's equity is a retail-driven policy-and-NSE proxy.** ~9.71 lakh shareholders; FII 3.42%, DII 1.66% (Sep-26) [V screener]; 52-week range ₹46.2–108 [V]. It trades on headlines (NSE IPO, merger) far more than on quarterly numbers.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Healthy DFI / sovereign lender:**
PAT = Loan book × (yield − cost of funds) + free-capital earnings + fees − opex − credit cost − tax
= AUM × NIM + fees − opex − credit cost − tax.

**IFCI today (holding company in run-off):**
Owners' PAT = [Recoveries recognised (write-backs + Stage 3 interest) + dividends + rent + fees + fair-value gains] − finance cost on legacy debt − opex − new impairments − tax + 52.86% × SHCIL PAT + other subsidiaries.
**Cash equation (what matters):** Cash in (recoveries + dividends + rent + fees + asset sales + GoI equity) − cash out (interest + principal + opex) ≥ 0.

### C.1 Worked example 1 — a NaBFID-type infra DFI, ₹1,00,000 Cr book [E — ILLUSTRATIVE, calibrated to NaBFID FY26]
- Funded by ₹25,000 Cr free capital + ₹80,000 Cr borrowings at 7.0%.
- Yield 9.0% on ₹1,00,000 Cr = ₹9,000 Cr; interest cost ₹5,600 Cr → NII ₹3,400 Cr (NIM ~3.4%).
- Opex at 8% cost-to-income ≈ ₹280 Cr; credit cost (standard-asset provisions) 0.4% = ₹400 Cr → PBT ₹2,720 Cr; tax nil (holiday) → **PAT ~₹2,700 Cr; ROA ~2.6%, ROE ~10.8%**. Compare NaBFID actual FY26 PAT ₹3,037 Cr, ROE ~11.5% [V-chart].

### C.2 Worked example 2 — IFCI standalone FY26 actual (₹ Cr) [V AR lines]
- Interest income 341.3 + dividends 186.0 + rent 53.6 + fees 79.1 + FV gains 237.7 + other 26.4 = **924.1**
- − finance cost 416.1 − impairment 279.3 − employees 106.4 − D&A 24.4 − other 52.3 = PBT **45.5**; deferred-tax credit 6.2 → **PAT 51.7**.
- **Cash view [E]:** FY26 standalone operating cash flow −₹67 Cr; financing 0 [V screener]; debt service ₹548 Cr paid from recoveries/liquidity [V AR].

### C.3 Worked example 3 — IFCI consolidated → owners' PAT (FY26, ₹ Cr) [V AR; split E]
- Consolidated PAT 434.7 = owners 180.9 + NCI 253.8.
- NCI ≈ 47.14% × SHCIL PAT ~₹538 Cr ≈ ₹253.6 Cr → consistent [E] — so **SHCIL generates ~₹538 Cr of the ₹435 Cr group PAT; the rest of the group (parent + other subs, after eliminations of SHCIL dividends to IFCI) loses ~₹100 Cr** [E].

### C.4 Worked example 4 — what a merger does to IFCI's equation [E — ILLUSTRATIVE]
- Pre-merger IFCI shareholders own 52.86% of SHCIL; post-merger they would own a share of a combined entity in which SHCIL minorities (47.14%) get IFCI shares at a swap ratio. If SHCIL is valued at ~₹20,000 Cr (NSE stake ~₹16,000–18,000 Cr post-tax/after any sale + core ~₹3,000–4,000 Cr) [E], minorities' 47.14% ≈ ₹9,400 Cr would be paid in new IFCI shares — **at today's ₹59 that is ~160 crore new shares (+59% on 269.4 crore)** [E].
