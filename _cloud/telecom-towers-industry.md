## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **"Revenue" includes a ~36% energy pass-through.** Q2 FY27 revenue ₹85,150 Mn = **sharing (rental) ₹54,410 Mn (63.9%) + energy reimbursements ₹30,696 Mn (36.0%)** + ₹44 Mn equipment [V QR]. FY26: sharing ₹2,09,280 Mn + energy ₹1,15,495 Mn of ₹3,24,931 Mn [V QR Q4FY26]. The energy line is billed at roughly cost and currently runs at a **loss** (trap 2). Grow/shrink comparisons, margins and EV/sales must say which revenue. Altius reports only **"Adjusted Revenue"**, which **excludes** pass-through and site-rent reimbursements — so compare Altius ₹12,262 Cr to Indus **sharing** revenue ₹20,928 Cr, never to Indus's ₹32,493 Cr.
2. **Energy margin is negative.** Energy margin = (energy reimbursement − energy cost) ÷ energy reimbursement. Indus: **−3.6% Q2 FY27, −4.6% Q1 FY27, −4.8% Q2 FY26** (management figures) [V Q2 call]; FY26 ≈ **−3.9%** [E: (1,15,495 − 1,19,956) ÷ 1,15,495; quarterly energy costs from QR]. So the "pass-through" costs Indus ~₹450 Cr a year. Energy is a **margin drag, not a profit centre**, and diesel prices (West Asia shock: Delhi retail diesel ₹87.67 → ₹95.20/litre in four May-2026 hikes; bulk diesel +₹22/litre on 20-Mar-2026 [V-snip]) matter through this line.
3. **Three EBITDAs (Ind AS 116).** (a) **Reported EBITDA** (post-Ind AS 116: site-land lease rentals are NOT in opex; they appear as depreciation of right-of-use assets + interest on lease liabilities) = ₹46,384 Mn Q2 FY27, 54.5% [V]. (b) **Adjusted (lease-adjusted) EBITDA** = reported EBITDA − repayment of lease liabilities incl. interest = **₹35,059 Mn Q2 FY27 (41.2% of revenue)**; FY26 ₹1,38,629 Mn (42.7%) [V QR]. (c) Screener "operating profit" ₹4,590 Cr Q2 FY27 (54%) — close to (a). **Global towercos report pre-IFRS 16-style "Adjusted EBITDA" that also deducts ground rent** — compare their margins to (b), not (a). Lease-adjusted EBITDA ÷ sharing revenue = **~66% FY26** for Indus vs Altius cash EBITDA ÷ adjusted revenue **~68%** [E].
4. **Net cash vs net debt — both are "right".** Indus Sep-26: **net debt WITH lease liabilities ₹1,75,158 Mn (₹17,516 Cr)**; lease liabilities ₹2,09,520 Mn; **net CASH WITHOUT leases ₹34,362 Mn (₹3,436 Cr)** [V QR]. Mar-26: ₹1,52,936 Mn / net cash ₹49,124 Mn. Screener shows "borrowings ₹22,059 Cr" (= lease liabilities + ~₹1,037 Cr short-term borrowings) and investments ₹4,423 Cr. The primer's "net cash ~₹4,900 Cr — a rarity" is true only ex-leases. **For EV, use net debt incl. leases with reported EBITDA (6.4–6.7×), or net cash ex-leases with lease-adjusted EBITDA (~7.3×)** — never mix bases.
5. **FY25 and Q3 FY25 profits are inflated by Vi write-backs; FY23 is depressed by provisions.** FY25 EBITDA ₹2,08,447 Mn (69.2%) and PAT ₹99,317 Mn include **~₹51 bn of reversal of doubtful-debt allowances** (cash-flow statement: allowances (net) **−₹50,771 Mn** FY25) [V QR Q4FY26; V Q4FY26 call]. Q3 FY25 alone: EBITDA ₹69,971 Mn (92.7% margin on screener). FY23 EBITDA ₹97,670 Mn (34.4%) and PAT ₹20,400 Mn after big Vi provisions [V QR Q4FY24]. Smaller write-backs: ~₹0.9 bn Q1 FY26, ~₹2.0 bn Q2 FY26 (cash-flow line −₹1,900 Mn), ~₹2.3 bn Q4 FY25 [V calls]. **Normalised FY26 growth: EBITDA +11.4%, PAT +13%** (management) [V Q4FY26 call]. Q2 FY27 YoY: EBITDA +0.6% reported, **+5.2% ex write-back**; PAT −2.2% reported, **+6.4% ex** [V].
6. **"Tenancy ratio" has at least four versions.** Indus: **closing sharing factor 1.63** (Sep-26) and **average sharing factor 1.62** (Q2 FY27) on macro towers only; lean co-locations (14,037) excluded; "including leaner sites" co-locations = 4,54,190 (management quotes "~454,200") [V QR; V call]. Altius quotes "1.2×" on a sites base that includes IBS/small cells (258k+ sites, 315k+ tenancies → 1.22×) [V Altius]. Global towercos quote "tenants per tower" (Helios 2.17×) or "lease-up". Keep the basis.
7. **Tower counts: Indus vs DoT vs Altius are not additive.** DoT counted **8.43 lakh mobile towers and 31.44 lakh BTS at 31-Oct-2025** [V-snip DoT year-end review]. DoT "towers" include operator-captive and BSNL sites and possibly poles/roof-tops; Indus 2.60 lakh (Dec-25) + Altius ~2.58 lakh = ~5.2 lakh = ~62% of DoT's count [E]. Do not compute market shares from mixed dates without saying so.
8. **The Airtel tower purchase was ~10,100 towers in Mar-2025, not "~16,000 in 2024".** Business Transfer Agreement 7-Feb-2025; completed **24-Mar-2025**; **10,093 macro towers + 2,179 lean co-locations** (initially booked as 10,380 + 2,226, trued up in Q1 FY26) [V QR Q2FY27 note 4]. Consideration ~₹1,982 Cr (₹19,820 Mn in FY25 + ₹610 Mn in FY26 per related-party note) [V AR FY26]. FY25 tower additions (+29,569) therefore include ~10.4k acquired towers; organic adds were ~19k [E]. (The ~16,000 figure circulated in early-2025 press for Airtel + Hexacom; the closing count is lower.)
9. **Shareholding "promoter" = Airtel.** Screener promoter 51.26% (Sep-26) vs primer "~48–50%". Airtel crossed 50.005% via Indus's buyback of 5.68 crore shares in Q2 FY25 (₹2,640 Cr at ₹465 [K for price]) [V QR], then crept to 51.03% (Sep-25) and 51.26% (Mar-26) [V screener]. Airtel consolidates Indus line by line, so **Airtel's consolidated revenue includes Indus's revenue from Vi, Jio and BSNL** (and eliminates Airtel's own rent).
10. **Two "ROCE"s in the same quarter.** The Q2 FY27 call says "pretax ROCE 25.3% and post-tax ROE 19.2%"; the Quarterly Report table says **pre-tax ROCE (LTM) 19.2%, pre-tax ROE 25.3%, post-tax ROE 18.8%** [V]. The CFO misspoke; use the QR (19.2% pre-tax ROCE on capital employed that includes lease liabilities). Screener ROCE 19.5%, ROE 19.1%.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **A landlord duopoly renting to a three-and-a-half-player telecom market.**
  - Indus: **2,70,480 macro towers, 4,40,153 macro co-locations, closing tenancy 1.63** (30-Sep-2026) [V QR Q2FY27].
  - Altius (Brookfield-sponsored InvIT; Summit Digitel = ex-Reliance Jio towers, Elevar = ex-ATC India, Crest Digitel = IBS/small cells): **258k+ sites, 315k+ tenancies, ~1.2×** (31-Mar-2026) [V Altius pres 11-May-2026].
  - Together ≈ **5.3 lakh sites and ~7.5 lakh tenancies** — roughly **80% of India's ~9.3 lakh macro tenancies** (Analysys Mason: 928k at Dec-2025) [E from V].
  - Customers: Bharti Airtel (+Hexacom), Reliance Jio, Vodafone Idea (Vi), BSNL/MTNL. Private three hold **92.7%** of 1,300 Mn mobile subscribers (Sep-2026) [V QR Q2FY27].
- **The economics are a fixed-cost annuity whose lever is tenancy.**
  - Indus sharing revenue per tower per month **₹66,532** and per co-location **₹41,036** (Q2 FY27) [V]. A new tower costs **~₹20–25 lakh** (management guidance cited by Nomura on the Q2 call) [V call].
  - Sharing revenue per tower has **fallen** from ₹80,467 (FY22) to ₹66,995 (FY26) as the tenancy ratio fell from 1.81 to 1.62 [V QR Q4FY24, Q4FY26] — the result of exits (Vi network consolidation) and of building single-tenant towers for Airtel.
  - Q2 FY27 is the first uptick in closing tenancy (1.62 → 1.63) after ~6 quarters flat, driven by "a pickup in rollouts by a major customer" (Vi) [V call].
- **The sector's biggest swing factor is Vodafone Idea's solvency, now government-backed.**
  - Government owns **49.02%** of Vi (since Apr/Jun-2025) [V screener; V-sib]. Vi's AGR dues were reassessed down from **₹87,695 Cr to ₹64,046 Cr** with a 5-year moratorium in 2026 [V-sib telecom-operators.html]; Vi booked a **+₹51,970 Cr** profit in the Mar-2026 quarter (the reassessment gain) [V screener].
  - Vi plans **₹45,000 Cr of capex over three years**; Q1 FY27 capex only ~₹1,930 Cr; guided ~₹9,100 Cr over Q2–Q3 FY27 (~3,500 4G sites/month); first ₹6,400 Cr bank tranche raised; an SBI-led consortium is still assessing ~₹35,000 Cr [V-snip, 7-Nov-2026 preview]. Q2 FY27 loss ₹5,975 Cr on ₹11,884 Cr revenue [V screener].
  - For towercos this means **(a) collections risk largely cured** (Indus trade receivables ₹4,794 Cr at Sep-26 vs ₹5,305 Cr Dec-25 [V]; FY25 write-back ~₹5,100 Cr) and **(b) Vi is the main source of second-tenant growth** (analysts: up to ~15,000 incremental Indus tenancies FY27–28 [V-snip telecomlive]).
- **Jio's contract renewals are the new risk.** Many Indus site contracts signed H2-2016/H1-2017 (Jio launch) come up **late-2026 to early-2027** (Jefferies) [V-snip]. Jio's own towers sit in Altius (anchor MSA ~30 years); Macquarie flags migration risk; MOSL builds ~5k exits into FY27; Nomura says Indus is worth buying "even if it loses all Jio tenancies" [V-snip]. Indus: "As of now, we continue to operate the tenancy portfolio with all our customers" [V Q2 call].
- **Airtel controls the biggest landlord — and is in-sourcing tenancies to it.**
  - Airtel group revenue to Indus FY26 ≈ **₹20,994 Cr GST-inclusive** (Airtel ₹19,583 Cr + fellow subsidiaries incl. Hexacom ₹1,411 Cr) ≈ **~55% of Indus revenue ex-GST** [E from V AR FY26 related-party note].
  - Indus's Q1 FY27 growth came partly from "movement of their [customers'] expired tenancy portfolios to Indus" — i.e., Airtel shifting tenancies off rival towercos (Altius/Elevar) to Indus as contracts expire [V Q1 call]. That is margin-accretive for Indus but raises the related-party question.
- **Capital returns restarted; capital structure is the next debate.**
  - FY26 dividend ₹14/share = **~100% of FY26 FCF** (₹3,743 Cr company FCF) [V QR; V Q4 call]. No formal minimum payout; board decides annually [V Q4 call].
  - Indus is **net cash ex-leases (₹3,436 Cr)** and **0.97× net debt incl. leases / LTM EBITDA** [V QR]. Global towercos run 3.4–6×+ (Helios 3.4× [V-snip]; Altius net debt/AUM 45%, ~5× cash EBITDA [E]). A shareholder on the Q2 call pushed for an InvIT structure, leverage and buybacks [V call].
- **Valuation (13-Nov-2026 close, screener):** m-cap **₹1,04,471 Cr** at ₹396; **P/E 14.7×** TTM; P/B 2.6× (BV ₹150); dividend yield 3.54%; EV (incl. leases) ≈ ₹1,21,987 Cr → **EV/TTM EBITDA ≈ 6.7×** [E]; company's own EV/EBITDA 6.40× at 30-Sep-2026 (price ₹373.20) [V QR]. US towercos trade ~18–25× EBITDA (AMT ~17.8×, CCI ~25×, SBAC ~18.6×) [V-snip].

## 1. What the sub-sector does: products, value chain, where value is captured

### 1.1 What a tower company sells

| Product / service | What it is | Typical spec | Who buys |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ground-based tower (GBT)** | Steel lattice/monopole on leased land with shelter/outdoor cabinets, DG set, batteries, power interface | 2,500–4,000 sq ft; 30–50 m; 2–3 tenants [V QR Q2FY27 table] | All MNOs; rural/semi-urban coverage |
| **Roof-top tower (RTT)** | Mast on a building roof | 300–1,000 sq ft; 6–21 m; 2–3 tenants [V QR] | Urban capacity |
| **Ground-based mast (GBM)** | Slim pole on small plot | 100–500 sq ft; 24–40 m; 2–3 tenants [V QR] | Urban/semi-urban |
| **Smart pole / feather site / lean towers** | Street-furniture poles, micro-sites | 50–150 sq ft; 3–12 m; 1–2 tenants [V QR] | 5G densification; Indus "lean co-locations" 14,037 [V] |
| **In-building solutions (IBS) / DAS** | Shared indoor antennas in malls, airports, metros, tunnels | — | Indus (Vizag airport, IMC 2026 [V Q2 call]); Altius's Crest Digitel; iBus Network [K] |
| **Small cells** | Low-power radios on poles/walls | — | Mostly future 5G densification; Altius "Small Cells and IBS Sites" [V Altius site] |
| **Fibre to the site / backhaul** | Dark fibre, ducts | — | IP-I registrants may own dark fibre/RoW/ducts [V-snip TAIPA]; fiberisation of Indian towers ~36% [V-snip, older DoT figure] |
| **Energy management** | Grid connection, DG, solar, batteries; billed to tenants as "energy reimbursement" | — | All tenants; Indus 49,001 solar sites, 297 MW solar, ~73,244 "low diesel" sites [V QR/call] |

- **Passive vs active split.** Tower companies own **passive** infrastructure (steel, shelter, DG, power, batteries, security cabin). **Active** infrastructure (antennas, radios/BTS, baseband, transmission, fibre POPs) belongs to the operator [V QR §2.1]. Indian licensing (IP-I registration with DoT) historically barred passive infra providers from active elements; Indus's QR argues IP-Is could later offer shared RAN/transport ("active sharing… reduce capex by 33–35% and opex by 25–33%", citing BEREC) [V QR].
- **Contract form.** Master Service Agreement (MSA) per customer + a service contract per tower; all customers on a tower "offered the same terms" (non-discriminatory, legacy of the JV) [V QR]. **Annual escalation clauses** (~2.5% [V Q2 call: "2.5% annual rental escalation"]), **loading charges** for extra antennas/equipment, **exit penalties**; new contracts and renewals **minimum 10 years** [V Q2 call]. Weighted average residual service-contract life **5.89 years**; **minimum lease payment receivable ₹11,92,303 Mn (~₹1.19 lakh Cr)** at 30-Sep-2026 [V QR]. Altius: WALE ~16 years; 55% of tenancies locked for up to 30 years (remaining 24.4 yrs, = Jio anchor), 45% locked up to 10 years (remaining 4.3 yrs) [V Altius].

### 1.2 Value chain (inputs → tower → operator → subscriber)

1. **Inputs:** steel tower structures (fabricators e.g., KEC, Skipper, Kalpataru [K]); DG sets, VRLA and lithium-ion batteries, rectifiers/SMPS, solar panels; land (site owners paid monthly rent); grid power (state discoms); diesel (OMCs); O&M field contractors.
2. **Tower company:** acquires site, builds tower (capex ₹20–25 lakh new macro tower [V call]), connects power, maintains uptime (Indus 99.97% Q2 FY27 [V]), manages energy, bills rent + energy.
3. **Operator (tenant):** installs radios/antennas, backhaul; pays rent (Ind AS 116 in the telco's books → below EBITDA) and energy.
4. **End user:** 1,300 Mn mobile subscribers (Sep-2026) [V QR]; 5G subscribers 463.1 Mn (Jun-2026) [V QR citing TRAI].

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **At the tower company, through tenancy.** The second and third tenancies on an existing tower add ~₹41k/month of rent each against small incremental cost (some upgrade capex, "not a zero-capex solution" [V call]). That is why Indus's sharing revenue per tower was ₹80k/month at 1.81× (FY22) vs ₹67k at 1.62× (FY26) [V].
- **Site landlords** capture ground rent (Indus lease repayments ₹4,113 Cr FY26 ≈ ₹13–14k per tower per month [E]).
- **Energy** is value-destructive for the towerco at present (negative energy margin, trap 2) but is a moat versus captive sites: Indus claims lower unit energy cost than "other providers" [V call].
- **Operators capture the savings**: Indus's QR frames sharing as the reason operators lease rather than build [V]. Telco cost structure: tower and site rentals ≈ **8–12% of telco revenue** (escalators ~2–2.5%/yr), below EBITDA under Ind AS 116 [V-sib telecom-operators.html].

Most 5G radios went onto **existing** towers (loading revenue, not new tenancies) — "5G is actually loading on the same tower" [V Q2 call]. Densification (more sites per km²) is the next phase [V call].
3. **Vi's catch-up capex.** ₹45,000 Cr / 3 years; 5G in 225 towns across its 17 priority circles [V QR]; ~3,500 4G sites/month guided for Q2–Q3 FY27 [V-snip]. Each new Vi site on an Indus tower is typically a second/third tenancy — the highest-margin growth in the sector.
4. **Airtel's coverage and capacity build** (and tenancy in-sourcing from rival towercos to Indus) [V Q1 call]. Airtel Q2 FY27 revenue ₹60,878 Cr, OPM 57% [V screener].
5. **Jio**: largest operator, mostly self-hosted on Altius (Summit) under a 30-year MSA [V Altius]; partial Indus tenant (~12–13% of Indus revenue [V-snip MOSL]).
6. **BSNL/MTNL**: BSNL's ~1 lakh-site indigenous 4G rollout (TCS/Tejas/C-DOT) [K] — mostly on BSNL's own towers; incremental for Indus at the margin ("BSNL rollout" cited in primer).
7. **Rural coverage programmes** (USOF/Digital Bharat Nidhi 4G saturation — ~24–25k uncovered villages) [K].
8. **New verticals:** IBS (metros, airports, tunnels — Indus "incremental market share gains in new IBS deployments for two consecutive years" [V Q2 call]); smart-meter-ready sites (1.4 lakh Indus sites have smart meters [V]); future FWA/edge/6G (DoT 6G Spectrum Roadmap, Bharat 6G Alliance) [V QR].
9. **Africa (Indus only):** Zambia, Uganda, Nigeria with Airtel Africa as anchor [V]. Airtel Africa's sites in those three markets were reported as small in number (one source: <500 of 37,579) — scale is limited near-term [V-snip].

**Demand risk list:** Jio renewal/migration (above); operator consolidation (historic: 12 operators → 3+1, each merger = exits); active sharing/RAN sharing between operators (fewer tenancies per site); Vi funding failure; BSNL-Vi network-sharing proposals [V-snip sahi blog title "vodafone idea bsnl network sharing pact"].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **2000–2007: captive towers.** Each operator built its own towers; Bharti Infratel (2006) and Indus JV (Nov-2007: Bharti Infratel 42%, Vodafone 42%, Idea 16%) created shared towers [V QR].
2. **2008–2012: the land-grab.** 2G licence boom (2008 licences) → 12+ operators → tower rush; Bharti Infratel IPO Dec-2012 [K].
3. **2012–2016: consolidation of tenants begins** (2G licence cancellation Feb-2012; Telenor/MTS/Videocon exits) [K].
4. **2016–2019: the Jio shock.** Jio launch (Sep-2016) → price war → Aircel, RCom, Tata Teleservices, Telenor exit (2017–19) → **mass tenancy exits** for towercos; **Vodafone–Idea merger (Aug-2018)** → overlapping tenancies cancelled (exit penalties negotiated) [V-snip telecomtalk; K].
5. **2019–2020: AGR and merger.** Supreme Court AGR verdict (Oct-2019) imperils Vi; Indus–Infratel merger completes 19-Nov-2020 (Vi takes ₹3,764 Cr cash for its 11.15%) [V QR].
6. **2021–2023: Vi receivables crisis.** Vi under-pays rent; Indus provisions (₹2,298 Cr in Q3 FY23 [primer]); FY23 EBITDA margin 34%; dividend stops after FY22 [V screener payout 0% FY23–FY25].
7. **2023–2024: tower-building boom + Vi rescue.** Indus capex peaks ₹9,698 Cr (FY24) for Airtel/BSNL/rural rollout; tenancy ratio falls 1.78 → 1.68 [V]. Vi pays monthly from Jan-2023 [primer], FPO ₹18,000 Cr (Apr-2024) [V-sib]. Vodafone sells Indus in stages (Jun–Dec 2024) [V QR]. Indus buyback (Q2 FY25) lifts Airtel above 50% [V QR].
8. **2024–2025: write-back year & re-shaping.** ATC India → Brookfield (Sep-2024); Indus becomes Airtel subsidiary (Nov-2024); FY25 write-back ~₹5,100 Cr; Airtel towers to Indus (Mar-2025) [V].
9. **2025–2026: normalisation.** Govt 49% of Vi (Apr-2025); Vi AGR reassessed (2026); Indus Africa approval (2-Sep-2025); dividend resumes (₹14, May-2026 recommendation); West Asia war (Feb-2026) lifts diesel and disrupts tower manufacturing in Q1 FY27 ("now resolved") [V Q1 call]; Q2 FY27 tenancy uptick.

**Cycle character:** Tower demand cycles follow **operator capex and operator consolidation**, not GDP. Each consolidation wave (2012, 2017–19) cut tenancy ratios; each rollout wave (4G 2016–18, 5G 2022–24, Vi 2026–28) builds towers first and tenancies later.

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Active / RAN sharing and network consolidation** — two operators sharing one radio (MORAN/MOCN) or a Vi–BSNL pact would cut tenancies per site [K; V-snip headline]. Indus's own QR promotes IP-Is offering shared RAN, i.e., Indus might capture this rather than lose to it [V QR].
2. **Small cells and street furniture** — 5G densification may shift spend from macro towers to poles/small cells where Indus's share is lower and economics thinner (lean co-location rent ₹16,867/month vs ₹41,036 macro [V]). Indus lean additions collapsed to 13 in Q2 FY27 as customers moved back "towards the macro or bigger towers" [V Q2 call].
3. **Satellite direct-to-device (Starlink/OneWeb/Jio-SES)** — complements rural coverage; a threat only to marginal rural single-tenant sites [K].
4. **Energy transition** — lithium-ion + solar + smart meters cut diesel (−11% YoY [V]); risk that tenants demand the savings (energy is billed at cost) — so the margin effect is small, but uptime/SLA and ESG improve [V call].
5. **Operator in-sourcing** — Jio already owns (via Altius contract) its estate; Airtel prefers Indus (its subsidiary). Vi is the only "free" tenant left — and BSNL.
6. **AI/edge and 6G** — longer-term tailwinds (DoT 6G roadmap 2030) [V QR]; edge data centres at tower sites are speculative.
7. **Tower-design deflation** — leaner towers lower capex/tower but also rent/tenancy.

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Tenancy ratio is the master variable.** Indus's sharing revenue per tower fell 17% (₹80,467 → ₹66,532/month, FY22 → Q2 FY27) as closing tenancy fell 1.81 → 1.62 and recovered to 1.63 [V]. Every 0.01 of tenancy ≈ 2,700 co-locations ≈ ₹133 Cr/yr of sharing revenue at ₹41k/month, almost all incremental to EBITDA [E]. The 2023–25 build of single-tenant towers for Airtel/BSNL/rural diluted the ratio; Vi's capex is now the main refill.
2. **Vodafone Idea's survival — now a quasi-sovereign question.** Govt owns 49.02% of Vi, has cut its AGR bill to ₹64,046 Cr and given a 5-year moratorium [V screener; V-sib]. Vi's receivables crisis cost Indus provisions (FY23 PAT ₹2,040 Cr) and then a ~₹5,100 Cr write-back (FY25) [V]. Today Vi pays monthly and its ₹45,000 Cr capex plan depends on ~₹35,000 Cr of bank debt still under review [V-snip]. Vi is simultaneously the biggest credit risk and the biggest growth driver for Indus.
3. **Contract renewal and the Jio question.** Indus's book has only 5.9 years of residual life [V], and the 2016–17 Jio-era contracts roll over in late-2026–early-2027 [V-snip Jefferies]. Jio's preferred landlord is Altius (Summit, 30-yr MSA) [V]. Renewals come with discounts ("we do have to give the discount on renewals as per the framework agreed") [V Q1 call]. Outcome: either flat rents with retained tenancies, or exits.
4. **Airtel's control — anchor, consolidator and related party.** Airtel owns 51.26% and is ~55% of Indus revenue [V; E]; it sold its residual towers to Indus (₹1,982 Cr, Mar-2025) and moves expiring third-party tenancies to Indus [V]. It is the anchor for Indus's Africa push [V]. The upside is assured demand; the risk is pricing and capital-allocation decisions made with Airtel's interests in mind (e.g., Africa capex rather than higher payouts).
5. **Energy and cost-to-serve.** Energy is ~36% of Indus revenue and ~37% of costs; the margin is negative (−3.6% to −4.8%) [V]. Diesel shocks (West Asia 2026) hit the towerco until pass-through catches up; solar (49,001 sites), lithium-ion and smart meters are the structural defence [V]. Lower unit energy cost is also Indus's competitive weapon for winning tenancies from other towercos [V call].
6. **5G densification vs. capex intensity.** 5.93 lakh 5G BTS (Sep-2026) mostly loaded onto existing towers [V]. The next phase — densification and in-building — needs new sites; Indus's capex rose to ₹8,817 Cr FY26 (27% of revenue) and maintenance capex to ₹2,182 Cr [V]. FCF conversion (company FCF ₹3,743 Cr FY26 = 21% of EBITDA) is the swing variable for dividends [E from V].
7. **Capital structure and payout policy.** Indus has no dividend policy beyond "steady and progressive" board discretion, paid ~100% of FY26 FCF, is net cash ex-leases, and faces calls for leverage/InvIT/buyback [V]. Altius shows the alternative (45% net debt/AUM, ~98% of NDCF distributed) [V]. Rates matter: RBI repo 5.50% (7-Oct-2026) [V-sib].
8. **Ind AS 116 and accounting optics.** Reported EBITDA margin (55%) vs lease-adjusted (~42% of revenue) vs global "adjusted EBITDA" conventions; revenue equalisation (straight-lining) and Vi write-backs make headline growth unreliable [V].

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Sharing revenue = towers × tenancy ratio × rent per tenancy × 12 (+ loading, escalation, less renewal discounts/revenue equalisation)**
**Total revenue = sharing revenue + energy reimbursement (≈ energy cost × (1 + energy margin))**
**Reported EBITDA = sharing revenue + energy margin − O&M/people/other**
**Lease-adjusted EBITDA = reported EBITDA − ground-lease payments**
**FCF ≈ lease-adjusted EBITDA − capex − tax ± working capital (receivables!)**

Worked example — Indus, Q2 FY27 annualised [E from V]:
- Average towers ~2.69 lakh × average sharing factor 1.62 = ~4.36 lakh co-locations.
- × ₹41,036/month × 12 = **₹21,470 Cr** sharing revenue (actual Q2 × 4 = ₹21,764 Cr; difference = lean co-locations ₹284 Cr/yr [E: 14,037 × ₹16,867 × 12] — ties out).
- + energy reimbursement ₹12,278 Cr (Q2 × 4) → total revenue **₹34,060 Cr** (actual Q2 × 4 = ₹34,060 Cr).
- − energy cost ₹12,732 Cr (energy margin −₹454 Cr) − other opex ₹2,774 Cr → **reported EBITDA ₹18,554 Cr (54.5%)**.
- − ground leases ₹4,530 Cr → **lease-adjusted EBITDA ₹14,024 Cr (41.2%)**.
- − capex ~₹8,500–9,600 Cr (FY26 ₹8,817 Cr; Q2 run-rate ₹9,597 Cr) − cash tax ~₹2,400 Cr ± working capital → **FCF ~₹2,500–4,700 Cr** (TTM company FCF ₹4,695 Cr [E: sum of four quarters 7,878 + 10,934 + 14,362 + 13,774 ₹ Mn]).
- **Sensitivities:** +0.01 tenancy ≈ +₹133 Cr sharing revenue/EBITDA; +1 pp energy margin ≈ +₹123 Cr EBITDA; +1% rent per tenancy ≈ +₹215 Cr; loss of all Jio tenancies (~12–13% of revenue) ≈ −₹4,000–4,300 Cr revenue, mostly high-margin [E; worst case, not a forecast].

Worked example — Altius FY26 [V/E]: Adjusted revenue ₹12,262 Cr → cash EBITDA ₹8,301 Cr (67.7%) → interest at ~8.09% on ~₹44,800 Cr gross debt ≈ ₹3,600 Cr [E] → maintenance capex/tax → distributions ₹4,770 Cr (~98% of NDCF) [V].
