## READ THIS FIRST: nine definitional traps

1. **India Glycols is now a different company from the one in its FY26 numbers.**
   - FY26 net revenue ₹4,211 Cr split: Bio-fuel ~₹1,470 Cr, Potable spirits ~₹1,331 Cr, Chemicals (BSPC) ~₹1,203 Cr, Ennature ~₹208 Cr [primer].
   - After 1-Sep-2026 the listed INDIAGLYCO keeps only **BSPC + industrial gases (~₹1,203 Cr + gases of FY26 revenue ≈ 29% of the whole)** [E].
   - Spirits (the profit engine — ~₹285 Cr of ~₹548 Cr segment EBIT in FY26) and bio-fuel have left to IGL Spirits [primer; E].
   - **Do not put IGL's FY26 P&L next to its October market cap.** In Part 3 aggregates IGL is shown on the pre-demerger P&L (for "sector revenue") and **excluded from all valuation aggregates**.
   - Only the retained BSPC business is really a "commodity chemical" in this sub-sector's sense; spirits overlap the Alcohol dossier (alcohol-industry.md, where IGL was not a member).
2. **Gross vs net revenue (India Glycols only).** Gross revenue from operations FY26 ~₹9,827 Cr includes state excise on liquor and pass-through; **net revenue ₹4,211 Cr** is what screener shows and what this file uses [primer; V screener]. Q1 FY27: gross ~₹2,988 Cr vs net ₹1,130 Cr [V-snip]. Never add IGL gross revenue to anyone's revenue.
3. **Tata Chemicals: consolidated vs standalone vs "pre-exceptional".**
   - FY26 consolidated: revenue ₹14,584 Cr, operating profit (OP) ₹1,805 Cr (12%), **reported PAT −₹1,715 Cr** after a **₹1,956 Cr exceptional** (Lostock UK closure, US goodwill impairment, gratuity change) [primer; V screener: FY26 other income −₹1,473 Cr is where screener books the exceptional]. Pre-exceptional PAT ~₹241 Cr [primer].
   - FY26 **standalone (India)**: revenue ~₹4,831 Cr, EBITDA ~₹954 Cr, PAT ~₹606 Cr [primer]. India earns more than the whole group.
   - Q1 FY27: consolidated PAT **₹60 Cr** (screener) but **attributable to owners ≈ −₹17 Cr** (minorities, chiefly Rallis's ~45% outside holders, take a share of a profitable Rallis) [V-snip kotakneo/scanx]. Standalone PAT **₹343 Cr** [V-snip scanx]. Quote the basis every time.
   - **New segments from 1-Apr-2026:** "Living Essentials" (salt, bicarb, specialty nutrition), "Industrial Essentials" (soda ash and related), "Farm Essentials" (Rallis) [V-snip scanx]. The primer's "Basic Chemistry / Specialty" split is now obsolete.
4. **Tata Chemicals' net debt has three numbers.**

6. **Styrenix: standalone vs consolidated, and a Q1 FY26 base conflict.**
   - FY26 consolidated (incl. Thailand, acquired Jan-2025): revenue ₹3,438 Cr, OP ₹344 Cr, PAT ₹183 Cr [V screener]. Standalone India: ~₹2,640 Cr / PAT ~₹234 Cr / ~14% margin [primer]. Thailand dilutes.

   - Q1 FY27: **revenue +22% while volume fell 24.5%** (70,842 t vs 93,853 t) — styrene price inflation after the West Asia shock [V-snip multibagg/arthneeti]. Always quote tonnes first.

8. **"Spread" means different things in each archetype.**
   - Soda ash: realisation (₹/t) − cash cost (salt + limestone/trona + coke + coal/steam + ammonia make-up + freight).
   - Styrenics: polymer price − (SM + butadiene + acrylonitrile) per tonne, usually quoted in **$/t "delta"** (SPL: GPPS delta normal ~$200/t; HIPS ~$275–300/t) [V-snip SPL Q1 call].
   - Bromine: realisation − energy (steam) + chlorine + acid; feedstock brine is free.
   - India Glycols: product price − ethanol/molasses cost per litre/kg.
   - EBITDA/tonne is comparable only inside one archetype.
9. **"Capacity" for Tata Chemicals mixes owned, operated and partially-owned.** Group soda ash "~5.5 mtpa" in the primer included **UK Lostock (now closed)**. Post-closure, owned capacity is ~**4.8–5.0 mtpa** [E: Mithapur ~1.09 + Green River ~2.5–2.6 + Magadi ~0.3–0.4 + residual]. The "60% natural" share therefore rises to ~**60–65%**. Quote "about 5 mtpa" and name the sites.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Six members, three unrelated chemistries, one common force: China.**
  - **Inorganic/marine (Tata Chemicals, GHCL, Archean):** soda ash, salt, bicarbonate, bromine, potash — FY26 revenue ₹18,729 Cr, OP ₹2,734 Cr (14.6%) [E: sum of screener].
  - **Styrenics (Supreme Petrochem, Styrenix):** polystyrene/EPS/XPS and ABS/SAN from imported styrene — ₹8,844 Cr, OP ₹869 Cr (9.8%) [E].
  - **Bio-based glycols/ethanol (India Glycols, now split):** ₹4,211 Cr net revenue, OP ₹649 Cr (15.4%) pre-demerger [V screener].
  - **All six: FY26 revenue ₹31,784 Cr (−0.5% YoY on a mixed base), OP ₹4,252 Cr (13.4%), reported PAT −₹332 Cr (₹1,624 Cr ex Tata's ₹1,956 Cr exceptional); m-cap ₹44,053 Cr on 21-Oct-2026 (₹42,011 Cr ex the post-demerger IGL stub)** [E: sums of screener 21-Oct-26].
- **FY26 was the trough of a three-year soda ash collapse; Q1 FY27 shows styrenics boom and soda ash still bust.**
  - **Chinese soda ash** hit its lowest level in nearly a decade: light ~**RMB 1,156/t** end-Jun-2026 (SunSirs) vs a 2021–22 peak of ~RMB 3,000–3,500/t [V-snip; K]; dense FOB Qingdao **$185/t** (Jun-2026, Expana) [V-snip]; Chinese export prices **$160–180/t FOB** and Chinese inventories a **record 1.73 mt** (Tata Q1 FY27 commentary) [V-snip scanx].
  - Tata's **US** soda ash earned **EBITDA of US$1/t** in Q1 FY27 vs US$41/t a year earlier, at 97% utilisation and +13% volume (614 kt) [V-snip DSIJ/kotakneo]. Industrial Essentials segment: **EBIT −₹70 Cr** [V-snip].
  - **Styrenics** had the opposite quarter: West-Asia-driven styrene volatility blew out deltas (GPPS ~$200 → >$300/t; HIPS ~$400/t vs a normal $275–300) [V-snip SPL call]. **Supreme Petrochem Q1 FY27 OP margin 19%, PAT ₹238 Cr (+190%); Styrenix OP margin 22%, PAT ₹138 Cr (+165%)** — on volumes **down ~24%** at both [V screener; V-snip]. SPL called the margin "an aberration"; deltas normalised from end-June.
- **India is the importer at the margin in every one of these molecules.**
  - **Soda ash:** India installed ~**4.65 mtpa** (FY26); net imports ~**700 kt in FY26 vs 313 kt in FY22**; import share **25–26% vs a historic ~15%** [V-snip polymerupdate/simplywall; V Outlook Business, Oct-2025]. India is ~**6% of world demand, growing ~6%/yr** (GHCL MD) [V].

  - **MEG:** India's MEG is dominated by Reliance's petro-route; IGL is the only molasses/ethanol-route maker and has largely exited commodity MEG [primer; K].
  - **Bromine** is the exception: India (Archean) is a **net exporter**; Archean exports ~78% of revenue [primer].
- **Trade remedy is the swing variable for soda ash.**
  - DGTR **anti-dumping final findings of 29-Sep-2025** (case AD(OI)-29/2024) recommended duties of **$79–113/t on Turkey, Russia, USA and Iran** (Sisecam $17–27/t); corrigendum 4-Nov-2025 [V-snip scanx; V ICC corrigendum PDF title]. **No Finance Ministry customs notification was found** — treat as **not imposed (lapsed)** unless the writer finds the notification [E inference; FLAG].

### 1.2 Value chains (inputs → customer)

1. **Synthetic soda ash (Solvay / ammonia-soda), India & UK model** — Tata Mithapur, GHCL Sutrapada, Nirma, DCW, RSPL, Tuticorin Alkali.
   - Inputs per tonne of soda ash [K, engineering norms; E ranges]: **~1.5–1.6 t salt (NaCl as brine), ~1.1–1.3 t limestone, ~0.1 t coke (for the lime kiln), ammonia make-up ~1–3 kg (recycled), steam ~2.5–4 t and power ~60–100 kWh**; total energy ~**10–14 GJ/t**.
   - By-product: calcium chloride liquor (mostly waste — the effluent problem); "**dual process**" (Chinese "joint-soda", Hou's process) co-produces **ammonium chloride fertiliser** instead (~1 t NH₄Cl per t soda ash), so its soda ash cost depends on NH₄Cl prices.
   - Coastal Gujarat clusters exist because salt (solar pans), limestone and port access sit together [primer GHCL].
2. **Natural soda ash (trona)** — Tata Green River (Wyoming), Tata Magadi (Kenya, lake trona), WE Soda/Genesis (Wyoming + Türkiye solution mining), Sisecam/Ciner (Wyoming), Inner Mongolia Boyuan (Alxa).
   - Mine/solution-mine trona → crush/dissolve → calcine → crystallise → dry. Energy ~**5–7 GJ/t** (about half of synthetic) and no CaCl₂ waste [K].
   - Cash cost at the mine: US ~**$60–90/t** ex-works; delivered to Asia adds ~$40–70/t rail + ocean freight [E/K; FLAG — industry-consultant figures, not re-verified].
   - GHCL's MD: "natural soda ash costs almost half as much as synthetic" [V Outlook Business, Oct-2025].
3. **Marine bromine + salt (Archean model)** — sub-soil brine from the Rann of Kutch (Hajipir) → chlorine-blowing-out of bromine (steam stripping) → salt crystallisation in pans → SOP recovery from bitterns → export via Kandla/Mundra [primer]. Inputs: brine (free), chlorine, sulphuric acid, steam/power, pan maintenance, ocean freight.
4. **Styrenics** — Crude/naphtha → benzene + ethylene → **ethylbenzene → styrene monomer (SM)** (overseas: Middle East, Korea, Taiwan, China, Singapore, US) → imported by ship into Nagothane (SPL) / Gujarat (Styrenix) → polymerised into PS/HIPS (+ polybutadiene rubber), EPS (pentane-impregnated beads), ABS (SM + acrylonitrile + butadiene/PBL latex), SAN → granules sold to moulders/OEMs [primer SPL/Styrenix].
   - **Feedstock is ~75–85% of cost**; conversion is ~₹5–10/kg [E].
5. **Bio-route glycols (IGL)** — molasses/grain → ethanol → dehydration to bio-ethylene → EO → MEG or ethoxylates, glycol ethers, PEGs → personal-care/agro/textile formulators (Clariant, Dow, BASF, L'Oréal named as customers [primer]).

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **In soda ash, value sits with the lowest-cost tonne, and that is now a natural tonne.** GHCL's FY25 EBITDA ~₹7,500–8,000/t vs FY26 ~₹6,000/t [E: OP ÷ ~1.1–1.15 mt sold]; Tata US EBITDA fell from ~$41/t to ~$1/t (Q1 FY26 → Q1 FY27) [V-snip]. Synthetic producers inside India survive because **freight (~$40–70/t across oceans) + the MIP + any duty** fence the market [primer GHCL; E].
- **In styrenics, value sits with the SM producer most of the time and the polymeriser only when deltas blow out.** A normal GPPS delta of ~$200/t (~₹19,000/t at ₹94.9) less conversion ~₹6,000–8,000/t leaves ~₹10–12k/t EBITDA (~₹10–12/kg) [E, calibrated to SPL FY26 OP ₹525 Cr ÷ ~0.36–0.38 mt]. In Q1 FY27 SPL earned **~₹47,000/t** (₹332 Cr ÷ 70,842 t) [E].
- **In bromine, value is extraction rent** (free brine) and the price cycle; derivatives (flame retardants, CBFs) add conversion margin but need qualification [primer].
- **In bio-glycols, value moved from MEG (lost to petro-giants) to specialties and to the alcohol itself** — IGL's FY26 segment EBIT margins: spirits 21.4%, chemicals 11.7%, bio-fuel 7.8%, Ennature 3.5% [primer].

**Writer note:** the shift from **ADD** to a **safeguard/QR** reflects how cheap the competing imports are.
- ADD is country-specific and needs a dumping margin.
- A safeguard covers all origins and needs an "unforeseen surge" causing "serious injury".
- Natural soda ash from the US and Türkiye is low-cost rather than dumped, so ADD struggles to catch it.
- A QR would cap import volumes; a safeguard duty would add an ad valorem %. Neither has been decided.

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **Seawater-chemistry era (1939–1990s).** Tata Chemicals was founded at Mithapur in 1939 [primer]. Saurashtra Chemicals, DCW and Gujarat Heavy Chemicals (GHCL, Sutrapada, 1980s) built synthetic soda ash on the Saurashtra coast. Nirma entered soda ash in the 1990s to integrate its detergents [K].
2. **Globalisation and leverage (2005–2010).**
   - Tata bought Brunner Mond (UK + Magadi, 2005–06) and General Chemical Industrial Products (Green River, 2008), briefly becoming world #2 [primer].
   - Nirma bought Searles Valley (2007) [K].
   - GHCL bought a Romanian soda ash plant (later exited) [primer GHCL].
3. **Restructuring decade (2010s).** UK Winnington closed (2014). GHCL deleveraged. Tata exited urea/fertilisers (2018) and demerged its consumer business to Tata Consumer (Feb-2020) [primer; K].
4. **Covid-to-energy-shock super-cycle (FY22–FY23).** Soda ash, bromine and styrene spreads all peaked:
   - Chinese soda ash ~RMB 3,000–3,500/t (2021–22) [K].
   - Mumbai soda ash peaked in May-2022 at ~₹53/kg, implied by the later "−45% to ₹29.5/kg" [V-snip].
   - Archean: FY23 OPM 44%, and its IPO (Nov-2022, ₹1,462 Cr) came at the top [V screener; primer].
   - GHCL FY24 PAT ₹794 Cr; Tata FY24 OP ₹2,847 Cr [V screener].
5. **The Chinese natural-soda-ash shock (2023–2026).**
   - Boyuan's Alxa natural soda ash (Phase-1 ~5 mt, 2023) and other Inner Mongolia additions (>10 mt) turned China from importer to exporter.
   - Prices fell to decade lows by mid-2026: light ~RMB 1,156/t; FOB $160–185/t [V-snip].
   - India's import share doubled to 25–26%. Mumbai soda ash fell ~45% from peak to ₹29.5/kg (Jun-2025) [V-snip].
   - Tata took a ₹1,956 Cr FY26 exceptional and closed Lostock; WE Soda bought Genesis; Solvay cut Torrelavega [primer; K].
6. **Bromine bust and spike (FY24–FY27).**
   - China bromine averaged ~RMB 21,900/t at the start of 2025 and 36,100 by end-2025 [V-snip SunSirs].
   - It **spiked above RMB 70,000/t in April 2026**: ICL cut operating rates in the Iran–Israel conflict, and January floods hit JBL (Jordan). It fell back to ~RMB 34,000–36,000/t by mid-June [V-snip SunSirs].
   - Archean's Q1 FY27 bromine realisation rose ~50% YoY [V-snip].
7. **Styrenics ownership reset (2022–2025).** Shiva Performance Materials bought INEOS's ~61% of INEOS Styrolution India (agreed 2022; completed Mar-2023; renamed Styrenix 5-Jan-2023), then INEOS Styrolution Thailand (Jan-2025) [primer]. SPL climbed into EPS, XPS and mABS.
8. **Policy churn on polymers (2025–2026).** 14 polymer QCOs were rescinded (Nov-2025) [V-sib]. The West Asia war (Feb–Apr 2026) produced the widest styrenics deltas in years (Q1 FY27) [V-snip].
9. **Unbundling (2026).** IGL's three-way demerger took effect on 1-Sep-2026 [V-snip].

## 11. Technology and disruption risks
- **Natural vs synthetic** is the structural disruption already under way. Every new tonne of Chinese natural soda ash pushes a European or Indian synthetic tonne up the cost curve [primer; V Outlook].
- **Decarbonisation:**
  - Synthetic soda ash emits ~0.4–0.5 t CO₂/t (process + energy) [K]. EU CBAM does not yet cover soda ash [K].
  - Tata's Magadi runs the world's first electric soda ash calciner [primer]. Tata UK captures CO₂ for bicarb [K].
- **Glass demand risk:** the 2024–25 solar-glass overbuild in China proved demand can disappoint. A shift to thinner glass and bifacial modules lowers soda ash per GW [K/E].
- **Lithium-ion vs sodium-ion:** sodium-ion cathodes use soda ash, a small upside; Li-carbonate refining also needs soda ash [K].
- **Bromine:** flame-retardant substitution (phosphorus-based alternatives; EU regulatory pressure on some BFRs) vs new demand from Zn-Br and flow batteries [K].
- **Styrenics:**
  - PP compounds replace HIPS/ABS in some appliances.
  - Paper and PLA replace PS disposables.
  - Chemical recycling of PS is a niche positive [K].
- **Bio-route:** bio-MEG competes with petro-MEG at $450–550/t. The "green" premium depends on brand owners' Scope-3 commitments [primer].
- **SiC semiconductors (Archean):** the technology risk sits entirely outside the core business. Clas-SiC (UK) is a small 6-inch fab [K/V-snip].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert leads with)

1. **China's natural soda ash has rewritten the global cost curve, and India is where the displaced tonnes land.**
   - Inner Mongolia added >10 mt of mostly natural capacity in ~2 years [V Outlook]. Chinese prices are at decade lows: light soda ash ~RMB 1,156/t, FOB $160–185/t [V-snip].
   - Chinese exports into SE Asia push US and Turkish natural soda ash towards India.
   - Result: India's import share went from ~15% to 25–26% [V]. Tata US EBITDA fell to $1/t [V-snip]. GHCL's EBITDA/t fell ~25% [E].
   - **No Indian producer controls its price. The cycle turns on Chinese discipline and on the trade-remedy decision.**
2. **Trade policy is the second price-setter.**
   - The 2025 ADD recommendation on Turkey/Russia/US/Iran ($79–113/t) appears to have lapsed [FLAG].
   - A safeguard/QR investigation (initiated 16-Mar-2026) is due ~16-Nov-2026 [V-snip]. The MIP (₹20,108/t) has proven porous [V].
   - A safeguard duty of even 10–15% (~₹2,500–4,000/t) would be worth roughly half of GHCL's current EBITDA/t [E].
3. **Spread businesses whose spreads are set abroad.**
   - Styrenics deltas are set in Korea, China and Singapore. In Q1 FY27 the GPPS delta went from $200 to >$300 and HIPS reached ~$400 [V-snip], so SPL/Styrenix margins doubled on falling volume.
   - Bromine is set by ICL's operating rate and Shandong supply. It went from RMB 36k to 70k+ and back within three months [V-snip].
   - Normalised earnings are therefore far from both the FY26 trough and the Q1 FY27 peak. The writer must teach mid-cycle thinking.
4. **Resource and captive-input moats decide who survives the trough.**
   - GHCL: captive salt, limestone and lignite; FY26 ROCE 17% at the bottom [V].
   - Tata: Green River trona with ~100 years of reserves [primer].
   - Archean: free Kutch brine, so OPM stayed 22% even in FY26 [V].
   - The converters (SPL, Styrenix) have no feedstock moat; their moat is domestic scale, grade breadth and service.
5. **Capital allocation at the bottom: building, diversifying, unbundling.**
   - GHCL commits ₹6,500 Cr to double capacity into a glut [V].
   - Archean commits to a ₹2,067 Cr SiC fab [V-snip].
   - Tata pivots to specialty silica/nutrition and rationalises the UK [primer].
   - Styrenix doubles ABS; SPL adds PS/mABS [V-snip].
   - IGL splits into three [V-snip].
   - The quality of these decisions — not the cycle — is what differentiates the next five years.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**General form:** EBITDA = Σ over products of [capacity × utilisation × (realisation − variable cash cost per tonne)] − fixed costs.

**Per archetype:**
- **Soda ash:** EBITDA = tonnes × (realisation − [salt + limestone + coke + energy + ammonia + freight]) − fixed costs. Equivalently: EBITDA = tonnes × EBITDA/t.
- **Styrenics:** EBITDA = tonnes × [(polymer price − SM price × ~1.0 t/t − other monomers) − conversion cost] ± inventory gain/loss.
- **Bromine + salt:** EBITDA = Br tonnes × (Br price − Br cash cost) + salt tonnes × (FOB salt price − salt cash cost incl. freight) + derivatives margin − fixed costs.
- **Bio-glycols:** EBITDA = Σ litres of ethanol × (value per litre in its end-use − feedstock cost per litre) − conversion.

### C.1 Worked example 1 — one tonne of synthetic soda ash in India (GHCL-type, FY26) [E composite]
Calibrated to GHCL FY26: revenue ₹3,064 Cr, OP ₹690 Cr, ~1.15 mt equivalent sold.

| Line | ₹/t |
|---|---|
| Realisation (net of discounts; blended light/dense + bicarb uplift) | ~26,600 |
| Salt (captive, ~1.55 t) | ~1,500 |
| Limestone (captive, ~1.2 t) + coke (~0.1 t) | ~3,500 |
| Energy: coal/lignite for steam and power (~10–12 GJ) | ~8,000 |
| Ammonia make-up, chemicals, packing | ~1,300 |
| Freight-out | ~2,100 |
| Employees, repairs, overheads | ~4,200 |
| **EBITDA** | **~6,000 (22.5%)** |
| Depreciation (₹111 Cr ÷ 1.15 mt) | ~970 |
| Net other income − interest | ~+550 |
| Tax @ 25% | ~1,400 |
| **PAT** | **~4,100/t** → × 1.15 mt ≈ **₹472 Cr** [V screener ✔] |

- **Sensitivity:** a ₹1,000/t price change ≈ ₹115 Cr of EBITDA (±17%).
- **FY25 check:** EBITDA/t ~₹7,600 (₹877 Cr ÷ ~1.15 mt).

### C.2 Worked example 2 — one tonne of natural soda ash, Tata US (Green River), exported to SE Asia vs sold domestically [E composite]
