**Units, conventions and FX — read before using any number**
- ₹ crore (Cr) = 10 million. ₹ lakh = 100,000. AVTL's filings are in **₹ lakh**; its investor presentation is in **₹ million (mn)**. ₹1 Cr = ₹10 mn = 100 lakh.
- **Liquid capacity** is quoted in **cbm (cubic metres) = kl (kilolitres)** — the two are identical (1 m³ = 1,000 litres). "Lakh kl" = 100,000 kl. 1.67 mn cbm = 16.7 lakh kl.
- **Gas capacity** is quoted in **MT (metric tonnes) of static capacity** (what the tanks hold at one moment). **Throughput** is in **MMT or mtpa** (million tonnes per year moved through). The ratio of the two is "throughput turns". LPG density ≈ 0.51–0.58 t/m³ (propane/butane mix) [K] — so 1 MT of LPG ≈ 1.8–2.0 cbm; never add LPG MT to liquid cbm.
- **Ammonia** static capacity also in MT (refrigerated at −33 °C) [K].
- FY27 = Apr-2026 to Mar-2027. **Q2 FY27 = Jul–Sep 2026** (the latest reported quarter). AVTL, Aegis Logistics and the OMCs have March year-ends; Royal Vopak reports calendar years (Q3 2026 = Jul–Sep 2026, same window as Q2 FY27).

## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Restated history: FY25 is ₹789 Cr, not ₹621 Cr.**
   - AVTL **restated FY25 and FY26** for the **common-control consolidation of Hindustan Aegis LPG (HALPG)** [V Aug-2026 presentation footnote: "FY25 & FY26 numbers restated due to consolidation of HALPG"].
   - Restated FY25: revenue **₹789.2 Cr**, EBITDA **₹574.8 Cr**, PAT **₹224.8 Cr** [V presentation; V screener]. Our primer uses the **pre-restatement** FY25 (₹621 Cr revenue, ₹127 Cr PAT) and therefore shows FY26 revenue growth of "~49%". On a like-for-like basis FY26 growth is **+17.0%** (₹923.1 Cr vs ₹789.2 Cr).
   - FY24 "₹570 Cr" in the primer is **total income** (incl. other income) from the RHP; revenue from operations was **₹561.8 Cr** [V presentation; V chittorgarh: "Total Income 570.12"].
2. **kl = cbm; MT ≠ cbm; static ≠ throughput.** Liquid: 1 cbm = 1 kl. LPG: capacity in MT static, volume in MMT throughput. AVTL's LPG terminals are "designed" for **~84.75 throughput turns** (CRISIL, Dec-2024) [V presentation] but actually ran ~**20×** in FY26 (3.9 MMT on a static base that grew from 70,800 to 225,800 MT during the year) [E]. A "1 mtpa ammonia terminal" (management) is a throughput claim on 36,000 MT of static tankage.
3. **"~2.78 lakh MT LPG" in the primer is not a company number.** The company's own figures: **225,800 MT static LPG today** (Kandla 48,000 + Pipavav 70,800 + New Mangalore 82,000 + Haldia 25,000) and **355,100 MT incl. announced** (adds JNPA 129,284 MT = 77,286 + 51,998) [V Aug-2026 presentation; PL]. Ammonia 36,000 MT is separate.
4. **AVTL is not "Aegis".** Aegis Logistics (AEGISLOG) is the listed parent that **consolidates AVTL as a subsidiary** (AVTL calls it "Holding Company") [V AR FY26]. Aegis Logistics' consolidated revenue (₹8,333 Cr FY26) **includes** AVTL's ₹923 Cr; its borrowings (₹4,150 Cr) include AVTL's ₹3,731 Cr. Aegis also keeps assets **outside** AVTL: the **Mumbai (Trombay) liquid and LPG terminals**, LPG sourcing, distribution (bulk/auto-gas/packed), and soon Tristar [V transcript: "Mumbai… is part of Aegis Logistics and not part of Aegis Vopak"]. On calls, "the Liquid Division ₹187 Cr revenue, 2 million CBM, ₹3,600/cbm" is **Aegis Logistics' consolidated liquid division**, not AVTL's (AVTL Q2 liquid revenue ₹128.9 Cr) [V].
5. **Consolidated PAT includes minority interests.** HALPG is 75% owned and Aegis Terminal (Pipavav) ~86% (96% less 10% to ITOCHU) [V]. Q2 FY27 consolidated PAT **₹96.4 Cr** vs EPS ₹0.78 × 110.8 Cr shares ≈ **₹86 Cr attributable** [E]. TTM EPS ₹2.74 ⇒ attributable TTM PAT ≈ **₹304 Cr** vs consolidated ₹329 Cr [E from screener]. Screener's **P/E 104** uses EPS (attributable); dividing m-cap by screener's PAT gives ~96×. Non-controlling interest on the balance sheet: **₹141.8 Cr** (Sep-26) [V].
6. **Debt has three numbers; lease liabilities are ~40% of it.**
   - Screener "borrowings" **₹4,121 Cr (Sep-26)** = bank/NCD borrowings **₹2,495 Cr** (non-current ₹535.8 Cr + current ₹1,958.9 Cr) + **lease liabilities ₹1,626 Cr** (port land leases capitalised under Ind AS 116) [V results balance sheet; E arithmetic].
   - Cash ₹326 Cr + bank ₹4.4 Cr ⇒ **net debt ex-leases ≈ ₹2,164 Cr; incl. leases ≈ ₹3,790 Cr** (Sep-26) [E].
   - Mar-26: borrowings ₹2,118 Cr (LT ₹346.9 + ST ₹1,770.7) + leases ₹1,613 Cr = ₹3,731 Cr; cash ₹307 Cr ⇒ ND **₹1,811 Cr ex-leases (2.6× FY26 EBITDA)** or **₹3,424 Cr incl. leases (5.0×)** [V presentation; E]. The primer's "~5.4×" is the lease-inclusive basis on a slightly different cash figure.
   - Because Ind AS EBITDA excludes lease rent, **lease-inclusive debt is the consistent basis for EV/EBITDA**. Use ex-lease for covenant-style "net debt/EBITDA" talk (management cap: **gearing ≤0.6× and net debt ≤3.5× EBITDA**) [V presentation; transcript].
   - Note the **short-term skew**: ₹1,959 Cr of the ₹2,495 Cr bank debt is current (Sep-26); current ratio **0.36** [V]. Probably bridge/construction finance pending refinancing or equity — flag.
7. **IPO share count.** Pre-IPO shares ~98.9 Cr (equity capital ₹988.8 Cr at FV ₹10 after a bonus — AR shows "utilisation for bonus issue ₹95,370 lakh") ; IPO added **11.915 Cr shares at ₹235**; post-issue **110.80 Cr shares** [V chittorgarh; V balance sheet equity capital ₹110,799 lakh]. FY26 EPS uses weighted shares (~108 Cr) [E]; quarterly EPS from Q2 FY26 onward uses 110.8 Cr.
8. **Segment "results" are not EBITDA.** The quarterly filing gives segment revenue and **segment results after depreciation, before finance cost and unallocated expenses**. The annual report gives **segment EBITDA** (FY26: Liquid ₹313.7 Cr, Gas ₹414.5 Cr; sum ₹728 Cr vs reported group EBITDA ₹686.5 Cr — the ₹42 Cr gap is unallocated cost) [V]. On the 3-Nov call management gave **no segment EBITDA for AVTL** ("we gave only consolidated EBITDA") [V].
9. **"Throughput" vs "storage" revenue models and who earns the LPG margin.** AVTL earns a **fee** per tonne handled (≈₹1,200–1,300/t in LPG) and rental per cbm in liquids. The **₹7,000/t LPG distribution margin** and the "₹10,000/t ammonia (storage + distribution)" economics quoted on calls are **Aegis Logistics-level** (sourcing/distribution sits in Aegis) — except that management described ammonia distribution in the first person on the AVTL question; **who books ammonia distribution margin (AVTL vs Aegis) is unresolved** — flag [V transcript].
10. **Market-share claims use different denominators.** "Largest third-party liquid storage, **25.53%** of India's third-party liquid capacity" and "**~11.5%** of India's LPG static capacity (Dec-2024), now **>20%**" are CRISIL (RHP) and PL figures [V PL; V-snip Business India]. Management said on 3-Nov "**more than 30%** of India's LPG static capacity" and "India has **~1 million MT** of static LPG storage" — the 30% appears to be **Aegis group (incl. Mumbai/Trombay 21,000 MT) + AVTL** and possibly incl. announced JNPA [V transcript; E]. Quote each with its basis. "AVTL's ports handle ~61% of LPG imports and ~23% of liquid imports" is a **port-level FY24 statistic** (CRISIL), not AVTL's share of throughput [V PL].

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **One listed pure-play, a crowded unlisted/captive field.** Indian port storage splits into (a) **OMC captive tankage** (IOC, BPCL, HPCL — LPG import terminals, product depots, ~214 bottling plants with 23.04 MMTPA rated capacity [V Angel One, Apr-2026 PPAC data]); (b) **strategic reserves** (ISPRL, 5.33 MMT crude in rock caverns) [V Business Today]; (c) **independent/third-party terminals** — AVTL (largest), IMC Ltd (unlisted, ~1.12 mn kl, 14 ports), Cisternina (KKR; buying Ganesh Benzoplast's ~500,000 kl), IndianOil Petronas (IPPL: LPG at Ennore/Haldia), JSW Infrastructure (JNPA liquid berths, Fujairah), Adani Ports (Mundra LPG via MLTPL/Adani Total, crude SPMs), plus dozens of single-port tank-farm owners at Kandla/JNPA/Haldia [V PL; V Sahi; K]. Only AVTL is a listed pure-play; Aegis Logistics is the listed parent (storage + LPG trading/distribution).
- **AVTL at a glance (latest).**
  - Capacity: **1.67 mn cbm liquid** (22 operating tank terminals, 6 ports) + **225,800 MT static LPG** (4 ports) + **36,000 MT ammonia** (Pipavav, commissioned Q2 FY27); incl. announced projects **2.14 mn cbm liquid and 355,100 MT LPG** [V Aug-2026 presentation; transcript].
  - FY26: revenue **₹923.1 Cr (+17.0%)** = Liquid ₹440.5 Cr (+27.8%) + Gas ₹482.6 Cr (+8.6%); EBITDA **₹686.5 Cr (74.4%)**; PAT **₹341.9 Cr** (consolidated) [V].
  - H1 FY27: revenue **₹504.6 Cr (+14.6%)**, EBITDA **₹392 Cr (77.7%)**, PAT **₹165.8 Cr** (vs ₹178.7 Cr H1 FY26, −7%) [V]. TTM revenue ₹987 Cr, EBITDA ₹750 Cr, PAT ₹329 Cr [V screener].
  - Valuation (13-Nov-2026): **m-cap ₹31,688 Cr; P/E 104× TTM (attributable EPS); P/B 7.1×; EV/TTM EBITDA ≈ 47.5× (lease-inclusive EV ≈ ₹35,600 Cr)**; ROCE 7.6%; dividend yield 0.07% [V screener; E EV].
- **The industry's money is made at a physical pinch-point.** Everything imported in bulk liquid or gas must land in a shore tank. Storage earns a **fee**, not a commodity spread, so EBITDA margins are **70–80%** for an efficient independent (AVTL 74–79%; Vopak proportional 58% [V]; IPPL 28–37%; Ganesh Benzoplast ~22% [V PL]). Returns are set by **capex per cbm/MT, utilisation/turns, and tariff** — and land-lease cost at ports.
- **Three demand rivers, one shock.**
  - **LPG**: India consumed **33.2 MMT in FY26 (+6%)**, produced **13.1 MMT** and imported **~21.5 MMT** (≈65%) [V PTI/Outlook Business; V PL table]. Nearly **90% of LPG imports transited Hormuz** before the war [V-sib refining: MoPNG 27-Mar-2026].
  - **Chemicals/POL/edible oils**: POL & crude at major ports **272.3 MMT in FY26 (+16.2%)**, 29.8% of major-port cargo [V MoPSW]; India bulk liquid-chemicals trade ~**15.7 MMT (FY24) → 18–19 MMT (FY29E)** (CRISIL) [V presentation].
  - **Ammonia**: imports ~**2.3–2.6 MMT/yr**, ~15% of demand, heading to **~3.3 MMT by FY29** (CRISIL) [V PL].
  - **The 2026 West Asia war** cut India's LPG supply (Mar-26 consumption −12.8%; Apr–Jul −16.5%; active domestic customers fell 39 lakh — the first decline on record) and swung sourcing to the US (US share of LPG imports 12–14% in Jan–Feb → 55% in May, 73% in July per Kpler-based reports) [V PTI; V PSU Watch; V-snip]. It also triggered a **policy push for strategic storage**: a **30-day LPG reserve directive to OMCs (29-May-2026)** and a **reported $42 bn, 10-year strategic fuel reserve plan** (+28 MMT crude, +9 MMT LNG, **+4 MMT LPG** storage; not yet Cabinet-approved) [V AIR; V Business Today/Reuters].
- **AVTL's shock was mild and mostly in Q1.** Gas revenue fell to ₹107.2 Cr in Q1 FY27 (−3.5% YoY, −12.4% QoQ) with throughput 0.9 MMT; liquids rose +30.6% [V presentation incl. errata]. Q2 recovered: gas ₹142.0 Cr (+12.5%), throughput **1.1 MMT**; group EBITDA margin a record 78.6% [V]. Take-or-pay contracts plus liquid growth absorbed the gas dip; PAT fell only because of **finance cost (₹40 Cr/qtr vs ₹19–20 Cr a year ago)** as capex debt replaced IPO-repaid debt [V].
- **The equity story is capacity × time.** Management targets **~US$1.2 bn cumulative capex "by next year"** and **~US$5 bn by 2030–31**, funded at **≤0.6× gearing / ≤3.5× net debt/EBITDA** [V transcript; presentation]. PL models revenue **₹1,224 Cr (FY27E) → ₹2,851 Cr (FY29E)** and EBITDA **₹922 Cr → ₹2,212 Cr** (48% CAGR) [V PL]. Against that, **FY26 FCF was ~₹3 Cr** (CFO ₹702 Cr, CFI −₹3,077 Cr incl. acquisitions) and ROCE is **7.6%** [V screener]. The stock pays for the build-out years in advance: **~47× TTM EV/EBITDA vs Royal Vopak ~9.5× (2026E, PL)** [V PL].

### 1.2 The value chain (ship → tank → hinterland)

1. **Sourcing & ocean freight** — Saudi Aramco/ADNOC/QatarEnergy/US Gulf LPG (VLGC, ~44,000–50,000 t cargoes); chemical tankers (Stolt, Odfjell); product tankers. Freight and demurrage are big swing costs (VLGC capability cuts unit freight) [K; V transcript].
2. **Port & jetty** — port authority or BOT concessionaire charges vessel-related dues and **wharfage per tonne** (Deendayal, JNPA, NMPA, Haldia/SMP Kolkata, Kochi, private Pipavav (APM Terminals/GPPL) [V-sib ports].
3. **Terminal (the toll gate)** — pipeline from jetty to tank (AVTL owns its jetty pipelines; Kandla has **6 jetties and 33 stainless-steel pipelines**) [V PL]; storage; blending/heating; customs bonded storage; safety (PESO licence, fire systems).
4. **Evacuation** — road tanker bays (Kandla **200 liquid + 16 LPG bays**), rail gantries (Pipavav **32-bay LPG rail gantry**; Mangalore LPG rail gantry FY27), cross-country LPG pipelines (**Jamnagar–Loni 3.25 mtpa**; **Kandla–Gorakhpur 8.25 mtpa, 2,805 km**, ~96% complete, Kandla–Lucknow section charged; **Haldia–Panagarh 1.45 mtpa**; Uran–Chakan–Shikrapur ~1.0 mtpa; Mangalore–Hassan–Cherlapalli FY28) [V PL; V transcript; V presentation].
5. **Bottling & distribution** — OMC bottling plants (214 plants, 23.04 MMTPA), distributors; private bulk/auto-gas (Aegis distribution 5.18 lakh t in H1 FY27) [V].

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **Terminals capture a fixed fee on a volume they do not own.** For AVTL: LPG ≈ **₹1,237/t FY26** (gas revenue ₹482.6 Cr ÷ 3.9 MMT) and liquids ≈ **₹2,630/cbm/yr of installed capacity (₹219/cbm/month)** or ~₹290–300/kl/month of occupied space at ~75% utilisation [E from V data].
- **LPG trading/distribution captures the commodity margin** — Aegis Logistics' distribution EBITDA "a shade above ₹7,000/t" in Q2 FY27 (vs ~₹4,000/t in 2024–25) [V transcript]. That margin is ~5–6× the terminal fee per tonne but is **cyclical and price-led** (Q1 FY27's war quarter was "a price-led margin").
- **Ports capture wharfage and land rent.** Major-port land rents are reset off market value (Deendayal: **6% of market value p.a., revised every five years** per a reported lease dispute [V-snip taxtmi]); JNPA tank-farm land lease reset reported as **~10×** in 2026 (Ganesh Benzoplast commentary) [V Sahi — detail not confirmed]. This is the main **value-leakage risk** for terminal owners on port land.
- **Pipelines and rail** capture the inland leg; a terminal tied to a trunk pipeline (Kandla/Pipavav → KGPL; Kandla → JLPL) earns more turns on the same tanks.
- **OMCs** keep captive tankage for security of supply and cost; they outsource throughput where they lack jetty/land (AVTL's Haldia LPG terminal has an **exclusive HPCL agreement to FY2038**) [V presentation].

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Household LPG (the anchor, ~85–90% of LPG volume).** ~33 crore active domestic connections, ~10.3 crore under **PMUY** [V-sib refining]. Targeted subsidy ₹300/cylinder for PMUY (12 refills/yr) [V presentation]; PMUY subsidy **₹12,212 Cr in FY26** and DBT subsidy ₹1,646 Cr (vs ₹366 Cr) [V PSU Watch]. Aadhaar biometric authentication for subsidised refills from **1-Oct-2026** may trim recorded domestic demand (diversion to commercial use) [V-sib refining].
2. **Commercial/industrial LPG** — hotels, Morbi ceramic kilns (Gujarat), glass, auto-gas. Most price-elastic and the first to be rationed: commercial supply capped at **20% of normal from 12-Mar-2026**, restrictions fully withdrawn **25-Jun-2026**; bulk LPG Apr–Jul **−79.8%** [V PSU Watch]. Morbi's switch to gas/LPG and back moves Aegis distribution volumes and APSEZ rail volumes (Morbi ICD near zero in Q1 FY27) [V-sib ports].
3. **Chemical imports.** India imports most of its methanol, acetic acid, styrene (100% of SM imported), phenol, aromatics and many intermediates; each tonne needs port tankage [V-sib commodity-chem; K]. CRISIL cluster split of bulk-chemical demand: **Gujarat 39%, Mumbai 17%, East 12%, South-East 14%, others 18%** — AVTL is present in all but the South-East (Chennai/Ennore/Vizag) [V presentation].
4. **Edible oils.** India imports ~15–16 MMT of vegetable oils a year (palm ~55–60%) [K; see edible-oil.html]; Kandla and Haldia are the hubs. Duty changes shift volumes between crude and refined oil.
5. **POL trade.** Product exports from Reliance/Nayara go through captive jetties; independents earn on **imports, blending, bunkering and coastal movements** (AVTL "handled coastal movement of liquids along with import and exports") [V presentation]. Coastal cargo at major ports +8.3% FY26 [V MoPSW].
6. **Ammonia (new).** DAP/NPK plants near ports import ammonia; Hindustan Zinc's new DAP plant anchors AVTL Pipavav (15-year take-or-pay; **8,000–10,000 t contracted**, 20–25% first-year utilisation expected) [V PL; V transcript]. India's fertiliser requirement **67.7 MMT FY26 (+4%)** [V PL].
7. **Strategic and security stocks (new driver, 2026).** OMCs must plan **≥30 days of LPG** on top of commercial stocks (MoPNG, 29-May-2026) [V AIR]. 30 days × 33.2 MMT/yr ≈ **2.7 MMT** of LPG inventory vs ~1 mn MT static port capacity and ~18 days total system cover today ⇒ a **gap of ~0.6–1.7 MMT of LPG storage** depending on how bottling-plant tankage is counted [E]. The reported 10-year plan sizes it at **+4 MMT LPG** [V Business Today].
8. **Energy transition** — methanol bunkering at Kandla (green push) [V-snip]; green ammonia at Kandla (L&T Energy MoU with AVTL, non-binding) [V PL]; ethanol/biofuels storage (E20 blending) [K]; LCO₂ storage exploration [primer].

## 4. Supply side and cost structure

### 4.1 Key inputs

- **Port land and waterfront** — leased from major port authorities (Deendayal, JNPA, NMPA, SMP Kolkata/Haldia, Cochin) under land-allotment policy (30-year leases typical; upfront premium or annual lease rent; escalation; reset off market value) or from private ports (Pipavav/APM Terminals). AVTL's lease liabilities **₹1,626 Cr (Sep-26)**; right-of-use assets ~₹1,650–1,890 Cr gross (AR) [V]. Lease rent paid to Aegis Logistics ₹3.1 Cr [V AR].
- **Steel and construction** — tank shells (carbon/stainless), spheres, refrigerated double-wall tanks, pipelines, fire systems. Tanks have a **design life of ~40 years** [V presentation].
- **Power and nitrogen** (blanketing, heating, BOG compressors for refrigerated LPG/ammonia).
- **People** — AVTL employee cost ₹51.8 Cr FY26 (5.6% of revenue) [V].
- **Capital** — the dominant "input". AVTL FY26 depreciation ₹207.7 Cr + finance cost ₹109.7 Cr = **34% of revenue** [V].

### 5.3 Imports and China

- Unlike manufacturing sub-sectors, there is no import competition for storage itself; the industry is a **beneficiary of import dependence**. China matters through **chemical trade flows** (Chinese surplus chemicals → more Indian imports → more tank demand; Indian anti-dumping duties → fewer imports) [K; see commodity-chem.html].
- **The swing is domestic substitution:** every new refinery/cracker that makes LPG, methanol or aromatics in India reduces imports through third-party terminals. Refinery capacity rises from **267 MMTPA (FY26) to ~291 MMTPA by FY28E** (HRRL Barmer COD 22-Jun-2026, Panipat/Gujarat/Barauni expansions) [V PL; V-sib refining] — which PL models as lifting LPG output only from 13.1 to ~14 MMT.

A B-type business books the LPG as revenue, so its margin percentage looks small but EBITDA per tonne (~₹7,000/t distribution) is far larger than the ~₹1,240/t AVTL earns. **Do not compare margin % across archetypes** — compare EBITDA per tonne or per cbm and ROCE.

- LPG: throughput-based with take-or-pay elements. **Haldia (HALPG) has an exclusive HPCL agreement until FY2038** [V presentation]. - Ammonia: **15-year take-or-pay with Hindustan Zinc** [V].

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Ports, land and concessions

- **Major Port Authorities Act, 2021** (replaced the Major Port Trusts Act 1963 from 3-Nov-2021): port boards set their own tariffs (TAMP abolished for majors), may lease land on their own policy; PPP concessions follow the Model Concession Agreement (30-year typical for berths) [K]. AVTL's AR still lists the Major Port Trusts Act among applicable laws [V AR].
- **Land Policy for Major Ports (2014; revised guidelines 2023 [K])**: land allotted by tender/auction on **upfront premium or annual lease rent**; **lease rent revised periodically off market value** (Deendayal dispute record: "6% of market value as rent per annum… revised every five years") [V-snip]; leases up to 30 years (extendable; port-led industries longer in some cases) [K]. This is the industry's **largest non-operating risk**: a reset like the reported **~10× JNPA tank-farm rent reset** (2026, Ganesh Benzoplast commentary) can erase a liquid terminal's margin [V Sahi — detail not confirmed; FLAG].
- **Indian Ports Act, 2025** (replaced the 1908 Act): sets up State Maritime Boards to fix non-major-port tariffs; relevant to Pipavav (Gujarat Maritime Board) [V-sib ports].
- **Disputes:** AVTL carries **₹6.75 Cr of advances paid under protest to Deendayal Port Trust** (₹5.74 Cr impaired) [V AR] — a sign of tariff/lease disputes at Kandla.
- **Concession ends:** the **Pipavav port concession (GPPL/APM Terminals, with Gujarat Maritime Board) ends 29-Sep-2028**; GMB sent GPPL a comfort letter (late Aug-2026) to start processing an extension (90 days to file an investment/business plan) [V-sib ports]. AVTL's Pipavav LPG (70,800 MT), liquid (116,620 cbm) and ammonia (36,000 MT) assets sit inside that port, so **AVTL's Pipavav sub-lease/terminal rights depend on the GPPL extension** [E; FLAG — AVTL's own agreement terms not disclosed]. Mundra's concession ends 16-Feb-2031 [V-sib ports].

### 7.2 Safety and environment

- **PESO** (Petroleum & Explosives Safety Organisation) licences under the Petroleum Act/Rules, **Static & Mobile Pressure Vessels (Unfired) Rules** for LPG/ammonia; **OISD** standards (OISD-144 LPG installations, OISD-118 layouts) [K].
- **MSIHC Rules 1989** (hazardous chemicals), **Manufacture, Storage & Import of Hazardous Chemicals**; CRZ (coastal regulation zone) clearances; state pollution boards [K].
- Ammonia adds toxic-gas emergency planning (off-site plans) — a barrier to entry PL and management cite ("only 2–3 third-party ammonia terminals") [V PL].

### 7.3 LPG policy

- **Pricing:** domestic LPG price administered (Delhi 14.2 kg ₹942 on 1-Aug-2026) with under-recoveries borne by OMCs and periodic government compensation (₹30,000 Cr package vs ~₹62,000 Cr under-recovery by Aug-2026) [V PSU Watch; V-sib refining].
- **PMUY**: free connections since 2016; ₹300/cylinder targeted subsidy (12 refills/yr) [V presentation].
- **Private import ("parallel marketing")**: private LPG importers (Aegis, others) may import and sell non-domestic LPG; direct private imports collapsed **−82.2% Apr–Jul 2026** as Gulf supply was prioritised for households [V PSU Watch].
- **Rationing tools proven in 2026**: commercial cap (20% → 30% → 50% → 70% → full on 25-Jun), refinery diversion of feedstock to LPG (production record ~92,000 t/day by late May) [V AIR; V-sib refining].
- **Aadhaar biometric authentication** for subsidised refills from 1-Oct-2026 [V-sib refining].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **1970s–1990s: state monopoly.** POL and LPG imports through OMC and port-trust installations; Indian Oil Tanking (IOC–Oiltanking) and private tank farms appear after 1990s liberalisation; Kandla becomes the chemicals/edible-oil port [K].
2. **1990s–2000s: Aegis builds the private LPG import chain.** Aegis Logistics (Chandaria family; Mumbai Trombay terminals) builds India's first private LPG import terminal at Mumbai and Pipavav, pioneers auto-gas and parallel-marketed LPG [K; V presentation: "50+ years"].
3. **2005–2015: ISPRL Phase 1** built (Vizag 2015, Mangalore 2016, Padur 2018) — 5.33 MMT [K].
4. **2016: PMUY** launched; LPG consumption accelerates (FY14–26 CAGR ~6%); imports rise from ~8 MMT (FY15) to 21.5 MMT (FY26) [V presentation; V PL].
5. **2021: Aegis–Vopak JV.** Vopak India B.V. buys into Aegis's terminal business; company renamed Aegis Vopak Terminals (Aug-2021; originally incorporated May-2013 as Aegis LPG Logistics (Pipavav)) [V PL]. Pre-JV capacity: **556,000 cbm liquid; 67,000 MT LPG** (Nov-2021) [V presentation].
6. **2022–2025: roll-up.** CRL Terminals and four Kandla liquid terminals (545,713 cbm, 2022); Haldia +51,050 cbm (2023); Kochi and Mangalore liquid acquisitions (2024); Mangalore 74,390 cbm and JNPA 104,750 cbm (2025) [V PL].
7. **May–Jun 2025: IPO.** ₹2,800 Cr fresh issue at ₹235 (band ₹223–235), 26–28 May; anchor ₹1,260 Cr; subscribed **1.66× (chittorgarh) / ~2.1× (BS)**; listed **2-Jun-2025 at ₹220 (−6.4%)** [V chittorgarh; V-snip BS]. DRHP size had been ₹3,500 Cr [V-snip]. Proceeds: debt repayment ₹2,015.95 Cr; **acquisition of the New Mangalore cryogenic LPG terminal ₹671.30 Cr**; GCP ₹3.63 Cr; issue expenses ₹109.12 Cr [V chittorgarh].
8. **FY26: the LPG step-change.** New Mangalore 82,000 MT (19-Jun-2025) and Pipavav 48,000 MT (10-Jul-2025) take static LPG from 70,800 to 152,800 → 200,800 MT; HALPG (+25,000 MT, Jan-2026) → 225,800 MT; gas throughput 1.6 → **3.9 MMT (+145%)** [V AR; V PL].
9. **28-Feb-2026: West Asia war.** Hormuz disrupted; India's LPG supply cut; commercial LPG rationed from 12-Mar; LPG consumption −12.8% in March, −16.5% Apr–Jul; US becomes India's largest LPG supplier [V; V-snip]. AVTL Q1 FY27 gas revenue −3.5%, throughput 0.9 MMT; liquids +30.6% [V].
10. **May–Aug 2026: storage policy turns.** 30-day LPG reserve directive (29-May); $42 bn reserve plan reported (5-Aug); ISPRL Phase 2 progress reported (13-Aug) [V].
11. **Q2 FY27: ammonia commissioned; JNPA bottling live; margin record 78.6%.** Parent signs Tristar (US$809 mn) and launches QIP [V].
12. **Global cycle context:** Vopak's occupancy peaked ~2022–23 (oil contango, energy-security storage), eased to ~91% by 2025 while chemical storage demand stayed weak [V Vopak FY25: "Demand for chemical storage services continued to be weak"].

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **Domestic substitution of imports.** New refineries and petrochemical complexes (HPCL Barmer/HRRL, Panipat expansion, BPCL Bina petchem, Reliance) reduce imports of LPG/chemicals at the margin; LPG output grows only ~2%/yr (PL) [V PL; V-sib refining].
2. **PNG/electric cooking substitution of LPG.** PNG incentive scheme approved **18-Aug-2026**, effective 1-Sep-2026 [V PSU Watch]; city-gas expansion (gas-distribution.html) and induction cooking erode urban LPG; active LPG customers fell for the first time in 2026 [V].
3. **Pipelines vs terminals.** KGPL (8.25 mtpa) and JLPL doubling help AVTL's Kandla/Pipavav **but** also let OMCs move LPG from their own terminals to more bottling plants — pipelines raise the value of whichever terminal is connected [V PL].
4. **Energy transition in liquids.** Lower long-run gasoline/diesel trade; growth in ethanol (E20+), biodiesel, methanol, ammonia, LCO₂ — the same tanks can often be repurposed (Vopak: ~€650 mn committed to energy-transition infrastructure) [V Vopak Q3].
5. **Ammonia/hydrogen.** National Green Hydrogen Mission targets **5 MMTPA green H₂ by 2030**; hydrogen hubs at **Kandla, Paradip, Tuticorin** [V PL]. Green ammonia exports would need export-side storage — upside for AVTL Kandla (L&T Energy MoU, non-binding) [V PL].
6. **Safety events.** LPG/ammonia accidents (BLEVE, toxic release) are low-probability, high-severity; insurance and licence suspension risk [K; V PL key risks].
7. **Digital/automation** — tank gauging, terminal management systems; Vopak's playbook; modest cost lever [K].
8. **Larger ships.** VLGC-capable jetties (Kandla done; Pipavav APM Terminals jetty in progress) cut freight and demurrage — a competitive edge for terminals that have them [V transcript].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert leads with)

1. **India's LPG import dependence, and the 2026 proof of how fragile it is.** India imports ~65% of its LPG (21.5 of 33.2 MMT in FY26) and ~90% of that came through Hormuz before 28-Feb-2026 [V PL; V-sib refining]. The war cut March consumption 12.8% and Apr–Jul 16.5%, forced rationing of commercial LPG and an 82% fall in private imports [V]. Supply swung to the US (US share 12–14% in Jan–Feb → 55% in May → 73% in July per Kpler-based reports) [V-snip]. Longer voyages and VLGC cargoes favour large refrigerated terminals with VLGC jetties: AVTL's Kandla (VLGC-compliant), Pipavav and New Mangalore. The terminal business proved more resilient than LPG trading: AVTL's Q1 gas revenue fell just 3.5% YoY and recovered to +12.5% in Q2 [V].
2. **Strategic storage becomes policy.** Pre-war India had ~1 mn MT of static LPG storage ("not even 10 days") and ~9–10 days of crude in SPRs [V transcript; V-sib refining]. Since then: a 30-day LPG reserve directive to OMCs (29-May-2026), a reported $42 bn ten-year plan including **+4 MMT of LPG storage** funded by a ₹1.29/kg levy, and ISPRL Phase 2 moving [V]. The addressable LPG storage build (~4 MMT) is ~**18× AVTL's 225,800 MT** [E]. Whether OMCs **contract** independent capacity or **build** captive capacity (at 3–8× AVTL's capex per MT) decides who captures it. Nothing is contracted yet.
3. **Capex × utilisation × time: the build-out is the equity story.** AVTL's assets doubled in two years (gross block ₹3,491 Cr Mar-24 → ₹7,104 Cr Sep-26) [V screener]. Management plans US$5 bn by 2030–31, roughly **10× today's EBITDA** [V; E]. New gas assets earn ~6.5% pre-tax at ~25% of design throughput; liquids ~11.6% [E from V]. Returns rise only if throughput turns rise — which depends on pipelines (KGPL), VLGC jetties and rail, all third-party infrastructure.
4. **Port land and concessions are the hidden cost line.** Lease liabilities are ~40% of AVTL's gross debt (₹1,626 Cr) [V]. Land-rent resets (Deendayal: 6% of market value, 5-yearly; a reported ~10× JNPA reset) and concession expiries (**Pipavav 29-Sep-2028**) can reprice the business [V-snip; V-sib ports]. AVTL already has ₹6.75 Cr of "advances under protest" with Deendayal [V AR].
5. **Customer and related-party concentration.** One gas customer is **25% of consolidated revenue** [V AR]. The OMCs dominate LPG and HPCL has Haldia exclusively to FY2038 [V]. Related parties (Aegis entities) are **~16% of revenue** and the seller of most assets AVTL bought in FY26 [V AR; E]. Take-or-pay protects volumes, but renewal pricing sits with a few buyers who also build their own terminals.
6. **Financing: from IPO cash to capex debt.** The IPO repaid ~₹2,016 Cr of debt. Finance cost fell to ₹19–20 Cr/qtr in Q2–Q3 FY26, then rose back to ₹39–41 Cr/qtr as ~₹3,077 Cr of FY26 investment and acquisitions were debt-funded [V screener]. Gross bank debt reached ₹2,495 Cr by Sep-26, ~₹1,959 Cr of it short-term [V]. With MPS dilution due by Jun-2028, the equity to fund US$2 bn of capex at 0.6× gearing will come from **promoter sell-downs/QIPs**. That is supply overhang for a 13%-float stock [V transcript; E].
7. **Competition: captive OMCs, port owners, and a new PE consolidator.** In LPG, OMCs build their own terminals (BPCL +140 TMT; Petronet's mooted ~3 MMT propane at Dahej) [V Angel One; V transcript]. In liquids, IMC is ~2/3 of AVTL's size across 14 ports; KKR's Cisternina is assembling 0.5 → 1.5 mn kl [V PL; V-snip]; and JSW/Adani control their own ports [V-sib ports]. AVTL's moat is multi-port scale, low capex per MT, Vopak's engineering and the Aegis LPG ecosystem.
8. **Energy transition optionality (ammonia, methanol, biofuels).** Pipavav ammonia (36,000 MT, 15-year TOP with Hindustan Zinc, ITOCHU 10% → 25%) is India's first independent ammonia terminal [V]. Management talks of 8–10 ammonia terminals by 2030 [V PL]. Treat this as optionality: first-year utilisation is guided at only 20–25% [V PL].

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Liquids:**
Revenue = Capacity (cbm) × Utilisation × Monthly rate (₹/kl) × 12 + throughput/handling extras

**Gas (LPG/ammonia):**
Revenue = Throughput (t) × Fee (₹/t) [+ reserved-capacity/take-or-pay minimums]
         = Static capacity (MT) × Turns × Fee

**Company:**
EBITDA = Revenue × ~75% (fixed-cost base: staff, power, repairs, insurance)
EBIT = EBITDA − Depreciation (~40-year tank life; RoU on port leases)
PAT = (EBIT − Interest on debt and leases + Other income) × (1 − ~16–20% tax) − Minorities

**Value creation test:** EBIT ÷ capex per unit > post-tax cost of capital (~10–11% [E]); management's project hurdle is a **~15% post-tax IRR** [V PL].

### C.1 Worked example — AVTL liquids, FY26 (reconciles to the reported ₹440.5 Cr) [E]
- Capacity 1,673,383 cbm × 75% utilisation = 1,255,000 occupied kl.
- Average rate ~₹292/kl/month × 12 = ₹3,505/kl/yr.
- 1,255,000 × ₹3,505 = **₹440 Cr** ✔ (reported ₹440.5 Cr).
- Segment EBITDA ₹313.7 Cr ⇒ **71.2%** margin; **₹1,875 per cbm of capacity**; ₹2,500 per occupied kl.
- H1 FY27 run-rate: ₹255.4 Cr × 2 = ₹511 Cr on the same 1.67 mn cbm ⇒ ₹3,051/cbm/yr — **+16% yield**, from the JNPA (101,900 cbm) and Mangalore (75,230 cbm) tanks at high-value locations [V transcript: "more revenue in locations of higher realization"].

### C.2 Worked example — AVTL LPG, FY26 [E]
- Throughput 3.9 MMT × ₹1,237/t = **₹482.6 Cr** (reported) ✔.
- Segment EBITDA ₹414.5 Cr ⇒ 85.9% margin; **₹1,063/t**.
- Static base ~225,800 MT at year-end (average ~190,000 MT [E]) ⇒ ~20 turns.
- **If turns reached 40×** on 225,800 MT ⇒ 9.0 MMT × ₹1,237 = ₹1,117 Cr revenue (+₹634 Cr), EBITDA ~₹960 Cr — **with no new capex**. That gap is the gas bull case.

### C.3 Worked example — one new LPG terminal (New Mangalore, 82,000 MT, ₹671 Cr) [E]
- At 1.5 MMT/yr (18 turns) × ₹1,240/t = ₹186 Cr revenue; EBITDA at 85% = ₹158 Cr; depreciation (40 yrs) ₹17 Cr; EBIT ₹141 Cr ⇒ **21% pre-tax ROCE**.
- At 0.75 MMT (9 turns) ⇒ EBIT ~₹62 Cr ⇒ **9%**.
- Breakeven vs a 10% cost of capital ≈ **0.8 MMT/yr** [E]. Management's "6.0 mtpa design" implies huge headroom; actual volumes per terminal are not disclosed [FLAG].

### C.4 Worked example — ammonia, Pipavav (36,000 MT; ~₹475–525 Cr) [E]
- Management framing: storage ₹2,750/t + distribution ~₹7,000/t = ~₹10,000/t; "capable to do 1 million tons"; "even if you start at 25%" [V transcript].
- **Storage-only at 250,000 t/yr**: ₹2,750 × 0.25 MMT = **₹69 Cr revenue**; EBITDA ~₹55 Cr ⇒ ~11% pre-tax EBITDA yield on ₹500 Cr.
- **Contracted floor only (HZL 8,000–10,000 t, if per month ⇒ ~100,000–120,000 t/yr [E; FLAG on units])**: ~₹30 Cr revenue.
- **With distribution margin** (if booked in AVTL) at 250,000 t × ₹7,000 = +₹175 Cr gross margin — the swing factor; who books it is unresolved (trap 9).

### C.5 Worked example — a greenfield liquid tank at JNPA [E]
- Capex ~₹20,000–25,000/cbm (J2 blended; Aegis Mumbai ₹19,500/cbm) [E from V].
- Revenue at 90% × ₹4,000/kl/yr (premium location) = ₹3,600/cbm; EBITDA 75% = ₹2,700; depreciation ₹550 (40 yrs, incl. lease) ⇒ EBIT ₹2,150 ⇒ **~9–11% pre-tax ROCE**.
- Return relies on the premium Mumbai-region rate. A Kandla-type rate (~₹2,600) at the same capex would give ~6%. That is why AVTL buys brownfield tanks cheaply (₹5,300–17,000/kl) where it can [V-snip].
