## READ THIS FIRST: ten definitional traps

1. **Three different "EBITDA"s for the same company — never compare reported Ind AS EBITDA across this sector.**
   - Under **Ind AS 116** the head-lease rent is removed from operating cost and replaced by depreciation of a right-of-use (RoU) asset plus interest on a lease liability. Reported EBITDA therefore excludes the operator's single largest cost.
   - FY26 reported EBITDA margins: **WeWork 65.2%** (company) / 64% (screener OP); **Smartworks 64.3%**; **Awfis 36.8%** [V]. These are not comparable to each other or to any normal business.
   - Each company publishes its own "rent-paid" measure, and **the three definitions differ** (table in trap 2 and §B1). Use them, label them, and never add them without saying so.
2. **The three adjusted-EBITDA definitions.**

| Company | Name used | Definition (company's words) | FY26 | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WeWork India | "IGAAP-equivalent EBITDA" | Reverses Ind AS 116 and other Ind AS adjustments; rent expensed as under old Indian GAAP (accrual, straight-lined); after ESOP cost, pre-exceptional; FY26 includes ₹9 Cr one-time IPO cost | **₹499.2 Cr** | 20.2% of total revenue ₹2,477.4 Cr (20.5% ex IPO cost) [V PPT] |
| Smartworks | "Normalised EBITDA" | Reported EBITDA **less repayment of lease liabilities** (principal + interest as paid in the cash-flow statement) | **₹314.4 Cr** | 17.5% of revenue ₹1,795.8 Cr [V PPT] |
| Awfis | "Normalised EBITDA" (FY26) → **"Cash EBITDA"** (new from Q1 FY27) | FY26: reported EBITDA − "effect of Ind AS 116" (₹349 Cr) + other Ind AS (₹13 Cr). From Q1 FY27: reported EBITDA + finance-lease rent received − **rental payments traced from the cash-flow statement** + non-cash ESOP; validated by MSKA (BDO) under an agreed-upon-procedures engagement | **₹213 Cr** (FY26 normalised) | 14.3% [V PPT]. Q1 FY27 cash EBITDA ₹44 Cr = **10.1%** of normalised revenue ₹437 Cr [V PPT] |

   - **Awfis changed its definition between FY26 and Q1 FY27.** The old "normalised" (accrual) margin was ~14%; the new cash margin is ~10%. Q1 FY26 restated on the new method = ₹33 Cr (10.2%) [V PPT]. FY27 guidance is **cash EBITDA ₹190–200 Cr on revenue >₹1,800 Cr (~10.5–11%)** [V transcript]. Compare Awfis FY27 to its FY26 only on the same basis.
   - **WeWork's IGAAP figure is not a cash figure.** In FY26 the IGAAP adjustment removed ₹1,124.6 Cr from EBITDA, while rent actually paid to landlords (cash-flow statement) was ₹1,014.4 Cr plus ₹22.0 Cr finance-lease payments [V PPT]. The accrual rent charge exceeds cash paid because of straight-lining over rent-free periods on new centres and CAM/other items [E]. On a Smartworks-style "reported EBITDA − all lease cash payments" basis WeWork FY26 would be **₹1,623.8 − ₹1,036.4 = ₹587.4 Cr (24.1% of revenue from operations)** [E from V inputs]. Prefer the company's ₹499.2 Cr in prose; show the cash version only in the reconciliation table.
3. **Three different "PAT"s too.**
   - WeWork FY26: reported Ind AS PAT **₹74.9 Cr** (incl. a ₹22 Cr deferred-tax credit); IGAAP-equivalent PAT pre-exceptional **₹179.0 Cr** [V PPT]. Screener shows ₹75 Cr.
   - WeWork FY25: reported Ind AS PAT **₹128.2 Cr** was driven by a **₹285 Cr deferred-tax credit** on a pre-tax loss of ₹156.8 Cr; IGAAP-equivalent PAT pre-exceptional ₹76.6 Cr, and after a ₹112 Cr exceptional item, **−₹35.4 Cr** [V PPT]. The "maiden profit" of FY25 was a tax event.
   - Smartworks FY26: reported PAT **₹10.5 Cr**; normalised PAT **₹96.6 Cr** [V PPT].
   - Awfis FY26: reported PAT **₹71 Cr**; normalised PBT ₹103 Cr; FY25 reported PAT ₹68 Cr included a **₹25 Cr exceptional gain** (sale of Awfis Care) → pre-exceptional PBT ₹44 Cr [V PPT].
   - **Ind AS front-loads lease cost** (interest is highest early in a lease), so a fast-growing operator's Ind AS PAT is *lower* than its IGAAP/normalised PAT. WeWork Q1 FY27: Ind AS PAT **−₹4.1 Cr** vs IGAAP **+₹53.2 Cr** [V PPT]. A "loss" headline for Q2 FY27 is plausible and not alarming by itself.
4. **"Debt" on screener is mostly lease liability.**
   - Screener borrowings Mar-26: WeWork **₹5,550 Cr**, Smartworks **₹4,778 Cr**, Awfis **₹1,501 Cr** [V screener].
   - Actual bank/NCD debt: WeWork gross **₹332.8 Cr** (cash ₹344.5 Cr → **net cash ₹11.7 Cr**, Mar-26; net debt ₹31.6 Cr Jun-26); Smartworks gross **₹207.0 Cr** (cash & bank incl. deposits ₹263.1 Cr → **net cash ₹56.1 Cr**, Mar-26; net debt ₹5.6 Cr Jun-26); Awfis borrowings **₹51 Cr** (net debt/equity −0.20 → **net cash ~₹110 Cr**, Mar-26 [E]; company states net debt **−₹45 Cr**, Jun-26) [V PPTs].
   - Lease liabilities Mar-26: WeWork **₹5,217.5 Cr** (₹4,567.2 non-current + ₹650.3 current) [V PPT balance sheet]; Smartworks **₹4,527.5 Cr standalone** (₹3,871.8 + ₹655.7) [V AR], ~₹4,571 Cr consolidated [E = screener borrowings − gross debt]; Awfis **₹1,450 Cr** (₹1,055 + ₹395) [V PPT].
   - **Use net debt ex-leases for leverage; show lease liabilities separately as "committed rent".** Never compute "net debt/EBITDA" by dividing screener borrowings by screener OP.
5. **Capacity units differ: desks vs seats vs sq ft (SBA vs chargeable vs leasable).**
   - WeWork: **desks** (133.6k operational, Jun-26) and **members** (113.4k); 9.1 msf operational *leasable* area [V PPT].
   - Smartworks: **seats** (238k operational capacity; 193k occupied) and **SBA** (10.4 msf operational; 16.9 msf total incl. LOIs) [V PPT]. Smartworks' seats-per-sq-ft is denser (~23 seats per 1,000 sq ft SBA vs WeWork ~15 desks per 1,000 sq ft) [E] — enterprise managed floors are packed tighter.
   - Awfis: **seats** (159k operational; 170k incl. under fit-out; 185k signed) and **chargeable area** (8.4 msf total supply; 9.1 msf signed) [V PPT]. Awfis' MA seats are on landlords' balance sheets; Smartworks' and WeWork's are on their own.
   - Do not rank "largest operator" without naming the unit: Awfis is largest by **centres** (242 operational); Smartworks largest by **sq ft** (10.4 msf operational, 16.9 msf secured); WeWork largest by **revenue** (₹2,440 Cr) and **market cap** (₹8,770 Cr).
6. **Occupancy has four flavours.**
   - Portfolio vs mature (WeWork: ">12 months operational"; Awfis: ">12 months vintage"; Smartworks: "mature centres").
   - **Committed** occupancy (Smartworks) counts signed-but-not-yet-moved-in clients; "occupancy" counts billing seats. Smartworks Jun-26: occupancy **81%**, committed **86%**; mature **89%**, committed mature **92%** [V PPT].
   - WeWork Jun-26: portfolio **84.9%**, mature **87.5%**, growth centres **64.9%** [V PPT]. Awfis Jun-26: blended **76%**, mature **83%** [V PPT].
   - **Breakeven occupancy** (WeWork): 56.6% portfolio, Q1 FY27 [V PPT].
7. **"Lock-in" ≠ "tenure", and client lock-in ≠ landlord lock-in.**
   - Client side: Smartworks average total tenure **48 months**, lock-in **35 months** (Q1 FY27; 1,000+ seat cohort); WeWork portfolio average commitment term **27 months**, large enterprise 33 (Q1 FY27); Awfis weighted total tenure **38 months**, lock-in **26 months** (Q1 FY27) [V PPTs].
   - Landlord side: head leases are typically **9–15 years** with a **3–5 year operator lock-in** [K; primer]; Awfis MA arrangements run **5–9 years** [V Awfis AR].
   - The useful number is **contracted client revenue ÷ contracted rent**: WeWork **2.9×** (₹3,363 Cr locked-in core revenue vs ₹1,152 Cr locked-in rent, Jun-26); Smartworks **~2.1×** (₹3,539.7 Cr lock-in revenue vs ₹1,696.1 Cr landlord lock-in rent; mature footprint 2.7×, new 0.8×) [V PPTs]. Awfis does not publish this.
8. **"Revenue" includes non-rental items whose mix moves margins.**
   - Smartworks' "other operating revenue" jumped to **₹202.3 Cr in FY26 (11% of revenue, +139%)** and ₹68.3 Cr in Q1 FY27 (+202% YoY): ancillary services, **termination income**, software, construction/fit-out, traded goods [V PPT]. Lease-rental revenue grew only **24% in FY26 and 34% in Q1 FY27** vs headline 31% / 44%.
   - WeWork VAS includes **customisation income** (₹47 Cr in Q4 FY26, lumpy) [V transcript]; Q1 FY27 VAS ₹57.5 Cr.
   - Awfis **Transform** (design-and-build) was ₹257 Cr in FY26 (17% of revenue; −8% YoY), ₹73 Cr in Q1 FY27; 92% third-party [V PPT]. It is a low-margin contracting business inside a "rental" company.
9. **WeWork India is not WeWork Inc.** WeWork India is an Embassy-controlled licensee. WeWork Inc. (US) filed Chapter 11 in Nov-2023 and emerged in 2024 under Yardi-controlled ownership [K]. The global entity is a **15.17%** shareholder via **1 Ariel Way Tenant Ltd** (down from 23.45% pre-IPO; sold in the OFS) and the brand licensor (WeWork International Ltd), paid **₹59.2 Cr of "management fees" in FY26 (≈2.4% of revenue from operations)** [V AR FY26]. Do not quote WeWork Inc.'s history as WeWork India's.
10. **Calendar-year market data vs fiscal-year company data, and consultant definitions.** CBRE "absorption" (66.4 msf, 9M CY2026) and Colliers "Grade A leasing" (54.4 msf, 9M CY2026) measure different things; flex shares quoted by operators are CBRE's. Quote the consultant and the period every time (§2).

- **The market has split the cohort.** On 23-Oct-2026:
  - WeWork trades at **99× Ind AS P/E, ~49× FY26 IGAAP PAT, ~17.6× FY26 IGAAP EBITDA** (EV ex-leases) [E].
  - Smartworks **180× reported P/E, ~52× normalised PAT, ~16× FY26 normalised EBITDA** [E].
  - Awfis **18.7× P/E, ~7.2× FY26 normalised EBITDA, ~7.9× FY27 cash-EBITDA guidance** [E]. Awfis is **−64% over one year** (52-week high ₹639 → ₹221) [V screener]: a large client exit across five centres, 2021-cohort landlord rent resets, a definitional reset from ~14% "normalised" to ~10% "cash" EBITDA margin, and sponsor selling (Peak XV, Bisque) [V transcript; V AR; E for attribution — no single trigger was found in the press].
- **Risks are cyclical, not structural (yet).** The asset-liability mismatch (fixed long leases vs shorter client licences), the IT-services hiring slowdown and AI-led headcount rationalisation, GCC concentration (WeWork: 65% of core revenue from "global members", 46% North America), supply build-up by smaller operators, and promoter/sponsor overhangs.

## 1. What the sub-sector does: products, value chain, where value is captured

### 1.1 Products (by member)

| Product | What it is | Typical buyer | Members |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Managed office (MO)** | Private, client-branded floor or building designed, fitted out and run by the operator for one client; 3–5-year terms; all-inclusive per-seat fee | Enterprises, GCCs (500–5,000+ seats) | Smartworks (core: ~41% of rental revenue from 1,000+ seat clients), WeWork (managed office 23.7% of revenue in Q1 FY27), Awfis (MO + "Partial MO") |
| **Private offices / dedicated desks** | Enclosed cabins and reserved desks inside a shared centre; 6–36-month licences | SMEs, startups, enterprise project teams | WeWork (64.1% of Q1 FY27 revenue together with core), Awfis (core), Smartworks (smaller share) |
| **Hot desks / day passes / memberships** | Unreserved seats, "All Access" roaming passes, on-demand bookings | Freelancers, hybrid workers | WeWork "Digital products" (₹26.2 Cr Q1 FY27, 3.8%; ~80% EBITDA margin per management), Awfis on-demand |
| **Virtual office** | Registered address, mail handling, GST registration support | Small firms, new entrants to a state | WeWork (Digital), Awfis (allied services) |
| **Value-added services (VAS)** | Parking, F&B, events, IT, customisation, employee transport, member marketplaces | Existing members | WeWork VAS 8.4% (₹57.5 Cr Q1 FY27); WeWork "Member Services" B2B marketplace launched 15-Jul-2026; Smartworks food courts, gyms, crèches (~3.6% [primer]) |
| **Design & build (D&B) / fit-out for third parties** | Turnkey interior fit-outs for corporates who keep their own leases | Corporates | **Awfis Transform** (₹257 Cr FY26; 92% third-party in Q1 FY27; ₹200 Cr won/in delivery); WeWork "RIVET" D&B and PM consultancy (new, FY26 PPT); Smartworks (in "other operating revenue") |
| **Workplace software** | Space-management SaaS | Enterprises | WeWork resells "Workplace" (Yardi) software; WeWork Workplace LLC bills WeWork India 75% of the licence fee [V AR] |

### 1.2 Value chain (land → building → fit-out → seat → service)

1. **Landowner / developer** builds a Grade-A office (cost ~₹9,000/sq ft all-in on owned land [V-sib realty.html]) and leases it at ~₹70–170 per sq ft per month depending on city (§4.1). Large institutional landlords: Embassy Office Parks REIT, Mindspace Business Parks REIT (K Raheja), Brookfield India REIT, Knowledge Realty Trust (Sattva–Blackstone, listed Aug-2025 [K]), DLF/DLF Cyber City, RMZ, Prestige, Panchshil, Hiranandani, Tata Realty, Salarpuria Sattva.
2. **Flex operator** signs a head lease (straight lease, 9–15 years, 3–5-year lock-in, 4–5% annual or 15% every three years escalation [K]) or a **managed-aggregation / revenue-share** agreement (landlord funds part or all of the fit-out; operator pays a share of revenue or a profit share; Awfis tenures 5–9 years [V AR]) or a pure **management contract** (operator runs the centre for a fee; landlord takes the risk) [K].
3. **Fit-out** (capex): interiors, furniture, MEP/HVAC upgrades, IT, cafeteria. Smartworks reports ~₹1,350/sq ft [primer; company claim]; WeWork's new Pune centre implies **₹2,737/sq ft or ₹1.53 lakh per desk** (₹44 Cr for 1,60,744 sq ft / 2,881 desks) [E from V filing]. Awfis' MA model shifts 40–100% of this to the landlord [primer; V AR].
4. **Sales** via direct enterprise teams and brokers (Smartworks brokerage 2.5% of lease-rental revenue [V PPT]; WeWork brokerage accrual ₹25.5 Cr in Q1 FY27 ≈ 3.7% of revenue [E]).
5. **Operations:** facility management, housekeeping, security, utilities (CAM), community, F&B. Smartworks opex ~₹30–35/sq ft/month [primer]; WeWork opex/sq ft +5.6% YoY in Q1 FY27 [V PPT].
6. **Client** pays a per-seat (or per-floor) monthly fee covering rent, fit-out, services and amenities.

### 1.3 Where value is captured
- **The spread between client fee and landlord rent** (WeWork: revenue ≈ 2.8–3.0× rent; Smartworks targets "rental revenue at least 2× rental expense" [V PPTs]), multiplied by **occupancy**, minus opex and fit-out depreciation.
- **Ancillary layers on the same square foot** (digital ~80% EBITDA margin per WeWork; VAS; D&B) — small in revenue, big in incremental margin [V transcript].
- **Landlords capture value too:** flex operators are now the largest single tenant category in India; a REIT's flex share of leasing rises with it, and landlords increasingly co-invest (MA) or run their own flex brands (Embassy's stake in WeWork India; DLF and RMZ's CoWrks [K]).

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **GCCs (global capability centres).** 2,117 GCCs / 3,728 units in India, US$98.4 bn revenue, 2.36 mn staff (FY26E, NASSCOM–Zinnov) [V Awfis PPT; V-sib it-services.html]. GCCs took ~42% of 9M CY2026 absorption (28 msf) [V-snip CBRE]. More than 100 new GCCs opened in FY26 [V-sib it-services.html]. By city (GCC units, FY26E): Bengaluru 1,080, Hyderabad 515, NCR 490, Pune 475, Chennai 405, Mumbai 375 [V Awfis PPT]. Member exposure: Awfis 100+ GCC clients, **24%** of rental revenue (Q1 FY27); Smartworks GCCs **~21%** (from 15% in FY26); WeWork "global members" **65%** of core revenue, North America 46% [V PPTs].
2. **"Core + flex" enterprise real-estate strategies.** Occupiers keep a long-leased core and put 15–20% of their portfolio in flex to handle uncertainty, pilots and ramp-ups [V-snip Colliers; V PPTs]. This is why enterprise share of member revenue is high: Smartworks **92%** enterprise; WeWork **77%** enterprise members (>200 FTE); Awfis **64%** enterprise/MNC clients [V PPTs].
3. **Capex avoidance.** A corporate fit-out costs ₹2,500–4,000/sq ft plus 9–12 months of project time [K, indicative]; flex converts that to opex and a 45–60-day move-in (Smartworks delivers fit-outs in 45–60 days [V PPT]).
4. **Hybrid work and distributed teams.** Multi-city clients: Smartworks 35% of revenue (Q1 FY27); Awfis multi-centre clients 48% of client base (FY26); WeWork "All Access" roaming product [V PPTs].
5. **Domestic occupiers and BFSI.** WeWork FY26 desk sales: BFSI **9,036 desks (+176%)**, technology 11,142 (+19%), telecom 3,687 (+281%), IT services & consulting **2,684 (−43%)** [V PPT]. Smartworks: ~75% of Q1 FY27 new revenue came from non-IT sectors; IT/ITeS share of portfolio down from ~44% (FY24) to ~35% (Q1 FY27) [V PPT].
6. **Tier-2 cities.** Awfis has 25 centres / 13.2k seats in tier-2 (8% of seats) [V PPT]; Smartworks entered Indore, Kochi, Coimbatore, Jaipur [V PPT]. Tier-2 is the low-rent, low-price, high-growth frontier.
7. **Headwinds on demand:**
   - **IT-services hiring.** The it-services report documents a hiring freeze at tier-1 vendors and AI-led pyramid compression [V-sib]. WeWork's IT services & consulting desk sales fell 43% in FY26 [V]. Mitigant: GCCs and BFSI are hiring.
   - **AI headcount risk.** Operators argue the opposite (Redseer: ~79 msf of net-new AI-led demand by CY2030; GCC AI workforce 181k → 730k by CY2030P) [V Smartworks PPT — sponsored research; treat as advocacy].
   - **US policy.** H-1B fee and US tariffs push work offshore to GCCs (tailwind), but a US recession would hit GCC expansion budgets (headwind) [V-sib it-services.html; E].

## 7. Regulation and policy

1. **Accounting: Ind AS 116 (Leases), effective 1-Apr-2019** — the single most important "regulation" for reading this sector (§B1). Sub-lease of a full floor to one MO client can be classified as a **finance lease** by the operator (Awfis), removing that rent from revenue and booking a finance-lease receivable; Awfis adds it back in "normalised revenue" (₹12 Cr in Q1 FY27) [V PPT].
2. **SEBI ICDR / LODR:** non-GAAP measures (IGAAP-equivalent, normalised, cash EBITDA) are permitted in presentations with reconciliations; Awfis had its Q1 FY27 reconciliation **AUP-validated by MSKA (BDO network)** [V]. SEBI kept WeWork India's first DRHP "in abeyance" (2025) before approving the refiled offer [V-snip Outlook Business].
3. **GST:** 18% GST on renting of commercial property and on co-working licence fees [K]. GST 2.0 (22-Sep-2025) rationalised slabs but left commercial renting at 18% [K, verify]. ITC on construction of immovable property is blocked (Sec 17(5)(d) CGST) — fit-out capex ITC is partly blocked depending on whether fixtures are "plant" (Safari Retreats SC judgment, Oct-2024, then the retrospective amendment in Budget 2025) [K]. WeWork received an ₹11.01 Cr ITC show-cause notice on 1-Oct-2026 [V screener]. **ITC eligibility on fit-outs is a live cost item.**
4. **SEZ rules:** many IT-park floors are in SEZs; flex operators in SEZs need units/co-developer approvals; SEZ reforms allowing partial de-notification and non-processing-area use (2025–26) expand usable supply for flex [K, verify].
5. **Stamp duty and registration** on leases (state levies, ~1–5% of average annual rent) [K]; leave-and-licence registration in Maharashtra.
6. **State GCC policies** (Karnataka, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Gujarat GIFT City, UP, Maharashtra) offer rent/capex subsidies for GCCs, some redeemable in co-working/plug-and-play space [V-sib it-services.html for the policy list; K for flex eligibility].
7. **REIT regulation** (SEBI REIT Regulations; SM-REITs from 2024): REITs report flex tenancy; BHIVE and others pursue SM-REIT routes [K].
8. **Labour codes** (in force 21-Nov-2025 [K, verify]) — modest cost effect on contracted housekeeping/security staff.

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

- **2014–2017: birth.** Awfis founded Dec-2014 (first centre Delhi, 2015); Smartworks founded 2016 (Kolkata roots; Neetish Sarda, Harsh Binani); WeWork enters India 2016–17 via a licence to Embassy (WeWork India incorporated 2016); IndiQube 2015; Innov8, 91springboard, CoWrks, Table Space all founded 2015–2017 [K; V screener "incorporated 2016" for WeWork India].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Ind AS 116 makes the headline numbers meaningless — rent-paid EBITDA is the true operating line.**
   - Reported EBITDA margins 37–65% fall to 10–20% after rent; PAT is depressed further by front-loaded lease interest on new centres.
   - Each company publishes a different adjustment (IGAAP-equivalent, normalised, cash). Comparing them is the analyst's first job (§B1).
2. **The asset-liability mismatch (duration risk).**
   - Operators owe ₹11,200 Cr of lease liabilities (sum, Mar-26 [E]) against ~2–4-year client commitments.
   - Contracted cover (WeWork 2.9×, Smartworks ~2.1×) and pre-commitment of new centres (Awfis Partial MO 50%+, Smartworks pre-fills) are the mitigants.
3. **Occupancy × realisation against fixed rent is the entire P&L.**
   - ~₹25 Cr of annual EBITDA per occupancy point for WeWork [E]; breakeven ~57%.
   - Mature occupancy 83–89% across the three; ramping centres drag.
4. **GCC- and enterprise-led demand at record levels.**
   - Flex = 27% of Q2 CY2026 leasing; GCCs 42% of 9M CY2026 absorption.
   - Enterprise share 64–92% of member revenue; that lengthens tenure but concentrates risk on global corporate cost cycles.
5. **Model choice drives capital efficiency and risk-sharing.**
   - Straight lease (WeWork, Smartworks) maximises spread; managed aggregation (Awfis) shares spread and risk with landlords and lifts ROCE but caps margin.
6. **Landlord relationships are a moat.**
   - WeWork's promoter Embassy is India's largest office developer/REIT sponsor; WeWork centres sit in Embassy assets (Manyata, Embassy TechVillage, Embassy GolfLinks named in PPTs [V]).
   - Smartworks takes whole buildings from non-institutional landlords (~66% of SBA) and institutional developers (DLF, Hiranandani, Tata Realty, Panchshil, Mindspace) [V].
7. **Supply and price competition.** Flex stock +20% a year; Hyderabad and Pune vacancies ~19–21% mean landlords will offer flex operators better terms but rivals can undercut seat prices [E].
8. **Sponsor/promoter overhangs and governance.** WeWork promoter pledge (~15% of shares); 1 Ariel Way (15.17%) may sell further; Awfis' sponsors exited (Peak XV reclassified; Bisque 9.66%); Smartworks' promoters 58.3% with low FII (0.36%) [V].

## B. Bespoke deep-dive themes (own sections in the report)

### B1. "Three EBITDAs": reading a flex operator through Ind AS 116 (CENTREPIECE)

**How Ind AS 116 rewires the P&L** (per ₹100 of rent on a new 10-year lease) [E, mechanism]
- Old Indian GAAP/IGAAP: rent ₹100 expensed in opex, every year (straight-lined).
- Ind AS 116: the PV of all future rent goes on the balance sheet as a **lease liability**, matched by a **right-of-use asset**. Each year: depreciation of the RoU (straight-line ≈ PV ÷ term) + interest on the outstanding liability (high early, low late). Opex excludes rent → EBITDA rises by ~₹100. Total charge (dep. + interest) is **higher than ₹100 in early years and lower later** → young, fast-growing portfolios show lower Ind AS PAT than IGAAP PAT.
- Cash flow: the rent payment is split into "principal" and "interest" and shown in **financing** cash flow → reported CFO is inflated by the full rent. Screener's "CFO/EBITDA ~105–118%" and "FCF" are therefore meaningless for this sector.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Revenue = Operational seats × Occupancy × Price per seat per month × 12 + Ancillary (VAS, digital, D&B)**
**Rent-paid EBITDA = Revenue − Landlord rent (fixed for SL; revenue share for MA) − CAM & centre opex − brokerage & S&M − brand/management fees − corporate overhead**
**Adjusted PBT = Rent-paid EBITDA − fit-out depreciation − interest on real debt + other income**

### C.1 Worked example 1 — a 1,000-desk straight-lease centre in Bengaluru (WeWork-type), steady state [E composite]
- Area ~65,000 sq ft leasable (≈15 desks per 1,000 sq ft) [E from V density].
- Rent ₹95/sq ft/month (Bengaluru market rent ₹93.7 [V-snip CRE Matrix]) → **₹7.4 Cr a year**.
- Fit-out ₹2,700/sq ft → **₹17.6 Cr** capex [E]; depreciated over ~8 years → ₹2.2 Cr a year.
- Price ₹18,000/desk/month; occupancy 85% → 850 × ₹18,000 × 12 = **₹18.4 Cr** core; + VAS/digital ~12% → **₹20.6 Cr** total revenue. Revenue/rent = 2.8× (matches WeWork's disclosed 2.8×) [E].
- Centre opex (CAM, power, housekeeping, staff, F&B) ~₹5.4 Cr (26% of revenue) [E]; brokerage/S&M ~₹1.2 Cr; management fee 2.4% ₹0.5 Cr.
- **Centre EBITDA ≈ 20.6 − 7.4 − 5.4 − 1.2 − 0.5 = ₹6.1 Cr (≈30%)** — consistent with WeWork's 27.8–31.0% centre-level margin [V].
- Less corporate overhead ~10% of revenue (₹2.1 Cr) → company-level EBITDA ~₹4.0 Cr (~19–20%) — consistent with 19.8–20.2% [V].
- Less fit-out depreciation ₹2.2 Cr → EBIT ₹1.8 Cr (~9%).
- **Payback on ₹17.6 Cr capex ≈ 2.9 years of centre EBITDA** [E].
- Breakeven occupancy: (rent 7.4 + opex ~4.5 fixed part + S&M 1.2 + fee 0.5) ÷ (revenue per occupancy point ₹0.24 Cr) ≈ **57%** — matches WeWork's disclosed 56.6% [E].
- Sensitivity: occupancy 75% → revenue ₹18.2 Cr, centre EBITDA ~₹3.9 Cr (−36%); 90% → ₹21.8 Cr, ~₹7.1 Cr (+16%) [E].

### C.2 Worked example 2 — a 3,000-seat enterprise managed campus (Smartworks-type) [E composite]
- 130,000 sq ft SBA (~23 seats per 1,000 sq ft) at a wholesale rent of ₹60/sq ft/month → **₹9.4 Cr** rent a year.
- Fit-out ₹1,350/sq ft → **₹17.6 Cr** [primer/company claim].
- Price ₹8,300/seat/month; committed occupancy 92% (mature) → 2,760 × ₹8,300 × 12 = **₹27.5 Cr**; other revenue (+10%) → ₹30.2 Cr. Revenue/rent ≈ 3.2× at full maturity; portfolio average is ~2× because of new centres [E].
- Opex ~28% of revenue (₹8.5 Cr) [V ratio]; staff/other ~7% (₹2.1 Cr).
- **Normalised EBITDA ≈ 30.2 − 9.4 − 8.5 − 2.1 = ₹10.2 Cr (~34% at maturity)** vs the 17.5–19.6% portfolio average (new centres, unfilled LOIs, overhead) [E].
- Client lock-in 35 months × ₹27.5 Cr/12 = ₹80 Cr contracted vs landlord lock-in (say 5 years × ₹9.4 Cr = ₹47 Cr) → cover ~1.7× per centre at signing, rising as seats fill [E] (company mature cover 2.7× [V]).

### C.3 Worked example 3 — Awfis MA centre vs SL centre, 500 seats [E composite, illustrative]
- SL: Awfis funds fit-out ₹6 Cr; pays fixed rent ₹2.4 Cr/yr; revenue at 80% occupancy 400 × ₹9,700 × 12 = ₹4.66 Cr; opex ₹1.2 Cr → centre EBITDA ₹1.06 Cr (23%); ROCE on ₹6 Cr ~10% after depreciation [E].
- MA (landlord funds ~70% of fit-out; Awfis capex ₹1.8 Cr): revenue ₹4.66 Cr; landlord revenue share ~50% of revenue = ₹2.33 Cr; opex ₹1.2 Cr → centre EBITDA ₹1.13 Cr (24%); return on Awfis capex ~45–55% [E] — the mechanism behind Awfis' 55–60% cash ROCE [V].
- At 60% occupancy: SL centre EBITDA falls to ~₹0.13 Cr (rent fixed), MA centre to ~₹0.35 Cr (share flexes) [E]. **MA halves the downside; SL keeps the upside.**

### C.4 Company-level reconciliations [E from V]

### 4.2.1 WeWork India Management (WEWORK · 544570)

**A. Management-commentary digest: Q1 FY27 call, 17-Jul-2026** (MD & CEO Karan Virwani, CFO Clifford Lobo; transcript filed on BSE 23-Jul-2026) [V]. Paraphrased.

**Guidance**
- **FY27 growth.** Revenue and IGAAP EBITDA will each grow ">20%". The IGAAP EBITDA margin should hold at 19–20% through the expansion and rise in H2. EBITDA should rise every quarter from the ₹138 Cr Q1 base. Last year's path was ₹82 Cr → ₹165 Cr; the MD said this year's step-up will be "not as steep".
- **Capex.** FY27 capex guidance of **₹500–600 Cr** is held. It could rise if large managed-office (MO) deals land, which would be known by Q2.
- **Capacity.** About **20,000 seats** open between Q1-end and October; ~6,000 of these opened in July, mostly MO. H1 additions total ~22k desks. Operational area should reach **10.3 msf / ~155k desks by Mar-27**. FY28 should grow at a similar pace (to ~12 msf).
  - Supply visibility: ~100% for 12 months, ~90% for 18 months, ~80% for 24 months.
- **Q2 margin.** Management does not expect a margin dip in Q2: ~7,000 of the Q2 openings are pre-sold MO seats, whereas Q1's openings were speculative WeWork-branded seats.
- **Revenue mix.** VAS is guided at **11–12% of revenue plus 3–4% digital (~15–16%)**. Digital earns ~80% EBITDA margin.

**Accounting change**
- Large, client-specific MO **customisation will now be amortised over the client's contract term**, not booked upfront.
- Customisation used to run at ₹30–50 Cr a year. FY26 spiked on JP Morgan, T-Mobile and Amazon MO deals; Q4 FY26 was ~₹47 Cr and Q1 FY27 ₹9.5 Cr.
- The new run-rate is **₹10–15 Cr a quarter**, almost entirely flow-through.
- Cash comes upfront, so CFO will run ahead of revenue.

**New businesses**
- **Rivet** (design-and-build / PM consultancy): 10–15% margin, all of it flowing to PAT, with no capex. It booked no material revenue in Q1.
- **Member Services marketplace** (launched 15-Jul): listing fees plus a **6–16% take rate**, booked mostly as net margin rather than gross revenue.

**Pricing and spreads**
- Desks sold without discounting; the revenue-to-rent multiple held at **2.9–3.0×** across markets.
- Aerocity (Delhi) desks sell at **₹25,000–35,000 a month**.
- Southern MO deals price at about half of that but earn a wider spread (2.8× to above 3×).

**Pledge**
- The IPO was cut from ~₹4,000 Cr to ~₹3,000 Cr, which left ~₹570 Cr of Embassy debt secured on ~15% of WeWork India's shares.
- ~30 lakh shares are to be released next quarter. The aim is to clear the pledge in FY27 through Embassy asset sales or a block sale.
- **A block sale is the overhang to watch around Q2 results.**

**Five analyst Q&A points**
1. *Exits (Avendus):* 12.7k desks were sold but net members rose only ~3.1k. Management's explanation is the cohort shift: ~8k seats moved from growth to mature (mature seats 110k → 118k; mature members 98k → 103k), and the mature cohort's centre margin held at ~28%.
2. *Backlog (Tusk):* the ₹3,363 Cr locked-in figure is remaining contract value, not 27 months of revenue. Renewals were 84% in Q1. Locked-in revenue rose 60% YoY against +30% for locked-in rent: ₹4.7 of contracted revenue per ₹1 of new rent.
3. *REIT competition (Waterfield):* REITs want 9-year commitments and do not want facility-management risk; WeWork sells 3–5-year flexibility. Example given: Microsoft's first MO, a 5-year contract, renewed for 5+5 years.
4. *Refurbishment:* branded centres are refreshed in years 6–7 at **5–10% of initial capex**. When an MO client leaves, there is "no cost outlay": back-to-back landlord terms and capex is recovered within the client's term.
5. *Brokerage (Sundaram):* cash is paid upfront but expensed over the commitment term, so FY26 cash brokerage of ₹134.3 Cr ran far above the P&L charge.

## 4.5 New insights the first pass missed

1. **Cash rent is growing faster than revenue at Awfis, and that, not "definitions", explains the margin reset.**

2. **Awfis' collapse is mostly a Nov-25 to Mar-26 event, shared with peers; only the non-recovery is Awfis-specific.**

   - WeWork then hit a 52-week high of ₹755 (7-Aug-2026); Awfis made new lows.
