## READ THIS FIRST: nine definitional traps

1. **Bata's EBITDA has two numbers. Pre-Ind AS 116 it is about half the reported figure.**
   - Screener/reported FY26 OP **₹726 Cr = 20.6% margin** (post Ind AS 116: store rent sits below EBITDA as ROU depreciation + lease interest) [V screener].
   - HDFC Securities' annual table shows FY24/FY25/FY26 EBITDA of **₹443 / ₹384 / ₹348 Cr**, with FY27E at an 11.3% margin [V fetched HSIE PDF, 4-Jun-26]. On FY26 revenue of ₹3,516 Cr that is **9.9%**. It is clearly a **pre-Ind AS 116 (rent-as-opex) basis** [E: my reading; the table is not labelled, but the "Post IND-AS 116 EPS" line and the 11–12% margins only fit pre-116].
   - So **rent and lease costs ≈ ₹378 Cr ≈ 10.7% of revenue** [E: 726 − 348].
   - The primer's "~18% EBITDA (Ind AS 116)" matches **neither** (screener 20.6%; HSIE quarterly "adj. EBITDAM" Q4 20.9%). It may be EBITDA after the VRS one-off. Use **20.6% (post-116)** and **~9.9% (pre-116)**, and say which.
   - Bata's screener "borrowings" of ₹1,387 Cr are **100% lease liabilities; bank debt is zero** [V screener schedule]. Treat Bata as **net cash ~₹532 Cr ex-lease** (cash ₹496 Cr + investments ₹36 Cr).
2. **Relaxo: pairs vs ASP, and the price hike that changes Q1 FY27's meaning.**
   - In Q1 FY27 revenue rose +7.7% while **volume fell 2% to 42 mn pairs** and **ASP rose 10% to ₹166** [V-snip]. The cause was **calibrated consumer price increases of 15–18%**, offsetting raw-material inflation and a **25–30% surge in Haryana labour wages** [V IDBI PDF].
   - Relaxo's growth is now price-led, not volume-led. FY27 guidance is "4–5% volume growth over two years" [V IDBI].
   - The primer's "~18 crore pairs" and "~₹150 realisation" are **derived** (FY26 ₹2,702 Cr ÷ ~₹152 ≈ **~17.8 Cr pairs** [E]). The quarterly ASP is now ₹166, not ₹150.
   - Relaxo's "EBITDA" in broker/company tables (FY26 ₹374 Cr, 13.8%) ≈ screener OP ₹377 Cr (14.0%). Immaterial.
3. **Company EBITDA includes other income at Campus and Redtape. Screener OP does not.**
   - **Campus FY26:** company EBITDA ₹314.7 Cr (17.5%) [primer; V-snip]; screener OP ₹292 Cr (16.5%) [V]; ICICI Direct ₹291.9 Cr (16.5%) [V]. The gap ≈ other income ₹23 Cr. **Campus's 17–19% FY27 margin guidance is on the company basis** [V-snip].
   - **Redtape FY26:** primer EBITDA "~₹459 Cr, ~19%" vs **screener OP ₹353 Cr (14.6%)** with **other income ₹132 Cr** (FY25 ₹151 Cr) [V screener].
   - Redtape Q1 FY27: company EBITDA ₹101 Cr (20.4%) vs OP ex-OI ₹84 Cr (17.4%) [V press release; V screener].
   - **Redtape's other income was ~41% of FY26 PBT** (₹132 Cr of ₹324 Cr) [E]. Its nature is not explained in anything fetched — §H gap. Use screener OP for all cross-company margins.
4. **Campus: net vs gross revenue, and which entity.**
   - Campus reports **revenue from operations net of trade discounts and schemes**. Older investor decks also show a higher gross-sales line [K; verify on the deck]. Always use revenue from operations (FY26 ₹1,774 Cr).
   - Use the **standalone** screener page. Its consolidated page is frozen at FY22.
   - **ASP basis:** ₹683 FY26 (primer, company) = revenue ÷ pairs on the company's own basis. Q1 FY27 ASP ₹674 vs ₹671 [V-snip].
5. **Redtape's financial year and history.**
   - Redtape Ltd was incorporated in 2021. Its first reported period (FY22) is a **3-month stub**; FY23 is the first full year [V screener "Mar 2022 3m"].
   - **Do not compute a 5-year CAGR.** Use FY23–FY26 (revenue CAGR **18.1%** [E]).
   - Pre-FY23 history of the RED TAPE brand sits inside **Mirza International** (FY22 Mirza consolidated revenue ₹1,399 Cr, pre-demerger) [V screener].
   - The primer's "FY23 revenue ~₹1,296 Cr" conflicts with screener **₹1,468 Cr**. Use screener.
   - The face value is ₹2 after a corporate action (equity capital ₹28 Cr → ₹111 Cr in FY25, consistent with a ~3:1 bonus) [V screener; E on the ratio]. Pre-FY25 per-share data must be adjusted.
6. **Consolidated vs standalone.**
   - Bata consolidated ≈ standalone (tiny subsidiaries).
   - Relaxo is standalone-only.
   - Campus: use standalone.
   - Redtape: consolidated (₹481 Cr Q1 revenue) ≈ standalone (₹480 Cr). Small PAT differences: consolidated ₹44.5 Cr vs standalone ₹46.9 Cr [V-snip; V press release].
   - Quote the basis whenever Redtape PAT is used.
7. **"Stores" means different things at each company.**
   - **Bata:** "nearly 2,000 stores (company-owned and franchise)" [V Q4 press release]. **Franchise 772 at Mar-26** [V HSIE]; "750 franchise stores" in Q1 FY27 commentary [V-snip investywise; likely "750+" rounding]. Company-owned ≈ **~1,230** [E = ~2,000 − ~772]. Plus **17,000+ MBOs** in 1,678 towns [V-snip].
   - **Redtape:** **702 "stores"** at 30-Jun-2026 [V press release]. These include **132 shop-in-shops**, 297 "Exclusive Mega Showroom (Online)", 256 "Exclusive Showroom (Offline)", 6 sports outlets and 11 factory shops. **70.5% are franchisee-run.** The primer's "223 EBOs in 161 cities" is a narrower and older count.
   - **Relaxo:** ">420 EBOs" plus ~70,000 MBO locations [V IDBI].
   - **Campus:** "~300–305 EBOs" plus 2,000–2,300+ LFS counters and 23,000+ retail touchpoints [V ICICI].
   - **Metro (ref.):** 1,053 stores (all brand formats incl. Crocs EBOs) [V-snip].
   - Never add these into a "sector store count" without labelling.
8. **GST threshold is on transaction value per pair, not MRP. It moved twice.**
   - **Pre-Jan-2022:** ≤₹1,000 at 5%; >₹1,000 at 18% [K].
   - **1-Jan-2022 to 21-Sep-2025:** ≤₹1,000 at **12%**; >₹1,000 at **18%** [V-snip; K for the 2022 change].
   - **From 22-Sep-2025 (GST 2.0):** ≤₹2,500 at **5%**; >₹2,500 at **18%** [V-snip, PIB GST 2.0 document and CLE circular].
   - So the **₹1,001–2,500 band fell 13 points (18% → 5%)**. That band is Campus's core and Bata's Power/North Star/Comfit range. The ≤₹1,000 band (Relaxo's core) fell 7 points.
   - Footwear parts (HSN 6406) are reported at 18% [V-snip busy.in; verify]. That creates an **inverted duty structure** for makers whose inputs (EVA, PU, rubber compounds, chemicals) carry 18% [K; FLAG].
9. **Two different "QCO deadlines" exist. Do not conflate them.**
   - (a) **Mandatory BIS certification for manufacture/import** under the 2024 QCOs (from 1-Aug-2024 for large/medium; earlier phases from 2023) [V-snip BIS circular].
   - (b) **The legacy-stock clearance deadline**, before which pre-QCO, non-certified stock may still be sold. It was extended on **12-Jun-2026 from 31-Jul-2026 to 31-Jul-2027** [V PIB 9-Jul-2026].
   - Headlines saying "QCO deferred to 2027" refer to (b) only.

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Four listed members, four different models, one shared problem: a mass market that stopped growing in volume.**
  - **Bata (brand-retailer, ~50 mn pairs [V], ASP ~₹700 [E]):**
    - FY26 revenue ₹3,516 Cr (+0.8%), OP ₹726 Cr (20.6% post-116; ~9.9% pre-116), PAT ₹134 Cr (−59%) [V screener; V HSIE].
    - Q1 FY27: revenue +3.9%, PAT +23% [V screener].
  - **Relaxo (mass manufacturer, ~17–18 Cr pairs [E], ASP ₹152 FY26 [E] → ₹166 Q1 FY27 [V-snip]):**
    - FY26 revenue ₹2,702 Cr (−3.2%), OP ₹377 Cr (14.0%), PAT ₹179 Cr (+5%) [V screener].
    - Revenue has fallen two years running from the FY24 peak of ₹2,914 Cr.
  - **Redtape (fashion brand-retailer, asset-light sourcing, footwear ~63% + apparel ~34%) [primer]:**
    - FY26 revenue ₹2,419 Cr (+19.7%), OP ₹353 Cr (14.6%), PAT ₹241 Cr (+42%) [V screener].
    - Q1 FY27 growth slowed to +3.6% [V].
  - **Campus (sports/athleisure "masstige" maker, 26 mn pairs, ASP ₹683) [primer; V-snip]:**
    - FY26 revenue ₹1,774 Cr (+11.4%), OP ₹292 Cr (16.5%), PAT ₹150 Cr (+24%) [V screener].
    - Q1 FY27: revenue +12.2%, volume +11.7% [V-snip].
  - **All four (straight sums, screener FY26):**
    - Revenue **₹10,411 Cr (+5.2%)**, OP **₹1,748 Cr (16.8%)**, PAT **₹704 Cr (−11.1%)**.
    - M-cap **₹26,632 Cr** (23-Oct close) → **37.8× aggregate FY26 PAT** [E: sums].
    - Ex-Bata, PAT **rose 23.6%**. Bata's collapse (₹331 → ₹134 Cr) is the whole aggregate decline.
- **The market is huge, low per capita and still two-thirds informal.**
  - India produced **~3.0 bn pairs in 2025 (12.2% of world output, #2 after China's 13.7 bn)** and consumed **~2.8 bn pairs (#2 after China's 4.8 bn; US 1.95 bn)** [V-snip World Footwear Yearbook 2026].
  - Per capita ≈ **1.9–2.0 pairs/yr** vs 3.2 global, 7.2 US, 2.9 China [V ICICI Direct note, citing industry report; E on 2025 arithmetic: 2.8 bn ÷ ~1.45 bn people ≈ 1.93].
  - Market value ≈ **₹1.38–1.45 lakh Cr in FY25** (CRISIL via ICICI Direct/5paisa; 15–17% CAGR FY22–25 off a Covid-depressed base) [V-snip]. Organised share was **~30% in FY20, projected 36–40% by FY25** [V-snip].
  - **The four members together are only ~7% of the market by value** [E: ₹10,411 Cr ÷ ~₹1.45 lakh Cr], and roughly **9–10% by pairs** [E: ~50 + ~178 + 26 + Redtape (n/d) ≈ 260 mn+ of 2.8 bn].
- **Policy has turned decisively pro-organised in 2024–26.** Three levers:
  - (1) **BIS QCOs.** Mandatory certification for 24+ footwear products from 1-Aug-2024 (2024 orders). Legacy-stock window extended to 31-Jul-2027 [V PIB].
  - (2) **GST 2.0.** Footwear ≤₹2,500 cut to 5% from 22-Sep-2025, from 12% (≤₹1,000) / 18% (₹1,001–2,500) [V-snip]. Relaxo says the cut "significantly enhanced the competitiveness of organised branded players" and drove distributor restocking [V IDBI concall notes].
  - (3) **E-invoicing/GST compliance thresholds** steadily pulling small makers into the tax net [K].
  - **The real-world result so far is modest.** Organised members' FY26 revenue +5%; Q1 FY27 +6.1% [E sums]. The volume recovery is concentrated in Campus (+11.7%) and Bata (Q4 FY26 +2.8%, "volume-led" Q1) [V; V-snip]. **Relaxo's volumes are still falling** (−2% Q1 FY27).
- **Margins are being squeezed from cost, not from price, in 2026.**
  - The West Asia shock lifted crude-linked inputs (EVA, PU, PVC, rubber chemicals, solvents, packaging) and freight from March 2026 [V-sib]. "PU foam prices increased significantly" (Versatile Group, Sept-2026) [V Outlook Business/PTI].
  - **Labour:** Haryana wage revisions of 25–30% hit Relaxo [V IDBI]; Metro also cites minimum-wage revisions [V-snip].
  - **The responses diverge, and that is the story:**
    - Relaxo raised consumer prices **15–18%** [V IDBI].
    - Redtape **kept MRPs unchanged** and protected margin by **cutting marketplace discounting** (e-commerce share fell from 30% to 22%) [V press release].
    - Bata pushed **full-price sell-through via "Zero Base Merchandising"** (gross margin +130 bp in Q1 FY27) [V-snip].
    - Campus held ASP flat (₹674) and grew volume [V-snip].
- **Valuations have de-rated hard. The market has stopped paying "consumer-compounder" multiples for footwear.**
  - One-year price change [V screener 24-Oct-26]: **Bata −46%, Relaxo −37%, Campus −33%, Redtape −22%**.
  - Since the primers were written (Jul–Aug 2026), m-caps fell further:
    - Bata ₹15,500 Cr → **₹8,147 Cr** (−47%; partly a stale primer figure — HSIE had ₹8,600 Cr on 3-Jun).
    - Relaxo ₹10,200 Cr → **₹6,950 Cr** (IDBI: ₹8,555 Cr on 1-Jun).
    - Redtape ₹7,275 Cr → **₹5,753 Cr**.
    - Campus (ICICI ₹6,784 Cr on 21-Aug) → **₹5,782 Cr**.
  - Screener P/E: Bata **46.9×** (TTM, on depressed earnings; 3.94% dividend yield after the ₹25 interim), Relaxo **37.5×**, Campus **37.5×**, Redtape **23.3×**. Metro Brands (ref.) still trades at **53.9×** with m-cap ₹22,335 Cr — **84% of all four members combined** [V screener].

### 1.2 Value chain (inputs → customer)

1. **Raw materials.** Petrochemical polymers are the bulk by weight for non-leather footwear. Indicative input mix for a mass non-leather maker [E, composite from industry practice; FLAG — no company discloses a split]:
   - EVA (ethylene-vinyl acetate) for soles and slippers ~25–35% of material cost.
   - PU (polyols + MDI/TDI isocyanates) and PU foam for midsoles and comfort footwear ~15–20%.
   - PVC compound ~10–15%; natural/synthetic rubber and TPR ~10–15%.
   - Textiles and mesh/knit uppers, synthetic leather (PU/PVC-coated fabric) ~15–20%.
   - Adhesives, solvents, chemicals and packaging ~10%.
   - **Leather** footwear inputs are hides and finished leather (India is a major tanner: Kanpur, Agra, Ambur–Vaniyambadi, Kolkata). Finished leather imports carry a **10% duty**, which the CLE wants removed [V Outlook Business/PTI 16-Sep-26].
2. **Component manufacture:** soles (injection or compression moulding), uppers (cutting, stitching, knitting), insoles. Campus makes **49.6% of soles and 37.2% of uppers in-house** [V ICICI]. Relaxo is heavily backward-integrated (moulding in-house) [primer].
3. **Assembly.** Two methods: **DIP / direct-injection** (cheap, mass) vs **stuck-on / cemented** (higher ASP, sneakers). Campus is shifting from DIP to stuck-on [V ICICI]. Assembly is labour-intensive. Footwear and leather employ **~4.42 mn people** [V CLE via PTI].
4. **Brand & design.** Collections run 3–4 seasons a year. Campus launches ~250–270 styles a year from 1,900 active styles, with a 60–90-day design-to-market cycle vs an industry 90–120 days [V ICICI].
5. **Distribution.** Five routes:
   - **(a) General trade:** distributor → multi-brand outlet (MBO). This is Relaxo's backbone (~550 distributors, ~70,000 retailers) [primer; V IDBI], Campus (~51% of Q4 FY26 revenue via trade) [primer] and Bata (17,000+ MBOs) [V-snip].
   - **(b) Exclusive brand outlets (EBOs):** company-owned or franchise.
   - **(c) Large-format stores** and shop-in-shops.
   - **(d) Marketplaces:** Amazon, Flipkart, Myntra, Ajio, Nykaa Fashion; quick commerce at the margin.
   - **(e) D2C own website/app.**
6. **Consumer.** About **250,000 customers a day** walk into Bata stores alone [V Bata press release].

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **Gross margin is set by brand, not by manufacturing** [screener material-cost proxies; company GMs]:
  - **Bata** FY26 GM ~56–57% (Q4 FY26 56.4%) [V HSIE].
  - **Metro** ~57% (Q2 FY27 57.4%) [V-snip].
  - **Campus** 53.5% [primer].
  - **Redtape** ~47.5% (Q1 FY27) [V-snip, company].
  - **Relaxo**'s 66.7% (Q1 FY27) [V-snip] is high **because it manufactures in-house**. Conversion costs (wages, power) sit outside material cost, so GM looks high but OP margin is only ~14–15%.
  - **GM is not comparable across makers and retailers.**
- **Operating margin (post-116, OP) ranks the models** [V screener FY26]:
  - Premium multi-brand retailer (Metro 30.5%) > brand-retailer with owned stores (Bata 20.6%) > sports brand-maker (Campus 16.5%) > fashion brand on sourcing (Redtape 14.6%) > mass maker (Relaxo 14.0%).
  - **Pre-116 the order changes.** Bata falls to ~9.9% [E/V HSIE], below Relaxo (~13% pre-116 [E: Relaxo lease liabilities only ₹233 Cr, so the 116 uplift is ~1 point]).
- **The share of retail value:** on a ₹1,000 MRP pair sold through general trade, the retailer keeps ~25–35%, the distributor ~5–8%, GST takes ~4.8% (at 5%) and the brand-maker's net realisation is ~55–65% of MRP [E; FLAG — composite of industry practice, not disclosed]. Through an owned EBO the brand keeps the retail margin but pays rent and staff. Through a marketplace it pays a 20–35% commission plus returns and logistics [E].

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Replacement-led, low-ticket, rural-heavy mass demand** (Relaxo, Bata basics, unorganised). Drivers are rural wages, food inflation and the monsoon (slipper and sandal season is summer and monsoon). Demand was weak across FY23–FY25: Relaxo revenue fell from ₹2,914 Cr (FY24) to ₹2,702 Cr (FY26) [V screener]. Relaxo cited "muted value-footwear demand" through FY25–26 [primer].
2. **Premiumisation and trading up within brands.**
   - Campus revenue from **>₹1,500 products: 47.3% (FY24) → 48.9% (FY25) → 56.2% (FY26)** [V ICICI]. The primer's "58.4%" is the company's ">₹1,500 sneakers" metric. Basis differs; see §3.4.
   - Bata sneaker volumes **+109% in FY26, to ~12.7% of pairs** [primer, company]. Bata premium (Hush Puppies, Power) "continued to outpace overall growth" [V press release].
   - Relaxo's Sparx is ~40% of revenue on ~16% of pairs [primer, FY25 basis].
3. **Casualisation / sneakerisation.** A secular shift from formal leather shoes to sneakers.
   - Campus sneaker volume salience **~4% FY24 → 12–13% FY26** [V ICICI]. Campus guides **+30% sneaker growth in FY27** [V-snip].
   - Redtape pivoted from formal to sneakers and apparel [primer] and bought Sprandi (sports) in Q1 FY27 [V].
4. **Women's and kids' under-penetration.** Relaxo's revenue mix is **70% men / 25% ladies / 5% kids** [V IDBI]. All members are targeting women and kids.
5. **Festive/wedding calendar.** Diwali and wedding timing shift demand between Q2 and Q3. **In FY27 Diwali falls in Nov-2026** (8-Nov-2026 [K]), so festive buying shifts into Q3. Metro quantified a **~250–350 bp drag on Q2 FY27 growth** [V-snip]. **Expect members' Q2 FY27 (Sep-26) prints to look soft for calendar reasons.** Compare on H1 or on Q2+Q3.
6. **GST 2.0 price effect.**
   - For a ₹1,999 shoe, the GST-inclusive price drops ~11% (1.18 → 1.05) if the full cut is passed on. For a ₹799 chappal it drops ~6.25% (1.12 → 1.05) [E arithmetic].
   - Relaxo noted "strong recovery in distributor restocking" after the cut [V IDBI]. Bata's Q3 FY26 (Dec-25) revenue rose 2.7% [V screener: 945 vs 919], and Q4 FY26 rose 5% "volume-led" [V].
   - **The effect looks real but small**, partly offset by 2026 inflation and the West Asia shock.
7. **2026 macro shock.** Crude, LPG and freight spiked from 28-Feb-2026. Consumer sentiment was hit; Bata, Relaxo and Metro all flagged "geopolitical tensions and inflationary pressures" [V HSIE; V IDBI]. The RBI hiked to 5.50% on 7-Oct-2026, a headwind for discretionary spend into H2 FY27 [house fact].
8. **Channel shift to online.**
   - Bata e-commerce grew ~26% in Q4 FY26 and +13% in Q1 FY27. Bata.com grew +25% [V HSIE; V-snip].
   - Campus D2C is 47.9% of FY26 revenue (online ~37.8% of Q4 FY26) [V ICICI; primer].
   - Redtape's online share was ~30% in FY26, **cut to 22% in Q1 FY27** to avoid marketplace discounting [V-snip; V press release].
   - Metro's e-commerce/omni share is 14.2% [V-snip].

### 4.1 Key inputs and their price drivers

| Input | Main uses | Price driver | 2026 status |

- **Crude sensitivity [E composite; FLAG].**
  - For a mass maker like Relaxo, materials are ~33% of revenue at a 66.7% GM (Q1 FY27) [V-snip GM], and roughly **60–70% of those materials are crude-linked** [E].
  - A **20% rise in polymer prices** therefore lifts material cost by ~4–5 points of revenue before price action [E: 33% × 65% × 20% ≈ 4.3 pts]. That is roughly **30% of Relaxo's 14% OP margin**. This explains the 15–18% price hikes.
  - For Bata (GM ~56%; part sourced, part made) a 20% polymer move ≈ 2–3 pts [E]. For Campus (GM 53.5%) ≈ 3–4 pts [E].
  - **Past evidence:** FY23, when crude spiked post-Ukraine, cut Relaxo's OPM from 15.8% (FY22) to 12.3% (FY23) and Campus's from 20.3% to 17.1% [V screener].

### 5.3 China and imports

- **China** makes 55.6% of the world's footwear [V-snip WFY]. Before the QCOs it was the main source of India's imported sports shoes and of components (soles, uppers, moulds, machinery).
- **The QCOs require every foreign factory supplying India to hold a BIS licence.** That raised the barrier for small Chinese/Vietnamese suppliers and for importer-brands (D2C brands that imported). Global brands certified their main factories [K].
- **Components remain import-dependent** (machinery, moulds, some EVA and PU chemicals). The Focus Product Scheme was meant to fund component and machinery manufacturing in India [V-snip; §7.4].
- **The China+1 opportunity in exports is largely unrealised.** India's footwear exports (~$2–2.5 bn of the $4.89 bn CLE total [E/K]) are a fraction of Vietnam's (~US$20+ bn [K]). CLE concedes Indian products "will not get price advantage over goods from countries like Vietnam and Bangladesh" [V PTI].

### 7.2 GST

| Period | ≤₹1,000/pair | ₹1,001–2,500 | >₹2,500 | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jul-2017 – Dec-2021 | 5% | 18% | 18% | [K] |

- Footwear parts/components (HSN 6406) are reportedly 18% [V-snip busy.in; verify]. Polymer inputs are at 18% [K]. Because output is at 5%, many makers accumulate input-tax credit: an **inverted duty structure**, with refunds available under Sec 54(3) for inputs (not services) [K; FLAG].
- **Who gains most:**
  - **Campus.** ASP ~₹670–680; the bulk of the range is under ₹2,500 MRP and in the ₹1,001–2,500 band, which fell 13 points [E from ICICI price bands; Élan range ₹1,500–2,500 / launches up to ₹2,599 [V ICICI] — NB launches priced ₹2,501–2,599 sit just above the threshold].
  - **Relaxo.** Core ≤₹1,000 band, −7 points. New sports/athleisure range "up to ₹2,500 MRP" was deliberately priced at the threshold [V IDBI].
  - **Bata.** Mass and mid in the 5% band; **Hush Puppies and premium (>₹2,500) stay at 18%** [E].
  - **Redtape.** Mid-premium; a material share is likely >₹2,500 [E; FLAG].
  - **Metro.** "Footwear maker Metro Brands finds new spring in its step after GST cut" (Business Standard, 17-Sep-2025) [V-snip headline]. Metro's ASP is high, but its average bill is pair-level and many pairs sit ₹1,500–2,500 [E].
- **Price-point effects:**
  - **The ₹2,500 cliff:** a pair priced at ₹2,501 pays 18% vs 5% at ₹2,500. Brands now cluster MRPs at ₹2,499 or below [E; FLAG].
  - Retailer bodies (RAI) sought 5% above ₹2,500 too [V-snip Outlook Business].
  - The unorganised segment often under-reports value. A lower rate **narrows the evasion advantage**, a formalisation tailwind [E].

After SCOTUS (20-Feb-2026), Sec 122 imposed 10% to 24-Jul-2026, then a 10% Sec 301 on top of MFN. US MFN duties on footwear are themselves high (many lines 8.5–37.5%, some 48% on rubber/plastic footwear) [K]. Competitors China and Vietnam face 12.5% Sec 301 [house fact].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

- **1931–1973: Bata builds Indian footwear retail.** Bata starts in Konnagar (1931–32), builds Batanagar (1934), and lists as Bata India in 1973 [primer; K].
- **1976–2000s: the rubber-chappal era.** Relaxo is founded by the Dua brothers (1976) and incorporated (1984) [primer]. It grows on Hawaii chappals and lists in 1995. The Sparx (2000s) and Flite brands take it up-market [primer].
- **2005–2019: organised sports-shoe brands.** Campus is established as an affordable sports brand (incorporated 2008 [V screener]). TPG/QRG invest in 2017 [K; primer says "PE capital 2017"]. Metro Brands builds its multi-brand chain.
- **FY20 (Apr-2019): Ind AS 116** moves rent below EBITDA. **Bata's reported OPM jumps from 17% (FY19) to 28% (FY20)** with no change in the business [V screener]. Its "borrowings" go from 0 to ₹1,249 Cr of lease liabilities [V].
- **FY21: Covid.** Bata's revenue halves to ₹1,708 Cr and it posts a loss (−₹89 Cr) [V screener]. Relaxo hits a record 21% OPM on cheap inputs and value demand [V screener].
- **FY22–FY23: re-opening, IPO wave and the input-cost spike.** Campus IPO (offer period late Apr-2022, listed May-2022; pure OFS) [K] and Metro Brands IPO (Dec-2021) [K]. Relaxo's PAT peaks at ₹292 Cr in FY21 / ₹233 Cr in FY22. Crude/EVA spike after Feb-2022 → Relaxo OPM 12% (FY23) [V screener]. **GST on footwear ≤₹1,000 raised 5%→12% (1-Jan-2022)** [K].
- **FY23–FY24: demerger and QCO.** Redtape is demerged from Mirza and lists (Aug-2023) [primer]. Footwear QCOs are enforced (Jul-2023 large/medium; 2024 orders from 1-Aug-2024) [§7.1]. Bata's promoter stake steps down to 50.16% (FY23) [V screener]. Campus's FY24 revenue falls 2.4% (channel destocking) [V screener].
- **FY25–FY26: the mass slowdown and GST 2.0.** Relaxo's revenue falls in FY25 (−4.3%) and FY26 (−3.2%) [V]. Bata's revenue is flat for three years at ~₹3,450–3,520 Cr [V]. **GST 2.0 (22-Sep-2025)** cuts ≤₹2,500 footwear to 5% [V-snip]. Q3–Q4 FY26 recover [V screener]. Bata's FY26 PAT collapses to ₹134 Cr on deleverage, VRS and royalty forex losses [V].
- **2026: West Asia shock and de-rating.** War from 28-Feb-2026; polymers +43% in three weeks [V-sib]. Relaxo raises prices 15–18% [V IDBI]. Footwear stocks hit 52-week lows in March [V-snip]. The QCO legacy-stock window is extended to Jul-2027 (12-Jun) [V PIB]. India–UK CETA enters into force (15-Jul) [V]. Members' share prices are down 22–46% over 12 months [V screener].

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **D2C / marketplace disintermediation.** Online-native sneaker brands (Asian, Neeman's, etc.) compete on design velocity and price.
   - Campus moved to ~48% D2C to defend itself [V ICICI]. Redtape reached ~30% online, then **pulled back to 22%** because marketplace discounting was destroying margin [V].
   - Quick commerce (Blinkit/Zepto/Instamart now list slippers and basic shoes) is an emerging channel for value chappals [K; FLAG].
2. **Manufacturing automation.** Injection moulding of EVA/PU (one-shot moulding, knit uppers) cuts labour content. This favours scaled makers (Relaxo, Campus) as wages rise 25–30% [V IDBI wage data; E inference].
3. **Inventory analytics / "zero-base merchandising".** Bata's ZBM was rolled out to 550 stores (Mar-26) → 700 (May-26) → 775 (Q1 FY27), ~80% of business. ZBM stores showed turnover +4.6% and volume +6.1% vs control stores, and stock turns of 2.54× [V HSIE; V-snip].
4. **Global brands moving down-market.** Nike/Adidas/Puma pricing ₹2,000–3,000 entry ranges after GST 2.0 would hit Campus's top end [E; risk].
5. **Policy reversal.** QCO dilution under trade-deal pressure (US/EU/UK FTAs) is a specific risk. The primer flags it for Campus [primer]. The Jun-2026 amendment eased compliance (R&D samples, legacy stock) but kept the regime [V PIB].
6. **Sustainability / EPR.** Footwear is not yet under plastic EPR rules [K; FLAG]. The EU deforestation and chemical (REACH) rules matter for exporters.

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Volume stagnation in mass footwear (2023–25) and a thin, price-led 2026 recovery.**
   - India's consumption is ~2.8 bn pairs at ~1.9–2.0 per capita [V-snip WFY; V ICICI]. Yet the listed mass leaders shrank: Relaxo's revenue fell from ₹2,914 Cr to ₹2,702 Cr over FY24–26, and Bata's grew 0.6% a year over FY23–26 [V screener].
   - Q1 FY27 "recovery" is mostly price: Relaxo volume −2% with ASP +10% [V-snip]. Bata is "volume-led" but only +3.9% [V].
   - Rural real-income squeeze, food inflation, the 2026 fuel shock and an RBI hike are the reasons [house; E]. The analyst's first question every quarter is **pairs, not rupees**.
2. **Formalisation by policy: QCO + GST 2.0.**
   - The BIS QCOs made certification mandatory for manufacture, import and sale (2024 orders; legacy-stock deadline 31-Jul-2027) [V PIB].
   - GST 2.0 cut ≤₹2,500 footwear to 5%, removing a 13-point disadvantage in the ₹1,001–2,500 band [V-snip].
   - Together they **raise the cost of being informal or an importer**. Organised share should rise from ~36–40% [V-snip] toward ~50% over five years [E].
   - Evidence so far: Relaxo's distributor restocking after the GST cut [V IDBI], and Campus volumes +11.7% in Q1 FY27 [V-snip]. Weak enforcement on micro units limits the speed.
3. **Premiumisation and casualisation: sneakers and the ₹1,500–3,000 band.**
   - The mid-premium band is growing ~13% vs ~8% for mass [V ICICI].
   - Campus's >₹1,500 share rose to 56.2%, Bata's sneakers to 12.7% of pairs, and Relaxo's Sparx is the largest brand [V ICICI; primer].
   - Global brands own >₹4,000. The fight is whether Indian brands can hold the ₹1,500–3,000 band against global entry ranges and D2C natives.
   - **GST's ₹2,500 cliff now caps that band's pricing** [E].
4. **Channel economics: owned stores vs franchise vs D2C vs marketplaces.**
   - Bata is moving the fixed-cost risk to franchisees (772 franchise of ~2,000; target ~1,000 franchise) [V HSIE].
   - Redtape is 70.5% franchised [V]. Campus is 48% D2C [V ICICI]. Relaxo is mostly general trade.
   - Rent (Bata: ~10.7% of revenue [E]) and marketplace commissions (20–35% [E]) decide margins.
   - **The pre-Ind AS 116 margin is the honest comparator.**
5. **Input-cost and wage shock (2026).**
   - Crude-linked polymers (EVA, PU, PVC, rubber) are ~60–70% of a mass maker's materials [E]. They spiked after 28-Feb-2026 [V-sib], and Haryana wages rose 25–30% [V IDBI].
   - Members chose different responses: Relaxo raised price, Redtape held MRP, Bata relied on full-price sell-through, Campus held ASP. Their Q2–Q3 FY27 margins will **show who had pricing power**.
6. **Capital allocation and working capital.**
   - Inventory days run 164 (Bata) to 306 (Redtape) [V screener].
   - Bata pays out heavily: FY26 dividend payout 86%, plus a ₹25 interim in FY27 [V screener; V-snip]. It also carries a **royalty liability in foreign currency to the Bata group**, with a ₹22.4 Cr forex loss in Q4 FY26 [V].
   - Relaxo is net cash (₹460 Cr) but under-utilised, with ROCE at 11%.
   - Redtape is the only member with material bank debt (₹385 Cr), funding inventory.
7. **Valuation reset (2025–26).**
   - Members are down 22–46% over 12 months [V screener]. The group trades at **37.8× FY26 PAT** [E] vs Metro at 53.9× [V].
   - The market now pays for proven growth (Metro, Campus) over brand legacy (Bata). Bata's HSIE target is ₹720, REDUCE [V].
   - "Quality footwear compounder" is no longer the default frame.

## B. Bespoke deep-dive themes (own sections in the report)

### B1. "Two policies, one wall": QCO + GST 2.0 and the organised share
- **Mechanics:**
  - QCO → foreign-plant BIS licences, product testing and marking, legacy-stock deadline [V PIB].
  - GST → a 5% rate up to ₹2,500 [V-snip].
  - Who must comply: large/medium from 2023–24; small/micro phased [V-snip BIS FAQ].
- **Winners/losers:**
  - Domestic BIS-licensed brand-makers (Campus, Relaxo, Bata) win.
  - Importer-brands and micro workshops lose.
  - Global brands are neutral (they certify).
  - Retailers with old stock got relief to Jul-2027.
- **Evidence to show:**
  - Members' quarterly growth turning positive after Sep-2025 (§3.1 table).
  - Relaxo's restocking comment [V IDBI].
  - Campus volume +11.7% [V-snip].
- **Caveat:** no fetched import data proves the "import wall" (§H).

### B2. "Pairs vs price": why Relaxo shrank, Campus grew and Bata stalled
- Use the revenue identity (pairs × ASP) with each company's own numbers:
  - **Relaxo:** ~17.8 Cr pairs × ₹152 [E]. Q1 FY27 4.2 Cr × ₹166 [V-snip].
  - **Campus:** 26 mn × ₹683 [primer]. Q1 FY27 5.71 mn × ₹674 [V-snip].
  - **Bata:** ~50 mn × ~₹700 [V; E].
- **The punchline:** Relaxo sells ~3.5× Bata's pairs at about one-fifth of the ASP. Campus sells half Bata's pairs at a similar ASP but grows 10× faster.
- Mass volume has been the problem since FY23. Premium price-mix is the answer for each company in a different way.

### B3. "The margin that isn't there": Ind AS 116, franchising and true store economics at Bata

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Generic:** Revenue = Σ over channels (pairs × net realisation per pair). Then:
- EBITDA (post-116) = Revenue × GM − (staff + A&P + royalty + freight + other opex).
- Pre-116 EBITDA = EBITDA (post-116) − rent.
- EBIT = EBITDA (post-116) − depreciation (incl. ROU) − lease interest.

**Retailer variant:** Revenue = doors × average sq ft × revenue/sq ft (≈ SSSG × new-store contribution) + franchise wholesale + MBO + online.

### C.1 Worked example 1 — Relaxo, one quarter (Q1 FY27) [V-snip/V screener inputs; E arithmetic]
- Pairs 4.20 Cr × ₹166 = **₹697 Cr** (reported ₹705 Cr; the gap is other operating revenue/rounding).
- GM 66.7% → gross profit ₹470 Cr. Less staff, power, A&P, freight and other ≈ ₹362 Cr → **OP ₹108 Cr (15.3%)** [V screener].
- Less depreciation ₹40 Cr, interest ₹6 Cr, plus other income ₹13 Cr → PBT ₹75 Cr → tax 27% → **PAT ₹55 Cr** [V screener].
- **Sensitivity:**
  - +1% volume at constant ASP ≈ +₹7 Cr revenue and ≈ +₹3–4 Cr OP (≈ 50% drop-through on fixed plant costs) [E].
  - +₹1/pair of ASP ≈ +₹4.2 Cr revenue, almost all to OP.
  - +10% polymer cost ≈ −₹15 Cr OP per quarter before pricing [E: materials ~33% × 65% crude-linked × 10% × ₹705 Cr].

### C.2 Worked example 2 — Bata, FY26 annual, post- vs pre-116 [V screener; V HSIE; E]
