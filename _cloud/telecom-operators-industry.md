**Units, conventions and FX**
- ₹ crore (Cr) = 10 million. ₹ lakh crore (L Cr) = ₹1 trillion. mn = million subscribers. GB = gigabytes per user per month unless stated. MOU = minutes of use per user per month. ARPU = average revenue per user per month in ₹.

- "Circle" = Licensed Service Area (LSA). India has 22 LSAs: Metro (Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata), A, B and C categories.
- "Wireless subscribers" (TRAI) = mobile SIMs including M2M cellular connections; "wireless (mobile + FWA)" adds fixed-wireless access. TRAI's ARPU is computed on its own revenue/subscriber definitions and is not comparable with company ARPU (see trap 2).

## READ THIS FIRST: twelve definitional traps

1. **Airtel consolidated ≠ Airtel India ≠ Airtel India mobile.**
   - FY26 consolidated revenue ₹2,10,973 Cr [V screener] includes **Airtel Africa** (~$6.4 bn ≈ ₹55k Cr at FY26 rupee rates [primer; E]), **Indus Towers** (consolidated as a subsidiary since Nov-2024 [K]; Indus FY26 revenue ~₹30k Cr+ [K, verify], intra-group rentals eliminated), Homes, Airtel Business, DTH, and **Bharti Hexacom** (70% subsidiary).
   - Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue was ₹58,539 Cr; **India** revenue ₹41,214 Cr (70% of the group) [V Airtel release].
   - Mobile ARPU (₹264) applies only to **India mobile**, which includes Hexacom's circles.
   - Never compare Airtel consolidated margins (57.4%) with Jio or Vi without saying so. India EBITDA margin was **60.1%** in Q1 FY27; consolidated **57.4%** [V].
2. **ARPU has four incompatible definitions.**
   - **TRAI wireless ARPU** = all-India access revenue ÷ all wireless subscriptions **including M2M**. Q1 FY27: **₹187.12**; prepaid ₹200.72; **postpaid only ₹114.26**, because M2M/IoT and enterprise SIMs sit in postpaid [V TRAI PR 135/2026].
   - **Airtel** ARPU (₹264) = mobile revenue ÷ average customers, on its own customer definition.
   - **Jio** ARPU (₹215.60) is reported by RJIL.
   - **Vi** reports "customer ARPU" (₹195), which excludes M2M and is computed on its reported base.
   - **Hexacom** ARPU (₹259) is circle-level.
   - The ₹69 gap between Airtel (₹264) and Jio (₹216) partly reflects Jio's larger low-ARPU base and different treatment of FWA and other revenue. Treat the comparison as directional only.
   - Do not juxtapose TRAI's ₹187 with any company figure.
3. **Subscriber counts differ by source.**
   - Vi: TRAI says **199.57 mn (Aug-26)** [V]; the company says **193.1 mn (Jun-26)** [V-snip]. TRAI counts all SIMs on the network, and Vi's active (VLR) share is only **83.84%** [V TRAI Aug].
   - Airtel: TRAI **491.6 mn** mobile (Aug-26) includes **86.06 mn M2M** connections [V]. Airtel's own India customer base is ~**492 mn** total across all products [E: 681 mn group − 189 mn Africa]. Airtel's mobile base excluding M2M is far smaller (~370–380 mn [primer ~373 mn]).
   - Jio: **508.4 mn TRAI (Aug-26)** vs **533.3 mn company (Jun-26)** [V; V inc42]. Jio's company count includes FWA/broadband.
   - **Always quote the basis.**
4. **Indus Towers is inside Airtel's consolidated numbers.**
   - Airtel has consolidated Indus since it lifted its stake above 50% (~Nov-2024) [K].
   - Airtel's FY25 other income of **₹20,662 Cr** [V screener] includes the one-off **re-measurement gain on Indus consolidation** (~₹14k Cr+ [K, verify]). That is why FY25 PAT (₹37,481 Cr) exceeds FY26 (₹33,823 Cr) [V screener].
   - Indus's tower rentals from Jio and Vi now show up as Airtel revenue/EBITDA, and its leases sit inside Airtel's borrowings.
   - In any aggregate with the "Other Telecom" sub-sector, **do not add Indus Towers on top of Airtel consolidated**.
5. **Airtel PAT: screener vs "attributable" vs "pre-exceptional".**
   - Screener's FY26 **Net Profit ₹33,823 Cr** is profit **including minority interest** (Africa ~20–38% outside holders, Indus ~50%, Hexacom 30%) [V screener].
   - Screener's EPS of **₹43.81** implies **attributable PAT of ~₹26,700 Cr** [E: 43.81 × ~609 Cr shares]. This matches the primer's "reported PAT ~₹26,700 Cr".
   - **The primer mislabels ₹33,800 Cr as "PAT before exceptionals".** It is reported profit including minorities (see §3.4).
   - Q1 FY27: screener ₹10,012 Cr (incl. MI) vs **attributable ₹8,167 Cr** [V-snip Upstox] vs **net income before exceptional items ₹8,057 Cr** (company definition) [V].
   - Use attributable for P/E and EPS. Screener's P/E of 37.8 is on attributable EPS (price ₹1,892 ÷ screener TTM EPS ₹48.65 ≈ 38.9×; screener's 37.8 likely uses a slightly different EPS — immaterial).
6. **Vodafone Idea's FY26 "profit" is an accounting gain.**
   - FY26 PAT **+₹34,552 Cr** [V screener] includes FY26 other income of **₹59,292 Cr**, of which Q4 alone was ₹57,595 Cr. The Q4 exceptional items were **₹57,491 Cr**, mainly the write-down of AGR obligations after the reassessment (₹87,695 → ₹64,046 Cr nominal) [V-snip; primer ~₹55,622 Cr].
   - Pre-exceptional FY26 loss ≈ **−₹23,000 to −₹25,000 Cr** [E: Q1–Q3 −₹17,418 Cr + Q4 underlying −₹5,515 Cr per primer ≈ −₹22.9k Cr].
   - Q1 FY27 loss of −₹3,754 Cr **also includes a ~₹1,611 Cr net exceptional gain** (₹1,816 Cr remeasurement of settlement assets − ₹205 Cr regulatory provision) [V-snip Sahi]. The underlying loss is ~−₹5,365 Cr [E].
   - **Never compute a P/E for Vi.** Book value is negative (−₹3.30/share [V screener]).
7. **Lease-inclusive vs lease-exclusive debt (Ind AS 116).**
   - Tower and site rentals are capitalised as lease liabilities. This moves rent below EBITDA into depreciation and interest, inflating "EBITDA".
   - Operators therefore report **EBITDAaL** (EBITDA after leases): Airtel Q1 FY27 EBITDA ₹33,599 Cr vs EBITDAaL ₹29,840 Cr; Hexacom ₹1,375 vs ₹1,211 Cr [V].
   - Vi reports **"cash EBITDA"**: FY26 ₹9,217 Cr vs reported EBITDA ₹19,003 Cr [primer]. Vi's lease-rental outflow is ~₹9.8k Cr/yr, mostly to Indus.
   - Leverage ratios: Airtel **net debt/EBITDA 1.17×** (incl. leases) vs **net debt ex-leases/EBITDAaL 0.69×** (Q1 FY27) [V]. Hexacom **0.80× vs 0.20×** [V].
   - **Always say which.** Screener "Borrowings" include lease liabilities and deferred spectrum/AGR obligations.
8. **Deferred spectrum and AGR obligations are "debt" but owed to the government.**
   - Vi's screener borrowings of **₹1,92,528 Cr (Mar-26)** [V] are overwhelmingly government dues: deferred spectrum ~₹1.27 L Cr (nominal), AGR carried at PV (~₹24,880 Cr vs ₹64,046 Cr nominal) [primer], plus leases. **Bank debt was just ₹211 Cr at Jun-26** [V TelecomTalk].
   - TTML's ₹19,646 Cr of borrowings (Sep-26) [V BSE] = DoT dues + loans/CP backed by Tata Sons + preference shares.
   - The government is both creditor and (for Vi) 49% shareholder.
9. **Negative net worth makes ROE, P/B and ROCE meaningless for Vi and TTML.**
   - Vi book value −₹3.30/share; net worth ≈ −₹35,758 Cr (equity ₹1,08,343 Cr + reserves −₹1,44,101 Cr, Mar-26) [V screener].
   - TTML net worth −₹18,933 Cr (Sep-26) [V BSE]. Screener shows a "ROCE" of 55.6% for TTML because capital employed is distorted. **Ignore it.**
   - TCOM's ROE of 32.6% [V screener] is inflated by a thin equity base (book value ₹119/share; reserves only ₹3,162 Cr).
10. **Bharti Hexacom is inside Airtel consolidated.** Hexacom's ₹9,354 Cr FY26 revenue and ₹1,733 Cr PAT are consolidated line by line into Airtel. In aggregates, "all five" double-counts Hexacom (3.2% of the sum of revenue). Part 3 shows both "all five (straight sum)" and "all ex-Hexacom (no double count)".
11. **Hexacom's "North-East" does NOT include Assam.**
    - The NE LSA = Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura [V Hexacom Q1 FY27 release, "About Bharti Hexacom"].
    - **Assam is a separate LSA run by Airtel (parent).** Our primer wrongly lists Assam (see §3.4).
12. **Tata Communications' and TTML's "telecom" is enterprise, not consumer.**
    - TCOM sells data (connectivity + digital platforms) and wholesale voice to enterprises and carriers globally. ~**30% of TCOM's revenue is from India** [K, verify]. It has no SIM customers.
    - TTML sells enterprise connectivity in Maharashtra/Goa (brand Tata Tele Business Services, TTBS). Its consumer mobile business went to Airtel in 2019 [K: Airtel–Tata Tele CMB merger effective Jul-2019].
    - ARPU and subscriber KPIs do not apply. Use data revenue growth, digital-portfolio share and EBITDA margin (TCOM), and connections and interest cover (TTML).

## 0. Top-line framing (what an expert leads with)

- **Indian telecom is a three-player private oligopoly plus a state operator, and since the Jul-2024 tariff hike it has been in a "harvest" phase.**
  - TRAI Aug-2026 mobile shares [V TelecomTalk/TRAI]:

    | Operator | Subscribers | Share |
    |---|---|---|
    | Jio | 508.43 mn | 39.31% |
    | Airtel | 491.60 mn | 38.01% |
    | Vi | 199.57 mn | 15.43% |
    | BSNL | 93.67 mn | 7.24% |
    | MTNL | 0.15 mn | 0.01% |

  - Private operators hold **92.75%**. Jio + Airtel = **77.3%** of subscribers, and roughly **~80%+ of industry access revenue** [E; TRAI PIR revenue split by operator not fetched — FLAG].
  - Industry AGR was **₹88,796 Cr in Q1 FY27 (+9.19% YoY)**; gross revenue ₹1,06,797 Cr (+10.50% YoY) [V TRAI PR 135/2026]. Annualised AGR is **~₹3.55 lakh Cr** [E].
- **The five listed members are 1 + 1 + 1 + 2.**
  - **Airtel** (₹2.11 L Cr revenue; ₹11.81 L Cr m-cap) is **72.5% of members' FY26 revenue and 82% of their m-cap** [E: screener sums].
  - **Vi** is the distressed #3, kept alive by the state (49.02% owner).
  - **Hexacom** is a 70%-owned listed slice of Airtel (Rajasthan + NE).
  - **TCOM and TTML** are enterprise/B2B businesses that share only the licence regime with the mobile operators.
  - **All five, FY26:** revenue **₹2,91,163 Cr (+16.7%)**, OP **₹1,45,702 Cr (50.0%)**, reported PAT **₹70,890 Cr**, distorted by Vi's ₹34,552 Cr accounting gain. **M-cap ₹14.40 L Cr** (24-Oct-26) [E: sums of screener]. Ex-Hexacom (no double count): revenue ₹2,81,809 Cr, OP ₹1,40,812 Cr [E].

  - Last industry hike: **3–4 Jul 2024**. Jio ~12–25%, Airtel ~11–21%, Vi ~10–24% [K].
  - **No hike has happened in 2025 or 2026 so far** [V-snip: no announcement found]. Brokers model **12–20%** (Morgan Stanley 16–20%; Centrum 12–15% "in 3–4 months") [V-snip TelecomTalk/Angel One]. TelecomTalk reports operators are holding back because of the Iran conflict and cost-of-living pressure [V-snip].

## 1. What the sub-sector does: services, value chain, where value is captured

### 1.1 Services (by member)

| Service | What it is | Customers | Members (and benchmark) |
|---|---|---|---|

| **Enterprise connectivity** | Leased lines, MPLS/VPN, SD-WAN, internet, SIP trunks, toll-free, managed security, IoT/M2M | Corporates, government, carriers | Airtel Business, TCOM, TTML, Jio enterprise, Vi Business, BSNL, RailTel/PowerTel (Other Telecom) |
| **Global/subsea + wholesale** | International bandwidth (subsea cables), IP transit, wholesale voice | Carriers, OTTs, hyperscalers | TCOM (~30% of world internet routes claimed [primer]), Airtel (2Africa, SEA-ME-WE stakes [K]), Jio (IAX/IEX cables [K]) |
| **Digital platforms** | Cloud, CPaaS (messaging/OTP), UCaaS, media/live video, IoT, security | Enterprises | TCOM (digital portfolio ₹10,621 Cr FY26 [primer]; Kaleyra CPaaS), Airtel IQ, Jio |

| **Passive infrastructure** | Towers, fibre, data centres | Telcos, hyperscalers | Indus (Airtel subsidiary), Nxtra (Airtel DCs), Jio's own towers (in an InvIT, Digital Fibre/Summit [K]) |

### 1.2 Value chain (inputs → customer) and who earns what

1. **Spectrum (from DoT, by auction).**
   - Paid upfront or over **20 instalments** after a 2-year moratorium (post-2021 reforms) [K].
   - Annual **spectrum usage charge (SUC)** abolished for spectrum bought in 2022 and later auctions [K: 2021 reforms].
   - Spectrum is the largest balance-sheet item for mobile operators: Airtel fixed assets ₹4.06 L Cr (Mar-26) [V screener], including spectrum.
2. **Network equipment (RAN/core) from Ericsson, Nokia, Samsung.**
   - Jio uses a largely in-house/Samsung 5G SA core [K]. Vi added Samsung in its 2025 capex [K].
   - "Trusted source" rules (2021) effectively exclude Huawei/ZTE [K].
3. **Passive infrastructure: towers (Indus ~2.4 lakh+ towers [K]; Summit/Jio InvIT; ATC India sold to Brookfield, now DTL [K]), fibre, power.**
   - Rentals are capitalised as leases (Ind AS 116).
   - **Energy is ~the largest opex line** after network/licence costs [E; K].
4. **Licence fee and charges to government.**
   - **Licence fee 8% of AGR** (includes a 5% USOF/"Digital Bharat Nidhi" component) [K].
   - SUC on pre-2022 spectrum: ~3–4% weighted [K].
   - Industry licence fee was **₹7,103 Cr in Q1 FY27** [V TRAI]. That is ~₹28k Cr a year, ~8% of AGR by construction.
5. **Distribution:** retailers earn recharge commission of ~3–5% [E]; digital recharge (UPI) is rising. **Interconnect**: termination charges are zero since Jan-2021 (bill-and-keep) [K].
6. **Customer:** ~1.15 bn mobile subscriptions excluding M2M [V TRAI: 1,147.65 mn], ~1.09 bn broadband subscriptions [V TRAI], 47.8 mn wireline [V TRAI].

### 1.3 Where value is captured

- **Scale owners of spectrum + network with pricing discipline.** Jio and Airtel each earn **~52–60% EBITDA margins** (Jio 51.9% FY26; Airtel India 60.1% Q1 FY27) [V]. Vi earns **42–43%** on a sub-scale base and pays heavy leases [V screener].
- **Tower companies earn utility-like returns.** Indus EBITDA margins of ~50%+ [K], but they carry customer-concentration risk (Vi receivables).
- **Government captures ~8% of AGR** in licence fee, plus spectrum payments and GST (18% on the consumer bill) [K].
- **Enterprise specialists (TCOM)** capture lower margins (~19–21% EBITDA) because they resell third-party last-mile and voice; digital platforms are still loss-making (digital EBITDA margin −3.6% in Q2 FY27) [V Business Today].
- **Over-the-top apps** (WhatsApp, YouTube, Netflix) capture usage value without paying the network. Operators have lobbied for a "fair share"/network-usage fee; **TRAI and DoT have not adopted one** [K; TRAI 2023 consultation].

## 3. Demand drivers and end-markets

1. **Tariff repair (price).**
   - Hikes: Dec-2019 (~40%, the first after Jio's 2016 launch), **Nov/Dec-2021 (~20–25%)**, **Jul-2024 (Jio 12–25%, Airtel 11–21%, Vi 10–24%)** [K].
   - Base prepaid plans now: Airtel ~₹199 (28 days) / Jio ~₹189 [K, verify]. Operators have also cut cheap entry plans and data-only packs; in Aug-2025 Jio dropped its ₹249 1 GB/day plan, read as a "stealth hike" [V-snip Business Standard Aug-2025 headline].
   - The next hike is the swing factor (see §12).
2. **Premiumisation (mix).** 2G → 4G/5G smartphone, prepaid → postpaid, single SIM → family/convergent bundles.
   - Airtel postpaid base **30.0 mn** after a record **+1.0 mn** in Q1 FY27 [V].
   - Smartphone data customers grew 21.1 mn YoY at Airtel [V].
   - Vi's 4G/5G base is **130.1 mn of 193.1 mn** (67%) [V-snip], vs 80% at Airtel. That gap is Vi's upgrade runway, if its network improves.
3. **Data consumption.** +30–36% per user per year at Airtel/Hexacom [V]; video ~70%+ of traffic [K]. Higher usage pushes users into higher-tier packs. Unlimited 5G was effectively bundled free on ≥2 GB/day plans until operators restricted it in 2024 [K].
4. **Home broadband (fixed + FWA).**
   - Only ~48 mn wireline connections for ~300 mn households [V TRAI; K]. Wired penetration is ~15% of households [E].
   - FWA is closing the gap: 5G FWA rose from **13.21 mn to 13.45 mn in one month** (Jul→Aug 2026) [V TRAI].
   - Airtel Homes: **14.7 mn** (+3.7 mn YoY), revenue +33.2% [V]. Jio fixed broadband 28.6 mn (Jun-26) [V inc42].
5. **Enterprise digitisation.** Cloud/data-centre interconnect, SD-WAN, CPaaS (OTP/WhatsApp messaging), IoT (M2M 140.5 mn; smart meters under RDSS [K]).
   - Airtel Business +12.0% YoY (Q1 FY27) [V]; TCOM data +12.2% (Q2 FY27) [V-snip/V].
   - **DC-to-DC connectivity for AI** is cited by TCOM's CEO [V Business Today].
6. **Rural and Bharat.** BharatNet (fibre to 2.5 lakh gram panchayats; Amended BharatNet ₹1.39 L Cr [K]) and 4G saturation projects (USOF-funded BSNL 4G).
7. **Demographics.** Smartphone base ~700–750 mn [K/E]; Airtel 80% smartphone share; feature-phone users are the last ~20–25%.

**Negative drivers:**
- **SIM consolidation.** After each hike, users drop secondary SIMs. This happened after Jul-2024, when Jio/Airtel/Vi together lost ~2.5 crore subscribers in Jul–Sep 2024 while BSNL gained [K]. A 2026/27 hike would repeat this.
- **OTT substitution** of voice/SMS.
- **Cost-of-living pressure** (2026 inflation spike; WPI 11.4% [V-sib]) delaying hikes.

## 7. Regulation and policy

### 7.1 Regulators and law

- **DoT (Department of Telecommunications)** is the licensor and spectrum assigner. It collects licence fee/SUC and spectrum instalments. The **Digital Communications Commission (DCC)** is DoT's apex decision body [K].
- **TRAI** recommends (spectrum pricing, satcom, tariffs/QoS) and regulates tariffs (forbearance plus reporting), interconnect and QoS. It publishes monthly subscription and quarterly PIR data [V].
- **TDSAT** is the appellate tribunal; the Supreme Court is the final court (AGR, spectrum) [K].
- **Telecommunications Act, 2023** (passed Dec-2023; replaces the Telegraph Act 1885) [K]:
  - Administrative allotment of satellite spectrum (First Schedule).
  - Right-of-way framework.
  - Digital Bharat Nidhi (replacing USOF).
  - Authorisation (in place of licence) regime, being phased in through rules in 2024–26.
  - Biometric KYC.
  - Government interception and suspension powers.
- **2021 telecom reforms package (15-Sep-2021)** [K]:
  - 4-year moratorium on AGR and spectrum dues, with an option to convert the interest into equity. Vi exercised it; the GoI got ~33% in Feb-2023.
  - Non-telecom revenue excluded from AGR **prospectively**.
  - SUC abolished for future auctions.
  - 100% FDI via the automatic route.
  - Spectrum tenure raised from 20 to 30 years; spectrum surrender allowed after 10 years.
  - Bank guarantees for LF/SUC rationalised.
- **KYC / fraud (Sanchar Saathi, CEIR):** DoT levies frequent small penalties for subscriber-verification lapses. Airtel disclosed ₹1.5 lakh (13-Oct-2026), ₹7.36 lakh + ₹1.27 lakh (Oct) and ₹1.41 lakh + ₹2.12 lakh (28-Sep) [V screener announcements]. These amounts are immaterial but recurring.
- **Net neutrality (TRAI 2016–17)** bars differential pricing by content. "OTT fair share" has been debated but **not adopted** [K].
- **Tariffs:** TRAI forbearance. Telcos must file tariffs and keep a voice/SMS-only plan; TRAI mandated voice-only STVs in Dec-2024/Jan-2025 [K].

### 7.2 AGR — the defining legal saga

1. **Origin:** the 1999 New Telecom Policy moved licensees to revenue share. DoT defined "gross revenue" to include non-telecom income (interest, dividends, asset sales, rent). The operators disputed this from 2005 [K].
2. **24-Oct-2019:** the Supreme Court upheld DoT's definition, with **~₹92,642 Cr licence fee + ~₹55,054 Cr SUC** in dues including interest, penalty and interest on penalty, across operators [K]. A 1-Sep-2020 order allowed **10-year staggered payment** (10% upfront by Mar-2021, rest by Mar-2031) and barred recalculation [K].
3. **Dues as finalised:**
   - Vi ~₹58,254 Cr (DoT) [K].
   - Airtel ~₹43,980 Cr (DoT; Airtel self-assessed ~₹13k Cr and paid ~₹18k Cr) [K].
   - **Tata group ₹16,798 Cr up to FY2016-17**, apportioned between TTSL and TTML [V TTML filing note 2c].
4. **Sep-2021:** 4-year moratorium. Instalments start **31-Mar-2026, then six annual instalments** [V TTML note 2c; K].
5. **Feb-2023:** GoI converted ~₹16,133 Cr of Vi interest into equity, taking ~33% [K].
6. **Mar/Apr-2025:** GoI converted **₹36,950 Cr of spectrum-auction dues** into equity, taking ~49% [primer; V screener: Govt 22.63% (Mar-25) → 49.02% (Jun-25)].
7. **May-2025:** SC dismissed Vi, Airtel and Tata pleas to waive interest/penalty [primer; K].
8. **Oct-2025:** SC allowed the government to reconsider Vi's grievances [V-snip].
9. **Late 2025 / early 2026:** Cabinet **froze Vi's AGR dues at ₹87,695 Cr (FY07–FY19)**, repayable from FY32 [V-snip Outlook Business]. DoT committee reassessed to **₹64,046 Cr as of 31-Dec-2025**, communicated **30-Apr-2026** [V-snip Arihant/ETV Bharat]. Schedule: **≥₹100 Cr/yr FY32–FY35; six equal instalments FY36–FY41** [V-snip; primer].
10. **Mar-2026:** DoT **refused to extend the Vi relief to Airtel and Tata** (company-specific, distress-based) [primer].
11. **30-Mar-2026:** **TTML paid its first AGR instalment of ₹652.99 Cr**. DoT finalised liability up to FY2018-19, giving TTML a write-back of ₹666.70 Cr (with an offsetting provision in TTSL) [V TTML filing].
12. **Disputed extra demand:** a **₹9,450 Cr** DoT demand on Vi (₹2,774 Cr post-merger; ₹5,675 Cr pre-merger Vodafone-era) is under challenge; resolution not found [V-snip].
13. **Airtel's AGR:** it continues to pay instalments, and the overhang is manageable (Airtel's FY26 FCF ₹76,683 Cr vs AGR instalments of a few thousand crore a year [E]). The primer's figure of "~₹1.9 L Cr gross debt incl. leases and deferred spectrum/AGR" matches screener borrowings of ₹1,95,412 Cr [V].

## 8. History and cycles (narrative; full timeline in §G)

1. **1994–2002: licences and the fixed-fee crisis.** NTP-94 auctioned circle licences. High fixed fees caused defaults, leading to **NTP-99 migration to revenue share** (root of AGR). VSNL was privatised to Tata (2002), becoming TCOM [K].
2. **2003–2010: hypergrowth and CDMA/GSM competition.** Unified access licences; incoming calls became free (2003). Tata Teleservices launched CDMA (2002–05) and later GSM with **DoCoMo** (2008). Airtel bought **Zain Africa ($10.7 bn, 2010)** [primer]. The **2008 2G allocation** at 2001 prices led to the **2012 SC cancellation of 122 licences** [K].
3. **2010–2016: 3G/BWA auctions and 12+ operators.** The 2010 3G/BWA auction raised ~₹1.06 L Cr. Infotel (BWA pan-India) was bought by RIL and became **Jio** [K]. Tata–DoCoMo exit dispute ($1.2 bn award, 2016–17) [K].
4. **Sep-2016: Jio launches** with free voice and data until Mar-2017. Industry ARPU collapsed ~₹170 → ~₹100. **Consolidation:** Telenor India → Airtel (2017); Tata Tele CMB → Airtel (2017 announced; effective 2019); Aircel, RCom and Videocon exited; Vodafone + Idea merged (Aug-2018) [K].
5. **2019: AGR judgment (24-Oct-2019)** and the first post-Jio hike (Dec-2019). Vi and Airtel booked massive provisions: Vi FY20 loss −₹73,878 Cr; Airtel FY20 −₹30,664 Cr [V screener].
6. **2020–21: COVID data surge**; Jio raised ~₹1.52 L Cr from Meta, Google and PE (2020); Sep-2021 reform package; Nov/Dec-2021 hikes (~20–25%) [K].
7. **2022–2024: 5G.** 5G auction (Aug-2022, ₹1.5 L Cr); launch Oct-2022; fastest 5G rollout globally (~4.5 lakh sites in ~2 years [K]). Vi govt conversion to ~33% (Feb-2023). **Vi FPO ₹18,000 Cr (Apr-2024)**. **Hexacom IPO (Apr-2024, OFS by TCIL)**. **Jul-2024 tariff hikes**. Airtel takes control of Indus (Nov-2024) [K/primer].
8. **2025–2026: harvest + Vi rescue.** Vi govt stake 49% (Mar/Apr-2025); Vi 5G launch (Mar-2025); AGR frozen and reassessed to ₹64,046 Cr (Apr-2026); Airtel FY26 revenue >₹2.1 L Cr, ROCE 18% [V screener]. **Jio IPO (Oct-2026)**. Satcom licences but no launch. No tariff hike since Jul-2024.

**Cycle pattern:** each hike → 2–3 quarters of SIM consolidation → ARPU step-up → margin expansion → capex/spectrum cycle (2010 3G, 2015–16 4G, 2022 5G) → leverage up → next hike. The period since 2021 is the first time the industry has had **three rational players and no entrant**, which is the core of the bull case.

## 11. Technology and disruption risks

1. **5G monetisation gap.** Unlimited 5G was bundled into 4G-priced packs. Operators have restricted it to ≥2 GB/day plans, but there is still no explicit 5G premium [K]. FWA is the main 5G revenue use case so far (13.45 mn 5G FWA, Aug-26 [V]).
2. **Satcom (Starlink/OneWeb/Kuiper/Jio-SES).** A rural and enterprise complement at current prices (see §7.4). The risk rises if direct-to-device (D2D) satellite service scales and is licensed. Airtel and Jio have hedged via distribution deals [K].
3. **OTT voice/messaging.** Has already hollowed out voice and SMS revenue. CPaaS (OTP/WhatsApp Business) partly recaptures it via enterprise messaging, which matters for TCOM/Kaleyra and Airtel IQ [K].
4. **AI and data centres.** An opportunity (Nxtra, TCOM, Jio–Google/Meta AI partnerships [K]) and a capex claim. TCOM's "Unified AI Stack" and DC-to-DC connectivity are cited as growth drivers [V Business Today].
5. **Open RAN / indigenous 4G/5G stack (BSNL–TCS–Tejas).** A cost-down lever. If BSNL's network becomes competitive, it adds a state-backed price-taker [K].
6. **eSIM / iSIM** lowers switching costs. **Fraud/spam (AI-based spam filters)** adds compliance costs [K].
7. **Energy transition** for sites (solar, Li-ion) reduces the opex/₹100 of revenue [K].
8. **Cyber/sovereignty** rules (trusted sources, data localisation) raise costs but protect incumbents [K].

## A. The forces that define this sub-sector (what an expert analyst leads with)

1. **Market structure: three rational players, one on life support.**
   - Jio (39.3%) and Airtel (38.0%) are financially strong and earn 52–60% margins. Vi (15.4%) survives on state support. BSNL (7.2%) is a policy tool [V TRAI].
   - Pricing power depends on all three behaving rationally. A Vi collapse would create a duopoly (better pricing but regulatory risk). A recapitalised, aggressive Vi would mean worse pricing.
   - The state's 49% stake makes collapse unlikely and aggression unaffordable. This is the "managed three-player market" equilibrium [E].
2. **ARPU and the tariff-hike cadence.**
   - Industry ARPU must rise ~40–60% from TRAI's ₹187 to earn a return on the ~₹4–5 L Cr of spectrum and network capital deployed since 2016 [E].
   - Hikes have come every 2–2.5 years (Dec-2019, Nov-2021, Jul-2024). The next is **overdue** (27 months as of Oct-2026), delayed by inflation and the conflict [V-snip].
   - Premiumisation delivers ~5–7% ARPU growth a year without a hike: Airtel +5.6% YoY, Jio +3.3%, Vi +10.2% [V].
3. **The capex cycle has turned: from 5G build to free cash flow.**
   - Airtel FCF: ₹38,970 Cr (FY24) → ₹58,990 Cr (FY25) → **₹76,683 Cr (FY26)** [V screener]. Net debt/EBITDA **1.70× → 1.17×** (Jun-25 → Jun-26) [V].
   - Capital now goes to dividends (Airtel ₹24/share FY26 [primer]; payout 55% [V screener]), deleveraging, FWA, data centres and Africa.
   - The risk is a new spectrum or satcom-driven capex cycle.
4. **Government as creditor, regulator and shareholder.**
   - AGR and spectrum dues (Vi ~₹1.9 L Cr; TTML ~₹19.6k Cr; Airtel tens of thousands of crore) make the DoT the largest lender to the sector.
   - Every major equity event of the last 5 years has been a government decision: 2021 package, 2023 and 2025 Vi conversions, 2026 AGR reassessment.
   - Investors in Vi and TTML are underwriting **policy**, not operations.
5. **Convergence and enterprise.**
   - Growth outside mobile: Airtel Homes +33%, Airtel Business +12%, Hexacom Homes +61% [V]; Jio fixed broadband 28.6 mn [V inc42].
   - Enterprise digital (TCOM digital +18.6%) [V] is the next battleground.
   - This is where the "telco" multiple gets justified or not.

## C. The REVENUE / PROFIT EQUATION

**Mobile operator:**
Revenue = Σ (subscribers × ARPU × 12) + homes (connections × ARPU) + enterprise + other
EBITDA = Revenue × (1 − licence fee/SUC% − network opex% − SG&A%)
FCF = EBITDA − lease payments − capex − spectrum/AGR instalments − interest − tax

**Enterprise (TCOM):**
Revenue = data (connectivity + digital) + voice. EBITDA = Σ segment revenue × segment margin (core connectivity ~25–30%, digital −4% → +10% target, voice ~5–10%) [E].

### C.1 Worked example 1 — Airtel India mobile, one quarter (Q1 FY27) [E composite on V inputs]
- Mobile customers (excl. M2M) ~375 mn [E: primer 373 mn + growth].
- ARPU ₹264 [V] → mobile revenue ≈ 375 mn × ₹264 × 3 = **₹29,700 Cr/quarter** [E]. Cross-check: India revenue ₹41,214 Cr [V] less Homes (~₹2,000 Cr), Business (~₹5,700 Cr), DTH ₹773 Cr, passive/other ≈ ₹30–32k Cr for mobile — consistent.

- Mobile EBITDA ≈ **₹17,800 Cr (~60%)** [E; India EBITDA margin 60.1% V].
