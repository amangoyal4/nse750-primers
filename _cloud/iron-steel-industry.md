## 0. One-paragraph orientation

India is the world's #2 crude-steel producer (164.9 Mt in CY2025, +10.4% y/y, vs China 960.8 Mt, −4.4%; world 1,848.9 Mt, −2.0%) [V: MER Sep-2026, Annexure II, citing worldsteel]. In FY26 (Apr-25–Mar-26) India made 168.4 Mt crude steel (+10.7%; revised to 170.15 Mt in the Ministry's Jul-2026 Overview) and consumed 163.7 Mt finished steel (+7.6%; revised 164.36 Mt) [V: MER Mar-2026; MoS Overview Jul-2026]. Installed crude capacity is ~222 MTPA (June 2026, Ministry of Steel via ANI, 24 Jul 2026) [V: latestly.com/ANI], so the industry runs at ~76% utilisation [E: 168.4/~220 average FY26 capacity]. The National Steel Policy 2017 (NSP-2017) target is 300 MTPA capacity / 255 Mt production by 2030-31 [K: NSP-2017 gazette, May 2017]. India is the one large market where steel demand still grows 7–9% a year, so it is the target of every surplus exporter (China 119 Mt exports in 2025) — hence the 3-year safeguard duty (12% → 11.5% → 11%, Apr-2025 to Apr-2028) [V: Outlook Business 31-Dec-2025; taxguru notification text]. FY27 has started slower: H1 (Apr–Sep 2026) crude output +2.9% to 85.1 Mt while consumption grew +7.5% to 84.9 Mt, inventories were drawn down by 2.56 Mt, and India swung back to being a net importer (−0.59 Mt) [V: MER Sep-2026]. Prices bottomed in Dec-2025 (Mumbai HRC retail ₹54,760/t incl. GST) and rallied to ₹76,900/t by 30-Sep-2026 [V: MER Dec-2025, Sep-2026], helped by a coking-coal spike to US$278/t FOB (Sep-2026) and the West-Asia/Hormuz energy-freight shock of Mar–Apr 2026.

## 1. What the sub-sector does — value chain and where value is captured

**Physical chain (BF-BOF, "integrated"):** iron ore (mined; Odisha ~55%, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Karnataka) → beneficiation / sintering / pelletising → blast furnace (with coke from imported coking coal + PCI coal) → hot metal → basic oxygen furnace (BOF) → ladle refining → continuous casting (slab/billet/bloom) → hot strip mill (HRC) → cold rolling (CRC) → coating (GP/GC, colour-coated, galvalume) / electrical steel (CRNO/CRGO) → service centres, OEMs, distributors.

**Secondary chain (DRI-EAF/IF):** iron ore fines/pellets + non-coking (thermal) coal in rotary kilns → sponge iron (DRI) → induction furnace (IF) or electric arc furnace (EAF), often with scrap and pig iron → billets → TMT rebar, wire rod, structurals (long products). Power is typically generated captively from waste heat (WHRB) and dolochar (AFBC) — a key cost edge for Chhattisgarh/Odisha/West Bengal mid-caps (Shyam, Sarda, GPIL, Jai Balaji, Jayaswal Neco, Gallantt).

**Gas-DRI route:** natural-gas (or syngas from coal gasification — JSPL Angul) based DRI → EAF. Gas-DRI capacity (AM/NS Hazira, JSW Dolvi, JSL's EAF/DRI, Essar legacy) was exposed in the March 2026 LNG force-majeure episode (see §9).

**Where value is captured** [E, reasoning]:
1. **Raw-material ownership** is the biggest single lever. Iron ore is ~20–30% of cash cost for an integrated mill; a captive-ore producer (SAIL ~100% captive, Tata Steel India ~100% captive pre-2030, NMDC Steel buys from NMDC at arm's length, JSW ~40–50% captive post-auction, JSPL partially captive) saves the merchant margin (NMDC's own EBITDA/t on ore is ₹1,500–2,500/t) — i.e., ~₹2,500–4,000 per tonne of steel. Lloyds Metals (Surjagarh, Maharashtra) is the purest play on this — it earns mining margin and DRI/pellet margin.
2. **Coking coal** cannot be owned meaningfully in India (domestic coking coal is high-ash; India imports ~85–90% of coking coal needs; ~58–60 Mt imported in FY25) [K: Ministry of Coal / Ministry of Steel; see §6]. Value here is "lost" abroad (Australia, Russia, US, Mozambique) and is the biggest swing factor in BF-BOF EBITDA/t.
3. **Downstream / value-added** (CRC, coated, electrical steel, auto grades): +₹6,000–15,000/t over HRC (CRC-HRC spread in Mumbai was ₹12,770/t in Sep-2026: 89,670 − 76,900 [V: MER Sep-2026]).
4. **Conversion scale and logistics** (coastal plants: JSW Dolvi, AM/NS Hazira, Tata Kalinganagar near Paradip/Dhamra ports) for imported coal and exports.
5. **Captive power/WHRB** for secondary producers (power is 15–20% of IF/EAF cost).

### 3.2 China — the external price setter

- **Production:** 1,005.09 Mt (2024) → **960.81 Mt (2025, −4.4%)** — first sub-1-bn year since 2019 [V: MER Sep-26 Annex II/worldsteel]. China's Jan–Aug 2026 output continued down: Aug-26 74.61 Mt (−3.7% y/y), May-26 84.36 Mt (−2.7%) [V MER Jun-26, Sep-26]. Beijing pledged (Dec-2025) to keep "controlling" crude-steel output and ban new illegal capacity through the 15th FYP (2026–30) [V-search: mining.com/Reuters 29-Dec-2025 headline; not fetched].
- **Domestic demand:** apparent consumption fell 5.4% to 808 Mt (Jan–Nov 2025) on the property slump [V: Caixin 14-Jan-2026].
- **Exports:** record **119 Mt in 2025 (+7.5%)**, above the 2015 peak of 112.4 Mt; average export price fell to **US$694/t** [V: Caixin 14-Jan-2026]. China's export-licence regime for ~245 steel products started 1-Jan-2026; Jan–Aug 2026 exports were **75.15 Mt (−3.0% y/y)**, August 10.16 Mt (+6.8% y/y) — i.e., licences have slowed but not stopped the flood [V: SteelOrbis 8-Sep-2026]. China's steel imports Jan–Aug 2026: 3.57 Mt (−10.5%) [V].
- **Mechanism for India:** Chinese HRC export FOB (~US$450–500/t in 2025) + freight ~US$20–30 → landed India ~₹44–47k/t vs Indian HRC ex-works ₹46–52k/t through 2025; without protection the import parity caps Indian prices. Korea (FTA/CEPA, zero basic duty), Japan (CEPA) and ASEAN (FTA) shipments are the other channel; Korea was the #1 source in FY26 (35.4%).

### 3.3 Trade-remedy regime (state as of Oct-2026)

**Safeguard duty on flat steel (the main defence)** [V: Outlook Business 31-Dec-2025; taxguru reproduction of Notification 02/2025-Customs (SG)]:
- Application by Indian Steel Association; DGTR initiation **19-Dec-2024**; provisional duty **12% ad valorem for 200 days from 21-Apr-2025** (Notification 01/2025-Cus (SG), GSR 243(E)); DGTR final findings **~16/18-Aug-2025** recommending three years; Finance Ministry definitive **Notification No. 02/2025-Customs (SG), 30-Dec-2025 (G.S.R. 931(E))**.
- **Rates: 12% (21-Apr-2025 → 20-Apr-2026); 11.5% (21-Apr-2026 → 20-Apr-2027); 11% (21-Apr-2027 → 20-Apr-2028).** Note: the taxguru transcription prints "5%" for year two; Outlook Business, S&P Global and DGTR's recommendation all say 11.5% — 11.5% is the credible figure and "5%" is a transcription error.
- **Gap:** no duty was levied between lapse of the provisional duty (~7-Nov-2025) and the 30-Dec-2025 notification (~7 weeks), which coincided with the Q3FY26 price trough.
- **Scope:** HS 7208, 7209, 7210, 7211, 7212, 7225, 7226 — HRC/sheets/plates, plate-mill plates, CRC, metallic-coated, colour-coated. Excluded: CRGO, CRNO, tinplate, stainless, electro-galvanised and a long list of speciality coated/clad products.
- **CIF floor (duty not applicable at or above):** HRC/sheets/plates **US$675/t**; HR plate-mill plates US$695/t; CRC US$824/t; metallic-coated US$861/t; colour-coated US$964/t.
- **Country carve-outs:** developing countries (Notification 19/2016-Cus NT) exempt except **China** (all five categories), **Vietnam** (HR, metallic-coated) and **Nepal** (CR, metallic-coated, colour-coated). Korea/Japan are developed — fully covered unless CIF ≥ floor.
- Effect: imports fell 31.7% in FY26; but the CIF floor means that when global prices rise (as in Sep–Oct 2026, HRC export FOB India US$680/t to Europe [V-search SMM 7-Oct-2026]) the safeguard stops binding — imports rose 24% in FY27 H1.

**Anti-dumping (selected live/recent)** [V-search; DGTR site not fetched]:
- Hot-rolled flat (non-stainless) from **Vietnam**: definitive ADD ~**US$121.55/t** for 5 years (late 2025); Hoa Phat exempt (0–10% margin band) [VOV].
- New ADD investigation on hot-rolled flat products from **China, Japan and Russia** (POI Jan–Dec 2025) initiated in 2026 [GMK Center headline; date not verified].
- Stainless cold-rolled flat from China, Indonesia, Vietnam: DGTR extended the deadline by 6 months to **28-Mar-2027** (SteelOrbis 25-Sep-2026) — relevant to Jindal Stainless.
- Earlier: ADD on certain stainless welded pipes, CRNO from China (2024–25), met coke (ADD on low-ash met coke 2025 investigation), and QCOs (BIS Quality Control Orders — >150 steel products under mandatory BIS licensing; a de facto non-tariff barrier) [K: Ministry of Steel QCO list].

**Basic customs duty:** 7.5% on most flat products (Korea/Japan/ASEAN get FTA concessional rates, ~0%); 2.5% on CRGO; coking coal and ferro-nickel duty-free; 2.5% on met coke [K: Customs Tariff 2025-26].

## 6. Raw materials: iron ore, coking coal, mining regime

### 6.1 Iron ore
- India ore production ~282 Mt (CY2024, worldsteel) [V]; ~290–300 Mt in FY26 [K: Ministry of Mines provisional]. Odisha ~55%, Chhattisgarh ~17%, Karnataka ~13%, Jharkhand ~10%, Goa/Maharashtra rest [K: IBM]. Grade: high-grade (Fe 62%+) is ~45% of reserves; industry is shifting to beneficiation + pellets (India pellet capacity ~130+ MTPA) [K].
- Consumption norm: BF-BOF ~1.5–1.6 t ore (as sinter + pellet + lump) per t crude steel; coal-DRI ~1.5–1.6 t pellet/lump per t DRI; plus ~1.1 t DRI per t IF steel ⇒ ~1.7–1.8 t ore per t steel on the DRI-IF route [K: JPC norms].
- NMDC is the price-setter (~45 Mt/yr; FY27 H1 production 27.27 Mt, +22.8%; sales 22.23 Mt flat [V MER Sep-26 Annex III]). Odisha merchant miners (OMC, Serajuddin, Rungta, Essel, KJS-turned-JSW, etc.) and captive auctioned mines supply the rest.
- Price path in §4.2. FY26 was a down-year for ore (NMDC lump 6,440 May-25 → 4,800 Mar-26) as auctioned-mine output rose, then recovered to ₹5,400–5,700 in Jun–Sep 2026 with steel prices.

### 6.2 The mining/auction regime — why "captive" is not what it used to be
- **MMDR Amendment 2015** (12-Jan-2015 ordinance): all new leases only by **auction**; captive leases of legacy holders extended to **31-Mar-2030**, merchant leases to **31-Mar-2020**; state PSU leases (NMDC, OMC, SAIL) extended for 20 years with an additional payment. DMF = 30% of royalty for pre-2015 leases, 10% for auctioned; NMET = 2% of royalty [K: MMDR Act s.8A, 9B, 9C].
- **Royalty on iron ore = 15% ad valorem** of the IBM-published average sale price (ASP) [V-search: secondary summary of MMDR Second Schedule; K].
- **Auction premium**: bidders quote a % of the value of mineral despatched (IBM state-wise ASP × quantity), payable monthly **over and above** royalty. **Odisha 2020 auctions** (leases expired 31-Mar-2020; ~23–24 working merchant mines): premiums of 90–150% were common — e.g., JSW won **Nuagaon at 95.2%** (India's largest working iron ore mine at the time; JSW took 4 mines in Feb-2020), Kaypee Thakurani technical-round 72.5% [V-search: BigMint Feb-2020 headlines]; later Odisha/Karnataka auctions cleared at 100–150%+ [K]. Odisha accounts for **34 of India's 101 auctioned mineral blocks** [V: Policy Circle Aug-2026].
- **Economic effect** [E]: an auctioned mine paying 100% premium + 15% royalty + DMF/NMET ≈ **~117% of ASP** in statutory payments ⇒ the cash cost of "captive" ore from an auctioned mine is roughly ASP × 1.17 + mining cost (₹300–600/t) + logistics — usually **at or above merchant price**. The real raw-material moat therefore belongs to **legacy, non-auctioned leases**: SAIL (captive leases, PSU extension), Tata Steel (legacy captive leases in Jharkhand/Odisha valid to **31-Mar-2030** — a known 2030 cost step-up risk), NMDC (PSU), and mining-led companies that won blocks at low premia in less-contested states (e.g., Lloyds' Surjagarh, Maharashtra, a 2007-vintage lease; GPIL's Ari Dongri/Boria Tibu and Sarda's Thakurani in Chhattisgarh/Odisha) [K, company packs to verify].
- **MMDR Amendment 2021** (28-Mar-2021): removed the captive/merchant distinction for future auctions; allowed captive mines to **sell up to 50% of annual production** after meeting own needs, on payment of an additional amount; allowed transfer of statutory clearances to new lessee (so auctioned mines restart quickly) [K: MMDR (Amendment) Act 2021].
- **Supreme Court, 25-Jul-2024 (*Mineral Area Development Authority v. SAIL*, 9-judge bench, 8:1)**: royalty is **not a tax**; States have legislative competence to tax mineral rights and mineral-bearing land. Follow-up order (14-Aug-2024): States may recover arrears from **1-Apr-2005**, **in 12 annual instalments from 1-Apr-2026**, **with no interest or penalty** for the period to 25-Jul-2024 [V: Policy Circle Aug-2026; Angel One summary]. Estimated arrears **₹1.5–2 lakh crore nationally; Odisha's potential claim >₹1 lakh crore** (much of it under the ORISED Act); BJD estimate: Odisha could lose ~₹12,000 cr/yr [V: Policy Circle]. Jharkhand passed a Mineral-Bearing Land Cess law (2024) [K].
- **MMDR (Amendment) Act 2026 — new Section 9D** [V: Bill No. 154 of 2026, PRS India PDF, introduced by G. Kishan Reddy, dated 7-Aug-2026; passed Lok Sabha 12-Aug and Rajya Sabha 13-Aug-2026 per Policy Circle; PIB factsheet 18-Aug-2026 (403, not fetched)]: (1) no State tax/cess/levy on **mineral rights or mineral-bearing lands** (whether based on quantity, value, royalty or otherwise) except under conditions prescribed by the Centre; (2) **any such levy not already deposited/recovered before commencement is "deemed to be invalid at all material times"** — i.e., the 2005–2024 arrears window opened by the SC is effectively closed for unpaid amounts; amounts already paid are **not refundable**. Commencement by Gazette notification (status as of 13-Oct-2026: not verified). Implication: removes a multi-thousand-crore contingent liability overhang for Tata Steel, SAIL, JSW, NMDC, JSPL and Odisha/Jharkhand miners (company packs should quantify their disclosed contingent liabilities); minor minerals stay with States; expect litigation by Odisha/Jharkhand on federalism grounds.
- State mineral receipts rose from ₹13,258 cr (2014-15) to ₹71,035 cr (2024-25); States' share of mineral revenue 65% → 88% (Mines Minister, per Policy Circle) [V].

### 6.3 Coking coal — the structural import dependence
- India imported **63.7 Mt of coking coal in FY26 (+12.4% from 56.6 Mt)**; total met coal incl. PCI **84.5 Mt** [V-search: MCX-based report via newkerala Aug-2026; fetch blocked 403 — treat as unverified]; CY2025 coking-coal imports 62.6 Mt (+9.8%, BigMint) [V-search]. Dependence on imports ≈ **85–90%** of coking coal needs [K: Ministry of Steel; domestic washed coking coal ~6–8 Mt usable].
- Sources: **Australia >50%**, US #2, **Russia 12.6% (Jan–Aug 2026, +53% y/y volumes, at 15–20% discounts)**, Mozambique, Canada, Indonesia (PCI) [V-search: India Sea Trade News 5-Aug-2026 / BigMint].
- Consumption norm: ~0.75–0.85 t coking coal + PCI per t hot metal (coke rate ~350–380 kg/thm + PCI ~150–180 kg/thm; ~1.35–1.45 t coal per t coke) ⇒ ~**0.8 t coal per t crude steel** for BF-BOF [K: JPC norms/company disclosures].
- Policy: "Mission Coking Coal" (Ministry of Coal, 2021) targets ~140 Mt domestic raw coking coal by 2030 and more washeries; coking coal blocks auctioned; Russia/Mongolia diversification MoUs; coking coal BCD 0% (2.5% on met coke; ADD investigations on met coke) [K].
- Sensitivity [E]: **US$10/t change in HCC ≈ ₹770/t of steel** (0.8 × 10 × ₹96); a US$50/t move ≈ ₹3,800/t ≈ 25–30% of a normal EBITDA/t. BF-BOF mills carry 1–1.5 months of inventory + 1 month in transit, so spot moves hit P&L with ~1 quarter lag.

### 6.4 Gas, power and the 2026 West-Asia shock
- **What happened** [V-search; dtnext/Reuters/GMK/BigMint]: early **Mar-2026**, QatarEnergy declared force majeure after production stopped following an Iranian drone strike; Petronet LNG's tankers could not reach Ras Laffan via Hormuz and it passed force majeure to GAIL/IOC/BPCL; gas marketers cut industrial supply by **10–40%**; government prioritised gas to households/fertiliser. **JSW Steel** received a Petronet force-majeure notice; an internal note (Reuters, mid-Mar) warned a unit could shut within days. **Jindal Stainless** ran plants at reduced capacity (fuel shortages). The Indian Steel Association's **7-Mar-2026** letter flagged a "huge adverse impact" from LPG/propane shortages (used in reheating furnaces, galvanising, small re-rollers). In **Apr-2026**, US–Iran talks collapsed, a US naval blockade of Iranian ports was announced, **Brent rose to ~US$95/bbl** and Australia–India freight (which had eased to ~US$20/t) turned up; BigMint noted higher freight raised import parity and supported domestic prices [V: BigMint 20-Apr-2026]. Steel trade through Hormuz fell **65% in Jan–Aug 2026** [V-search SMM]; Fastmarkets (Jun-2026) expects freight/insurance 10–20% above pre-war for 6–12 months [V-search].
- **Who is exposed:** gas-DRI (AM/NS Hazira, JSW Dolvi's DRI module, Essar-legacy units), stainless (JSL's ~65% gas-based route per its statement), galvanising/colour-coating lines (LPG/propane), and secondary rolling mills; coal-DRI and BF-BOF were relatively insulated (and benefited from higher import parity). Crude-steel output in Q1FY27 grew only 3.0% (Apr–Jun 2026) vs 11.9% a year before [V MER Jun-26].
- **Power:** captive coal-based and WHR power covers most needs of integrated mills; secondary mills in Punjab/Raipur faced grid outages again in Oct-2026 [V-search SMM 1-Oct-2026].

- **Autos:** ~11% of demand; passenger-vehicle output ~5 mn units/yr; auto is the core of value-added flats (CRC, galvanised, AHSS) [K: SIAM].
- **Housing/real estate:** PMAY-U 2.0 / PMAY-G (3 crore additional houses target, 2024) [K]; TMT demand is monsoon-seasonal (Jun–Sep).
- **Energy transition:** solar module mounting structures, transmission towers, wind towers, pipelines and electrical steel (CRGO/CRNO; India imports most CRGO) — incremental ~3–5 Mt/yr by 2030 [K/E].
- **Exports:** 6.6 Mt FY26 (~4% of production) — now constrained by EU TRQ cut (−47% quota, 50% out-of-quota duty from 1-Jul-2026), EU CBAM and US Section 232 50% tariff (Jun-2025) [V EC Access2Markets; K for US].

Key differences: (1) BF-BOF margins swing with the **HRC–coking-coal spread** (China-linked); DRI-IF margins swing with **TMT–(ore + thermal coal)** and are more domestic/regional. (2) Capex intensity: BF-BOF needs 3–4x the capital per tonne of IF, which is why IF capacity can be added in 18 months vs 36–48 months for a BF. (3) Decarbonisation: coal-DRI-IF is the most carbon-intensive route in India (~3+ tCO2/t), BF-BOF ~2.5, gas-DRI-EAF ~1.4–1.8, scrap-EAF ~0.7 — CCTS and CBAM penalise coal-DRI most. (4) Captive ore matters most where the lease is legacy (no auction premium): SAIL, Tata (until Mar-2030), NMDC Steel (arm's-length from NMDC), Lloyds.

## 10. The revenue/profit equation (C) — with worked ₹ examples

**Integrated mill:**
Revenue = Sales volume (t) × Realisation/t; Realisation/t = HRC benchmark (ex-works, ex-GST) ± mix premium (CRC +₹9–13k, coated +₹15–20k, long/TMT ≈ HRC −₹3–8k) − discounts; EBITDA/t = Realisation/t − [ore t/t × ore ₹/t + coal t/t × coal ₹/t + other conversion cost/t] (+ by-product/power credits); EBITDA = EBITDA/t × volume.

**Worked spread, Oct-2026 spot (Dataset-backed)** [E built from V inputs]:
- HRC ex-works Mumbai, ex-GST: **₹63,000/t** (SMM 7-Oct-2026 [V-search]; retail 76,900 incl. GST on 30-Sep [V MER]).
- Iron ore: 1.6 t × ₹6,200/t landed (NMDC fines ₹4,500 FOR [V] + royalty/DMF/NMET ~₹900 + rail freight ~₹800) = **₹9,920**.
- Coking coal: 0.8 t × ₹28,350/t landed ((US$265 FOB [V-search SMM 24-Sep] + US$22 freight) × ₹96 + ₹800 port/handling) = **₹22,680**.
- **Raw-material spread = 63,000 − 9,920 − 22,680 = ₹30,400/t.**
- Less conversion cost (other RM, power, labour, stores, freight-out, overheads, net of by-product credits) ~₹14,000–15,000/t ⇒ plus mix premium ~₹3,000–4,000 ⇒ **implied EBITDA ≈ ₹18,000–20,000/t** at spot — vs reported Q1FY27 EBITDA/t of ~₹19,162 (Tata Steel India) and ~₹15,013 (JSW, Ind-Ra) [V-search, company packs to verify]. Note Q1FY27 used ~₹56–58k HRC and US$238 coal.
- **Trough comparison, Q3FY26 (Dec-2025):** HRC ex-works ~₹46,000 (ICRA: "touching ₹46,000/MT in early-December 2025" [V]); coal (US$212+22)×₹90+800 = ₹21,860 × 0.8 = ₹17,490; ore 1.6 × ₹6,300 = ₹10,080 ⇒ **spread ₹18,430/t**, implied EBITDA ~₹7,500–8,500/t (~US$84–95) — matches ICRA's US$84/t for Q3FY26 [V].
- **Sensitivities per tonne of steel:** HRC ±₹1,000 → ±₹1,000; HCC ±US$10 → ∓₹770; INR ±₹1 (coal only) → ∓₹220 (but HRC import parity moves ~+₹650, so net +₹400 for a weaker rupee) [E]; ore ±₹500 → ∓₹800.
- **Volume leverage example:** a 30 MTPA producer selling 27 Mt at EBITDA/t ₹15,000 = ₹40,500 cr EBITDA; each ₹1,000/t = ₹2,700 cr (6–7% of EBITDA).

**Secondary DRI-IF (Raipur, 1-Sep-2026)** [E built from V prices]:
- Sponge iron (PDRI) ₹29,400–30,000/t; inputs ~1.55 t pellet/lump (₹6,000–6,500/t → ~₹9,500) + ~1.2 t thermal coal (₹6,500–8,000/t landed → ~₹8,500) + conversion ~₹3,500 ⇒ DRI cash cost ~₹21,500 ⇒ **DRI margin ~₹8,000/t** (merchant), higher with captive ore.
- Billet ₹42,400: 1.12 t DRI (₹33,000 at market) + 0.1 t scrap/pig (₹3,700) + IF power 700 kWh × ₹5 (₹3,500) + ferro-alloys/refractory/labour ₹2,500 ⇒ ₹42,700 ⇒ **billet margin ~₹0 on market DRI** — i.e., IF steelmaking only pays if the DRI is captive (integrated DRI→billet margin ~₹7,500–8,500/t) and power is captive (₹2–3/kWh WHR saves ~₹1,500–2,000/t). TMT rolling adds ₹2,500–4,000/t (Raipur rebar ₹45,100; branded TMT +₹3–6k).

## 12. History and cycles (with dates)
- **1991–92**: delicensing (1991) and price decontrol (16-Jan-1992) → private BF-BOF capacity (JSW, Essar, Ispat).
- **2002–2008 super-cycle**: China's urbanisation; Tata–Corus (Apr-2007, US$12 bn) at the peak.
- **2008–09 GFC**; **2011–2013**: SC mining bans in Karnataka (Jul-2011) and Goa (Sep-2012), Shah Commission in Odisha (2013) → ore shortage, JSW imported ore.
- **2014 coal block deallocation** (SC, 24-Sep-2014: 204 blocks cancelled) → JSPL hit; **2015–16** China exports ~112 Mt → Indian price collapse; MIP (Feb-2016) and ADDs/safeguard (2015–16) → steel NPA wave; **IBC 2016** resolutions (Bhushan Steel 2018, Essar 2019, Monnet, BPSL 2021).
- **FY21 COVID** (−5.3% consumption) → **FY22 peak** (HRC ~₹76k ex-works Apr-2022; record exports 13.5 Mt; EBITDA/t ₹20–25k+).
- **22-May-2022 export duty** → FY23 margin reset; coking coal spike (US$600+ spot, Mar-2022; US$354 Oct-2023).
- **FY24–FY25**: China exports climb (91 Mt 2023 → 111 Mt 2024 [K] → 119 Mt 2025 [V]); India net importer FY24–FY25 (9.55 Mt imports FY25 [V]); HRC slides to ~₹46k (Dec-2025).
- **Apr-2025 provisional safeguard**; **30-Dec-2025 final** 3-year safeguard; **Jan-2026** China export licences; EU CBAM definitive.
- **Mar–Apr 2026** West-Asia/Hormuz shock: LNG force majeure, LPG shortages, Brent ~US$95, freight up; rupee to ~₹95–96.
- **1-Jul-2026** EU TRQ regime (−47% quota, 50% duty); **Aug-2026** MMDR Amendment (Sec 9D); **Sep-2026** HCC US$278, Mumbai HRC ₹76,900 retail.
- **Cycle anatomy**: Indian steel cycles are ~3–5 years trough-to-trough, driven by China export surges (2015, 2024–25) and coking coal shocks (2016–17, 2021–22, 2023), with policy (export duty 2022; safeguard 2025) as a frequent swing factor.

## 14. Technology and disruption
- **Route shift**: BF-BOF remains ~64% of announced additions [V-search] because India lacks scrap (scrap ~30 Mt/yr availability; Steel Scrap Recycling Policy 2019) and cheap gas. Gas/hydrogen DRI: JSW (Salav DRI, green H2 pilots), Tata (H2 injection in BF, Jamshedpur 2023), JSPL (coal gasification DRI at Angul, the world's largest; plans for green-H2 DRI), AM/NS (Hazira, renewables 1 GW+). Green H2 needs ~₹200–250/kg → <₹150 to be viable [K: National Green Hydrogen Mission].
- **Scrap-EAF mini-mills**: Tata Ludhiana 0.75 MTPA EAF (2025–26); JSW Kadapa EAF; Shyam/Sarda in induction furnace upgrades. IF steel quality (P, S control) is a regulatory focus (BIS QCOs).
- **CCUS, renewable power PPAs (JSW Energy, Tata Power RE), WHRB, top-gas recycling, beneficiation of low-grade ore, slurry pipelines** (NMDC, JSW, AM/NS, SAIL Jamda–Bokaro 258 km for 8.3 Mt/yr [V ANI Jul-2026]) lower cost and CO2.
- **Value-added products**: CRGO (India imports most; JSW-JFE CRGO JV; Tata/Posco), AHSS for autos, tinplate (JSW Tarapur), coated/colour-coated for roofing and solar structures — PLI-supported.

## A. The defining forces (3–5) with reasoning
1. **The HRC–coking-coal spread (and the rupee) sets integrated-mill profits.** ~30–34% of BF-BOF revenue is imported coking coal; HRC is priced off Chinese/Asian import parity. The spread moved from ~₹18k/t (Dec-2025) to ~₹30k/t (Oct-2026) [E], a swing worth ~₹10,000/t of EBITDA — larger than any operational lever.
2. **China's surplus versus India's trade wall.** China exported a record 119 Mt in 2025 at US$694/t [V]; India's 12%/11.5%/11% safeguard (to Apr-2028) with CIF floors, plus ADDs and QCOs, are what keep Indian HRC at a premium to the Chinese offer. The wall is porous (FTAs with Korea/Japan/ASEAN; CIF floor), so Indian prices still track Asian prices with a lag.
3. **Domestic demand growth of 7–9% a year, led by public capex and housing.** Construction + infra ≈ 60% of demand [V]; FY27 Union capex ₹12.2 lakh cr [V]; per-capita use 109 kg vs world 209 kg [V]. This is the investment case for capacity going 220 → ~300 MTPA by FY31 — and also the oversupply risk if demand slips to CRISIL's 5–7%.
4. **Raw-material security and the mining fiscal regime.** Auction premia of 90–150% have made "captive" auctioned ore no cheaper than merchant ore; legacy leases (SAIL, Tata to 2030, NMDC, Lloyds' Surjagarh) are the true moat. The SC's 2024 ruling opened a ₹1.5–2 lakh cr state-tax liability that the MMDR 2026 Sec 9D has largely closed for unpaid amounts [V].
5. **Decarbonisation economics (CCTS, CBAM, green-steel taxonomy) arriving faster than the technology.** India's 2.54 tCO2/tcs [V] vs global 1.92 [V]; 64% of new capacity is still BF-BOF; CBAM definitive phase (Jan-2026) and the EU TRQ (Jul-2026) shrink the EU export outlet (ICRIER: −24%); CCTS targets start FY27.

## B. Deep-dive themes (2–4)
**B1. The safeguard + import-parity mechanics.** The duty only binds when CIF < US$675/t for HRC. With Chinese HRC FOB ~US$470–500 + freight ~US$25 → CIF ~US$500–525 ⇒ landed with 11.5% SGD + 7.5% BCD (non-FTA) + 1% SWS ≈ US$625 ⇒ ₹60,000/t at ₹96 — close to the Oct-2026 ex-works ₹63,000 [E]. Korean/Japanese HRC enters at 0% BCD under CEPA but pays SGD unless CIF ≥ US$675. ICRA noted in Feb-2026 that domestic HRC was at import parity "with slight premium (~US$3/t)" [V] — i.e., there is little headroom for further price hikes without global prices rising. The Sep–Oct 2026 rally was powered by global cost push (coal, freight, FX), not by the safeguard.

**B2. Capacity race to 300 MTPA — who adds, at what cost, and the FY28–FY30 oversupply question.** ~95–115 MTPA of additions are announced to FY31 (167 MTPA gross per industry counts) [E/V-search], dominated by JSW, AM/NS, SAIL, JSPL, Tata. At ₹4,000–8,000 cr per MTPA, that is ₹4–7 lakh crore of capex. With demand at 7–8% (~13 Mt/yr), the industry needs ~16 MTPA/yr of capacity at 80% utilisation — announced plans are slightly ahead of need, implying utilisation pressure in FY28–FY30 if projects land on time. Balance-sheet capacity is better than in 2014–18 (bank debt US$184/t vs US$450/t peak [V]).

**B3. Mining regime and the captive-ore advantage.** Sec 9D (Aug-2026) removes the retrospective state-tax overhang; but the Mar-2030 lapse of legacy captive leases (Tata Steel's Jharkhand/Odisha mines) and auction premia of 90–150% mean ore cost convergence between "captive" and "merchant" by FY31. The winners are holders of legacy or low-premium leases (SAIL, NMDC, Lloyds) and players who can beneficiate low-grade ore and pelletise.

**B4. Decarbonisation and export access.** CBAM default values with mark-ups (10% in 2026 → 30% from 2028) penalise unverified Indian data; India's flat default ~4.28 tCO2e/t vs plant-verified ~2.2–2.6 means **MRV quality is worth €100+/t** for EU exporters [E]. CCTS targets (2–9% intensity cuts by FY27) are modest; green-steel certificates already cover 97 producers [V]. Coal-DRI-IF secondary producers are the most exposed in the long run.
