00

The one-page view: four survivors, one model

India's listed classifieds all charge the side of the market that wants to be found, collect the cash a year in advance, and are now being judged on two new questions: what AI search does to their traffic, and what they do with the cash.

The four companies in this report were all founded before most Indians had used the internet. Justdial and IndiaMART began in 1996, one as a telephone directory and the other as an export catalogue; Naukri.com went live in 1997; CarTrade was incorporated in 2000. Thirty years later they are the listed survivors of India's first internet wave, and they still share one business model. Each runs a platform where one side of a market, the job-seeker, the home-buyer, the B2B buyer, the person searching for a plumber or a used car, uses the service free. The other side, the recruiter, the builder or broker, the small supplier, the car dealer or the lender with repossessed vehicles to sell, pays to be found.

That simple split explains almost everything else. Because the paying side buys visibility and leads in advance, usually as a 12-month package, these companies collect cash before they recognise revenue. Because the product is software and a sales force rather than factories, the cash they collect mostly stays with them. Together the four reported FY26 revenue of ₹6,847 crore (+13.7%), operating profit before other income of ₹2,277 crore (a 33.3% margin), other income of ₹1,700 crore, and reported profit after tax (PAT) of ₹2,979 crore. They hold ₹16,022 crore of cash and treasury investments between them, and were worth ₹1,06,109 crore at the close on 23 October 2026.

Five forces now decide how these businesses are valued, and the rest of the report is organised around them.

  • The hiring cycle has changed shape. Naukri is about three-quarters of Info Edge's billings and nearly all of its operating profit. Recruitment billings grew 10% in FY26, 17.5% in Q1 FY27 (about 15% after renewal timing) and 12.6% in Q2 FY27. That growth is not coming from IT-services hiring volume, which is flat; it is coming from global capability centres (GCCs), price increases, paid services for job-seekers and new AI products.
  • AI search is now a line in the accounts. Info Edge's education portal Shiksha saw billings fall 27.2% in Q2 FY27, which management blamed on Google sending it less traffic. IndiaMART's "business enquiries delivered" have fallen from 610 million in FY21 to 370 million in FY26. Platforms that own proprietary supply, such as Naukri's CV database, 99acres listings and OLX's classifieds, show no damage so far.
  • The small-business customer base has stopped growing at IndiaMART. Paying suppliers were 218,000 in June 2026, about where they were in March 2025, and growth is coming entirely from higher revenue per supplier. Justdial, selling to similar customers, grew paid campaigns 5.2% in Q2 FY27.
  • The cash piles are large enough to matter. Other income was 46% of the group's FY26 profit before tax. Justdial's cash (₹6,114 crore) is larger than its market value (₹6,004 crore).
  • Info Edge is two companies. Its 12.38% of Eternal (Zomato's parent) and 12.42% of PB Fintech (Policybazaar) were worth about ₹43,861 crore at the 23 October close, 57% of Info Edge's ₹76,935 crore market value.

In plain terms

These are toll booths on the roads people use to find jobs, homes, suppliers, plumbers and used cars. The people searching pass through free; the businesses that want to be found pay a year's toll in advance. That makes the booths very profitable and very cash-rich. The question in 2026 is whether Google and ChatGPT are building a bypass, and the early answer is: for booths that only point the way, yes; for booths that own what is at the end of the road, not yet.

01

How the toll booth works: the free side and the paying side

Every member monetises the party that wants to be found. The product is visibility and leads; the price is set by how hard the payer finds it to go elsewhere.

A classifieds platform is a two-sided market. On one side are people looking for something: a job, a flat, a supplier of steel pipes, a dentist nearby, a second-hand Swift. On the other side are the people and businesses who want to be found by them. The platform's job is to gather enough of both that each side finds the other more easily there than anywhere else. Once that happens, the platform charges the side with the stronger economic motive. In India that is always the supply side: the recruiter who needs to fill a role, the builder who needs to sell flats, the small manufacturer who needs orders. Job-seekers, home-buyers and searchers pay little or nothing, because charging them would shrink the audience the paying side is buying.

The products differ by vertical but rhyme. Naukri sells employers access to Resdex, a searchable database of 118 million CVs, plus job postings, employer branding and newer AI tools such as AI-Rex, an automated recruiter priced at ₹3,500 per hiring mandate. 99acres sells builders project advertising and brokers listing packages. IndiaMART sells small suppliers Silver, Gold and Platinum subscriptions that place their catalogue in front of buyers and give them "BuyLeads" (buyer requirements they can respond to). Justdial sells local businesses priority placement in search results and leads from its app, website and phone line. CarTrade is the odd one out: it sells advertising and leads to car makers and dealers on CarWale and BikeWale, listing packages and buyer plans on OLX India, and runs physical-plus-online auctions through Shriram Automall, where banks and finance companies sell repossessed vehicles.

The price a platform can charge tracks how hard it is for the payer to go elsewhere. A recruiter hiring a mid-level engineer in Pune has few substitutes for searching 118 million Indian CVs, so Naukri earns about ₹1.46 lakh a year per billed employer and a recruitment margin near 58%. A small trader in Ludhiana can also get enquiries from Google Maps, WhatsApp groups and trade fairs, so IndiaMART earns ₹69,000 a year from its average paying supplier and Justdial about ₹21,000 per campaign, with operating margins of 26–36%. The rest of this report keeps returning to that gradient: proprietary supply and two-sided ownership command price; generic discovery does not.

In plain terms

The search side is the audience; the supply side is the customer. A classifieds company is only as strong as the paying side's lack of alternatives. Naukri's recruiters have few; IndiaMART's and Justdial's small businesses have many, which is why the same model earns 58% margins in one place and under 30% in another.

Two features of the model show up directly in the accounts. First, revenue is recognised over the life of a subscription, while cash arrives when it is sold, so the balance sheet carries a large "deferred revenue" liability: ₹1,965 crore at IndiaMART at March 2026, more than a year's revenue. Second, the cash collected in advance, plus profits that need little reinvestment, accumulates as treasury. Both features are examined in depth in section 14; the KPI masterclass in section 10 teaches how to read them.

02

A sliver of a ₹94,700 crore digital ad pool

The four members earn about 7% of what India spends on digital advertising, and most of the rest flows to Google and Meta. Their growth has slowed from the post-COVID surge to the low teens.

The broadest pool these companies draw from is Indian digital advertising and lead-generation spend. FICCI-EY's 2026 media report (released 24 March 2026) put India's total advertising at about ₹1.5 lakh crore in calendar 2025, up 13%, of which digital advertising was ₹94,700 crore (+26%), about 63% of the total. Within digital, search and social platforms led by Google and Meta took 64%, or roughly ₹60,600 crore. E-commerce and point-of-sale "retail media" took ₹22,000 crore, up 50% in a year. That leaves a residual of about ₹12,000 crore for everyone else, including publishers, classifieds and vertical apps. A separate tracker (dentsu-e4m) puts 2025 digital ad spend lower, at ₹71,621 crore, because it defines digital differently; this report uses FICCI-EY throughout.

Profit has grown faster than revenue because margins rose. In plain terms

These are not small businesses, but they are small next to Google and Meta, which each book several times the group's revenue from Indian advertisers. The members survive where they hold something the giants do not: a CV database, a property inventory, a supplier catalogue, a physical auction yard. Their growth has settled into the low teens, so the debate is now about margins and cash rather than about the size of the market.

03

Five demand engines, five different cycles

Hiring, housing, small-business health, the vehicle market and the ad market each drive one or more members. They rarely move together, which is why the group's revenue growth is steadier than any single engine.

Because each platform serves a different end-market, the members are exposed to different economic cycles. Naukri depends on white-collar hiring intent, 99acres on residential launches and unsold inventory, IndiaMART and Justdial on the number and health of small businesses willing to pay for leads, and CarTrade on car-maker marketing budgets, the used-vehicle trade and the flow of repossessed vehicles from lenders. Hiring: volume flat, value rising

Nasscom's FY26 review put India's technology industry at $315 billion of revenue (+6.1% on a restated base) with headcount of 59.5 lakh, up only 2.3%. Net hiring was about 1.35 lakh people, barely above FY25's 1.33 lakh and far below the 2021–22 boom. Naukri's own JobSpeak index of new job postings stood at 3,053 in September 2026, 2% above a year earlier, with AI and machine-learning roles up 20% and IT/software roles down 4%. Volume is flat. Section 04 explains why Naukri's billings have grown anyway, and why GCCs, not IT-services companies, now set the pace (for the IT-services side of the story, see the IT services report).

Housing: flat sales, rising inventory

Anarock's data for the top seven cities show a market that has plateaued at a high level. Sales in July–September 2026 were 1,00,220 units, 3% more than a year earlier; launches were 1,14,320 units, up 18%; unsold inventory reached 6,30,590 units, up 12%; and the average price rose 7% to ₹9,714 per square foot. Another tracker, PropEquity, which covers nine cities, shows sales down about 6%. For 99acres this mix is helpful: builders with unsold flats and fresh launches advertise harder than builders who are selling out. The catch is that NCR, 99acres' biggest market, saw sales fall. See the real estate report for the developers' side.

Small businesses: many, mostly unmonetised

India has more than six crore registered micro, small and medium enterprises. IndiaMART hosts 8.8 million supplier storefronts and Justdial 57.4 million business listings, so discovery is already broadly penetrated. Paying penetration is not: IndiaMART monetises about 2.5% of its storefronts and Justdial roughly 1.1% of its listings (calculated). The constraint is not reach but willingness to pay, which rises when small businesses are confident and falls when they are squeezed or when free alternatives such as Google Business Profile and WhatsApp get better. Section 12 examines why IndiaMART's paying count has stalled while Justdial's grows.

Vehicles: a used-car market growing faster than new

CRISIL Ratings expects India's used-car market to grow 7–9% in FY27 to more than 7 million units, implying about 6.4–6.5 million in FY26, against 4.64 million new passenger vehicles sold in FY26. That ratio of about 1.4 used cars per new car compares with 2.5–3.5 in mature markets, so the used side has more room to grow. The organised share of used-car sales has risen to about 26% from 20–21% in FY22. CarTrade earns from every stage: research on CarWale, listings and buyer plans on OLX, and auctions of repossessed and fleet vehicles at Shriram Automall, whose volumes rise when vehicle-loan defaults rise (see the NBFC report for the lenders' side). Two-wheelers matter through BikeWale; the two-wheelers report covers that market.

The diversification across these engines is real. In FY26 a flat hiring market (for volume), a flat housing market (for sales) and a stalled SME paying base still produced 13.7% group revenue growth, because pricing at Naukri, inventory-driven advertising at 99acres and the used-vehicle cycle at CarTrade offset one another. The risk is the one factor common to all five engines: dependence on search traffic, which section 11 takes up.

In plain terms

No single economic cycle moves this group. When hiring slows, property launches may be rising; when small businesses are cautious, used-car trading may be booming. What every engine shares is a reliance on people searching online, and on Google sending those searchers to the members' sites. That common thread is the real cyclical risk.

04

Hiring moved to the GCCs, and Naukri moved with it

IT-services volume is flat, yet Naukri's billings re-accelerated in 2026. The growth is a third price, a third new products and a third volume, and the volume now comes from global capability centres and smaller cities.

For two decades the simplest way to forecast Naukri was to watch Infosys, TCS and Wipro. When the big IT-services firms hired, Naukri's billings boomed; when they froze, Naukri's growth stalled. That link has weakened. Indian IT services added very few people in FY24–FY26, yet Naukri's recruitment billings grew 10% in FY26 and then 17.5% in Q1 FY27 and 12.6% in Q2 FY27. Understanding why is the single most important input into Info Edge's operating value, because recruitment produced ₹1,277 crore of FY26 operating profit against a standalone total of ₹1,138 crore: the other verticals together lost money.

Where the billings come from

Info Edge's FY26 annual report breaks India B2B recruitment billings down by customer type. Margins: the operating leverage is large

Because almost all of Naukri's costs are people and marketing that do not scale with billings, extra revenue drops to profit at a very high rate. Recruitment revenue rose from ₹541.5 crore in Q1 FY26 to ₹611.8 crore in Q1 FY27 (+13.0%), while segment operating profit rose from ₹284.3 crore to ₹356.4 crore (+25.4%). What could break the pattern

Three things would hurt. First, a GCC slowdown. GCCs are now the marginal buyer, so US politics on tariffs, visas or offshoring would hit Naukri harder than another IT-services slump. Second, mid-tier hiring volume, which management called "the joker in the pack": JobSpeak at +2% says postings are flat, and price increases cannot outrun flat volume forever. Third, AI substitution in sourcing itself, where large employers use AI agents on LinkedIn or their own applicant-tracking systems instead of searching Resdex. Info Edge's answer is to sell those AI tools itself (AI-Rex, the Zwayam applicant-tracking system) and to rely on owning the CV database. Management's own view, stated on the Q1 call, is that where Info Edge owns both sides of a marketplace AI strengthens it, and where it depends on traffic from a channel it does not own, AI hurts. Naukri is the clearest example of the first case. Shiksha, discussed in section 11, is the clearest example of the second.

The new labour codes, in force from 21 November 2025, add a small long-run positive. They triggered one-off gratuity charges across the group, but by pushing employers toward formal contracts they may lift the share of hiring that runs through formal channels such as Naukri. Nothing in the FY26 or Q1 FY27 numbers yet shows that effect.

Where ₹100 goes: people, not plant

There is no factory and almost no capex. Salaries for salespeople and engineers are the dominant cost, marketing is second, and how much of the remainder becomes profit depends on whether the platform must sell door to door.

A classifieds company's cost base looks nothing like an industrial company's. There are no raw materials, almost no physical assets (CarTrade's auction yards are the only exception) and little depreciation. The biggest cost is people: engineers and product staff who build the platform, and above all the sales and service teams who sign up and renew paying customers. IndiaMART employed 6,066 people in June 2026, about 5,400 of them in sales and servicing; Justdial had 13,337 employees in June 2026, of whom 10,965 were in sales. Employee cost was 44% of IndiaMART's FY26 revenue, 39% of CarTrade's and 38.85% of Info Edge's standalone revenue. The second cost is marketing, and here the companies diverge sharply. Info Edge spent ₹380.8 crore on advertising and promotion in FY26, 12.5% of standalone revenue, on TV and digital campaigns for Naukri and 99acres. CarTrade spent ₹32.7 crore (4.2%), Justdial about 3.4% (₹11.6 crore in Q2 FY27), and IndiaMART only ₹7–8 crore a quarter (roughly 2%), targeted at its most monetisable categories.

The sales force is the capacity

With no plant, the capacity analogue in this industry is the sales force and the supply of leads per paying customer. Winner takes the vertical; number two pays for the privilege

In each vertical one platform earns the rent and the rest fight for share at a loss. Info Edge's own segments prove it: the same company earns 57% in recruitment and loses money in property and matrimony.

Classifieds markets concentrate because of network effects. Job-seekers go where the jobs are, so recruiters go where the job-seekers are, which brings more jobs, and so on. Once one platform has the most supply and the most searchers in a vertical, rivals struggle to catch up even with heavy spending, and the leader can raise prices without losing customers. The result, in India as abroad, is winner-take-most: one dominant platform per vertical, earning high margins, with second and third players earning little or losing money. Data, competition and tax: the rules that matter

No regulation threatens the FY27 P&L. The one to model is India's data-protection law, whose consent rules arrive in May 2027 and touch the heart of the lead-sharing model. The one that would help, a competition law aimed at Google, is on the backburner.

Classifieds platforms are lightly regulated compared with banks or telecom companies. They need no licence to operate, they set their own prices, and their main legal status is that of an "intermediary" under the Information Technology Act, 2000, which gives them safe harbour for content posted by users provided they follow due-diligence rules. But four areas of policy matter: data protection, platform rules, competition and tax. Each is summarised here, with the one real change ahead flagged.

Data protection: the DPDP Rules and lead-sharing

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 received assent on 11 August 2023. Its rules were notified on 14 November 2025 (the gazette notification is dated 13 November) with an 18-month phased start, according to the Press Information Bureau's explainer. The Data Protection Board, which has four members and whose decisions can be appealed to the telecom appellate tribunal (TDSAT), came into being at once. The framework for "consent managers", Indian companies with a net worth of at least ₹2 crore that let users manage consents across services, starts at 12 months, around 13–14 November 2026. The substantive duties, covering notice, consent standards, security safeguards, breach reporting, children's data and the extra obligations of "Significant Data Fiduciaries", apply from around 13–14 May 2027. Penalties run up to ₹250 crore for failing to protect data, ₹200 crore for breach-notification and children's-data failures, and ₹50 crore for other breaches.

Why it matters here more than in most sectors: these businesses trade in personal data. Naukri holds 118 million CVs with names, phone numbers and employment histories. Jeevansathi holds sensitive matrimonial data. IndiaMART and Justdial distribute buyers' and searchers' phone numbers to paying businesses as "leads"; that is the product. Naukri and Jeevansathi could be designated Significant Data Fiduciaries, requiring a data-protection officer, impact assessments and audits. Our reading is that compliance cost is modest relative to these companies' margins. The larger risk, which is an estimate rather than anything the companies have quantified, is lead volume: if consent screens make buyers less willing to share contact details, fewer leads reach paying suppliers.

In plain terms

When you post a requirement on IndiaMART or call Justdial for a plumber, your phone number is what the platform sells to several businesses. From May 2027 the law requires clear permission for that. The companies can comply, but every extra consent click risks fewer people sharing their number, and fewer numbers means fewer leads for the businesses that pay.

Platform rules: intermediaries, fraud and fake listings

All four rely on the IT Act's section 79 safe harbour and must follow the IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021: a grievance officer, takedown timelines and due diligence on listings. The compliance flashpoints are fake job postings and recruitment fraud on Naukri, scams and fraudulent listings on OLX, Justdial and IndiaMART, and display of RERA registration numbers on property project ads on 99acres. Maharashtra's and Uttar Pradesh's real-estate regulators have penalised portals for listing unregistered projects. MeitY proposed rules on labelling AI-generated content in late 2025; their final status was not confirmed in our sources. Trust tools are therefore a competitive weapon as well as a compliance cost: IndiaMART says 99% of its paid sellers are email, phone and GST-verified and it offers buyer payment protection of up to ₹5 lakh for TrustSEAL sellers.

Competition: the Digital Competition Bill would help, but it is parked

The Ministry of Corporate Affairs published a draft Digital Competition Bill in March 2024. It would impose upfront conduct rules on "Systemically Significant Digital Enterprises", with thresholds such as at least one crore end-users or 10,000 business users, targeting gatekeepers like Google, Meta and Amazon for self-preferencing and data misuse. In August 2025 Parliament's Standing Committee on Finance urged an ex-ante regime, but by July 2026 the Bill was reported to be "on the backburner", with the Competition Commission considering a fresh market study of the digital economy. None of the four members would be caught by the thresholds; they would be beneficiaries, because the Bill's main target is search self-preferencing, and Google's AI answers are exactly that from a portal's point of view. Precedent exists: a 2018 Competition Commission order fined Google ₹135.86 crore for search bias in a case Matrimony.com helped bring. Today, though, the members' complaint that AI Overviews use their content and keep users on Google has no Indian regulatory remedy. The Competition Commission's deal-value threshold of ₹2,000 crore (from September 2024) is not a constraint at the members' acquisition sizes.

Tax: three points worth knowing

  • GST on online services is 18%. Most of IndiaMART's and Justdial's paying customers are GST-registered and claim input credit, so GST is largely neutral; unregistered micro-merchants bear it as a cost.
  • Equalisation levy: the 6% levy on online advertising paid to non-residents such as Google and Meta was abolished from 1 April 2025. That slightly lowers the cost of advertising with the foreign giants, a small negative for domestic ad-funded portals.
  • Capital gains at Info Edge: listed long-term gains are taxed at 12.5% plus surcharge and cess, about 14.95% for a company on our estimate. A full sale of the Eternal and PB Fintech stakes (about ₹43,861 crore of value on about ₹714 crore of cost) would carry roughly ₹6,451 crore of tax. That is one quantifiable part of the holding-company discount discussed in section 13. Treasury income at all four is taxed at the corporate rate (about 25.2%), so a 7% pre-tax debt-fund yield is about 5.2% after tax.

Thirty years, five cycles

From phone directories and export catalogues to apps, IPOs and AI search: each era changed who could reach the searcher, and each one rearranged the winners.

All four members were founded in India's first internet wave and survived every cycle since. Their history explains three features of today's group: why Justdial still has a phone number as a brand, why Info Edge owns stakes in Zomato and Policybazaar, and why CarTrade owns a physical auction network and a C2C marketplace bought from a global group. The cycle drivers have been consistent: hiring for Naukri, property for 99acres, small-business health and Google's traffic for IndiaMART and Justdial, the vehicle and lending cycle for CarTrade, equity markets for Info Edge's portfolio, and bond yields for everyone's treasury income.

The five cycles in numbers

Web 1.0 survival (1996–2005). These companies were founded on business models that did not need mass internet use: a phone line (Justdial's 8888888888), a printed and then online export catalogue (IndiaMART), a job site for the few hundred thousand Indians with email (Naukri). That is why they survived the 2000 crash that killed many better-funded rivals.

First listings and the venture book (2006–2013). Info Edge's IPO in 2006 gave it cash it did not need for its core business. Rather than return it, Sanjeev Bikhchandani and Hitesh Oberoi seeded Policybazaar and Zomato. The ₹4.7 crore Zomato cheque of 2010 is now a stake worth about ₹38,025 crore. Justdial's 2013 IPO marked the high point of the horizontal local-search model.

Mobile disruption (2014–2019). Smartphones and Google Maps changed local discovery: a search for "dentist near me" now ended on a Google map with reviews and a call button, not on a directory page. Justdial's revenue grew only about 10% a year from FY15 to FY20 and its margins swung. IndiaMART went the other way, building a B2B supplier network that Google could not easily replicate, turning profitable and listing in 2019.

COVID shock and the IPO boom (2020–2022). Hiring froze, then boomed. IndiaMART's operating margin peaked at 49% in FY21 because lockdowns cut sales costs. Zomato's and PB Fintech's listings in 2021 turned Info Edge's portfolio into tens of thousands of crores of listed value; the "deemed disposal" accounting on Zomato's IPO produced an FY22 PAT of ₹12,882 crore, almost all of it a non-cash gain. Reliance bought control of Justdial, and CarTrade's private-equity owners sold their shares in its IPO.

In plain terms

Every decade has brought a new way for people to find things, and each one has hurt whichever member depended most on the old way. The KPI masterclass: reading a classifieds company Deep dive

Fourteen numbers decide whether a classifieds platform is getting stronger or weaker. Most of them are not on the face of the P&L, and several are defined differently by each company.

Revenue and profit are lagging, blended numbers for these businesses. Revenue is the slow release of cash collected months earlier; profit mixes the operating business with interest on a cash pile and, at Info Edge, the marks on a venture portfolio. To see what is actually happening you need the native measures: how much cash was billed this quarter, how many customers are paying, what each pays, how many leave, how much of the profit comes from treasury, and what the market is paying for the operating business once the cash and stakes are stripped out. This section teaches each one with a formula, a worked example, benchmarks, how the four members compare, and the trap to avoid.

The equation: from paying customers to profit

Every classifieds P&L can be written as two linked equations. The first turns customers into revenue. The number of paying customers times the average annual revenue per customer gives annual billings, the cash invoiced for subscriptions sold. Revenue is billings minus the part not yet earned, which goes into deferred revenue on the balance sheet. The second equation turns revenue into profit: subtract the cost of the sales force, engineers and marketing to get operating profit, then add treasury income on the cash pile to get profit before tax.

In plain terms

Count the paying customers, multiply by what each pays, and you have the cash coming in. Revenue is that cash, released slowly. Profit is revenue minus salaries and marketing, plus interest on the money in the bank. Everything else in this section is a way of checking each of those pieces.

1. Billings (collections) versus revenue

What it is. Billings, which IndiaMART and Justdial call collections, is the value of subscriptions invoiced or cash collected in a period. Revenue is what the accounting standards allow to be recognised in that period, spread over the subscription's life. Formula: revenue = billings − increase in deferred revenue. Worked example: a supplier buys a ₹1,20,000 annual package on 1 March. Billings that quarter are ₹1,20,000; revenue is ₹10,000 for March, and ₹30,000 in each of the next three quarters, with ₹20,000 in the quarter after that. Why it matters: billings lead revenue by one to three quarters. When billings grow faster than revenue, revenue growth will rise; when they grow slower, it will fall.

How the members compare. Info Edge's standalone billings grew 10.3% in FY26 while revenue grew 15.0%: revenue was still riding FY25's billings, and the slowdown in billings was a warning. In Q1 FY27 the order flipped, with billings +14.4% and revenue +12.0%, so revenue growth should pick up. IndiaMART's consolidated collections grew 14.2% in FY26 against revenue +13.0%, but in Q1 FY27 collections slowed to +8% while revenue grew 11%, the opposite signal. Justdial's Q2 FY27 collections grew 11.7% against revenue +13.1%. Benchmarks: good is billings growth above 15% and at least as fast as revenue; average is 8–15% with the gap within two points; weak is below 8% or well below revenue growth.

3. Paying customers

What it is. The number of businesses paying for a subscription at period end. Formula: closing payers = opening payers + gross adds − churned payers. Worked example: a platform starting the year with 220,000 payers that signs 60,000 new ones and loses 62,000 ends at 218,000; the net change of −2,000 hides 122,000 businesses moving in or out. Why it matters: paying customers are the volume engine; ARPU can rise for a few years on its own, but not forever.

How the members compare. IndiaMART's paying suppliers rose from 152,000 in FY21 to 203,000 in FY23 and then stalled: 214,000 (FY24), 217,000 (FY25), 220,000 (FY26) and 218,000 at June 2026, after a net loss of 1,852 in Q1 FY27 and about 1,200 in Q4 FY26. Justdial's paid campaigns rose 5.2% year on year to 656,520 at September 2026, accelerating from +3.5% in June. Naukri's India B2B billed customers rose about 14% in FY26 to about 146,000, mostly smaller tier-2 and tier-3 firms. CarTrade does not report a comparable paying count. Benchmarks: net growth above 8% is good, 2–8% average, under 2% weak.

How to read a classifieds company's quarterly results in ten minutes

  1. Billings or collections growth, year on year (Info Edge's operating update, IndiaMART's and Justdial's results). This is the leading indicator. Red flag: below 8%, or more than two points below revenue growth.
  2. Paying customers and their net change (IndiaMART suppliers, Justdial campaigns, Naukri billed customers). Red flag: a second or third negative quarter.
  3. ARPU or revenue per customer. Healthy with volume growth; a warning when it is the only engine.
  4. Deferred revenue versus a year ago. It should grow at least as fast as revenue. Red flag: flat deferred with rising revenue (Justdial in Sep-26).
  5. Operating profit and margin, before other income, and the segment table (Naukri's margin, 99acres' loss, CarTrade's three segments). Red flag: revenue up, operating profit flat for several quarters.
  6. Other income, and whether it includes mark-to-market gains or losses. Strip it out and re-read the profit growth.
  7. Exceptional items and minorities. Note labour-code charges, deemed gains, and the gap between consolidated and attributable PAT.
  8. Traffic and funnel data: unique visitors by channel, enquiries, time-share, app downloads. Red flag: falling mobile and app engagement, or enquiries falling faster than paying customers.
  9. Cash and capital returns. Treasury growth, dividends or buybacks. Red flag: cash building with no payout and no stated use.
  10. Management commentary on AI and competition. Listen for any mention of Google, referral traffic or LLMs, and for how new AI products are priced. For Info Edge also note total comprehensive income (the Eternal and PB marks).
In plain terms

Start with cash billed and customers paying, not with revenue and profit. Strip out interest income before judging the business. Check whether the company is using a different "EBITDA" from its peers. Then ask what the market is paying once the cash and investments are taken away. Those four steps will tell you more than the headline profit growth in any of these companies' results.

AI ate the search box? A zero-click scorecard Deep dive

AI answers are now visible in two members' numbers. The damage follows one rule, stated by Info Edge's own management: platforms that own both sides of their market are strengthened; platforms that borrowed their traffic from Google are hurt.

For twenty years Google was these companies' best friend. A searcher typed "MBA colleges in Pune", "steel pipe suppliers in Ludhiana" or "AC repair near me", clicked a blue link, and landed on Shiksha, IndiaMART or Justdial, where the portal turned the visit into a lead it could sell. Generative AI changes that flow. Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, and chatbots such as ChatGPT and Gemini, increasingly answer the question on the results page, so the searcher never clicks. The industry calls this "zero-click". For a business whose entire revenue depends on visits turning into leads, zero-click is the most important structural risk in a generation, and in 2026 it began to show up in reported numbers.

What the global evidence says

No India-specific zero-click statistic is publicly available. The global data are consistent, though from varied and partly secondary sources. Chartbeat data reported by Press Gazette showed Google search referrals to publishers falling about 33% in the year to November 2025. Ahrefs found that the top organic result loses about 34.5% of its clicks when an AI Overview appears above it. A small sample of Google AI Mode sessions found about three-quarters ended with no click to any external site. Cloudflare data showed human traffic to finance, publishing and retail sites falling about 40% between June 2025 and April 2026. On the other side, Google said in June 2025 that AI Overviews drive more than a 10% increase in usage for the query types where they appear, in both the US and India, and it has launched AI Mode in India in English. More searching, fewer clicks out: that is exactly the combination that hurts a portal living on referrals.

Two questions decide exposure: how much of the platform's traffic arrives from third-party discovery for informational queries, and how much proprietary supply or two-sided ownership it controls.

How the members are responding: sell AI to the paying side

Every member's response has the same shape: rather than fight AI on the free side, sell AI tools to the side that pays. Info Edge sells AI-Rex (₹3,500 per mandate; 400-plus paid customers in Q1 FY27 and about 300 more in July), Talent and Salary Pulse (600–700 customers) and AI services for job-seekers inside Naukri 360. Justdial is building an AI CRM and lead-management suite, AI responses to reviews, WhatsApp-based onboarding and quotations, and a conversational request-for-quote flow on JD Mart that it says produces fully AI-qualified leads; AI, automation and B2B took about a third of its incremental Q2 FY27 spending. CarTrade runs VAYA AI agents for matching, pricing and condition assessment, built on small models trained on its own data, and an AI assistant across a 17-step new-car buying journey. IndiaMART runs an agentic voice call centre that handles more than one lakh calls a day (the manual centre managed about 80,000), used to verify buyers and capture intent rather than to cut costs, and uses AI for content and trust checks.

On whether to let AI systems use their data, the members are ambivalent. IndiaMART's Agarwal called it a "two-way sword": blocking AI crawlers risks being left out of AI answers, while exposing data gives "very little in return". There is an upside scenario in which these platforms become the supply layer that AI agents must query: an agent asked to find a CNC machine supplier or a flat in Whitefield still needs current, verified listings, and the members hold 132 million product listings (IndiaMART), 118 million CVs (Naukri) and 1.3 million property listings (99acres). No licensing deal had been announced by October 2026.

The analogues abroad

Abroad, the pattern matches the framework. Chegg, the US homework-help company, lost most of its value between 2023 and 2025 as students switched to ChatGPT; it is the Shiksha analogue. Yelp, the closest analogue to Justdial, has guided to slower advertising growth partly on changes in search. Classifieds with proprietary inventory, such as SEEK in recruitment and Rightmove and REA Group in property, have so far held up, though Rightmove is spending more on AI and guiding operating profit growth below revenue growth for 2026. The evidence supports the proprietary-supply argument for Naukri and 99acres, and warns against assuming that horizontal discovery businesses can simply wait it out.

In plain terms

AI search is not hurting the whole sector equally. It is hurting the portals that were essentially a well-organised index of public information. The question for 2027 is whether Google's AI Mode, in Hindi and other Indian languages, starts answering "find me a supplier", "find me a plumber" and "find me a flat" as well as it now answers "which college is best". If it does, IndiaMART and Justdial will feel what Shiksha is feeling now.

What to watch: Shiksha's billings in Info Edge's Q2 results (expected around mid-November) and Q3 update (early January); IndiaMART's unique business enquiries and active buyers on 27 October; Justdial's mobile visitors and app downloads in January; any Google announcement extending AI Mode to Indian languages; and any member signing a data-licensing deal with an AI company.

IndiaMART has stopped adding paying suppliers and grows by charging each one more; Justdial is adding campaigns but spending its margin to do it. The market values the first at 19 times earnings and the second at less than its cash.

IndiaMART and Justdial sell to the same kind of customer: India's millions of small manufacturers, traders and service businesses who want more enquiries. Both sell prepaid subscriptions through large field and telephone sales teams. Both face the same free alternatives: Google Business Profile, Google Maps, WhatsApp Business and, increasingly, AI answers. Yet in 2026 their paying-customer counts moved in opposite directions, and so did their strategies.

The valuation paradox

The market's verdict is harsh on both but harsher on Justdial. IndiaMART trades at 19.2 times trailing earnings and about 12.5 times EV to operating profit, at its 52-week low and down 34% in a year, with domestic institutions selling (from 15.49% in March 2025 to 9.61% in September 2026). Why Justdial is cheaper despite growing faster: capital allocation and control. Justdial has paid no dividend since FY16 and has never bought back shares, so its cash compounds inside the company at a post-tax yield of about 5%. Related-party dealings with Reliance are negligible (₹1.4 crore of services bought from Jio entities in FY26; no revenue from Reliance), so five years after the takeover there is no visible commercial integration. The new Reliance-appointed team raises the odds of a decision on the cash, whether a payout, an integration or a corporate action, but nothing has been announced. IndiaMART, by contrast, has returned about ₹1,650 crore cumulatively through dividends and buybacks and paid out 76% of FY26 profit.

The common risk to both is that the AI-and-Google squeeze described in section 11 lowers the value of the leads they sell. IndiaMART's evidence is in its enquiry funnel; Justdial's in its app. The common opportunity is that small businesses still need someone to verify buyers, manage enquiries and handle payments, and both are building software and finance products to do that. IndiaMART's Busy and Justdial's merchant tools are the bets to watch.

Billings, float and treasury: the cash machine and what it is used for Deep dive

Customers prepay, costs are mostly salaries, and almost nothing needs to be built. The result is ₹16,022 crore of cash, 46% of group profit coming from treasury income, and very different answers to the question of what to do with it.

The prepaid-subscription model has a financial side that matters as much as the operating side. When a small supplier pays IndiaMART ₹1,20,000 in March for a two-year Platinum package, IndiaMART has the cash immediately but earns the revenue over 24 months. Multiply that by hundreds of thousands of customers and the company runs on its customers' money: working capital is deeply negative (IndiaMART's working-capital days were −301 at FY26 on screener, Justdial's −182) and cash builds up faster than profit. That float, plus high margins and almost no capital spending, is why three of the four members hold cash worth a large share of their market value.

CarWale and BikeWale are where Indians research new cars and bikes. Car makers buy display advertising and leads; dealers buy leads and dealer-management software. Consumer still enjoys tax shelters for "a couple of years", per management.

Remarketing: auctions of repossessed and used vehicles

Shriram Automall India Ltd (SAMIL) runs physical-plus-online auctions where banks, finance companies, fleet owners and car makers' captive financiers sell vehicles, mostly repossessed or end-of-lease, to dealers. It handled about 1.7 million auction listings and more than 2.5 lakh vehicles in FY26 with about 2 lakh registered buyers, across a network that, together with abSure and OLX outlets, gives CarTrade 500-plus physical locations. Revenue per vehicle sold works out to about ₹10,400–11,500 (₹259 crore ÷ 2.25–2.5 lakh vehicles, an INDICATIVE calculation). About 45% of the mix is retail and 41% repossession. Because repossessions rise when borrowers default, remarketing is partly counter-cyclical: it does well when the vehicle-finance cycle turns, which hedges the Consumer business (see the NBFC report).

Two ownership facts matter. CarTrade owns 55.43% of SAMIL; Shriram Finance owns the other 44.57% and is also SAMIL's largest institutional seller. Shareholders approved related-party transaction limits of ₹362.6 crore for SAMIL's dealings with Shriram Finance and ₹81 crore for CarTrade Exchange's. The relationship guarantees volume but concentrates risk in one lender, and about 45% of SAMIL's profit belongs to minority shareholders: of FY26 consolidated PAT of ₹243.5 crore, about ₹223 crore was attributable to CarTrade's shareholders.

In plain terms

When a borrower stops paying for a truck or a car, the lender takes it back and needs to sell it. Shriram Automall is where many of those vehicles are auctioned to dealers. CarTrade earns a fee each time, and the busiest seller is also its partner in the business, which is good for volume and a risk if that partner ever changes its mind.

CarTrade bought OLX India from Prosus in August 2023 for about ₹535.5 crore. In FY26 OLX earned revenue of ₹217.6 crore and EBITDA of ₹67.1 crore (31%), and in Q1 FY27 revenue grew 29% and EBITDA 76%. A reading guide

  • Growth is uniform; margins are not. Every subgroup grew revenue 10–16% in FY26 and about 11–12% in Q1 FY27. What separates the members is operating leverage: in Q1 FY27 Info Edge's operating profit grew about 31% and CarTrade's 43%, against IndiaMART's 11% and Justdial's about 1%.
  • Treasury dominates the bottom of the table. Other income was 46% of group PBT and 41% for the SME lead-gen businesses. Read PAT growth only after removing it: ex-Info Edge PAT fell 5% in FY26 mainly because treasury income fell.
  • Reported vs attributable PAT. The gap is ₹334 Cr for the group, almost all at Info Edge (₹313 Cr) and CarTrade (₹21 Cr). Use attributable PAT for P/E; the combined attributable P/E is about 40×.
  • The headline multiples mislead in opposite directions. Info Edge's EV/OP of 60.8× includes ₹43,861 Cr of stakes; strip them and it is about 24×.   • Return on equity is distorted by cash and marks. IndiaMART's 20.7% is the only figure close to the operating reality; Info Edge's 1.8% reflects equity swollen by fair-value gains, Justdial's 3.4% a cash pile earning bond yields.

The global lens: what mature classifieds look like

Abroad, the number-one classifieds platform in a vertical earns 55–70% margins. Naukri fits the template; IndiaMART and Justdial look more like Yelp, and are valued like it.

Global classifieds offer a useful map of where India's members could end up. In mature markets the leading platform in a vertical, whether jobs, property or cars, typically earns operating margins of 55–70%, because once it is the default destination it needs little marketing and can raise prices every year. Auto Trader in the UK earned a 63% group operating margin and 70% in its core marketplace in the year to March 2025, with revenue per retailer of £2,854 a month, up 5%. Rightmove, the UK property portal, earns an underlying margin of about 70%. REA Group in Australia earns roughly 55–60% in its home market. Horizontal local-search and lead-generation businesses earn far less: Yelp's margin is around 25%. Matrimony.com, India's listed matrimony leader, earned only 11% on ₹460 crore of FY26 revenue, which shows how hard it is to earn rent in a vertical with two or three strong players.

Naukri's management has said it does not want to slow AI investment either. Where AI substitutes for the product (Chegg in education), the damage abroad has been severe; where the platform owns inventory (SEEK, Rightmove, REA), it has so far been limited to higher costs. That is the same split this report draws among the Indian members.

Bull vs bear for the sub-sector

The bull case rests on proprietary supply, operating leverage and cash; the bear case on AI search, a stalled SME base and capital that may never reach shareholders.

The two cases below are for the sub-sector as a whole. Individual members sit at different points: Info Edge and CarTrade lean on the bull arguments, IndiaMART and Justdial are where the bear arguments bite hardest. Both sides are argued on the evidence in this report, with figures from FY26 and the latest quarter.

The bull case

  • Proprietary supply survives AI. Naukri's 118 million CVs, 99acres' 1.3 million listings, OLX's sellers and Shriram Automall's yards cannot be replicated by an AI answer; Naukri, 99acres and OLX grew billings or revenue 12–29% in the latest quarter.
  • Operating leverage is large. Naukri's incremental margin was about 100% in Q1 FY27; CarTrade's FY26 incremental margin 77%; group operating profit grew 22% in Q1 FY27 on 12% more revenue.
  • Hiring has a new engine. GCCs, price, Naukri 360 and AI products have re-accelerated recruitment billings without IT-services volume.
  • 99acres can break even. A 51% web time-share, a retreating rival (Housing.com sold) and rising launches give Info Edge a large potential profit swing.
  • Cash is a cushion and an option. ₹16,022 Cr of treasury; any payout or buyback at Justdial or IndiaMART would re-rate shares priced below or near their cash.
  • Cheap where it is weak. The SME lead-gen businesses trade at about 7× EV to operating profit; Info Edge's core at 22–34× post-tax earnings.

The bear case

  • AI search is spreading. Shiksha −27.2%; IndiaMART enquiries delivered down 39% in five years; Justdial app downloads −22%. If AI Mode reaches Indian languages, "find me a supplier/plumber" goes next.
  • The SME base has stopped growing. IndiaMART suppliers flat for over two years with ~7% monthly Silver churn; growth relies on price, which cannot rise forever.
  • Profits lean on the bond market. 46% of group PBT is other income, marked to market; a rate rise cuts profit with no change in the business.
  • Capital may stay trapped. Justdial has paid nothing for a decade under a 74% promoter; CarTrade pays nothing; Info Edge recycles into start-ups.
  • Valuation risk at the top. CarTrade at 57.6× trailing earnings prices its own ₹1,000 Cr plan; three flat quarters of revenue are a warning.
  • Info Edge is a bet on Eternal. 57% of its value moves with two other share prices; PB Fintech's halving already cost about ₹5,450 Cr of look-through value.

Weighing them: the evidence so far supports the proprietary-supply argument for Naukri, 99acres and OLX, and supports the bear argument for informational portals (Shiksha clearly; IndiaMART and Justdial probably). The cash cushion is real but its value to minority shareholders depends on decisions only controlling owners can make. The biggest single uncertainty for the sub-sector is not the hiring or housing cycle but the speed at which AI answers spread to transactional, local and Indian-language queries.

In plain terms

If you believe owning the inventory protects you from AI, the strong members look reasonably priced and the weak ones look cheap. If you believe AI will eventually answer almost every search, the weak ones are value traps and even Naukri's growth may slow. The next four quarters of enquiry, traffic and billing data will move that debate more than any results headline.
